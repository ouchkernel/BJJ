# Hidden Hand Principle — Quick Reference

## The point

One arm attacking vs. multiple defensive tools = a deficit. Hiding your hands from your opponent's awareness is one way to close that gap.

## Three manifestations

1. **Before the attack** — hide the hand (e.g., under your own pectoral) so your opponent forgets to track it. Reveal it into the strangle the moment he's distracted (chin rises).
2. **After penetrating the neck** — hide the thumb line behind the opponent's neck. Conceding the wrist is fine; the thumb line is what actually stops the strangle if grabbed. Cover with your chin if needed.
3. **During transitions** — never grab your own bicep (exposes the frame hand). Use the cross-wrist method: elbow in front of his shoulder, wrists crossed, hand to bicep, fingers to opposite shoulder. Neither hand gets exposed.

## Action steps

1. Hands should always be either on top of the opponent's hands, or hidden — never just sitting exposed.
2. Play concealment against revealing — hide, wait for distraction, then commit the hand decisively.
3. Once penetrated: thumb line behind the neck, always.
4. During any hand transition: cross-wrist method, never bicep-grab.

## Don't

Don't rest your strangling hand on the opponent's shoulder once penetrated — that exposes the thumb line and lets him pull you down.
