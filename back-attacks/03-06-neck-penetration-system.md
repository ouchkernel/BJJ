# Neck Penetration System

## Overview

Every hand-fighting sequence in the previous chapters builds toward the same outcome: an uncontested strangle hand, with both of the opponent's arms neutralized (one by a leg, one by a hand). This chapter covers what to actually do with that uncontested hand — how to penetrate the space under a tucked, resisting jawline and get your strangling arm into finishing position. The central metaphor is a knife: the thin edge enters first, the thick spine follows after.

---

## The problem: your wrist is too wide

- The measurement of success or failure here is the width of your wrist. If you can get your wrist underneath your training partner's jawline, you can strangle him.
- The problem: any reasonably athletic person can tuck his chin to his chest, and that gap is physically smaller than the width of your wrist.
- Both of his arms are pinned at this point, so he will use everything else available — chin tucked hard to chest — to prevent you penetrating under the jaw.

## The knife metaphor

- A knife has a thin, sharp edge and a much thicker spine. The thin edge creates the opening; the thick spine only gets through afterward, gradually, through the space the edge already created.
- You would never turn a knife around and try to cut with the thick spine directly — it's too thick to make the initial opening.
- The same logic applies to your penetrating hand: you cannot start with your (relatively thick) wrist and force it under a tucked chin. You must lead with progressively thinner parts of your hand, in order, and let the thicker parts follow through the opening already made.

## The sequence

1. **Thumb knuckle first** — the single knuckle of your thumb is the thinnest part of your hand available; it's the "edge of the blade."
2. **Flattened fist** — not a punching fist, but a fist with the knuckles straightened out, which keeps a thin cross-section as it follows the thumb knuckle in.
3. **Extended, flattened hand** — once the fist has entered, the hand opens and walks through, eventually letting the full wrist penetrate.
4. **Elbow follows** — the instant the wrist gets through, the elbow follows immediately behind it.

## Step-by-step mechanics

1. Position the thumb knuckle behind the opponent's ear and find the curve of the jaw line (the jaw goes in a curve, then a straight line).
2. Go behind that curve and wedge the thumb knuckle underneath the jaw, strongly.
3. Flatten the fist once the wedge is set.
4. Separate the hands and **finger-walk** across the opponent's chest — the same mechanical principle used to finger-walk an arm up from mount, using finger strength to generate movement against resistance, just applied horizontally across the chest here.
5. Continue finger-walking until the wrist penetrates.
6. The moment the wrist is through, the elbow follows around the corner.
7. Hide your thumb line behind your training partner's neck once you're through — don't leave it exposed.

## Common mistakes

- Extending the hand too early, before the initial thumb-knuckle wedge is set — exposes fingers to being grabbed by the opponent's free hand (rules aside, "some people play fast with the rules").
- Trying to lead with the wrist directly instead of the thumb knuckle — it's physically too thick to get through a tucked jawline.
- Leaving the thumb line exposed after penetrating — if the opponent gets access to it, you have to start the whole penetration over again.

## The general principle

- There is no strangulation without some exposure of the opponent's jawline — described as directly analogous to "no heel hook without heel exposure." (Exceptions to this rule exist and are addressed later in the course, but the general case governs most situations.)
- If you cannot penetrate the jawline, everything else accomplished in the back system up to that point is worthless.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Success is measured by whether your wrist can get under the jawline — and the tucked-chin gap is naturally narrower than your wrist.
- Use the knife metaphor: lead with the thinnest part (thumb knuckle), not the thickest (wrist).
- Sequence: thumb knuckle wedge under the jaw → flatten the fist → finger-walk across the chest → wrist penetrates → elbow follows immediately.
- Don't extend the hand and expose fingers before the initial wedge is set — fingers are vulnerable to grabs.
- Hide the thumb line behind the neck once through, or the opponent can force you to restart the whole penetration.
- No strangle without jawline exposure, as a general rule (exceptions covered later in the course).
