# Top Position (Principle)

## Overview

The fourth critical principle, and one that governs the hand-fighting core of the Straitjacket System (SJS). Whenever you're behind an opponent looking to strangle, having your hands on top of your opponent's hands (rather than underneath) is the single biggest determinant of how easy the strangle will be to land.

---

## Why top hand position matters

- If your hands are on top of your opponent's hands, there is nothing between your strangle arm and his throat — one quick movement and the strangle hand is in.
- If your opponent's hands are on top of yours, that path is blocked — you have to fight through his hands before you can even reach for the strangle.
- Because of this, there's a constant, ongoing battle for top-hand position any time you're behind an opponent.
- Danaher draws a direct analogy to the leg lock game: whoever controls top hand position in back attacks is analogous to whoever controls inside foot position in ashi garami. The athlete who dominates that specific structural battle will generally dominate the whole exchange.

## When you can't win top hand position

- One of your hands — the control hand — should be on top at all times if at all possible. But there's no guarantee you'll always be able to keep your strangle hand on top of your opponent's as well; sometimes yes, sometimes no.
- When the strangle-side hand fight is being shut down, bring in the legs. It doesn't have to be a hand on top — a leg over the opponent's arm accomplishes the same job.
- Sequence: look first for two hands on top (best of all: hands on top with cross grips — "that's the king"). If you can't get top hand position on the strangle side, settle for a leg over the top of that arm instead, freeing your strangle hand to attack uncontested.
- Avoid getting stuck in long, convoluted, back-and-forth battles for top hand position with no result. Once you reach a workable position, commit — bring the leg over the opponent's arm and take the free hand into the strangle.

## The general rule

- Something always has to be on top of the opponent's defensive hands — either a hand or a leg. Whichever it is, that's what wins the position.
- The athlete whose limbs (hand or leg) dominate top position will dominate the hand-fighting game and, from there, the strangle game from the back.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Top hand position removes any obstacle between your strangle arm and the opponent's throat — bottom hand position forces you to fight through his hands first.
- Top hand position from the back is the direct analog of inside foot position in ashi garami — whoever wins it wins the exchange.
- Control hand on top is close to mandatory; the strangle hand doesn't have to be — a leg over the opponent's arm can substitute.
- Best-case grip: two hands on top with cross grips.
- Don't get stuck in prolonged, unproductive hand-fighting stalemates — commit to bringing a leg over the arm once the moment presents itself.
