# Through Movement

## Overview

Reinforcement (post rear mount, body triangle, crossed feet) is the first line of defense, but it won't always hold — a good enough opponent will still beat the bottom hook sometimes. When that happens, the solution shifts from static reinforcement to mobility: actively moving your body around the opponent to recover the lost hook. This chapter covers the base case — opponent has beaten the bottom hook but not yet the knee — which is recoverable with a fairly simple movement.

---

## The situation

- Despite the best reinforcement, the opponent successfully beats the bottom hook.
- The key reassurance: a hook, on its own, is not the real measure of control — "a hook can always be replaced." What matters is the knee, and at this stage the opponent has not yet beaten the knee.

## The recovery movement

- The instant the foot/hook is lost, immediately **extend that leg out further**, straightening it. This pushes the knee further away from the opponent's body, meaning the opponent now has to travel a much greater distance to actually beat the knee (the real target) than he did to beat the low hook.
- The foot moves through a small sequence: ball of the foot touches the mat first, then rolls to the heel.
- From there, plant the foot and use it to push off the mat, switching your whole body toward the opposite direction — actively turning the opponent back the other way rather than passively letting him continue his escape.
- Bring the foot back up into position (body triangle or post rear mount, per the rules from the previous chapter) on what is now the newly recovered/reinforced side.
- Mechanical detail: bring the **foot** in, but keep the **knee** in position — the knee is what must stay in place; the foot is just the low-level extremity that holds it there.

## Avoiding the escape cycle

- A common mistake is to simply mirror the same loose hook setup on the new side, which just lets the opponent repeat the exact same escape and beat the new bottom hook too — creating an endless left-right-left-right chase.
- The goal is not just to recover, but to recover *and reinforce*, so the opponent's same escape mechanism can't work a second time. You need to stop his cycle of escape, create a static opponent, and only then move into finishing attacks (the hand-fighting system covered elsewhere in the course).

---

## Key Takeaways

- Losing the bottom hook is not the same as losing the position — the knee is the real target, and it's still intact at this stage.
- Recovery movement: extend the beaten leg to increase the distance to the knee, roll foot ball-to-heel, push off and turn the opponent back the other way, then re-lock (body triangle or post rear mount) on the new side.
- Bring the foot in, not the knee — the knee's position is what you're actually protecting.
- Recover once, then reinforce — don't let the opponent repeat the same escape and drag you into an endless side-to-side chase.
