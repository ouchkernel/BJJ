# Default Principle

## Overview

The seventh critical principle, and one Danaher considers among the most important of all ten. During a vigorous hand fight for top position, an opponent's chin will inevitably rise at some point — it's simply too demanding to hold the chin tucked to the chest through a hard, sustained struggle. The Default Principle says: the instant that happens, your hand should shoot in for the neck automatically, without conscious thought.

---

## Why the chin rises

- Chin-on-chest is not painful, but it is an uncomfortable and energy-consuming posture to maintain throughout a hard hand-fighting exchange.
- Because both athletes are absorbed in the hand fight, distraction is inevitable — and with it, some rise in the chin.
- You don't need a dramatic opening. You only need the opponent's chin to rise the width of your own wrist — a genuinely small change in angle. What looks like almost nothing (a small change in angle) is enough: if the opponent can stop your wrist from entering with his chin tucked, that same small rise is enough for your wrist to slide inside and underneath his neck.

## The instinctual response

- The moment you detect the chin has risen away from the chest by roughly the width of your wrist, your hand goes in — in one motion — to solidify the penetration that leads toward the stranglehold.
- This has to be a hardwired instinct, not something noticed and considered afterward ("oh, I had an opportunity"). By the time you consciously register the opportunity, it's already gone.
- Unless the opponent's chin is physically blocking your wrist, you should already be moving in on the default reflex — this should occur almost on autopilot.
- Once the hand is in, apply the hidden hand principle: bring it all the way around and behind toward the finish.

## The tactical payoff

- A constant, live threat of penetrating the neck keeps the opponent honest about chin position and creates its own distraction — he has to spend focus defending the chin, which makes it easier to win the broader hand fight and start trapping arms with the legs.
- This creates a genuine dilemma for the opponent: defend the arms (hand fight) and risk the chin rising into the default attack, or defend the chin and risk losing the arm-trapping battle.
- Play the opponent between these two threats — the default strangle attack on one hand, and arm-trapping through the ongoing hand fight on the other. Getting frustrated with one simply means falling back to the other.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The chin only needs to rise the width of your wrist — not a dramatic movement — for the default attack to fire.
- The moment that small rise is detected, the hand shoots in immediately; this must be a hardwired reflex, not a conscious decision made after the fact.
- Unless the opponent's chin is physically stopping your wrist, you should already be attacking on the default.
- The constant threat of this default attack forces the opponent to split attention between defending his chin and defending his arms, creating an exploitable dilemma.
- Alternate between the default strangle threat and arm-trapping via the hand fight — each reinforces the other.
