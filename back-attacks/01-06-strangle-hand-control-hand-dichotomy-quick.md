# Strangle Hand / Control Hand Dichotomy — Quick Reference

## The rule

Always run one control hand (under an arm) + one strangle hand (over the shoulder). Never two of either.

## Why not two control hands

- Control: excellent.
- Submission threat: zero — neither arm is positioned to attack the neck. Opponent has nothing to fear.

## Why not two strangle hands

- Submission threat: looks strong on paper.
- Control: gone — opponent turns freely inside both arms and escapes the back.

## Why one-and-one works

1. One arm keeps chest glued to the opponent's back (control hand under an arm).
2. One arm holds a live strangle threat (strangle hand over the shoulder).
3. You can follow the opponent's movement either direction while keeping both the threat and the connection intact.
4. This is the only configuration that survives strong defensive scrambling without losing the position.
