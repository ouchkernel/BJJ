# Cross Body Ride — Arm Attack: Hammer Lock

## Overview

When an opponent recognizes the threat of hip elevation and starts sliding down and away to deny leg attacks, that same defensive movement opens up an arm attack: the hammer lock. Rarely seen in submission grappling on its own (it's normally too easy to spin out of), it becomes potentially devastating when combined with Cross Body Ride control. Mechanically it produces the same kind of breaking pressure as a Kimura, but with a completely different gripping method.

---

## Recognizing the opportunity

- Once an opponent understands that hip elevation leads to leg attacks, they'll start sliding their shoulders down and away to deny that elevation.
- Adjustment when this happens: turn your toes to point toward the opponent's head rather than up at the ceiling. If your feet point at the ceiling, the opponent can easily pommel the leg free and escape. Pointing your toes toward their head makes pommeling out much harder.
- The further the opponent retreats to avoid leg attacks, the easier the arm attacks — hammer lock and Kimura-style pressure — become.

## Setting up the grip

1. As the opponent moves away, take the hand that was inside/wrist-deep on them and withdraw it.
2. Prop yourself up on your elbow — don't attempt this move flat/down on your shoulder, it's very difficult to enter from there. Get height first.
3. Take the opponent's hand and wedge it to the floor. Wedged against the mat, it's surprisingly difficult for the opponent to yank the arm free.
4. Cover the "wrist watch" area of the opponent's wrist (the area a wrist watch would sit) with the wrist watch area of your own wrist, so the two wrists cross each other.

## The hammer lock grip

1. From the crossed-wrist position, come down toward the opponent's elbow.
2. Cup your hand all the way through underneath the opponent's tricep, lifting and elevating their shoulder to expose the back of the shoulder and tricep to your second hand.
3. Bring the second hand through and grip **hand over hand** — never palm to palm. Locking palm to palm actually weakens the hold.
4. Push-hand goes in first, pull-hand goes in second — reversing that order costs you pulling power.

## Finishing mechanics

- Pull the opponent's elbow forward — this creates the pulling pressure — but the real key to breaking pressure (as with every joint attack in this series) is never a single-direction pull. It's **coupled forces in opposing directions**: as your hands pull the elbow, your own elbow pushes outward.
- Chin comes up to the elbow as the finishing pull/push is applied.
- Most of the pulling work is done by the bottom arm, reinforced by the top arm, giving a strong two-arm pull against the outward push of your elbow.
- There's a large follow-through available even after the opponent reaches breaking point — the position allows you to keep driving.

## Full sequence (alternate entry, demonstrated from the front)

1. Tilt the opponent down into Cross Body Ride from in front.
2. As the opponent scoots their shoulders down to deny leg attacks, come up and prop yourself on your arm.
3. Touch the back of your wrist to the back of the opponent's wrist.
4. Go forward, claim the arm — lift and punch through.
5. Push hand (lifting/claiming hand) goes in first, pull hand goes in second and reinforces.
6. Bring the elbow, pulling, while your own elbow pushes on the opponent's wrist — this produces the coupled breaking pressure.

## Full sequence (alternate entry, demonstrated moving in behind the opponent's face)

1. Tilt the opponent over into Cross Body Ride.
2. As the opponent scoots down the body to escape, push down into the mat and elevate your own body instead of chasing.
3. Cover the opponent's wrist so the wrists cross at roughly 90 degrees.
4. Bring body weight forward, catch, lift, and expose the arm.
5. One hand goes in, the other covers it.
6. Chin forward to the elbow — pull on the elbow while pushing with your own elbow, creating a severe turning effect (their elbow goes forward, their wrist goes back).
7. This coupled force produces incredible breaking pressure on the elbow joint.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The hammer lock only becomes a live option once the opponent has denied the leg attacks by scooting down and away — read that retreat as the trigger to switch targets.
- Turn your toes toward the opponent's head (not the ceiling) as they retreat, so they can't pommel the leg free.
- Grip is hand over hand, never palm to palm — palm to palm weakens the hold. Push hand goes in first, pull hand second.
- Breaking pressure always comes from coupled/opposing forces (hands pulling the elbow, your elbow pushing outward), never a straight-line pull — the same principle used across every joint lock in this series.
- The move must be built from height (propped on your elbow), not flat on your shoulder.
