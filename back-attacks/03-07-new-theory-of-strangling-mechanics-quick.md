# New Theory Of Strangling Mechanics — Quick Reference

## The shift

Classical strangle = forward contraction, elbow toward chin, powered by arms/pecs/shoulders. Rotational method = elbow rotates over the opponent's shoulder (like a gi collar strangle), powered by the back.

## Classical method's weakness

1. If opponent gets inside position on your forearm and pulls down hard, you can't close your elbows enough.
2. Purely arm/pec/shoulder powered — arms fatigue, strangle fades, performance drops afterward.

## Rotational method steps

1. Don't worry about lining the elbow up with the chin.
2. Once hands are closed, rotate the strangling elbow over the opponent's shoulder (right elbow for a right-hand strangle).
3. Look in the direction of the strangle — don't tuck your head down (it limits elbow rotation). Optionally rest your chin on the back of his skull to help the rotation.
4. Drive your chest forward into his back while your elbow pulls back — two coupled, opposite forces = scissoring pressure on the neck.

## Why it's stronger

1. Coupled forces (chest forward + elbow back) beat pure arm contraction, even against a full two-handed resisting pull.
2. Uses lats/back/scapula instead of arms/biceps — far more endurance (lats hold 2-3 min easily; biceps gas fast).
3. Works through elite resistance, not just against unresisting opponents.

## Proof

Demonstrated live: two people pulling as hard as possible on the strangling arm — rotational force goes through anyway.

## Real-world reference points

GSP's fast finish over Michael Bisping; Gordon and Nicky Ryan, Garry Tonon, Eddie Cummings finishing through hands/jaw against elite opponents — all attributed to this mechanism, not strength or conditioning.
