# Single Cross Grip — Quick Reference

## Why cross > straight

1. Cross grip uses his rib cage/torso to reinforce control.
2. Pins his elbow close to his body (vs. unrestrained with straight grip = breakout risk).
3. Your elbow gets reinforced by his rib cage too.
4. "Rope end" slipping effect — added hand width makes it harder for him to pull free.

## Build the single cross (pass-off)

1. Start from double straight grips.
2. Insert your hand between your existing two hands.
3. Turn his hand to expose the "suicide zone" of the wrist.
4. Slide the blade of your hand through, thumb covering the suicide zone.
5. He yanks → tightens the knot → you now have single cross grip.

## Once you have single cross — go immediately

1. Foot over the top, touch your own hand (prior perception).
2. Heel threads through, secondary hand goes behind his back.
3. Hunt for primary defensive hand, catch/pass/lock it at the knuckle line.
4. Result: uncontested strangle hand.

## Key fact

Single cross grip alone already blocks the wrist from rising above the chest/pectoral line — thumb's inside position stops it.

## Don't

Don't let yourself get stuck fighting a contested strangle (2 defensive hands + chin vs. 1 strangle hand = 3-to-1 outnumbered). That's the exact problem single cross exists to solve.
