# One-Handed Strangle — Quick Reference

## The point

When you can't extract your control arm for a conventional two-handed strangle, a properly built one-handed strangle (rotational method) delivers ~90% of the finishing power of the two-arm version. No special physical attributes required — just mechanics.

## When to use it

1. Behind training partner, forearm to the underside, hooked into a strangle-type position.
2. Control arm can't be extracted — opponent's primary defensive hand is fighting to keep it pinned/monitored.
3. Neck is penetrated and it's time to finish — go one-handed rather than fighting to free the second arm.

## Steps

1. Penetrate with chin down, flat fist method.
2. Walk the arm all the way through — don't stop at gripping the shoulder.
3. Hand tucks around the corner behind the opponent's back.
4. Chin comes forward to touch your own wrist — **this closes the circle**.
5. Rotate over the shoulder (rotational method) for the finish.

## The one rule: close the circle

- Hand penetrates through + jaw comes forward = closed circle = no escape.
- Hand forward + head back = open circle = opponent escapes through the gap.

## Don't

- Don't settle for just gripping the shoulder.
- Don't leave a gap between chin and wrist — it deteriorates into a weak pull on the throat.
- Don't assume you need a second arm to finish — the one-arm version still works even if the opponent frees an arm, grabs your forearm, and cranks the defense hard.
