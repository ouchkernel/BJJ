# Rear Triangle — Troubleshooting

## Overview

Common problems that come up while fighting for the Rear Triangle (Ushiro Sankaku) against a resisting opponent, and the specific fixes for each — plus the option to abandon the rear triangle mid-attack and switch to a front triangle when the opponent's escape attempt actually delivers him into a different finish.

---

## Problem: Opponent Has Very Strong Arms

- Concern: can you really force his knuckles to your chest against real strength? Yes — via the same 2-on-1 mechanism.
- Expose your wrist to the opponent's wrist and use the 2-on-1 grip. As he pulls and pulls against you, he's simply locked in place — use your **legs**, not your arms, to move his body down yours and connect his knuckles to your chest.
- Once the knuckles are on your chest, the position becomes a kind of stocks: one foot wedges into his armpit and blocks that arm, his neck is wedged by your thigh, and his hips are controlled through your hook. Bridging out of this is very difficult.
- From here it's an easy matter to move your body over, scissor the legs, and lock the beginnings of a triangle.

## Problem: Opponent Locks His Hands / Contracts The Elbow Down

- Some opponents successfully lock their hands together and pull the elbow down into a sound defensive position, making it hard to bring the near hand back up.
- Fix: reach for the **second (far) elbow** instead of fighting for the one he's denying you. Bring it up and hug it. Now gather both elbows together — this fully exposes the neck, and strangulation pressure from here is immense.
- Alternative: cut directly through inside the opponent's arms. As he makes both arms strongly resistant, focus on a pulling action that brings the far elbow up high — this weakens the near elbow's position, and both elbows can be drawn up into a good attacking position together. Two extended elbows can't defend the strangle at the same time.

## Avoid Convoluted Finishes

- When the opponent successfully buries his hands down near his stomach, the temptation is to chase — crunch forward, reach and pull for a leg. These moves are genuinely difficult under live conditions and generate exactly the kind of movement that lets the opponent turn and slip inside the triangle. It can turn a winning position into a disaster.
- The number one concern throughout stays the same: get the opponent's elbow above his forehead.
- From a seated position, use the sit to impart momentum rather than crunching forward — go elbow to elbow on the opponent and lift. This is often a better tactic than pulling on the wrist directly.
- If contact with the hands is lost entirely (hands buried down at his stomach): come in elbow to elbow, put your hand on your own knee, and finger-walk the hand up your own leg while leaning to the side — this brings his wrist up to your elbow and back into a winning position.
- Detail: you want your **knee inside his bicep**, not his bicep inside your knee. Physically pull to correct this if it's backwards before applying the squeeze.

## Switching Off Into Another Triangle

- Sometimes the opponent uses his free hand to interfere with and strip your feet apart. When you feel this happening, there's no harm in switching your body across to the other side.
- As he comes up chasing his feet, kick your legs over — this converts a rear triangle into a **front triangle (Omote Sankaku)** with a simple change in body orientation.
- Cast the leg over the top of his shoulder, bring the legs into a winning configuration, lock the figure-of-four, and you're in the perfect position to strangle for the finish.
- Core principle: whenever you have a rear triangle, you always have access to a front triangle. The opponent rising up in an attempt to escape doesn't get him free — it delivers him directly into a different finish.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Strong-armed opponent: don't muscle it with your arms — use the 2-on-1 grip and your legs to move his body and pin the knuckles to your chest.
- Opponent locks his hands and buries the near elbow: go for the far elbow instead, then gather both elbows together.
- Never chase a buried elbow with crunching or leg-grabbing — it generates escape-enabling movement. Always work back toward getting the elbow above the forehead.
- Knee inside the bicep, not the reverse — fix this before squeezing.
- If the opponent strips your feet and tries to come up, let him — switch orientation and take the front triangle. A rear triangle always has a front triangle available behind it.
