# Underarm Side — Quick Reference

## The point

Two ways to fall in the Straitjacket System: underarm side (toward your control arm) or overarm side (toward your strangle arm). "Underarm = weak side" is a myth — side is neutral, and the underarm side actually gives you a free leg to trap arms with.

## Rules

1. Never fall straight back in a straight line — it lets your opponent bridge and get his head above yours.
2. His head must always stay lower than yours. If it starts to rise: stop, pommel legs under him, get a single hook, break him to a single hip.
3. Falling toward the underarm/control-arm side leaves your top leg **unweighted** — use it to trap his arms, body-triangle, or post on his hip.
4. Falling toward the "strong"/overarm side pins your leg under his weight — you lose the ability to trap his secondary arm with it. This is why the "strong" side is actually worse for you.

## The deficit problem

- Him: 2 defensive hands + chin = up to 3 defensive tools.
- You: usually 1-2 offensive tools (hands only).
- Fix: use unweighted legs as offensive tools to trap arms and restore parity.
