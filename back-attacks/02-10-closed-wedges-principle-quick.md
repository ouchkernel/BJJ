# Closed Wedges Principle — Quick Reference

## The point

Closed wedges beat open wedges for security of grip. From the back, the knees (not just the feet) are the real source of lower-body control — close that wedge whenever your proportions allow it.

## Why it matters

1. Knees are the actual hooks — proven by the fact that a crab-ride foot position (no hooks inserted) still gives hip control from knee position alone.
2. Open feet let the opponent beat the foot first, then the knee, then escape the back.

## Preference order (best to worst)

1. Body triangle (figure-four) locked on the **outside** — strongest lock, general preference.
2. Body triangle locked on the **inside** — second choice.
3. Crossed feet locked over one hip — nearly as good; best option for short legs or a thick-waisted opponent.
4. Feet held close together without locking (post one foot on the hip, bring the other up close) — still technically open, but far better than wide-open symmetric feet.
5. Upper-body closure (tight seatbelt-style grip around head/shoulders) — last resort when lower-body closure is physically impossible.

## Action steps

1. Check your leg length against your opponent's waist size before choosing.
2. Default to the outside body triangle when it's achievable.
3. Falling back a tier is fine — feet-close-without-locking still beats symmetric open feet.
4. If lower body closure is truly out of reach, lock up the upper body instead and use it to maneuver and turn your opponent back into control.

## Don't

Don't leave your feet in a wide, symmetric open position — that's the one configuration that lets even small opponent movements beat your hooks.
