# Double Cross / Indirect Grip

## Overview

Moving further up the hierarchy: general preferences are cross over straight, and direct over indirect — but that doesn't make indirect grips useless. This chapter shows how to deliberately bait an intelligent opponent into a defensive reaction that hands you a double cross grip, even though the resulting grip on your strangle hand is technically indirect.

---

## The setup: baiting an intelligent defense

- You already have single cross (via the pass-off from the previous chapter).
- Your opponent, if he's thinking tactically, reasons that your strangle hand is going to enter along the same line as your cross grip — so he pre-positions his remaining defensive arm right there to block it. This is a smart, correct read on his part.
- Exploit it: willingly give him your strangle hand. Put it in and fake a strangle.
  - If he doesn't react to the fake, you strangle him right there.
  - If he does react, he commits a solid, direct grip onto your strangle hand to stop it.

## What that grip exchange actually gives you

- After he blocks: he has a **direct** grip on your strangle hand (his advantage). You have an **indirect** cross grip on that hand.
- But you have a **direct cross grip on the hand that actually counts** — his secondary defensive hand, which is the one your leg is hunting.
- He thinks he's winning ("I'm stopping the strangle, I'm doing fine, I'm surviving") — but he's misjudging which hand matters.

## Finishing sequence

1. Go over the top with your leg, use prior perception to touch your own hand.
2. Put his secondary hand behind his back.
3. He now has only one hand left (the one blocking your strangle hand). You have two functioning limbs against his one.
4. Fake the strangle again to get him pushing/committing on your strangle hand.
5. Go over the top and seize the knuckle line, stripping his grip off.
6. Result: an uncontested strangle hand — his blocking arm no longer offers any resistance.

## Technique detail: freeing a hand from a grip

- Never try to pull your hand free by force — that takes real strength and often fails against a determined grip.
- Instead: hold and immobilize the hand, then turn your wrist through the weakness of his thumb. It "comes out like magic."
- This detail applies broadly any time you need to strip a grip off your wrist in this system.

---

## Key Takeaways

- General preferences (cross > straight, direct > indirect) are guidelines, not absolutes — sometimes you work with what you're given.
- An intelligent opponent will try to block where he predicts your strangle hand is coming — bait this with a fake strangle from single cross.
- What matters is which hand ends up with the direct grip: an indirect grip on your strangle hand is acceptable if you get a direct cross grip on his secondary (leg-target) hand.
- To strip a gripped hand free: never pull against the grip — immobilize and turn the wrist through the thumb's weak point.
- End state is always the same: two arms neutralized (one by leg, one by hand-strip), an uncontested strangle hand, only his chin left.
