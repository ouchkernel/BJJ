# Four Point Position — Quick Reference

## What It Is

1. Opponent is up on hands and feet — no elbows, no knees on the mat.
2. Back slopes down to the floor — breaks traditional back-control tools.

## Why Standard Tools Fail

1. Seat belt: opponent walks backward + shrugs, you slip right off.
2. Symmetrical (square) hooks: opponent walks backward, drags your position with them, hooks get lost.
3. Crucifix: awkward, doesn't work well at this angle.

## Entering the Asymmetrical Rear Mount

1. From standing rear body lock, bump opponent down to four point.
2. Two-on-one pinch one of their legs immediately — don't leave a leg free.
3. Golden rule: NEVER go over-under. Stay behind both of the opponent's arms.
4. Throw the far hook in first (safe here — their weight is already on their hands).
5. Set hooks asymmetrically: one shallow/short (ankle-deep), one deep/long (knee-deep).
6. Arm in behind opponent's arm, head down next to theirs — load weight onto their hands.

## Breaking Them Down

- Normal opponent: elbow to wrist, draw both wrists together, break down to the floor.
- Strong opponent (Power Half Nelson): bar across back of neck, lock hands palm-to-palm, push head down/under the armpit, break down to the mat.

## Acid Test

1. Can you hang your full body weight off the opponent using legs only, no hands?
2. Can you bring your face in front of theirs hands-free?
3. If yes, your hook depth/asymmetry is correct.

## Don't

- Don't go over-under against four point — it's a slippage disaster.
- Don't use square/symmetrical hooks here.
