# Introduction

## Overview

A short framing chapter that opens Volume 7 and pivots the course from finishing mechanics to entries. The preceding volumes built out the main system and the auxiliary systems for attacking from the back — the centerpiece of the whole approach to back attacks and submissions. This chapter explains why the course now turns to a different problem: getting to the back in the first place.

---

## The gap this volume addresses

- Across the sport of jiu-jitsu, there is a large population of athletes who are highly skilled at getting to an opponent's back, and another large population who are good at retaining and maintaining the back once they have it.
- Only a small number of outstanding individuals can actually finish reliably from the back. That gap — plenty of access and control, not enough finishing — was the central problem the series as a whole was built to fix.
- The main emphasis of the video series has therefore been redressing that imbalance: showing a systematic approach to working toward submissions from a range of back positions.

## Why this volume covers entries

- Despite the series' primary focus being finishing, it would be unwise to release a complete instructional on the back without also showing how to get there in the first place.
- Back maintenance has already been covered. What's missing is the piece before it: how to establish hooks on a resisting opponent and get to rear mount so you can actually reach the positions studied in detail elsewhere in the series.
- This volume is dedicated to that: the approach to establishing hooks and rear mount position, working through it systematically so the viewer can arrive at the very positions already covered by the main and auxiliary systems.

## What follows

- General background theory on stances/postures and control focus points when working against a turtled opponent.
- The foot-entry vs. knee-entry decision for inserting hooks.
- A progression of concrete near-side entry techniques, including responses to resistance and the diagonal control principle.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The series' primary problem was a finishing gap: many can get to and hold the back, few can finish from it — that's what the main/auxiliary systems addressed.
- This volume exists because an instructional on the back is incomplete without covering how to get there against a resisting opponent.
- Everything that follows (stances, focus points, foot/knee entries, near-side entry techniques) is in service of reaching the same back positions already covered by the finishing systems.
