# Conclusion

## Overview

Closes out the back attack system with a summary of the full course, tying together the finishing system taught earlier in the series with the entries and winning positions covered across the "Establishing Hooks and Rear Mount" volumes.

---

## The State of the Art

- A large number of modern jiu-jitsu athletes are highly skilled at taking the back and maintaining it, but significantly fewer are highly adept at finishing from the back.
- The central purpose of this entire video series is to close that gap: a systematic approach to finishing from the back, not just controlling it.

## The Core System: The Straight Jacket

- The keynote of the system is the straight jacket system, designed to pin the opponent's defensive arms down in ways that greatly facilitate the rear naked strangle.
- Alongside the straight jacket, the series presented a new mechanical approach to the rear naked strangle itself.

## The Auxiliary Systems

- Surrounding the central straight jacket system is a group of auxiliary systems with a different focus: rather than pushing one move (the rear naked strangle) through one vulnerable target (the neck), they spread the attack out across the opponent's whole body.
- The series covered a range of these auxiliary systems so that when the neck isn't available, other finishing routes remain open.

## Getting to the Winning Positions

- The volume finished by covering how to actually get to the winning back-control positions where the straight jacket, rear naked strangle, and auxiliary systems can be deployed: near side entries, rolling entries (both upper-body/seat-belt-based and leg-based), and specialized answers like the asymmetrical rear mount against the four point position.
- These entry principles are described as ones used by the instructor's own athletes and other top competitors to consistently reach the positions that make the rest of the system work.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Taking and holding the back is common at a high level; finishing from the back reliably is rarer — this course is built to close that specific gap.
- The straight jacket system (pinning the defensive arms) plus a refined rear naked strangle mechanic form the core finishing method.
- Auxiliary systems exist to spread attacks across the whole body rather than betting everything on one strangle to one target.
- None of the finishing systems matter without reliable entries — the near side, rolling, and four-point/asymmetrical-mount entries covered in this volume are what make the whole system reachable in live competition.
