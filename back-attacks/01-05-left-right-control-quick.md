# Left/Right Control — Quick Reference

## The rule

You must be able to stop the opponent turning **both** left and right, at all times. One-sided control = they escape through the open side.

## Grip configurations, by what you have available

1. **One arm under (control hand) + one arm over (toward strangle):** elbow behind the body stops one direction, elbow in front of the far shoulder stops the other. Both elbows must be positioned — not just one.
2. **Real control = elbow position, not locked hands.** Locking hands only holds your elbows in place; you'll need to unlock and work with open hands to actually strangle. Keep elbows set (one behind, one in front of a shoulder) even with hands open.
3. **One hook + one hand only → use diagonal control.** Hook and hand must be on *opposite* sides of the body (e.g., right hook + left hand) so both turning directions are blocked. Same-side hook+hand leaves the other direction wide open.
4. **No upper-body grip at all → two hooks required**, ideally a body triangle. This is the only way to cover both directions on legs alone.

## Don't

- Don't rely on a same-side hook + hand — it blocks one direction and leaves the other completely open.
- Don't think locked hands = control. They just anchor your elbows; you still need open hands to threaten a strangle.
