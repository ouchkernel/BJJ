# Concentration/Diffusion Principle — Quick Reference

## The point

The Straitjacket System and the Auxiliary Systems work on opposite philosophies — concentration vs. diffusion — and beating a good opponent means playing them against each other.

## The two philosophies

1. **Straitjacket System = concentration.** One weapon (the strangling arm) against one localized target (the neck). Requires an elaborate hand-fighting system to break through heavily concentrated defense.
2. **Auxiliary Systems = diffusion.** Wide array of attacks — arm locks, leg locks, other strangles, spinal cranks — spread across the whole body.

## Action steps

1. Threaten the rear naked strangle constantly — this forces the opponent to concentrate his defense on the neck.
2. Diffuse into auxiliary attacks elsewhere on the body when the neck is well defended.
3. Alternate back and forth — concentrated threat, then diffuse threat, then back.
4. The opponent can't fully defend one concentrated point and a diffuse spread of threats at the same time — that tension is what creates the breakthrough.

## Don't

Don't rely on only one philosophy. Pure concentration runs into a wall of localized defense; pure diffusion never builds enough pressure on the single best finish. Use both, against each other.
