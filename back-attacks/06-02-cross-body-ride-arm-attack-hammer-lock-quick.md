# Cross Body Ride — Arm Attack: Hammer Lock — Quick Reference

## When to switch to this attack

- Opponent slides shoulders down/away to deny hip elevation → this is the trigger.
- Turn your toes toward their head (not the ceiling) so they can't pommel the leg free while retreating.
- The further they retreat from leg attacks, the easier this arm attack becomes.

## Setup, in order

1. Withdraw the hand that was wrist-deep on their far side.
2. Prop up on your elbow — get height, don't try this flat on your shoulder.
3. Wedge opponent's hand to the floor (hard for them to yank free once wedged).
4. Cross "wrist watch" to "wrist watch" (your wrist over theirs).

## Lock the grip

1. Cup underneath opponent's tricep, lift/expose the shoulder.
2. Second hand comes through **hand over hand** — never palm to palm (palm-to-palm = weaker hold).
3. Push hand in first, pull hand in second — order matters.

## Finish

1. Pull opponent's elbow forward/up.
2. Simultaneously push your own elbow outward — **coupled opposing forces**, not a straight pull.
3. Chin comes up to the elbow.
4. Bottom arm does most of the pulling, top arm reinforces.
5. Large follow-through available past breaking point.

## Don't

- Don't attempt the entry flat on your shoulder — prop up first.
- Don't lock palm to palm.
- Don't rely on a single-direction pull — it's the push/pull couple that breaks the arm.

## Effect

Same breaking mechanism as a Kimura, delivered through a different (hammer lock) grip — devastating when combined with Cross Body Ride control, even though the hammer lock alone is normally easy to spin out of.
