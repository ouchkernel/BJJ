# Near Side Entries 03 — Quick Reference

## Base sequence: tight waist → far-side foot entry

1. Tight waist grip from behind.
2. Knee as blocker, pull opponent over the knee, pull yourself out — breaks him down to hip + elbow (non-athletic position).
3. Switch to seat belt as your head crowds his head.
4. Knee penetrates easily (space is huge now).
5. **Exception to near-side-first:** he's broken down to a hip, so he can't stand up on you — safe to throw the far-side hook in as a **foot entry** while he's still recovering.
6. Fall to the side of the established hook, lock up back control.

## Reactions to his far leg

| His reaction | Your response |
|---|---|
| Knee comes to chest (partial block) | Pull his chest toward you + extend your bottom leg → hook goes in |
| Knee fully tucked to chest | Cross/hook your ankles over his hip, extend your whole body to pull his hips away from his chest, pass foot over the top |
| Directly blocks the far-side entry | Draw his body in (legs go light) → take near-side hook easily; for far side, go underneath, lock feet together, straighten body to pull his knee off his chest, then finish |

## Key rule

Near-side-first is the default (Ch. 4-5) — this chapter's far-side-first exception is only safe because the tight-waist breakdown has already put the opponent in a non-athletic hip/elbow position where he can't stand up to dislodge you.
