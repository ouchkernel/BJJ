# Cross Body Ride — Leg Attacks 2

## Overview

A continuation of the leg-attack menu available from the Cross Body Ride (Outside Scorpion). Builds on the earlier knee-compression entry with two closely related hip-flexor attacks — a "neck brace" method and an achilles-lock method that both produce the same breaking effect — and then shows the escalation available when the opponent's hip flexor proves too durable: an inside (inverted) heel hook applied without needing a full Ashi Garami entanglement.

---

## Foundation: diagonal control

- Cross Body Ride control rests on **diagonal control**: whenever you're behind an opponent you only need to block movement in two directions, and you can do that with two limbs while leaving the other two limbs free to attack.
- Example: a right hook blocks the opponent turning left. A grip on the opponent's left wrist/elbow blocks them turning right. Two limbs do all the restraining; two limbs are completely free for offense.
- Every leg attack in this position starts the same way: elevate the opponent's hips and square them to the ceiling (knees to chest, foot toward the ceiling). Leg attacks only really start once the hips are up.

## Entry recap: knee compression

- The established starting point for the leg-attack menu is compressing the opponent's knee — that threat is what forces the opponent's subsequent reactions and creates the openings below.

## Near-leg denial dynamic

- Pulling the opponent's leg in usually gets the leg straightened out in response, which denies the far leg.
- Whenever the far leg gets denied, come straight back to the near leg — it's always available.

## Hip Breaker — neck brace method

1. Catch the opponent's foot at the shoelaces/end of the lever, using an elbow wedge on the outside of their leg for control as they try to extend.
2. Physically draw the foot in and pinch it against the side of your neck.
3. Bring both hands up to the opponent's knee.
4. Extend the leg away while pulling down on the knee — same direction the opponent is already moving, complementing their own force.
5. This puts crushing, breaking tension directly on the **hip flexor tendon** (the tendon connecting the hip flexor to the hip), not the muscle belly — flexibility isn't a factor.

## Hip Breaker — achilles lock method

- Same effect, different route: instead of the neck-brace grip, pass directly into an achilles lock and lock the figure-4.
- Extending the leg the same way produces identical crushing pressure on the hip flexor tendon.
- Either grip (neck brace or achilles) gets you to the same hip-flexor breaking mechanism — use whichever is available.

## Escalation: inside (inverted) heel hook

If the hip flexor attack proves inadequate against a durable opponent:

1. From the cross grip already established, take the free arm and wrap it around the opponent's ankle.
2. Bring the "foot hand" to the opponent's foot and take the grip there.
3. Go hand-over-hand as the opponent tries to extend the leg and escape.
4. Apply the heel hook from the outside — this is an **inverted/inside heel hook applied without an Ashi Garami** entanglement. There's simply too much hand in the way for the opponent to straighten the leg.
5. Rotate inward to apply breaking pressure.

### Alternative passing route into the same lock

- Catch the opponent's foot at the end of the lever using the elbow-wedge control described above.
- Pass the foot through into vulnerable positions — to the neck, the armpit, or a hand-to-hand "pass-off" (hand comes in from outside, under, and passes the foot from hand to hand).
- Keep the heel pinned on your chest as the opponent tries to extend — reinforce with the second hand, then turn in for the lock.
- Works mirrored from either side; the mechanics (elbow wedge + end-of-lever grip + heel on chest) stay the same.

## If the opponent escapes the grip entirely

- If the opponent is strong enough to get the foot to the wrong side of your head and spring free, don't panic — go straight back to the original control and start the sequence again. The opponent isn't going anywhere from Cross Body Ride; you can always reset and re-enter.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Every leg attack from Cross Body Ride starts by elevating the opponent's hips — knees to chest, foot toward the ceiling.
- The neck-brace hip breaker and the achilles-lock hip breaker are two different grips producing the identical result: breaking tension on the hip flexor tendon, immune to flexibility.
- When the hip flexor attack isn't enough, escalate to an inside/inverted heel hook — it works without a full Ashi Garami because the hand-over-hand grip leaves the opponent no room to extend the leg.
- Losing the grip isn't a crisis — Cross Body Ride lets you reset to the original control and restart the attack sequence.
