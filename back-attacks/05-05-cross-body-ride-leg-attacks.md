# Cross Body Ride — Leg Attacks

## Overview

The third major auxiliary system, adapted from wrestling's cross-body ride (widely known in jiu-jitsu as the "twister" position, popularized largely through Eddie Bravo's work, though used here differently). Used when the opponent rides high on your body and neither arm-strangles nor the leg-based triangle systems are landing. It trades the strangle threat entirely for total-body control, then opens into a family of leg attacks that have nothing to do with Ashi Garami — a genuinely different species of leg attack from the course's usual leg-lock game.

---

## Why Switch To The Cross Body Ride

- Normal back control runs one arm over (the strangle threat) and one arm under (control). Sometimes the opponent does a good job of riding up high on your body, and the usual fixes — pommeling underneath, sitting down to put him back down — just aren't working.
- Whenever the over-the-top arm feels ineffective and strangles aren't proving fruitful, an excellent alternative is to put **both** arms underneath the opponent.
- Trade-off: this gives excellent control (whether via two one-on-one grips or a high body lock) but it sacrifices the strangle entirely — there's no strangle available with both arms under. To strangle again, one arm has to come back out.
- With two arms under, the mindset shifts from strangles to attacking the joints of the body.
- Bonus effect: because the opponent feels no strangle danger with both your arms underneath, he often gets confident and rides even higher — which is exactly the position this system wants him in.

## Building The Position

- Pass one foot over the opponent's leg; deliberately keep the other leg out in a crab-hooks position.
- Lift, elevate, and turn the opponent. Bring your knee in behind his buttocks to bump his weight further forward.
- Catch a figure-of-four with your legs, shoelace to shoelace with the opponent's foot. This leg lock is referred to as the **Outside Scorpion** — in 10th Planet terms, essentially a lockdown applied from the outside rather than the usual inside lockdown used from bottom half guard.
- Keep tension and hook in both feet, heels pinned to the mat — don't let the legs relax, or the opponent can start to pommel a leg free. Periodically elevate the feet if you feel him sitting up or his shoulders coming off the floor, to drop him back down, then return the heels to the mat.

## Diagonal Control

- Lower body: the Outside Scorpion controls the opponent's near-side hip through your legs.
- Upper body: secure his wrist and hook a wrist-deep grip over his forearm, gluing the entire forearm to your chest — this controls the far shoulder. Go wrist-deep, not elbow-deep.
- The result is diagonal control running from one shoulder to the opposite hip: turning into you is blocked by the legs, turning away is blocked by the arm/shoulder grip, sitting up is stopped by elevating him back down, and sliding away is stopped by the combination of legs and arm together.
- Once an opponent is held in this position, the extension and flailing he generates trying to escape opens up submissions across the length and breadth of his body.

## Leg Attack 1 — Calf Slicer (Far Leg)

- With the opponent's hips turned up toward the ceiling, hook shoelace to shoelace and pump your knees toward yourself to bring his foot in closer.
- Grip: five fingers across the **knuckle line of the toes** — not the toes themselves (they slip), not the shoelaces, and not the shin or ankle. This is the end of the lever; grip it correctly.
- Never reach your second hand inside the opponent's legs. He can catch that wrist between his own legs and armbar you off your own body position — a lock you're stuck holding yourself in. Always reach from the outside.
- Lock a figure-of-four, or the stronger variant: cock the foot and place it on your own Achilles.
- Actively turn the knee outward so the blade of the shin drives directly into the calf muscle — don't just pull down on the toes. Pushing with both feet together creates a slightly less secure but considerably stronger version of the lock.

## Leg Attack 2 — Knee Compression (Far Leg)

- Similar entry: knee and thigh into the opponent's tailbone, extract the bottom arm, secure a wrist-deep grip on the forearm glued to your chest, and bring his hips up into the air.
- Pump your knees toward your body; reach your arm around the hip — never in front of it, for the same armbar-counter reason as above. Drop and snatch the leg, passing it to your second hand.
- Lock a figure-of-four with the legs, or go foot to foot. Turn the shin so the blade drives into the **knee** this time (not the calf), and apply pressure by driving the hips forward.

## Leg Attack 3 — Near-Leg Hip Lock ("Wishbone")

- Switch targets to the near leg (facing the opposite direction from the previous two attacks).
- Common defense: the opponent uses his free leg to push your grip apart, or hooks it under his own ankle so pushing with the other foot resists your pull. When this happens, abandon the foot grip and sweep your grip up to the knee instead.
- Catch and hug the whole length of the leg with your arms crossing over, head next to the opponent's knee, so he can't extend the leg and slip free.
- Push outward for the finish. This is an attack on the structure of the hip joint itself, not the surrounding muscle — it works regardless of the opponent's flexibility, even against a full side split. Rotate the hip and pull it out in an arc rather than simply stretching the leg — pull the knees apart from each other "like the wishbone of a chicken" for real breaking pressure on the hip socket.
- If the opponent successfully extends the leg and your grip starts to slip: pass your hand underneath and lock a figure-of-four on the leg instead. This limits how far the leg can extend and delivers the same hip-breaking pressure through an alternate grip.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Cross Body Ride is the answer when an opponent rides high and neither strangles nor triangles are landing — trade the strangle for total control by putting both arms underneath.
- Build diagonal control: legs own the near hip (Outside Scorpion, shoelace to shoelace), arm owns the far shoulder (wrist-deep grip gluing the forearm to your chest).
- Heels stay pinned to the mat with tension in both feet; elevate only when he tries to sit up, then reset.
- Three leg attacks come off this same control: calf slicer and knee compression on the far leg (differ only in where the shin blade lands — calf vs. knee), and a hip-joint "wishbone" lock on the near leg that works regardless of flexibility.
- Never reach a hand inside the opponent's legs when gripping a foot — it hands him a self-inflicted armbar counter. Always reach from the outside.
