# Near Side Entries 05 — Quick Reference

## The Principle

1. Never fight an opponent's leg with your hand — use your own leg to restrain theirs instead.
2. One leg pins the opponent's leg (at the hamstring); the other leg enters as the hook.
3. Applies to both knee entries and foot entries.

## From a Breakdown

1. Break opponent down, elbow at the hip as usual.
2. Plant your near knee/shin low at the opponent's tailbone as they try to recover.
3. Weight settles on their hamstring — acts as a handbrake.
4. As they drive up, bring your head forward and slide the hook leg straight in.
5. Lock diagonal control (opposite arm).

## Leg Drag Scramble Version

1. Opponent turns away (to avoid giving up the pin) and tries to stand.
2. Sit your shin down on the hamstring for max contact.
3. Their leg is slowed — slide the other foot in as a hook.
4. Seize the far arm for diagonal control before falling to the mat.

## Knee-Entry Version

1. Pinch both knees around one of the opponent's legs (two-on-one wedge).
2. Transition the trapped leg into the hook.
3. Works with zero flexibility required.

## Don't

- Don't throw a hook in without first restraining the target leg — that's a beginner habit, not championship-level control.
