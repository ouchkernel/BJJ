# Technical Notes On Rear Naked Strangle — Quick Reference

## The point

Most rear naked strangle failures happen at one specific moment: the transition from control hand to support hand. Manage that transition correctly and the strangle holds together even against a strong defense.

## The two mistakes that kill the strangle

1. **Strangle hand to bicep** — hands your support hand to the opponent, who yanks it down. You lose control entirely; opponent turns inside.
2. **Support hand on top of the skull** — opponent strips it down easily. Same disaster.

## Correct transition sequence

1. Get behind, penetrate — control hand under the arm, strangle hand over the shoulder.
2. Hide the thumb line of the strangle hand behind the opponent's neck (if he gets the thumb line, he can stop the strangle).
3. Don't release the control hand too early.
4. When you do release it: drive your head IN and press against the opponent's escape direction (his escape = the direction your strangle-hand fingers point). Head compensates for the lost hand.
5. Withdraw the control hand **wrist over wrist** — cross wrists, never cross arms.
6. Elbow goes in front — okay to give up the elbow, never the wrist.
7. Punch across into position.

## Checkpoint

From in front of you (camera/onlooker view), you should be able to see the **fingernails of the support hand**. Can't see them → not penetrated enough → keep pushing through.

## Finish

- Chin over the wrist of the support hand, look toward the strangle.
- Rotational finish: elbow rotates over the shoulder (preferred).
- Contracting finish: elbows come together going forward (alternative).

## Don't

- Don't go strangle-hand-to-bicep. Ever.
- Don't rest the support hand on top of the skull.
- Don't cross arms — cross wrists.

## Applies everywhere

Same sequence works kneeling, with real hooks in, and on both the over and underside.
