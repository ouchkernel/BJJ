# Alignment And Deficit Problem — Quick Reference

## The two central problems of rear mount

1. **Alignment Problem**
2. **Deficit Problem**

Not sequential — must be solved at the same time in live rolling.

## Alignment Problem

- Rear mount naturally lines your spine up with the opponent's.
- Head trap = the basis of alignment (strangle arm one side, head the other, opponent's head trapped between).
- Opponent's whole escape plan = break this alignment, starting by attacking the head trap.
- Measure of alignment = is your chest still connected to his back? As it separates, strangle chances drop.
- Goal: stay aligned, full stop.

## Deficit Problem

- Count the weapons on each side:
  - **You:** typically 1 strangle arm (the other arm is tied up as a control hand). With a body triangle, up to 2.
  - **Them:** up to 3 defensive instruments — left hand, right hand, jaw. Even controlling one of his arms, he still has 1 free hand + jaw against your 1 strangle arm.
- You are **always outnumbered**, no matter how you configure your arms. This is true even though back is supposedly the best position in the sport.
- Solving this deficit is one of the most important jobs of the rest of the system.

## Don't

- Don't treat these as a checklist you solve one at a time — in a live scramble you're managing alignment and the numbers deficit simultaneously.
