# Neutrality Principle — Quick Reference

## The point

There's no "strong side" / "weak side" to falling with the back. Danaher's system is neutral left or right — what matters is keeping your strangle hand and head on opposite sides of your opponent's head, not which direction you fall.

## The one non-negotiable rule

1. Strangle hand and head must always be on **opposite sides** of the opponent's head.
2. Opponent's whole game: force your head and strangle hand onto the *same* side.
3. Your whole game: keep them apart, on opposite sides, at all times.

## How to make either side safe

1. Keep your strangle hand close to your own jaw (tight head position) — this closes the "circle" around the opponent's neck.
2. Gap between hand and head = opponent's head slides through = disaster (head and hand end up on the same side).
3. No gap = opponent can't escape, regardless of which side you fell to.
4. Losing head position? Stop the attack, re-control the head, reset — then continue.

## Terminology

- Use **over side** / **underside** — not "strong side" / "weak side."
- Falling to the underside has a bonus: unweighted leg available to trap and pin an opponent's arm.

## Don't

Don't believe one side is intrinsically stronger. Quality comes from maintaining hand/head connection, not from which side you chose.
