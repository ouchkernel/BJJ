# Reverse Top Lock System

## Overview

The second major auxiliary system. Borrows a well-known principle from armbars off closed guard — the "top lock" — and applies it from behind the back instead. Fixes a specific, common failure: haphazardly throwing a leg over an opponent's head for a Juji Gatame from the back tips him off and gets the leg caught or the head turned away. The Reverse Top Lock breaks that big, risky motion into two small, controllable phases, and opens directly into the Rear Triangle, the Front Triangle, or a reinforced Juji Gatame depending on how the opponent reacts.

---

## What A Top Lock Is (From Closed Guard)

- In the classic Juji Gatame from closed guard, if you go straight for the armbar against an experienced opponent, he sees the turn coming as you start it and pulls his arm free before your leg lands over his shoulder — the whole attempt collapses.
- The modern, high-percentage fix: control the opponent's shoulders and lock your feet **over** his shoulders first (the "top lock"), elevating your hips. This dramatically shortens the distance your cross-face leg has to travel, creates a good turning surface off his shoulder, and breaks the armbar into two distinct phases — first the top lock, then the turn into Juji Gatame — rather than one long, telegraphed motion.

## Why Just Throwing The Leg Over Fails From The Back

- From the back, if you abandon the strangling hand, swing it across, and lock a Kimura grip, an alert opponent immediately knows the strangle threat is gone. If you then just haphazardly throw your leg over his head for Juji Gatame, he can time and catch that leg.
- Once he's caught your leg, you have no cross-face leg. He can push your foot away, turn into you, and come up to his base — you're left holding a rather weak position.
- Even with the Kimura already locked, simply trying to throw the leg over invites the opponent to turn his head sharply into you, denying you the room to travel over his head — the "turn-in problem."

## Building The Reverse Top Lock

- Start exactly as before: forsake the strangling hand and bring it over to lock a Kimura grip on the opponent's arm.
- Instead of throwing the leg all the way to the head, apply the top-lock principle from the back: take your leg over his **shoulder** — a much shorter distance — and lock your feet over that far shoulder.
- As he tries to turn into you, you control the far shoulder directly through the top lock. Even against considerable effort, you can force his shoulders back down to the mat.

## Branch Points From The Reverse Top Lock

Once the shoulders are pinned to the mat, most opponents lock their hands together defensively. From this single position, without releasing the top lock, several attacks are available depending on what he does:

1. **Straight to the Rear Triangle.** If you can separate his hands and get a foot into the armpit, go directly into Ushiro Sankaku for the strangle — same mechanics covered in the rear-triangle chapters.
2. **He turns in → Front Triangle.** If he tries to turn hard into you to escape, he turns himself directly up into an Omote Sankaku (front triangle). His own escape attempt delivers him into the strangle.
3. **He stays flat → three-quarter Juji Gatame.** If he's smart enough to realize turning up is a disaster and stays where he is, lock a Juji Gatame from here — specifically a "three-quarter" version where the head **and** shoulders are controlled (not just the torso and chest, as in a conventional Juji Gatame). This makes the turnout escape dramatically harder to complete, and the breaking pressure on the shoulder is very strong.
4. **Kimura / Americana.** The same top-lock control also supports Kimura and Americana finishes if the arm won't straighten for an armbar.

## The Central Problem: The Turn-In

- Throughout this whole position, the opponent's single goal is to turn inside your legs and get back up to his base — once he's up on his knees he's far harder to control than when he's flat on his back.
- Counter: open your knee and actively drive his shoulder down to the mat every time he tries to turn up.
- If he locks his arms too tightly to progress toward the rear triangle, feed your second foot/shoelace inside instead. Either he completes the turn and rises directly into a front triangle, or he wisely stays down and you finish with the three-quarter Juji Gatame.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The Reverse Top Lock is the same principle that makes top-lock Juji Gatame from closed guard so effective, applied from the back: control the shoulder with your feet first, in two short phases, instead of one long telegraphed reach for the head.
- Build it from a Kimura grip → leg over the far shoulder → top lock → drive the shoulder to the mat.
- From there it branches without releasing control: Rear Triangle if you separate the hands, Front Triangle if he turns in, three-quarter Juji Gatame if he stays flat, or Kimura/Americana if the arm won't straighten.
- The opponent's whole game is turning in to get to his base — open the knee and drive his shoulder to the mat every time he tries.
- His "escape" (turning up) and his "defense" (staying down) both lead to a finish — that's what makes this position so strong.
