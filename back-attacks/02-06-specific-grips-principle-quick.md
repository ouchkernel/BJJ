# Specific Grips Principle — Quick Reference

## The point

Two grip families — straight and cross — target completely different parts of the arm and work through different mechanics. Know which one you're using.

## Straight grips

1. Left hand on his left hand, right hand on his right hand.
2. Target the knuckle line — grab all four knuckles (legal), never fingers/single digits (illegal).
3. Press his hand into his own ribcage.
4. Don't grab the wrist instead — he can just extend his arm and break it.

## Cross grips

1. Thumb underneath, inside position on his wrist, targeting the vein side.
2. Fingers wrap over the top, hold in tight.
3. Two effects stack together:
   - Straitjacket effect — elbows locked to ribs, escape limited to weak rotator cuff muscles.
   - Rope-and-knot effect — grip slides down the forearm and catches at the hand, like a knot at a rope's end.
4. Best case: both wrists controlled with cross grips.

## Bottom line

Cross grips > straight grips for raw control. But both have a job: straight = knuckle line + press to ribs; cross = wrist vein + straitjacket/knot effect.

## Don't

Don't grab the wrist with a straight grip, and don't grab fingers instead of the knuckle line — both weaken the control.
