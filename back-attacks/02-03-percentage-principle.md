# Percentage Principle

## Overview

The third critical principle, and a close cousin of the Primacy Principle. Where Primacy establishes strangles over joint locks in general, the Percentage Principle narrows the focus further: within the world of back strangles specifically, the rear naked strangle is so far ahead of every alternative that it deserves overwhelming, sustained emphasis. The same style of principle appeared in Danaher's leg lock system.

---

## Why the rear naked strangle gets the emphasis

- There is no serious debate: the rear naked strangle (mata leao) is the single most effective submission hold in nogi jiu-jitsu and in mixed martial arts. Nothing else is even close — not in finish rate, not in finality, not in certainty of the finish.
- Danaher's back system reflects this reality directly: it places a heavy, disproportionate emphasis on the rear naked strangle above every other method of finishing that the system contains.
- Once everything is locked in — control arm, strangle arm, head position — the level of control achieved is extraordinary, and it becomes very difficult for the opponent to defend once fully applied.

## How this shapes strategy

- If you're ever offered your opponent's back, the rear naked strangle should always be your first, concerted line of attack — not a token attempt before moving on.
- Don't just "flirt" with the rear naked strangle for a few seconds and then switch to something else the moment it's not immediately available. Batter down the walls with it first.
- Only pivot to another finishing option once you're genuinely stopped in your tracks for a considerable period of time — that's the signal your opponent has cast-iron defense to the RNS specifically, not just a temporary excuse to abandon it.
- The historical track record backs this up: for generations, the rear naked strangle has finished more people, under more circumstances, than any other submission hold. Applied correctly, it will do the same for you.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The rear naked strangle is statistically the highest-percentage submission in nogi grappling and MMA — nothing else is close.
- The back system is deliberately built around heavy emphasis on the RNS above all other finishes.
- Attack with sustained commitment to the RNS first whenever you have the back; don't abandon it after a brief attempt.
- Only switch to another finishing method once the opponent has demonstrated genuinely cast-iron defense against the RNS specifically, over a considerable stretch of time.
- Go high percentage: lean on what has historically finished the most people, not what's momentarily convenient.
