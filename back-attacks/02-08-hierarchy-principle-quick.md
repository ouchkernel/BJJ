# Hierarchy Principle — Quick Reference

## The point

Not all hand-fighting positions behind an opponent are equal — there's a clear hierarchy from worst to best. Know where you are and what you're climbing toward.

## Grading test

Can this grip easily pull the opponent's secondary hand/thumb below his pec (chest) line?
- Below the line = easy to hook the arm.
- Above the line = requires real flexibility.

## The hierarchy (worst → best)

1. Two straight grips, both bottom (he has top hand on strangle side) — escape this ASAP.
2. Two straight grips, one top (control side) / one bottom — adequate, workable.
3. Single cross grip — better; hold one hand, his other hand doesn't matter yet.
4. Double cross grip — the strongest position, often gifted to you when he tries to peel off a single cross grip.

## Action steps

1. Always fight hardest to keep top hand position on the control side.
2. Bottom hand is tolerable only on the strangle side (legs can back it up).
3. From single cross grip: he ignores your strangle hand → strangle immediately. He locks your wrist → foot over, hand behind back, strip the grip, uncontested strangle hand.
4. From double cross grip: foot over the top, lock in, uncontested strangle hand.

## Don't

Don't settle for two straight grips on bottom — pull back and reclaim top position before doing anything else.
