# Near Side Entries 03

## Overview

Introduces a tight-waist-based breakdown that creates a large enough opening to justify breaking the "near side first" rule established earlier — because the opponent, broken down to a hip and elbow, is in a non-athletic position and cannot simply stand up to dislodge you. Covers the base technique plus three variations for handling how the opponent's far leg reacts.

---

## Base technique: tight waist breakdown into a far-side foot entry

1. Start behind the opponent; establish a tight waist grip.
2. Move to the side; use your knee as a blocker and pull the opponent over the knee, then pull yourself out. This breaks him down to an elbow and a hip — a non-athletic position.
3. As your head crowds the opponent's head, switch to a seat belt grip.
4. With the opponent broken down like this, the space opened up is large ("you could drive a truck through it") — your knee penetrates very easily.
5. **Exception to the near-side-first rule:** because the opponent is broken down to a hip, he cannot simply stand up on you and dislodge you — his priority is getting back to turtle first. That safety margin means it's fine here to throw the **far-side hook in as a foot entry** (rather than the usual near-side knee entry) while he's still trying to recover to turtle.
6. Once the far-side hook lands, recline to the side of the established hook and fall over to that side.
7. From here, lock up whatever form of back control suits your and your opponent's body sizes.

### Repeated demonstration

- Behind the opponent → tight waist → hand down to the floor → knee blocks → pull opponent over the knee → pull body back to keep opponent broken down on the floor.
- As he tries to return to turtle, establish (or re-establish) the seat belt; as he moves, slide the knee into the gap between elbow and hip.
- With the opponent still trying to stand back up, throw the far-side foot entry in — permissible specifically because he's broken down to a hip, not because it's generally safe to skip near-side-first.
- Fall to the side of the established hook, then apply the second hook/lock of your choice.

## Variation A — opponent brings his knee to his chest

- If, while breaking the opponent down, you feel his knee come up to his chest, the far-side foot entry may no longer be available directly.
- Response: pull his chest back toward you; as you pull, extend your bottom leg. The hook goes in easily as his knee separates from his chest again.

## Variation B — opponent's knee is fully tucked to the chest

- When the opponent successfully tucks his knee tight to his chest, making the far-side entry hard to reach directly:
- Response: cross your feet/ankles and hook them over the opponent's hip. Extend your whole body to pull his hips away from his chest — this increases the distance between knee and chest, and you can then pass your foot in over the top.

## Variation C — opponent blocks the far-side foot entry directly

- If the opponent does a good job of blocking the far-side foot entry as your knee slides in:
- Response: draw his body in toward you; as his legs become light, the near-side hook is easily established. The far-side hook is still difficult because of his leg position.
- Go underneath the opponent and lock your feet together; straighten your whole body out to pull his knee away from his chest — this makes it relatively easy to establish the back positions you favor.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Tight waist breakdown to a hip/elbow removes the opponent's ability to simply stand up and dislodge you — that safety margin is what justifies throwing the far-side hook in as a foot entry, an explicit exception to the near-side-first default from earlier chapters.
- Base sequence: tight waist → knee-block breakdown to hip/elbow → seat belt → knee penetrates easily through the large opening → far-side foot entry → fall to the side of the established hook → lock up back control.
- If the opponent's knee comes to his chest and blocks the far-side entry: pull his chest toward you and extend your bottom leg to reopen the gap.
- If his knee is fully tucked: cross your ankles/hook them over his hip and extend your whole body to pull his hips away from his chest, then pass the foot over.
- If he directly blocks the far-side foot entry: draw his body in to lighten his legs and take the near-side hook easily first; for the far side, go underneath, lock your feet, and straighten your body to pull his knee away from his chest before finishing the entry.
