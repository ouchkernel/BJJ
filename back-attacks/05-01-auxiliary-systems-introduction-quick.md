# Introduction To The Auxiliary Systems — Quick Reference

## The point

SJS forces one weapon (your arms) through one small target (the neck). Even at its best, some days that breakthrough won't come. The auxiliary systems are what you switch to on those days.

## Straitjacket System vs. Auxiliary Systems

| | Straitjacket System | Auxiliary Systems |
|---|---|---|
| Attacking tool | Arms only | Whole body |
| Target | One small area (neck) | Whole opponent body |
| Effect | Concentrated force | Distributed, unpredictable attacks |
| Role | Primary — most finishes | Backup — for when the arms stall |

## Why legs first

1. Legs are ~2-3x stronger than arms.
2. Things that stop an arm strangle (hand interference, etc.) often don't stop a leg strangle.
3. First auxiliary system: switch from arm-strangle to leg-strangle via the Rear Triangle (Ushiro Sankaku).

## Don't

Don't treat the auxiliary systems as a replacement for SJS. SJS still does most of the work — these are what you reach for when it stalls.
