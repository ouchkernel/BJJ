# Solving The Pinned Arm Problem — Quick Reference

## The problem

Falling toward your strangle-hand side (the "traditional safe" choice) pins that same-side leg under his body weight — you lose the ability to hook and trap his arm, so the deficit problem can never be solved.

## Rule of thumb

| Starting point | Fall direction | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Already in rear mount | Away from the strangle hand | Keeps that leg free/unweighted to hook his arm later |
| Turtle position, arm hookable before you commit | Toward the strangle hand is fine (even better) | Hook goes in before the pin — combined body weight reinforces the trap |

## From rear mount: the fix

1. Fall away from the strangle hand.
2. Watch for his hand to drop below the line of his chest.
3. Come over the top with the now-free leg and hook/trap that arm.
4. Result: uncontested strangle hand (both his hands occupied — one by your control hand, one by your leg) — free hand to the jaw, go finish.

## From turtle: the fix

1. Hook the arm **before** falling/committing to the floor.
2. Then fall toward the strangle-hand side — the trap only gets stronger once both bodies' weight lands on it.

## Don't

Don't sit down first and then try to hook the arm on the strangle-hand side afterward — he's already sitting on that leg, hook is impossible. Only fix at that point: switch sides.
