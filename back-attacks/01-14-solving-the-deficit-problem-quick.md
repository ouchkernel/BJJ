# Solving The Deficit Problem — Quick Reference

## The problem, restated

- You: 1 offensive instrument (strangle arm), 2 max (with a body triangle freeing the control arm).
- Him: 2–3 defensive instruments (both arms + jaw, or 1 arm + jaw if you're already controlling one).
- You are always outnumbered from the "best position in the sport."

## Myth to drop: strong side / weak side

- Old rule: fall away from the gap next to your control hand (the "weak side" is where his head can slip through).
- Reality: don't think in strong/weak — think **underside / overside**, and treat both as equally safe once you close the gaps properly. Sometimes deliberately favor the "weak side" because it frees a leg to trap an arm.

## What actually matters: close the circle

1. **Upper body** — strangle hand over the shoulder, chin down to touch your own hand/fist (ear to ear). No gap for the head to escape through, either side.
2. **Lower body** — protected bottom hook (post rear mount or body lock, from the earlier chapter). Same idea, at the hips.

## The payoff

Once both circles are closed, side is irrelevant to safety — so pick whichever side sets up your leg-trapping (the actual deficit-solving move) instead of defaulting to the "traditional" safe side.
