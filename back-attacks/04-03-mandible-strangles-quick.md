# Mandible Strangles — Quick Reference

## The point

When you can't penetrate under the jaw, the mandible strangle (over the jawbone) still produces real unconsciousness — not pain compliance — if the mechanics are right.

## Why it works

1. No strangle is 100% efficient — strangulation reduces blood flow, it doesn't fully cut it off.
2. Four arteries feed the brain (2 front/carotid, 2 back/vertebral) — you don't need clean carotid contact to reduce flow enough.
3. World-class athletes don't tap to jaw/teeth pain — they tap to the threat of passing out.

## Setup steps

1. Prefer under-the-jaw penetration first; use mandible only when that's not available.
2. Match your elbow to the opponent's chin — pull the elbow around physically if needed. Requires deeper penetration than normal.
3. Use hand as backup to secure the elbow-to-chin alignment.
4. Apply the same cross-wrist / rotational method used for the standard strangle.
5. Rotate over the shoulder to finish.

## The real-vs-fake test

- **Real mandible strangle:** elbow touches the opponent's sternum.
- **Fake/failed version:** forearm over teeth or nose, trying to lift the opponent, no sternum contact — this just annoys people (and can draw referee penalties), it will never cause unconsciousness.

## Proof it's working

Bloodshot eyes. Can't be faked. Appears even when the opponent has full defensive grips locked in and zero jaw penetration.

## Don't

- Don't confuse discomfort with a real strangle.
- Don't settle for pressing the forearm over the teeth/nose — get the elbow to the sternum.
- Don't expect it to work as fast as an under-the-jaw strangle — it takes slightly longer but is just as effective.
