# Back Crucifix — Arm Locks — Quick Reference

## Why it works

Legs > arms, always — even a much stronger opponent's upper body won't out-power your legs. Best equalizer for smaller vs. bigger opponent.

## Entry 1 — wrestling referee's position (opponent trying to stand)

Trigger: opponent's elbows come off the mat in turtle (= they're about to stand).

1. Turn the corner as they rise.
2. Foot in to hook the arm immediately.
3. Hand underneath, **passing method** to draw their hand to you (don't reach/grab directly).
4. Lock up, legs into figure-4.

## Entry 2 — front headlock / opponent on all fours

1. Head across their body.
2. Enter with the "wrong" leg.
3. Hook and bring them down to the floor.

## Figure-4 arm lock — the adjustment period

The initial figure-4 is NOT enough by itself. Always adjust:

1. Open triangle, flare knees wide.
2. Knee-shuffle backward, free hand posted on floor (no seatbelt grip — unstable).
3. Wiggle hips until your **hip bone** (not inner thigh/groin) sits on the opponent's elbow.
4. Flare knees + arch forward (head up, pelvis forward) = the break.

Mistake to avoid: knees together = nothing to sprawl against.

## Passing the arm

1. Cross-face, extend the arm for direct pressure.
2. Opponent twists out to escape → switch your legs.
3. You're now set up on their **other** arm — repeat the figure-4/knee-shuffle/hip-to-elbow sequence.

## Alternative finish — scissor legs + head control

Use when triangle/figure-4 lets the opponent's elbow slip ("spaghetti").

1. Control the head: forearms crossed pinning ear to chest, OR back of hand across the jawline (not eyes/nose).
2. Don't triangle the legs — **scissor** them: one foot ankle-deep to the wrist, other leg wedges the armpit.
3. Bridge off the floor → opponent's elbow goes over your hip → break.

## The dilemma (use this to actually finish tough opponents)

- Arm pressure present but not breaking → release, flare hand through, go for the strangle.
- Opponent defends the strangle hard (chin tucked) → walk down the body, switch to the arm.
- Keep alternating. That's what cracks a determined opponent.

## Stay connected to the main system

Opponent bridges to fight the strangle → shift them with your hip, figure-4 the trapped arm, transition straight back into rear mount. Back Crucifix ↔ rear mount ↔ finishes — one connected system, never reset from scratch.
