# Through Movement 2

## Overview

A higher-level extension of the previous through-movement recovery. Here the opponent doesn't stop at beating the bottom hook — he beats the bottom knee as well, which the simple leg-extension recovery can no longer fix. This calls for a more sophisticated movement: the tripod, used purely as a mechanism to re-establish knee position on both sides of the opponent's body.

---

## The situation

- A more advanced opponent doesn't just beat the bottom hook — he continues on and beats the bottom knee too, escalating past what the basic through-movement fix (leg extension) can solve.
- Good news: even having lost the hook and knee, the head trap is often still intact — a strong seat-belt grip around the opponent still keeps the head on one side and the strangle arm on the other, so there's still some thread of a strangle available.
- Bad news: the leg on that side can no longer be used to "shovel" the opponent back the way it could when only the hook was lost — that option is gone once the knee itself is beaten.

## The tripod recovery

- Track the opponent's head instead of the leg: since his whole plan is to bring his head to one side, you must **move your own head in the same direction** — you cannot stay static while his head moves, or the head trap is lost outright.
- Use the bottom leg: put the ball of that foot on the mat and push, driving your own head to the mat and in front of his before his head can get in front of yours.
- From that position, push up into a **tripod** (weight momentarily on head and feet, body lifted) over the training partner.
- While elevated, lift and insert the knee back in on the far side of the opponent's arm, so the knee re-emerges in the correct control position a second time.
- The hook on that side then drags the opponent back into alignment, re-establishing a knee on either side of his body — full lower-body control restored.
- Finish by locking in a post rear mount or body triangle on the recovered side, exactly as with a single lost hook.

## The underlying principle

- The knee, not the foot/hook, is the true locus of lower-body control — this is repeated from the previous chapter and remains the guiding idea here.
- The tripod itself has no independent value; it is purely a mechanical device for recovering knee position once it's been lost. Don't think of it as a technique in its own right — think of it as the vehicle that gets your knee back where it needs to be.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Use this recovery when the opponent has beaten both the bottom hook AND the bottom knee — the simple leg-extension fix from the previous chapter is no longer enough.
- Match the opponent's head movement with your own head movement (via the bottom leg pushing off the mat) — staying static loses the head trap.
- The tripod is the mechanism: push up, lift, reinsert the knee on the far side of the opponent's arm, drag him back with the hook, and re-lock on the recovered side.
- The knee is always the real target of control — the tripod exists only to recover it, nothing more.
