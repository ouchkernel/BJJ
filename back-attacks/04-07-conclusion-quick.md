# Conclusion — Quick Reference

## The point

The Straitjacket System is the centerpiece of the whole back attack system. Regardless of which side you fall to, the goal is always the same: an uncontested strangle hand.

## Core reframe

- No "strong side" / "weak side." Just underarm side and overarm side — neither is better.
- Single goal either way: **uncontested strangle hand** — nothing between your strangle arm and the opponent's neck.
- Why the legs matter: opponent always has 2 defensive arms vs. your 1 strangle arm (the "deficit problem"). Legs make up the difference.

## Underarm side recap

1. Protect the bottom hook (high, locked, or at least weighted).
2. Trap secondary arm with the legs — double cross grip → leg hooks the arm behind his back.
3. Trap primary arm — nothing left between strangle arm and neck but the chin.
4. Flat fist penetration → rotational finish (one-arm, two-arm, or mandible).

## Overarm side recap

1. From turtle position: wedge behind the tricep, leg hooks around.
2. Roll through first, trap the arm second, use body weight to pin it.
3. If already fallen with the leg pinned: side-switch — trap primary arm with free leg, recover control hand, head pommel, then finish.

## The two problems this whole system solves

1. **Alignment problem** — strangle arm and head must stay on opposite sides of the opponent's head.
2. **Deficit problem** — you have one strangle arm against the opponent's two defensive arms; the legs close the gap.

## Bottom line

Which side you fall to doesn't matter. Reaching the uncontested strangle hand does.
