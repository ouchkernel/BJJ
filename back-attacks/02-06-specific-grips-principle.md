# Specific Grips Principle

## Overview

The sixth critical principle. Behind an opponent, exactly how you grip his hands matters a great deal. There are two main grip families — straight grips and cross grips — and each one targets a different part of the opponent's arm and works through a different mechanical principle.

---

## Straight grips

- Straight grip: left hand controls the opponent's left hand, right hand controls his right hand.
- Target: the **knuckle line**. Grabbing individual fingers is illegal (especially single digits), but grabbing all four knuckles together is fully legal and creates much stronger control by controlling the far end of the lever.
- Press the opponent's hand into his own ribcage with this grip — from there it's enormously difficult for him to free it.
- Grabbing the wrist instead of the knuckle line is a mistake: the opponent can simply extend his arm and break a wrist grip.
- Whether it's the primary or secondary defensive hand, straight grips always target the knuckle line.

## Cross grips

- Cross grip: thumb goes underneath and takes the inside position on the opponent's wrist, targeting the area where the veins of the wrist are exposed. Fingers wrap over the top, holding in.
- Two mechanical principles make the cross grip especially strong:
  1. **Straitjacket effect** — like a physical straitjacket, the grip locks the opponent's elbow to his own ribs (and locks your elbow to your ribs on the other side). This forces any escape attempt through only the weak rotator cuff muscles of the shoulder — very difficult to develop enough strength there to break free.
  2. **Rope-and-knot effect** — the forearm is thick, thins at the wrist, and thickens again at the hand (the "knot"). Your grip naturally slides down and catches on that knot, the same way a hand sliding down a rope catches on a knot tied at the end.
- Best of all is having both of the opponent's wrists controlled with cross grips simultaneously.

## Putting it together

- Straight grips and cross grips are gripped completely differently — know which one you're using and target the right part of the arm for each.
- Straight grips: dominate the knuckle line, press everything into the ribs.
- Cross grips: target the vein side of the wrist, get the straitjacket effect plus the rope-and-knot effect — this combination makes it very hard for the opponent to break away.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Straight grips target the knuckle line (all four knuckles, never fingers) and press the hand to the ribcage; gripping the wrist instead lets the opponent extend and break free.
- Cross grips target the vein side of the wrist and work through two combined mechanisms: the straitjacket effect (locks the escape to weak rotator cuff muscles) and the rope-and-knot effect (grip catches at the hand like a knot at the end of a rope).
- Cross grips are the stronger of the two grip families overall.
- Know exactly which grip you're using and target the correct part of the arm — mixing up the targets weakens both grip types.
