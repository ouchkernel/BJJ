# Overarm Side — Wedging Method — Quick Reference

## The point

Trap the arm *before* falling to the overarm side — while the opponent is still in turtle position — using a knee wedge. Two versions: closed turtle and open turtle.

## Turtle position types

| Type | Elbow/knee contact | Pro | Con |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open | No contact, elbows outside knees | Wide, stable base | Easy to penetrate/attack |
| Closed | Elbows inside, touching knees | Defensively tight | Easy to off-balance |

Law of extension/contraction: opponent can't have both stability and defensive tightness at once.

## Method 1 — Closed turtle

1. Tight waist grip, hand on floor, block his knee.
2. Knee wedge pulls him over → break down to a hip.
3. He bases out with an elbow (or you pin him outright).
4. Lock seatbelt around the corner; rise up; wedge behind his tricep.
5. Foot hooks around the corner; rake leg back, knee to floor. Arm trapped.
6. Head down, hips open, roll through — both body weights land on the trapped arm.
7. Insert hook → uncontested strangle hand → finish.

## Method 2 — Open turtle

1. Post on his two hands, rise turning head across him.
2. Knee penetrates behind his arm/tricep as a wedge.
3. Point knee OUT (away from centerline), heel in first — no flexibility needed this way.
4. Turn knee in, rake backward to catch the arm, drive knee to floor.
5. Arm around neck, head to floor, hips open, roll through.
6. His secondary arm traps under his own body weight; insert hook.
7. Chin down on the roll = easy mandible strangle. Otherwise: one-arm or two-arm finish.

## Key details

- Crossed feet: the **bottom** foot is the one actually controlling the trapped arm.
- Grip the knuckle line, not just the wrist, to prevent extraction.
- Order is reversed from normal: trap the arm FIRST, take rear mount SECOND.

## Don't

- Don't point the knee across the opponent's centerline first when hooking open turtle — it requires too much flexibility. Heel in first, knee out.
- Don't try to trap the arm after already falling on top of it — do it before you commit to the floor.
