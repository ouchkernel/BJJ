# Protecting The Bottom Hook — Quick Reference

## The problem

Conventional symmetric hooks put almost no elevation or weight on the bottom hook — a tiny movement beats it and starts the whole escape sequence.

## 3 reinforcement options

1. **Post rear mount** — post top foot on opponent's hip, drive weight through it (bridging motion), bring other foot up to meet it. Adds weight + elevation.
2. **Figure-4 body triangle** — lock triangle around the abdomen (outside leg preferred; inside-leg version is a fine compromise). Closed wedge, bottom hook reinforced by the other leg.
3. **Crossed feet under the hamstring** — feet crossed pointing opposite directions, going *over the hip* (not just behind the back). Closed wedge, near-body-triangle strength.

## Which one to use

| Opponent build | Your legs | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Big waist | Short | Post rear mount |
| Slim waist | Long | Body triangle |
| Big waist, no triangle possible | — | Crossed-ankle rear mount |

## Footlock fear — don't be intimidated

- Danger is real only when feet cross **inside** the opponent's hips poorly. Crossing **outside** the hips = minimal risk.
- Any footlock counter or leg-triangle-walk requires the opponent to **arch** (head back, pelvis forward) for pressure.
- Counter: stiff-arm his head with both hands the moment he arches → zero pressure → turn and extract your foot → return to finishing position.
- Beginners: still don't cross feet. Advanced level: fine, and the counter to any counter is trivial.
