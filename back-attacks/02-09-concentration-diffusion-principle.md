# Concentration/Diffusion Principle

## Overview

The ninth critical principle, and one of the most conceptually important for understanding how the back system as a whole is structured. It explains the relationship between the Straitjacket System (SJS) and the Auxiliary Systems by contrasting two opposite attacking philosophies: concentration of force versus diffusion of targets.

---

## Concentration of force: the Straitjacket System

- Most of the back system is, at its core, an elaborate hand-fighting system built to set up the single highest-percentage finish from the back: the rear naked strangle.
- The rear naked strangle uses one weapon — an arm — against one very specific, very localized target: the neck.
- That's a battle of concentration: one arm has to penetrate a small, heavily defended area to land one specific move.
- Because both athletes know exactly what's being attacked, the opponent can localize all his defensive resources around that single area. This is precisely why an elaborate hand-fighting system (straight grips, cross grips, hidden hand, default principle, hierarchy, etc.) is required — to fight through concentrated defense toward a concentrated target.

## Diffusion of targets: the Auxiliary Systems

- The Auxiliary Systems operate on the opposite principle: diffusion. Rather than one weapon against one target, they spread a wide array of different attacks — arm locks, leg locks, other strangleholds, even spinal cranks — across the whole length and breadth of the opponent's body.
- Where the SJS concentrates everything on one weapon and one target, the Auxiliary Systems diffuse attacks broadly, threatening many different parts of the body at once.

## Why the contrast matters

- These two philosophies are meant to be played against each other, not used in isolation.
- Alternating between a concentrated attack (the constant threat of the rear naked strangle) and diffuse attacks (the wide range of auxiliary submissions across the body) creates tension in the opponent's defense.
- The opponent cannot simultaneously defend one small, heavily-attacked area with total commitment and also defend threats appearing all over his body. Playing concentration against diffusion is how the breakthrough is ultimately achieved.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The Straitjacket System is built on concentration of force: one weapon (the strangling arm) against one localized target (the neck), requiring an elaborate hand-fighting system to break through concentrated defense.
- The Auxiliary Systems are built on diffusion: a wide array of different submissions spread across the whole body.
- Neither philosophy is used alone — the tension between them is the mechanism that creates openings.
- Practically: keep threatening the rear naked strangle to keep the opponent's defense concentrated, then diffuse into auxiliary attacks (and back again) to exploit the gaps that concentrated defense inevitably leaves open elsewhere.
