# Near Side Entries 01 — Quick Reference

## Definitions

- **Near side hook** = same side you're positioned on.
- **Far side hook** = opposite side, farther to travel.
- Default rule: go near side first (exceptions come later).

## Technique: low-amplitude knee entry (vs. tight/closed turtle)

1. Wrist grip → transition to seat belt.
2. Punch knee in behind opponent's tricep, over the thigh, pointed back toward his hip/crotch.
3. Other leg = drive/push leg.
4. Sag your weight down (not a forward roll) — pull with the seat belt, pinch knees around his hip.
5. Extend your near-side leg — knee control becomes the finished hook.
6. Open the second leg, shift hips into attacking position.

Angle-2 cue: grounding knee shovels under his body as you drag him down, lifting his hips; foot passes over his leg into the hook.

## Low-amplitude vs. high-amplitude

| | Low amplitude | High amplitude |
|---|---|---|
| Style | Drags opponent down | Athletic rolling entry |
| Risk | Low | Real risk of slipping off, landing bottom north-south |
| Look | Unremarkable | Spectacular |
| Used by top competitors | Most common | Less common |

Default to low amplitude.
