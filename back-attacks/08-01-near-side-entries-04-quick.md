# Near Side Entries 04 — Quick Reference

## Knee vs. Foot Entry

1. Knee entry = two steps (knee in, then extend leg) — easier, safer default.
2. Foot entry = one step, hook is complete instantly — faster, but needs strong body contact + a broken-down opponent.
3. Use foot entries against broken-down opponents; use knee entries in uncontrolled scrambles.

## Setup: Tight Waist Breakdown

1. From turtle, glue elbow (or wrist) into opponent's hip.
2. Step near knee across from hip to hip, pull opponent over.
3. Feel balance break, withdraw knee, break opponent down to a hip.
4. Keep chest-to-back contact; don't lock a full seat belt yet — keep elbow glued to hip instead.

## Forcing the Foot Entry

1. Opponent tries to climb back to turtle — elbow pressure blocks every route except scissoring the legs.
2. The scissor action opens the space for your foot.
3. Slide the whole shin along the mat as the foot enters — hook is complete on contact.
4. Immediately lock diagonal control (second hook).

## Don't

- Don't pick a leg up and step the hook in.
- Don't force a foot entry against a settled opponent with no body contact.
