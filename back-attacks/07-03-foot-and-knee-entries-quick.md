# Foot And Knee Entries — Quick Reference

## The choice

Every hook entry is either a **knee entry** (two-stage) or a **foot entry** (one-stage).

| | Foot | Knee |
|---|---|---|
| Stages | 1 (instant hook) | 2 (knee, then extend) |
| Flexibility | More | Less |
| Space needed | More (open turtle) | Less (works vs. tight turtle) |
| Chest-to-back contact | Harder to keep | Easier to keep |
| Beginner default? | No | Yes |

## Mistakes to avoid

1. Don't lift your chest off the opponent's back to reach for a hook.
2. Don't throw a leg in (especially far-side) with zero weight on the opponent.
3. Don't trust a technique just because it worked once — test: would it work on a resisting world champion?

## 2 red flags of a bad entry

1. You lose most/all upper-body contact.
2. You lose all weight/control over the opponent's movement.
   - Both at once = disaster.

## Knee entry mechanics (2 phases)

1. Knee penetrates the elbow-to-thigh gap (small target — works even vs. closed turtle). Upper leg pinches thigh like a hook.
2. Extend the leg — lower leg becomes the actual hook.

## Foot entry mechanics

Foot goes straight in as the hook, in one motion. Fast and clean when it works; needs more space/flexibility and more risk if contact is lost.
