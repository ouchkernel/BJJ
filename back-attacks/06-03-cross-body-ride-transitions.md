# Cross Body Ride — Transitions

## Overview

Two further ways Cross Body Ride distributes attacks beyond the legs and the hammer lock: a torso/spine attack (the "twister," made famous by Eddie Bravo) and a direct transition into strangleholds, culminating in the rear naked strangle. Together with the leg attacks and hammer lock, these complete a chain of submissions running the full length of the opponent's body — ankle, knee, hip, torso, arm, spine, neck.

---

## Twister: torso, rib, and spine attack

- Originally a wrestling move (called a "guillotine" in American high school wrestling) — that name is reserved in jiu-jitsu for the well-known choke, so it's known here as the twister.
- Use case: an alternative when the hammer lock isn't available or proving difficult.

### Classic entry

1. Take the hand that was wrist-deep and punch it through, transferring it behind the opponent's neck — the opponent's arm ends up trapped underneath yours.
2. Get your elbow down to the floor.
3. Go forward onto the opponent and get an arm underneath the head, around the neck.
4. From here, various locking grips can be used to draw the opponent's head in toward you.

### Bottom-arm-dominant version

- The bottom arm grips the opponent's head and draws them toward you, exerting pressure through the spine and rib cage for the submission.
- Works, but leaves the opponent's near-side shoulder free to move — the opponent can wiggle, push up, and use their free hand to start freeing the head.

### Top-arm-dominant version (preferred)

1. Come further forward onto the opponent's chest instead of staying bottom-arm dominant.
2. Control the top arm, then score with the bottom arm.
3. Lock the figure-4.
4. Get your head underneath the opponent's head.
- Result: much tighter lock, opponent's near-side shoulder is well controlled, slippage is nearly eliminated. Driving forward from here produces immense breaking pressure. Chest-to-chest contact eliminates the opponent's ability to wiggle free.

### Alternate entry into the top-arm version

1. Punch through, go up and around the opponent, and sit over their body.
2. Instead of locking the bottom arm directly, use the bottom hand to **feed** the top arm around.
3. Lock the figure-4 with the top arm; head goes underneath.
4. This pins the near-side shoulder from the start.

### Passing method detail (crowding the shoulder)

- Any failure to control the near-side shoulder lets the opponent's head move away and the arm slip — losing the position.
- Use the bottom hand to draw the opponent's head toward you and get chest-to-chest first.
- Once close enough, replace the bottom hand with the top arm, lock the figure-4, head underneath.
- A bottom-arm-only lock (even figure-4'd) leaves more space and less chest-to-chest contact — uncomfortable and will submit most people, but leaves the opponent wiggle room to lift their hands overhead and start escaping. The top-arm version removes that space almost entirely.

## Transitioning to strangles

- Cross Body Ride is also an excellent launch point into strangleholds. Tactically this transition is easy: at the moment of entry there's no strangle threat yet, so opponents often engage in defensive movements (locking their hands together) that end up making the transition to a strangle *easier*, not harder.

### When the opponent locks hands defensively

- If their hands can't be unlocked, the twister and hammer lock are both blocked. One fallback is simply returning to the legs. A better option:

1. Withdraw the hand controlling the crook of the opponent's elbow.
2. Pass that arm through elbow-deep underneath the opponent's neck.
3. Use your knees to elevate the opponent — but stay behind their elbow throughout. If the opponent's elbow gets to the floor between you, that's a problem.
4. Bring the opponent's elbow up onto your chest/sternum as you get your arm elbow-deep around the neck — with the arm elbow-deep, there's no way the opponent can bring the elbow back between you.
5. Secure a **1-on-1 grip** on the opponent's near-side upper arm/wrist — hands don't need to be locked here. This restores diagonal control: one side of the opponent's body controlled by the 1-on-1, the other by the leg hook (outside scorpion).

### Getting the angle for the strangle

1. Release the outside scorpion leg hook and replace it with a **crab hook**.
2. Lift and elevate the opponent, shifting your hips out.
3. Place a foot on the opponent's hip and bring the other leg up to catch your own foot — set up to lock the figure-4.
4. If the opponent is too big through the waist for a figure-4, instead post one foot on their hip and bring the second foot up high — this makes it very difficult for them to beat the bottom hook.
5. From here, transition into the highest-percentage of all back finishes: the rear naked strangle.

### Full alternate sequence (from a standard entry)

1. Enter Cross Body Ride normally — at this point both your arms are under the opponent's arms, so there's no strangle threat they need to worry about yet.
2. As the opponent locks their hands defensively (blocking twisters/hammer locks), control their elbow and tilt your body so the elbow comes onto your sternum.
3. Withdraw your arm and go underneath the opponent's head, bending your arm 90 degrees. With your chest in the way, the opponent can't bring the elbow between you even if you release the grip.
4. Shoot your other hand through into a 1-on-1 grip on the wrist.
5. That hand goes over the shoulder; bring a leg out and hook the opponent, shimmying your hips around the corner to bring your foot over their hip.
6. Lock the figure-4 if your leg length allows; otherwise post into rear mount and catch.
7. From here, go directly into the rear naked strangle.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The twister and the strangle transition both exploit the same idea: when the hammer lock or leg attacks are denied, the opponent's own defensive reaction opens the next attack.
- Top-arm dominance beats bottom-arm dominance on the twister — it pins the near-side shoulder, eliminates wiggle room, and produces far stronger breaking pressure through the spine and ribs.
- When the opponent locks their hands defensively, don't fight the lock directly — go elbow-deep under the neck while staying behind their elbow, establish a 1-on-1 grip, then swap the outside scorpion for a crab hook to get the angle for a strangle.
- This chain of transitions (legs → arm → torso/spine → strangle) is what makes Cross Body Ride a full-body attacking system rather than a single move — losing one attack just hands you the next.
