# Overarm Side — Side Switching Method — Quick Reference

## The point

Already fell to the overarm side with the opponent sitting on your bottom leg? You can't trap his secondary arm the normal way — use the free leg to trap the primary arm instead, then fix the two problems that creates.

## Steps

1. **Protect the bottom hook first** — post rear mount / body triangle, keep the bottom hook high or reinforced.
2. **Trap the primary arm with the free leg:**
   - Push opponent's primary hand below the chest line.
   - Unhook legs, hamstring goes OVER the elbow (not below — won't catch otherwise).
   - Heel of hand to inside of own thumb (trust proprioception).
   - Block over the elbow, free your arm, cross feet to tighten. (Bottom foot = the one controlling the arm.)
3. **Recover a control hand** (you lost it trapping the arm):
   - Cross-grip his wrist, pull, lift the elbow, slide your hand underneath.
4. **Fix the head/hand violation:**
   - This grip puts strangle hand and head on the SAME side — no strangle possible yet.
   - Head pommel: grab his head, elbow forward, pommel it to the other side. Restores opposite-side positioning.
5. **Finish:**
   - Chin down, flat fist method, walk the arm through.
   - One-handed or two-handed strangle.

## Golden rule (never break it)

Strangle arm and head must be on **opposite** sides of the opponent's head. If a technique puts them on the same side, it needs a head pommel before you can strangle.

## Don't

- Don't hamstring the elbow from below — must be above it.
- Don't forget you have no control hand after trapping the arm — recover one immediately via cross grip.
- Don't try to strangle before pommeling the head back to the correct side.
