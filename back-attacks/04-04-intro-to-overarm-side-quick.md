# Intro To Overarm Side — Quick Reference

## The point

The Straitjacket System has two halves: underarm side (done) and overarm side (starting now). Falling to the overarm side pins your own leg under the opponent's body weight — so the underarm-side leg-trapping game doesn't work here. New methods are needed.

## Key facts

1. The system is neutral — neither side is "weak" or "strong," just different.
2. Underarm-side finishes dominate statistically only because most opponents are conditioned to escape that way — not because the system favors it.
3. Falling to the overarm side puts opponent weight on that leg → that leg can't hook the opponent's secondary arm the normal way.
4. Because of that lost leg, the overarm-side approach has to be structurally different from the underarm-side game.

## What's coming next

- **Wedging method** — trap the arm *before* committing to the floor, typically off a turtle position.
- **Side switching method** — trap the arm *after* you've already fallen to the overarm side, using the free leg with modification.

## Don't

Don't assume the underarm-side arm-trapping mechanics transfer to the overarm side — the pinned leg makes that impossible; a different toolkit is required.
