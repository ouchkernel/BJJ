# Focus Points — Quick Reference

## 3 starting stances vs. turtle

1. **Knee-pinch squat** — both knees down, pinched on opponent's hips, diagonal line across his body. Best for mobility (can move if he stands).
2. **Grounded knee** — one knee down at same height as his lower back (never hamstring-across-lower-back), chest-to-back contact. Best for strong control.
3. **Knee-outside-hip** — rear knee outside his hip, other leg unweighted/up in line with his hands, usually with seat belt. Frees a leg for crucifix setups.

## 3 focus points (you can't control the whole body at once)

| Focus point | Grip | Real target | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hips | Tight waist | Far hip, via your **elbow** (not the stomach hold) | Breaks opponent down to hip + elbow; switch sides if stuck |
| Head/shoulders | Seat belt (5-finger grip to start) | Shoulder girdle or head | Hands-locked+head-back = mobility; shoulder rolled onto head = max downward pressure |
| Wrist | One-on-one behind the tricep | Knuckle line, not the wrist | Hardest grip to deny — only needs hand-width to enter, hard to grip-fight behind the arm |

## Decision steps

1. Read what kind of turtle (open/closed) he's in.
2. Default entry sequence: tight waist → he recovers → take a wrist → he clears it → come up to head/shoulders.
3. Any one of the three gets you a breakdown. Get good at all three and switch fluidly.

## Don't

- Don't focus on the stomach grip itself in a tight waist — the elbow-on-far-hip is the actual control.
- Don't grip the wrist directly in a one-on-one — grip the knuckle line so he can't extend the arm away.
