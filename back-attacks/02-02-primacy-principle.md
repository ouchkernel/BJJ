# Primacy Principle

## Overview

The second of the ten critical principles. Whenever a choice exists between finishing with a strangle or finishing with a joint lock, Danaher always prioritizes the strangle. This principle sets the default attacking priority for everything that follows in the back system.

---

## Strangles over joint locks

- From the back there's a vast menu of finishing options — rear naked strangle, arm locks, and more are all available from essentially the same position.
- Given a choice, strangleholds always take priority over joint locks, as a general rule.
- The reasoning is about finality. Strangles, when properly applied, have a certainty that joint locks don't match: you simply pass out. There is no amount of mental toughness, courage, or fortitude that gets an opponent through a correctly applied strangle.
- Joint locks don't carry that same guarantee. A strong-minded opponent — especially one with limited time left on the clock — may be willing to accept a broken arm or leg rather than tap. That acceptance is not possible against a strangle.
- Especially at world championship level, this finality gives strangles a decisive edge as the primary finishing tool.

## The two default weapons from the back

- Given the choice, Danaher's two favorite attacks from the back are:
  1. The **rear naked strangle** (mata leao) — using the arms with precision.
  2. The **rear triangle leg strangle** (ushiro sankaku) — using the power of the legs.
- Both are strangles, and both are extremely high percentage, just via different mechanisms — one through arm precision, one through leg power.
- Because of the extraordinary emphasis this system places on strangulation over joint locking, these two are the default starting points whenever the back is available — not an exhaustive commitment to only these two, but the first place to look.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Given a choice between a strangle and a joint lock from the back, take the strangle — its finality removes the opponent's ability to simply endure it.
- Joint locks can be gutted out by a tough, time-pressured opponent; well-applied strangles cannot.
- Default to the rear naked strangle (arm-based) and the rear triangle/ushiro sankaku (leg-based) as the two primary finishing tools whenever you have the back.
- This principle sets the priority order that the rest of the system's hand-fighting machinery is built to serve.
