# Double Straight Direct Grip

## Overview

This is the bottom rung of the hand-fighting hierarchy from the back. Two versions are shown: double straight grips where one grip is indirect (you're fighting from a compromised position), and double straight grips where both are direct (a genuine improvement). Both share the same underlying goal — getting your opponent's secondary-hand wrist below his chest line so your unweighted leg can trap it — and the same finishing mechanics using the knuckle line and "prior perception."

---

## The two demarcation targets

- **Primary defensive hand**: the hand that establishes inside position on your thumb along the **knuckle line**. This is the hand that stops you cold if left free — tie it up first, as early as possible, right at the knuckle line (never grip fingers — that's illegal; get underneath the knuckle line and close your thumb over the top).
- **Secondary defensive hand**: your minimum requirement is to get this hand's wrist below the line of your opponent's pectorals (the **chest line** / **critical demarcation line**).
  - If the wrist stays above the chest line, it takes extraordinary flexibility to hook your leg over it.
  - Once it's below the chest line, "even a 51-year-old man with a hip replacement" can easily foot-lock it and control it.

## Double straight, indirect grip (bottom of the hierarchy)

- Setup: you have top-hand dominance on your opponent's primary hand, but he has top-hand dominance on your strangle hand — two straight grips, but yours on the strangle side is **indirect**.
- Sequence:
  1. Try to strangle from the indirect grip and feel him stop you.
  2. Lift the control hand; use the indirect grip to drive his wrist below the pectoral line.
  3. The instant it goes below the line, use **prior perception** — the innate ability of one body part to find another (your foot will always find your own hand, even in total darkness) — to slap your foot to your own hand.
  4. Thread the foot through so your shoelaces go behind his back and touch his spine, closing a circle around the secondary arm.
- Result: primary hand controlled by your top-hand-dominant grip, secondary hand controlled by your leg — an **uncontested strangle hand**, with only his chin left to defend.
- Head-position caveat: if his head starts to beat yours, stop and use a wraparound grip on his head to correct it and pull it back into a winning position (a strangle grip is not needed for this — it's purely a head-position correction).

## Double straight, direct grip (upgrade)

- Key principle: **never fight into the strength of a grip — fight out of it.** A grip strong enough to stop your hand going over one shoulder is often shockingly weak at stopping it going over the other shoulder. If he holds with his right hand, the weakness is over his right shoulder.
- Sequence:
  1. When he grips you, go toward the weak direction of his grip instead of fighting the strong direction.
  2. Take a straight grip along the knuckle line, top-hand dominant — now you have **double straight, both direct**.
  3. This is superior to the indirect version because now you decide where his hand goes, not him — if he let go and re-gripped mid-fight you could no longer manipulate him into an arm trap, but as long as you hold him, you control the placement.
  4. Lift the control hand, push the secondary hand below the pectoral line, use prior perception to slap foot to hand, thread shoelaces behind his spine.
- Result: both of his arms are pinned. When he tries to release either one it's very difficult. Same head-wrap correction applies if his head starts to rise.

## Practical notes

- Double straight (either version) is good on its own — you can finish strangles from it directly.
- The main weakness, addressed in later chapters: at higher levels, straight grips leave the elbows unsupported, letting a strong opponent eventually yank free.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Priority order when hand-fighting from the back: control the primary hand at the knuckle line first, then drive the secondary wrist below the pectoral/chest line.
- The chest line is the make-or-break demarcation — above it, you need extraordinary flexibility to hook the leg over; below it, it's trivial.
- Never fight into the strength of a grip — go over the shoulder on the side he's weak, which is the opposite shoulder from where he's gripping.
- Prior perception (foot finds hand) is the mechanism used throughout: slap foot to hand, then thread shoelaces behind the back to the spine.
- Double straight direct is a genuine upgrade over indirect because you now dictate where his hand goes, not him.
- If his head starts to climb above yours, stop and correct with a head wrap before continuing.
