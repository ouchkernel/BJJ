# Volume 7 — Entering the Legs: Standing, Top, Bottom & Seated

> The entries volume. Everything so far assumed you were already attached to a leg; this volume shows how to *get there* from the four scenarios you actually face — **standing, on top, on bottom, and double-seated** — and argues that **bottom** is the best place to hunt legs. The connecting thread: adapt skills you already have (single legs, double legs, front headlocks, body locks, half guard) into leg-lock entries.

---

## Overview

Four scenarios, one philosophy: most leg-lock entries should come from **bottom position**, and the rest are modifications of standard wrestling/guard skills. Two ideas recur in every entry: **head outside the opponent's leg**, and after firing into the ashi garami, **track the opponent's head** to choose the subsystem (**head moves away → straight ashi garami; head comes toward you → cross ashi garami**).

Corrected terminology: **ashi garami**, **kuzushi**, **ashi sit** (sitting to ashi garami), **shin-to-shin guard**, **X-guard / reverse X-guard**.

---

## Why Bottom Position Is the Best Place to Hunt Legs

Counterintuitive but central. With you seated/supine and the opponent on knees or feet:
- **Four limbs vs. two.** You can manipulate with **two legs + two arms**; the opponent's legs are **weight-bearing**, so they pass mostly with **two arms**. It's effectively **4-on-2**.
- **Leg strength > arm strength.** Your legs are far stronger than their arms.
- **Inherent stability.** You're stable on your **buttocks/back**; they're only as stable as their **base** (two knees or two feet), so you can play a **kuzushi** game on them — and they have **no** kuzushi game on you. An inherent asymmetry in your favor.

---

## Entering from Bottom — The Elevation Game (the most important entry)

**The problem:** the opponent **shortens his legs** by putting knees on the mat; with knees down you can't get into most forms of Arumi ashi garami. **You must elevate him so the knees come off the floor.**

### What makes elevation possible (and what blocks it)
- **Head position is everything.** If **your** head is **below your own front deltoid/shoulders**, you cannot elevate — even with good grips. (This is exactly how an experienced opponent defends: knee in the center + **his head at your front delt** → he kills your elevation and drives your shoulders down.)
- **Maintain inside position with both feet — "cocked feet."** Don't leave "dead" feet inside the knees; **actively hook** the shoelaces inside the opponent's knees so your legs **follow** his pommeling. If you go passive, he steps a knee inside and **steals** inside position.
- **Thumb posts inside the biceps** so you can keep your head **below his** without being snapped down to a front headlock; bring your **head to his clavicles** so he can't get under your head/shoulders.

### The enabling skill: move forward without hands
You must be able to **pull your butt to your heels from seated, using only your feet** — not by posting hands (then you have nothing to grip with). Use the **blade of the foot** (toes pointed out), **not the sole**; both feet pull in unison with the head forward. Low-strength, high-importance.

### The cross-buttock elevator (finish)
With cocked feet, thumb posts, and head position set, pommel the hands through, then **elevate**, take the **angle**, thread the second foot **around**, bring his **hips/toes over your head**, and **drop** him down — into straight or cross ashi garami per his head.

> **The three keys to elevation** (repeated across entries): (1) get the opponent's **hips directly over your head**, (2) get an **angle**, (3) get your **knee inside the pocket of his hip/head**.

## Entering Against a Standing Opponent (you seated)

When the opponent stands over you, the battle is for **inside position** and the **space between your knees**.

- **Protect the inside space first.** As he comes forward, put a **foot across his lead foot** to claim inside position, then use the **blades of both feet** to **pull your body forward** to him.
- **Grip wrist-deep, palm down, around the lead leg** — not finger-deep with the palm facing the wall (insufficient). Wrist-deep + palm down lets you off-balance and **block the lead leg**.
- **Manipulate his step.** With the grip and a hand on the floor, **pull your left hand toward your right** to make him **step at will** — and **catch the foot mid-step** into an entry.
- **Near-leg vs. far-leg attack:**
  - **Near leg:** make him step so weight loads that leg, **chop the inside** of it, calibrate the second leg, fire into ashi garami.
  - **Far leg:** take an **outside grip**, put your **foot behind his far foot**, and **shoot beyond the far leg** (not through your own centerline) to land in a both-legs-controlled ashi garami he can't sweep out of.
- Often transitions through **shin-to-shin guard → X-guard**.

## Entering from a Stalled Takedown (the "ashi sit")

When a takedown stalls (partial sprawl/whizzer) and finishing it is more dangerous than it's worth, **sit back into ashi garami** instead.

- **Failed double-leg → ashi sit:** double-tricep grips, level change, feet together, drive into the legs; on a partial sprawl, **put the *inside* leg up** (not the outside leg as for a takedown), hand elbow-deep around the knee, slide your **head from hip to knee** (head **outside**), and slide into ashi garami.
- **Single-leg + sprawl:** as the leg goes back, **post your hand on the mat** (without it, a full sprawl flattens you), put your **foot inside the shoelaces**, then **knee-slide toward the opposite leg** so your **head comes out**, and draw him into ashi garami.
- **Knee-pull single:** control from the inside, **pull the knee** to draw him forward, bring your own knee around the corner into the legs.

## Entering from Standing Clinch Positions

- **Front headlock entry:** from an underhook, get your head dominant inside, bring his head down into a **front headlock**. As he comes up on his hands, **knee down, head behind**, reach across to **pick the far ankle**, second knee down, transfer the hand to his **knee**, foot up → into the legs. (Front headlock is among the most versatile attacking positions in the sport, and a clean door to the legs.)
- **Standing rear body lock (entering from a "bad" spot):** never stay **bolt upright and square** — **control his hands**, **turn your hip out** (go side-on, not back-exposed), **lower your level**, and **step a foot to inside position**. Then, like a standing switch, bring the free hand through to catch the **knee**, **post your hand**, put your **head outside his leg**, and **pull him around the corner** into ashi garami.

## Entering from Half Guard and Deep Half Guard

The "use skills you already have" theme at its strongest.

- **Half guard:** opposite of normal half guard — **scoot your hips *in*** under the opponent so your **center of gravity goes under his** (a back-heel on his calf + a butterfly hook stops his kick-out). When ready, **plant the foot**, **scoop your center of gravity under his**, **double-thumb to the armpit** to clear the head, knee to inside, **scoot grip** on the off-the-floor knee, transition to **reverse X-guard**, lift and drop.
- **Deep half guard (Bernardo Faria's specialty):** its great virtue is it **prevents chest-to-chest contact** (he can't pin you) **and takes his knee off the mat** (easier entry). From an arm around the waist (underneath his arm) + scoop grip, go **elbow-deep inside the leg**, **swing 180° around the corner** so you face the **same direction** he does, pommel a foot under his calf, **lift and feed your leg** through to a near-X-guard, add a **cross-arm frame**, switch to **reverse X-guard**, bring his **hips over your head** (angle + **knee inside the hip pocket**), lock the triangle, and **push across the line of the hips** to put him down.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating bottom position as **inferior** (it's a 4-on-2 advantage with a one-way kuzushi game).
- Letting **your own head drop below your front deltoid** when trying to elevate (elevation becomes impossible).
- Leaving **passive/dead feet** inside the knees instead of **cocked, actively hooking** feet (you lose inside position).
- Trying to **move forward by posting hands** instead of pulling with the **blades of the feet** (then you have nothing to grip).
- Gripping the standing leg **finger-deep, palm out** instead of **wrist-deep, palm down**.
- Failing to **protect the inside space** when a standing opponent drives in.
- On the far-leg attack, shooting **through your own centerline** instead of **beyond the far leg**.
- On a stalled takedown, putting the **outside** leg up (takedown habit) instead of the **inside** leg for the ashi sit.
- On any entry, ending with your **head inside** the leg / falling **between** the legs.

## Decision Points

- **Opponent kneeling, knees down** → **elevation game** (cocked feet, thumb posts, head to clavicles, blade-of-foot drive) to lift the knees off the mat.
- **Opponent standing over you** → protect the **inside space**, grip **wrist-deep palm-down**, manipulate his **step**; **near leg** (load + chop) or **far leg** (foot behind far foot, shoot beyond it).
- **Takedown stalls (sprawl/whizzer)** → don't force it; **ashi sit** with the **inside** leg up.
- **Front headlock / rear body lock** → convert to a leg entry (pick the far ankle / turn the hip out and pull around the corner).
- **In half guard** → scoot hips **in**, reverse X-guard. **In deep half** → swing 180°, reverse X-guard.
- **After any entry** → read the **head**: away → **straight ashi garami**; toward you → **cross ashi garami**.

## Terminology Introduced

- **Elevation game** — lifting the kneeling opponent so his knees leave the mat (the core bottom entry).
- **Cocked feet** — actively hooking the feet inside the opponent's knees to retain inside position.
- **Blade-of-foot drive** — pulling your hips forward from seated using the foot blades, no hands.
- **Ashi sit** — sitting back from a stalled takedown directly into ashi garami.
- **Shin-to-shin guard** — a transitional guard into X-guard entries.
- **Near-leg / far-leg attack** — the two options against a standing opponent.
- **Three keys to elevation** — hips over head, angle, knee inside the hip pocket.
