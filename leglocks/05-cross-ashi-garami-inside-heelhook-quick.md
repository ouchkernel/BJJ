# Volume 5 — Cross Ashi Garami & The Inside Heel Hook — Quick Reference

## Why this position is the apex

- Cross ashi garami, **feet inside** = minimal exposure of your own feet + maximal breaking power. Best position in the system.
- Target = **MCL** (inside knee). Worse than LCL — opponents can't fight through it like they can an outside heel hook.
- Both opponent's legs are in front of you (unlike straight ashi garami).

## Primary vs secondary leg

- **Primary** = the leg you're attacking.
- **Secondary** = the other leg — **~90% of their escape potential** lives here.
- Rule: submissions come from primary, escapes come from secondary. Control secondary while you break primary.

## The turnout problem (the central fight)

Opponent turns away → your head ends up **outside** their legs → you can't reach the heel. You need your head **inside**.

Two solution families:

### A. Double-leg (control both legs — preferred)
1. Keep opponent's knee inside your **knee line**. Distance opening? Go elbow-deep around the primary knee to re-anchor.
2. **Ankle-lace ashi garami**: Achilles-lock the secondary leg — manacles the ankles together.
3. Build a **five-point wedge**: 2 knees (outside legs) + hips (up from under) + hand (pulling down on legs) + elbow (over both ankles).
4. Upgrade to **six-point wedge**: knife-hand through the ankles, shoelaces to your neck. Then tilt, lock inside sankaku, reach back for the heel.
5. Can't reach the heel? You're leaning too far back — **sit up and forward**, match head heights, reach back.
6. Finish on secondary leg (lace-leg break): hands near the toes (not down the shin), cross wrists, hands to your centerline, pitch to your side, push both feet + pull one leg across the other.

### B. Single-leg (can't get the secondary leg)
You can't stop the turnout — so **slow it down** instead. Race for head-inside position. Apply all 4 brakes together:
1. Handbrake — hand around the knee, fingers through, wrist bent 90°, elbow closed.
2. Elbow break — elbow into inside position.
3. Head post — head on the floor, blocks the roll. (Most important of the four.)
4. Interfering foot — hook inside opponent's knee.

## The release problem

To finish the inside heel hook you usually have to let go of the secondary leg — and they turn out again the instant you do.

- **Strategy 1 — don't release.** Attack the secondary ankle instead (Achilles lock / reverse figure-four):
  - Feed the free hand inside and underneath, emerging below the ankle. Thumb near ankle, never down the shin.
  - Target the anterior tibialis tendon.
  - Elbow points toward opponent's hips.
  - Put the sole of their heel on your floating rib — beats "putting the boot on," lets you tighten the figure-four.
  - Creates a dilemma: secondary ankle vs. primary knee. Either way you win.
- **Strategy 2 — release on your terms.** Only let go once you already have a wrist around the heel. Insert a splitter hand (forearm between the legs) first, then release + finish.

## The interference (two-foot) problem

Two feet jammed in your armpit = pain only, no break. Fix:
1. Pitch to your side.
2. Reposition your knee from over the hip to inside their knee.
3. Push knee-to-knee — secondary leg pops out.
4. Fall back into the break.

Don't: try to move their leg with your arm. Don't push too hard (both feet pop = lose control).

## Common Mistakes checklist

- [ ] Feet outside instead of inside (more exposure, less control)
- [ ] Forgot the knee line still applies here
- [ ] Tried to move the secondary leg with your arm, not your legs
- [ ] Pushed too hard popping the secondary leg (both feet popped)
- [ ] Released the secondary leg before securing the heel wrist (Strategy 2)
- [ ] Achilles-lock thumb down the shin instead of below the ankle
- [ ] Leaning too far back, can't reach the heel
- [ ] In single-leg race, relying on your own speed instead of slowing them
- [ ] Tolerating two feet in the armpit instead of resolving interference first

## Decision Points

- Max break available → cross ashi garami, feet inside → inside heel hook (MCL).
- Opponent turning out, you have the secondary leg → double-leg (ankle lace / 5-6 point wedge).
- Opponent turning out, you don't have the secondary leg → single-leg (4 brakes).
- Need to finish but releasing frees the turnout → don't release (attack secondary ankle) OR release on your terms (splitter hand + heel secured first).
- Two feet jammed in your armpit → pitch to your side, knee-to-knee push.
- Can't reach the heel → sit up and forward, match head heights.

## Terminology

- **Cross ashi garami** — opponent's leg crosses your centerline; home of the inside heel hook.
- **Inside heel hook** — MCL-targeting finish; strongest leg break.
- **Primary / secondary leg** — attacked leg vs. escape-engine leg.
- **Turnout problem** — opponent turns away to hide the heel, puts your head outside.
- **Ankle-lace ashi garami** — Achilles-locking the secondary leg; a pin without weight.
- **Five-point / six-point wedge** — maximal double-leg control structures.
- **Double trouble** — controlling both legs to pin the opponent.
- **Splitter hand** — forearm inserted to split the legs before finishing.
- **Interference (two-foot) problem** — two feet in the armpit block a clean break.
