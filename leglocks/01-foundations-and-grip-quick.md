# Volume 1 — Foundations & Grip — Quick Reference

## The 4 mechanical principles

1. **Lever & fulcrum** — judge the fulcrum on 3 things: hardness (hard bone, not soft muscle), height (higher = stronger break, shorter distance), placement (efficient spot).
2. **Directionality of force** — same effort, aimed right, does far more. Redirect, don't muscle.
3. **Kuzushi** — get their center of gravity outside their base. Goal: extension + movement. "Give me a moving, extended opponent and I can submit them."
4. **Wedges** — the real tool of immobilization, not body weight. Must be on **both sides**, and **reinforced** (weight if pinning, locked legs if in ashi garami).

## System structure

- Leg locking (generic) → **ashi garami system** (specific, built around heel hooks).
- **6 essential skills** (fixed-ish sequence): grip & inside position → distance & connection → break to a hip → heel exposure → negation → break.
- **3 subsystems** (families of ashi garami): straight, cross, reverse.

| Family | Defining feature |
|---|---|
| Straight | Opponent's leg stays one side of your centerline |
| Cross | Opponent's leg crosses your centerline |
| Reverse | You face the **same** direction as opponent |

## Skill 1 — Grip & inside position: do this

1. **Two-on-one wrist grip.** Cover the first hand's thumb-direction weakness with the second hand, thumbs opposite. Up-grips (palms up) draw opponent forward, pair with butterfly hooks. Down-grips (palms down) for a standing opponent.
2. **Immovable elbow.** After gripping, pin elbow to your own hip — recruits lats/back, not arm strength.
3. **Centerline control.** Pull their gripped arm across your centerline (their right hand to your right hip).
4. **Move with the pull.** Never tug-of-war. Walk with their pull, dissipate it, then off-balance.
5. **If they grip you first:** switch to tricep grips (higher than elbow), draw both arms past your shoulders, elbows to hips.
6. **To get under for leg entry:** fight for underhooks (not overhooks). No body-locking — elbows in, hand behind shoulder, false grip wrist bent ~90°, elbow dominating theirs, both sides. Entry: pull head, shoot first hand through pinky-up, then second hand.
7. **End state:** hips under, elevate, get their hands to the floor. That's the gateway to the legs.

## Common mistakes

- [ ] Breaking over soft inner thigh instead of hard hip bone
- [ ] Working the short end of the lever
- [ ] Forcing direction instead of redirecting it
- [ ] Relying on body weight instead of wedges
- [ ] Wedge on only one side
- [ ] Gripping with no purpose (no plan to negate or get hands down)
- [ ] Leaving a grip's thumb-direction weakness uncovered
- [ ] Elbow drifting off your hip
- [ ] Staying stationary against a pulling opponent
- [ ] Body-locking the underhook (invites forearm-frame break)
- [ ] Entering legs from overhooks instead of underhooks

## Decision points

- Opponent's hands in front → two-on-one wrist grip.
- Opponent grips you first (collar/arms) → tricep grips, dominate from underneath.
- Need to access the legs → fight for underhooks, not overhooks.
- Opponent yanks to break your grip → move with the pull.
- Grip secured → free feet off-balance, drive hands to the mat.

## Terms

- **Ashi garami** — entangled legs, the whole system's positional basis.
- **Kuzushi** — off-balancing to create extension + movement.
- **Sankaku** — triangle (leg lock reinforcing a wedge).
- **Juji gatame** — straight armbar (illustrates lever/fulcrum).
- **The three families** — straight, cross, reverse ashi garami.
