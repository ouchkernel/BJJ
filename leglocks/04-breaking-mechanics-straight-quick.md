# Volume 4 — Breaking Mechanics: Finishing the Straight Ashi Garami Heel Hook — Quick Reference

## General theory of breaking (any joint)

1. **Opposing forces** — break force must run opposite your ashi garami force ("tear paper" — hands move opposite ways). Ashi garami one way, heel hook the other.
2. **Follow-through** — never be at full extension at the tap point. Keep range to drive through. Train with the one-handed drill.
3. **Tension / slack removal** — build tension before breaking (tight rope cuts easy, slack rope doesn't): back-heel the legs → align your head so your body is a straight line → both elbows to your ribs. Never attack a relaxed joint with a speed-jump.

## Set the lever, every time

- [ ] Toe line (big-toe to little-toe knuckle) into your bicep/armpit
- [ ] Forearm lined up with the **foot**, never the shin
- [ ] Wrist **below** the ankle, never above
- [ ] Finish on your **elbow**, not your shoulder — elbow to ribs (toe-push), then lift the heel

**Rule of head and hands:** head and hands move together, distance between them never changes. Head drifts from hands → power bleeds out.

## Grip progression — tighten as they resist

| Grip | When |
|---|---|
| Ten-finger | Loosest, starting point |
| Palm-to-palm | Most versatile — top elbow wedges on the shin, if you can get it there |
| Wrist-to-wrist | Use when you can't get the elbow on the shin (hand posted in your tricep) |
| Figure-four | Opponent hand-fighting hard — chest wedges the shoelaces, nothing to grab |
| Reverse figure-four | Strongest — "wrong" wrist under the heel, elbow points at their buttocks; works even from a weak ashi garami |

Start palm-to-palm, read resistance, tighten down the list as needed.

## Leg dominance — cycle by phase

1. Arumi ashi garami → neutral.
2. Heel exposure → **outside-leg dominant**.
3. **Breaking → inside-leg dominant.** Inside leg pushes down on their knee (rotates the upper leg) while your arms twist the ankle the other way.

**Cardinal error:** staying outside-leg dominant during the break — the outside leg pushes the opponent the *same* direction as the heel hook, assisting their escape. Inside-leg dominance lets you finish even one-handed.

## Hip weight

- [ ] Lift your hips off the floor at the break — hips down = pressure caps at the floor.
- Hard hip-bone fulcrum drives into the inside of their knee once hips are up.
- Finish with only feet and elbow on the mat.

## The shared-spiral problem

Heel hook = twisting/spiraling lock. If you keep the **same** ashi garami as the opponent's escape spiral, you both just roll forever with no result.

**Fix:** roll into **outside ashi garami** so your knees point across/down — opposing their spiral. Now if they keep rolling, they break their own leg on your ashi garami.

**Don't roll over the elbow** — it slows your rotation, they out-rotate you, they escape. Lock hands to ribs, roll over the **shoulder** instead — matches their rotation speed.

## Full cycle (straight ashi garami)

Butterfly guard → lift/elevate/hop into Arumi ashi garami (knee inside knee line) → inside ashi garami (outside-leg dominant, foot across centerline, false grip the heel) → far-hip ashi garami/sankaku (negate: can't separate legs / can't out-rotate / can't create distance) → roll over the **shoulder** into outside ashi garami (switch to inside-leg dominance) → build tension (back-heel → align head → elbows to ribs) → hips off the floor → push toes / pull heel → follow through.

## Don't

- [ ] Break ashi garami and heel hook in the same direction
- [ ] Reach the tap point at full extension
- [ ] Attack a relaxed joint with a speed-jump
- [ ] Line the forearm with the shin instead of the foot
- [ ] Let the wrist ride above the ankle
- [ ] Finish on the shoulder instead of the elbow
- [ ] Let your head drift from your hands
- [ ] Stay outside-leg dominant during the break
- [ ] Leave your hips on the mat during the break
- [ ] Roll over the elbow instead of the shoulder
- [ ] Keep the same ashi garami through the roll (shared spiral)

## Decision Points

- Can get your top elbow on the shin → palm-to-palm. Can't → wrist-to-wrist. Hard hand-fighting → figure-four. Opponent turning/twisting out → reverse figure-four.
- Exposing the heel → outside-leg dominant. Breaking → inside-leg dominant.
- Opponent keeps rolling (shared spiral) → get to outside ashi garami, roll over the shoulder, let them break themselves.
- Not enough power → check the order: back-heel → align head → elbows to ribs → hips off the floor → follow through.
- Less-skilled opponent → can finish even from Arumi ashi garami. Elite opponent → cycle all the way to outside ashi garami first.

## Terminology

- **Follow-through / one-handed drill** — finishing past the break point; the drill that trains it.
- **Tension / slack removal** — pre-loading the ligaments before breaking.
- **Toe push / heel pull** — the foot as a two-ended lever.
- **Rule of head and hands** — head and hands move together or power bleeds out.
- **Grip progression** — ten-finger → palm-to-palm → wrist-to-wrist → figure-four → reverse figure-four.
- **Inside-leg dominance** — the inside leg drives the break; cycle dominance by phase.
- **Shared spiral** — escape follows the same spiral as the lock; defeated by opposing spirals in outside ashi garami.
