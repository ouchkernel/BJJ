# Glossary

Consolidated terminology for the *Leglocks: Enter The System* notes — the Japanese terms, the positions, the recurring concepts, and the named problems. (The auto-transcript mangles most Japanese; the corrected spellings are used throughout the notes.)

---

## Japanese terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ashi garami** (足緘) | "Entangled legs." The positional basis of the entire system — typically **two of your legs controlling one of the opponent's**. The system is really an *ashi garami system*, not a generic leg-lock system. |
| **Arumi ashi garami** | The "standard" entering ashi garami (*arumi* ≈ "to enter"). The primary entry/connection position; **Step 1** of the four-step sequence. |
| **Kuzushi** (崩し) | Off-balancing. Taking the opponent's center of gravity outside their base of support to create **extension + movement** — the prerequisites for a submission game. |
| **Sankaku** (三角) | "Triangle." A triangle lock of the **legs**, used to reinforce a wedge (e.g., the far-hip ashi garami). *(The transcript repeatedly mis-hears this as "senkaku.")* |
| **Juji gatame** (十字固め) | The straight armbar. Used in Vol 1 to illustrate lever/fulcrum; it's a **linear** lock (contrast the **twisting** heel hook). |
| **Jigotai** (自護体) | A low, defensive stance with your head matched to the opponent's head level — used when approaching a seated opponent on top. |
| **Kani basami** (蟹挟) | A scissor-takedown action; referenced as a single-inside entry and as the level-change/hip-heist entry in the double-seated position. |
| **Imanari roll** | A rolling entry into ashi garami; **Gravity Drill 3** is the floor half of it. |

---

## The three families (subsystems)

| Position | Defining feature |
|---|---|
| **Straight ashi garami** | The opponent's leg goes over your hip and **stays one side of your centerline** (does not cross it). One leg in front of you, one behind. Finishes with the **outside heel hook**. |
| **Cross ashi garami** | The opponent's leg **crosses your centerline**. Both their legs are in front of you. The apex position (feet inside) for the **inside heel hook**. |
| **Reverse ashi garami** | The **only** family where you face the **same direction** as the opponent. Easy double-leg access, but the opponent can lock his legs defensively. A **gateway** into the other two families. |

## Specific ashi garami positions (by role in the sequence)

| Position | Role |
|---|---|
| **Arumi ashi garami** | **Entry & connection** (Step 1). Inside leg + outside leg; outside foot governs distance and back-heels; inside foot's shoelaces to the buttock. |
| **Inside ashi garami** | **Heel exposure** (Step 2). The inside foot crosses the opponent's centerline to expose the heel. |
| **Far-hip ashi garami** | **Negation** (Step 3). A **sankaku** with your feet over the opponent's *far* hip; closes all three escapes. |
| **Outside ashi garami** | **Breaking** (Step 4). Both feet **outside** the opponent's leg, locked (reinforced wedge), knees pointing **out** — opposite the heel-hook direction. |
| **Single-leg X / X-guard / reverse X-guard** | Breakdown positions used to put a standing opponent down to a hip. |
| **Double-seated position** | Both players seated with hands on each other's ankles; a 50-50-adjacent battle for inside position + distance. |

## Foot & grip placements

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Straight buttock** | Your foot's shoelaces placed to the opponent's *opposite* buttock, letting you **close knee over ankle** into a **closed (reinforced) wedge**. Preferred for a solid hold. |
| **Cross buttock** | Shoelaces to the *same-side* buttock (across the centerline). Leaves an **open** wedge, but needed for breakdowns and reverse-X entries (and to close the knees in the double-seated position). |
| **Back heel** | Drawing your heel toward your own buttock/hamstring so your foot **pulls** the opponent's quad in (rather than pushing into the ribcage, which pushes them *out*). |
| **Cocked feet** | Feet actively hooking inside the opponent's knees (not passive) so your legs follow his pommeling and keep inside position. |
| **Grip progression** | Increasingly tight hand locks: **ten-finger → palm-to-palm → wrist-to-wrist → figure-four → reverse figure-four** (circumference shrinks → tighter lock). |
| **Splitter hand** | A forearm inserted between the opponent's legs to split them apart before finishing. |

## Core concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **The four mechanical principles** | **Lever & fulcrum** (hardness · height · placement), **directionality of force**, **kuzushi**, and **wedges**. Everything in the system is an application of these. |
| **Wedge** | An inclined plane used to immobilize. The **primary** tool of immobilization (more than body weight). Best used on **both** sides of the body and **reinforced** (by weight in a pin, or by locking the legs in ashi garami). |
| **The six essential skills** | Grip & inside position · distance & connection · breaking to a hip (contingent) · heel exposure · negation · breaking. A largely fixed sequence; each step builds on the last. |
| **Inside position** | Your feet/knees inside the opponent's — the *prerequisite*. |
| **Inside control** | Actually governing the **space between the opponent's knees** (the *goal*). Read it by the **kneecap tell**: knees pointing apart = the space is controlled. |
| **Knee line** | The imaginary line joining *your* two knees. **You want the opponent's knee inside your knee line** — ~90% of failed leg locks trace to losing this. |
| **Ankle line / toe line / centerline** | Body demarcation lines used to manage distance and inside position. |
| **Push leg / pull leg** | The **scissoring** action of ashi garami: a **push leg** (wedge in front of the hip) and a **pull leg** (wedge behind the knee). Ashi garami is a *scissor*, not a *squeeze*. |
| **Rounded spine** | The "bottom of a rocking chair" spinal shape that lets you elevate a heavy opponent from bottom with minimal effort. A flat spine = a feeble ashi garami. |
| **Inside-leg dominance** | At the **break**, the **inside leg** drives the rotation (pushing the knee one way while the arms twist the ankle the other). Cycle dominance by phase: outside-leg dominant to *expose*, inside-leg dominant to *break*. |
| **Opposing forces** | (1) The **ashi garami force must oppose the break force** ("tear paper"), and (2) on the foot, **push the toes + pull the heel** simultaneously. |
| **Follow-through** | Reach the breaking point **and keep going through it** — never be at full extension at the tapping point. Trained with the **one-handed drill**. |
| **Tension / slack removal** | Pre-load the ligaments before breaking (the "tight rope cuts easily" analogy): back-heel → align the head → elbows to ribs → break in minimal distance. |
| **Rule of head and hands** | Your head and hands move **together** — the distance between them never changes. If the head drifts from the hands, power bleeds out. |
| **The three keys to elevation** | (1) opponent's hips over your head, (2) an angle, (3) your knee inside the pocket of his hip/head. |
| **Power curve** | Lifting **out and around** (not straight) from reverse X-guard to bring the hips over your head. |
| **Double trouble** | Controlling **both** of the opponent's legs to turn ashi garami into a pin without body weight. |
| **Dilemma** | Forcing the opponent into a position where **every** option is bad (e.g., back-or-leg, secondary-ankle-or-primary-knee). *"Good players create problems; great players create dilemmas."* |
| **Head position (primacy)** | In any heel-hook chase, the **single most important** finishing detail: get your **head inside the opponent's knee**, then use it as a **floor post** to stop his roll, then take the heel. Ranks **above** secondary-leg control. |

## The named problems

| Problem | Where | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Shared spiral** | Straight ashi garami | The opponent's escape follows the **same** spiral as your (twisting) heel hook → you both roll with no result. Beaten by finishing in **outside ashi garami** so the spirals oppose; roll over the **shoulder**, never the elbow. |
| **Turnout problem** | Cross ashi garami | The opponent turns **away** to hide the heel and put your head **outside** his legs (you need your head **inside** to attack the heel). |
| **Release problem** | Cross ashi garami | To finish the inside heel hook you must **release** the secondary leg — and then the opponent turns out again. Solved by **not releasing** (attack the secondary ankle) or **releasing under your conditions** (heel already secured). |
| **Interference (two-foot) problem** | Cross ashi garami | Two feet in your armpit interfere; a clean break needs **one**. Pop the secondary leg out with a **knee-to-knee** push (your legs, not your arm). |
| **Crossed-legs problem** | Reverse ashi garami | The opponent locks his legs so the **secondary leg defends the primary**. Solved by **untying the knot** or a **toe-hold/knee-bar dilemma**. |

## Finishes & anatomy

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Inside heel hook** | The strongest leg break; targets the **MCL** (medial/inside of the knee). From cross ashi garami. |
| **Outside heel hook** | Targets the **LCL** (lateral/outside of the knee) + ankle. From straight/outside ashi garami. Opponents can sometimes keep fighting through an LCL tear. |
| **Figure-four toe hold** | "A heel hook applied with the hands/arms" — same push-toes/pull-heel mechanics. One of the few locks usable **without** an ashi garami. |
| **Knee bar** | A **linear** supplementary finish (used in the reverse-ashi-garami dilemma and on its own). |
| **MCL / LCL** | Medial / lateral collateral ligaments of the knee — the heel-hook targets. |
| **Anterior tibialis** | Tendon on the front of the ankle; the target for the Achilles-lock variations on the secondary leg. |
| **"Putting the boot on"** | The standard Achilles-lock counter (pushing the foot through); defeated by placing the opponent's **heel sole on your floating rib**. |

## Drills

| Drill | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Gravity drills (×4)** | No-hands drills that use gravity as a pass/fail test of distance + connection: (1) facing, (2) facing away, (3) rolling inversion / half-Imanari, (4) ankle-line split. |
| **One-handed drill** | Finishing the heel hook with a **single hand** to develop **follow-through** (the legs become the engine). |
| **Pommeling / inside-position drill** | Cycling shut-out → single-inside → double-inside to build inside-position fluency. |
