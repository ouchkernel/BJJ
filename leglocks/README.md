# Danaher Leglocks: *Enter The System* — Study Notes

Structured, drillable study notes for John Danaher's 8-volume instructional ***Leglocks: Enter The System*** (the original "Enter The System" leg-lock series). Each volume has its own notes file, and each position links out to reputable pages with real photos/video (see [Position reference links](#position-reference-links)).

These notes are written to be **faithful to the source** and **usable at the mat**: principles are separated from techniques, mechanical details are preserved verbatim where they matter, and the Japanese terminology is corrected from the auto-transcript (e.g., *ashi garami*, *kuzushi*, *sankaku*, *juji gatame*).

> **Not affiliated with or endorsed by John Danaher or BJJ Fanatics.** These are personal study notes summarizing concepts for learning purposes. If you want the actual instruction — the live demonstrations, reps, and detail — buy the instructional.

## The big picture

Danaher is explicit that this is **not** a grab-bag of leg locks — it's a single **ashi garami system** built around the **heel hook**, the highest-percentage leg attack against a skilled, resisting opponent. ~95% of finishes in the system come from just **two** moves (the **inside** and **outside heel hook**), supplemented by the **knee bar** and **figure-four toe hold**.

The system has a definite structure:

### The six essential skills of heel hooking
A largely fixed sequence — each step builds on the previous one (*"a chain is only as strong as its weakest link"*):

1. **Grip and inside position** — get a working grip + your feet/knees inside ([Vol 1](01-foundations-and-grip.md), [Vol 2](02-inside-control-and-distance.md))
2. **Distance and connection** — the **knee line**; keep the opponent's knee inside yours ([Vol 2](02-inside-control-and-distance.md))
3. **Breaking down to a hip** — *contingent* (bottom position only) ([Vol 3](03-arumi-breaking-four-step-heelhook.md))
4. **Heel exposure** — get the heel out where you can attack it
5. **Negation** — shut down the opponent's escapes
6. **Breaking** — apply the finish

Steps 1–3 are taught as a **general system**; steps 4–6 are taught inside the **three subsystems**, because the exposure/negation/break details differ by family.

### The three subsystems (families of ashi garami)
📷 **See the positions:** [Basic ashi garami positions — Evolve MMA](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/basic-ashi-garami-positions-explained/) · [Leg-lock positions explained — Open Note Grappling](https://www.opennotegrappling.com/p/foundational-guide-to-leg-locks)

| Subsystem | Defining feature | Primary finish | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Straight** | Opponent's leg stays one side of your centerline | Outside heel hook (LCL + ankle) | [Vol 3](03-arumi-breaking-four-step-heelhook.md), [Vol 4](04-breaking-mechanics-straight.md) |
| **Cross** | Opponent's leg crosses your centerline | **Inside heel hook (MCL)** — the strongest break | [Vol 5](05-cross-ashi-garami-inside-heelhook.md) |
| **Reverse** | You face the **same direction** as the opponent | Gateway to the other two; toe hold / knee bar | [Vol 6](06-reverse-ashi-garami-toehold-entries.md) |

### Four mechanical principles (underpin everything)
**Lever & fulcrum** (hardness · height · placement) · **Directionality of force** · **Kuzushi** (off-balancing) · **Wedges** (the primary tool of immobilization; reinforced by locking the legs). Detailed in [Vol 1](01-foundations-and-grip.md).
