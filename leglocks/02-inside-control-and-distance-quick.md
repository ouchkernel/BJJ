# Volume 2 — Inside Control & Distance — Quick Reference

## Inside position vs inside control

- **Inside position** = feet/knees inside opponent's. Just the prerequisite.
- **Inside control** = you govern the space between their knees, via active shoelace-hooking into the backs of their knees. This is the actual goal.
- **Read it:** knees pointing apart = space controlled. Knees pointing same direction = they're free.

## Hierarchy of inside positions (bottom, best → worst)

1. **Double inside** — best, attack either leg
2. **Single inside** — decent, one-leg attacks only
3. **Neutral** — viable, pommel in from here
4. **Shut out** — intolerable, avoid actively

Drill the transitions between all four.

## Fix: opponent has inside control on you

1. Post hands on their shins.
2. Push the shin down just below your knee.
3. Turn your kneecap straight into their shin (face same direction as theirs).
4. Pull your foot up to inside position.
5. Repeat other side. Now their knees point apart — you control the space.

## Approaching a seated opponent from top — do this in order

1. Distance first: stay off the toe line, match head level (jigotai stance) — they must move first.
2. Thumb post (palm down, inside their shoulder) — drives their head over hips, makes feet light.
3. Cross-grip the wrist on the side you want to enter (tie up the arm that could defend that leg).
4. Step boldly into the knee line using the space the thumb post opened.
5. **Wrist-vs-ankle dilemma:** hands forward → take the hands. Hands back → take the ankles (pull first, then lift — never lift first).
6. After ankle pull/lift, immediately plant a foot on the centerline to lock in inside position.

## Skill 2 — the knee line

**Goal of every ashi garami:** opponent's knee inside YOUR knee line. Outside it = no hip control = anemic, escapable position.

- ~90% of leg lock failures = losing the knee line.
- Fix: palm up, recline backward to drag their knee back inside.

## 2 physical enablers (almost every bottom entry needs both)

1. **Rounded spine** — "bottom of a rocking chair" shape. Lets you elevate a heavy opponent with minimal effort. Flat spine = feeble ashi garami.
2. **Penetrate the ankle line** — hips must breach it using hands AND feet together (never one alone).

## Ashi garami = scissor, not squeeze

- **Push leg** — wedge in front of the hip, drives into thigh.
- **Pull leg** — wedge behind the knee, pulls toward your chest.
- Opposite directions, both sides of their leg. Finish: point your bottom knee at your top knee.

## The 4 gravity drills — do these

Common prereqs for all 4: rounded spine, penetrate ankle line, knees/chin to chest, shoelaces connected to hamstring, finish with **no hands**.

1. **Facing** (start here) — double-leg, pull hips past ankle line, throw legs up, knee line locks above theirs, hold with ashi garami alone.
2. **Facing away** — rounded spine, recline, double-leg, split one leg inside/one outside, connect shoelace to hamstring, second leg wedges behind knee.
3. **Rolling inversion (half-Imanari)** — turn 45° (never square), drive near hand wrist-deep inside their knee, two-on-one grip, invert, set the push/pull scissor.
4. **Ankle-line split** — shoulder to their ankle line, hold shin below knee, split legs (one becomes pull leg, one push leg), shoelace-behind-hamstring catch.

## Common mistakes

- [ ] Confusing inside position with inside control
- [ ] Letting opponent get double inside on you
- [ ] Stepping outside centerline on a top approach
- [ ] Approaching seated guard upright with open legs, or head too high
- [ ] Lifting an ankle grip before pulling
- [ ] Letting opponent's knee sit outside your knee line
- [ ] Playing with a flat spine
- [ ] Not penetrating the ankle line before throwing legs
- [ ] Treating ashi garami as a squeeze instead of a scissor

## Decision points

- Opponent has inside control on you → post shin, turn kneecap in, pommel to inside.
- Approaching seated opponent on top → distance → thumb post + cross-grip → step to knee line.
- Opponent keeps wrists back → attack ankles (pull then lift). Hands forward → attack hands.
- Opponent's knee outside your knee line → palm up, recline to drag it back in.
- Can't elevate from bottom → check posture (spine) and penetration (ankle line) first.

## Terms

- **Inside control** — governing the space between opponent's knees.
- **Knee line** — line joining your two knees; opponent's knee must be inside it.
- **Ankle line / toe line / centerline** — distance/control demarcation lines.
- **Jigotai** — defensive stance, head matched to opponent.
- **Push leg / pull leg** — the scissoring wedges of ashi garami.
- **Gravity drills** — 4 no-hands drills testing distance + connection.
- **Imanari roll** — rolling entry; Drill 3 is its floor half.
- **Kani basami** — leg-scissor takedown, referenced as single-inside entry.
