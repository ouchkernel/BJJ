# Volume 6 — Reverse Ashi Garami, Toe Hold & Knee Bar, Standing Entries — Quick Reference

## The minimalist philosophy

~95% of finishes come from just **2 moves**: inside heel hook + outside heel hook. Only 2 supplements taught, ever: **figure-four toe hold** and **knee bar**. Stop collecting exotic locks.

## Reverse ashi garami — the position

- Only ashi garami where you face the **same direction** as the opponent.
- Upside: both legs right in front → easy double-leg control.
- Downside (central problem): opponent can **lock his legs defensively** — secondary leg defends primary. This is the **crossed-legs problem**, unique to this position.

### Get stable first (control before submission)
1. Two-on-one on the primary leg: wrist-deep wedge under the knee + grip the heel, cinch toward you.
2. Scissor your legs, knees pointing the **same** direction (don't spread wide — that doesn't work).
3. Base reflexively with hand/foot if off-balanced, then **return to the wedge**.
4. Never release the knee (ankle can be released briefly, knee can't).
5. For real stability: knee to the floor, scoop both legs (double-leg-tackle control), pitch to a hip/shoulder.

## Solving the crossed-legs problem — 3 options

1. **Fall to the side** → dilemma between figure-four toe hold (one leg) and knee bar (other leg).
2. **Untie the knot**: come in at the toes, peel one leg across centerline to expose the other heel, untangle, heel hook the primary leg.
3. **Figure-four toe hold** directly from position (especially good for sport rules).

## Figure-four toe hold — mechanics

A toe hold = a heel hook done with hands/arms. Same push-toes + pull-heel idea.

1. Grip: pinky knuckle to opponent's smallest-toe knuckle (not down the shin — kills leverage).
2. Thread framing arm elbow-deep at the **ankle**, not the shin.
3. Push hand drives big toe toward the tailbone. Pull elbow sweeps down, back toward your own jaw. Both together — push alone or pull alone does nothing.
4. Keep the foot in your hip pocket (close). Extended leg = weaker finish.
5. Head moves back → elbow follows → hand pushes in (head-and-hands rule). Loads your bodyweight into the lock.

## Knee bar

Linear lock. Used in the crossed-legs dilemma alongside the toe hold, or standalone. Same principles as everything else: end of the lever, hard fulcrum, opposing forces, follow-through.

## Reverse ashi garami as a gateway

You can untangle from here into **straight** or **cross** ashi garami at will — it's a hub, not a dead end.

## Standing entries

**Universal rule: fall with your HEAD OUTSIDE the opponent's lead leg, landing ACROSS his body.** Falling between the legs / head inside = you get squashed. This is the #1 entry mistake — don't make it.

### Collar-tie entry
1. Tie up his tricep (blocks his other arm from reaching your leg).
2. Square him up: circle away from the far leg, pull.
3. Step foot to inside position.
4. Clear his collar tie by walking away from the arm (don't try to out-muscle a strip).
5. Fall across the second leg — your leg points across so your head ends up outside — fire into ashi garami.
6. Track his head: moves away → straight ashi garami. Squares up → cross ashi garami.

### Arm-drag entry
1. Against bent-over, hands-forward stance.
2. Match head level, tie wrists, arm-drag — but aim for the legs, not a takedown.
3. Needs: penetration step + grip on the leg + a different finish than a takedown.
4. Shoot past and behind the far leg, head ends up outside.
5. Fire into ashi garami, track the head as above.

## Common Mistakes checklist

- [ ] Spread base wide for stability (doesn't work — use wedges + go to a hip)
- [ ] Released the knee (ankle is OK to release briefly, knee never is)
- [ ] Went for the submission before securing control
- [ ] Toe hold: gripped down the shin instead of the toe knuckle
- [ ] Toe hold: threaded the arm at the shin instead of elbow-deep at the ankle
- [ ] Let the foot extend away from your hip on the toe hold
- [ ] Pushed OR pulled on the toe hold instead of coupling both
- [ ] Fell between the legs / head inside on a standing entry (cardinal error)
- [ ] Tried to strip a strong collar tie by pulling instead of walking away

## Decision Points

- Opponent locks his legs → fall to the side (toe-hold/knee-bar dilemma) OR untie the knot + heel hook.
- Heel hooks not legal / sport context → figure-four toe hold.
- On top in reverse ashi garami → use as gateway, track the head, drop into straight or cross.
- Standing, opponent in collar tie → collar-tie entry.
- Standing, opponent bent over hands-forward → arm-drag entry.
- Every standing entry → head outside, fall across — never between the legs.

## Terminology

- **Reverse ashi garami** — same-direction-facing ashi garami; gateway to the other two subsystems.
- **Crossed-legs problem** — secondary leg defends primary by locking the legs together.
- **Figure-four toe hold** — heel hook applied with hands/arms; usable without ashi garami.
- **Knee bar** — linear supplementary finish.
- **Double-leg-tackle control / pitch to a hip** — stability solution in reverse ashi garami.
- **Collar-tie entry / arm-drag entry** — the two standing entries.
- **Head outside, fall across** — the universal standing-entry safety rule.
