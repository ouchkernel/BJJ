# Counter Ankle Locks From 50-50

## Overview

Using the ankle lock as a counterattack against an opponent who plays 50-50 Ashi Garami and is hunting for a heel hook. Exploits an intrinsic weakness of all Ashi Garami variants: the attacking athlete always exposes some part of their own leg to the defender.

---

## Core principle

Any Ashi Garami — regardless of type — leaves the attacker with some leg exposure to the defender. An aware defender who knows what to look for can flip what looks like a dangerous situation into a dangerous situation **for the attacker.**

## Defensive setup in 50-50

- Don't point your toes into the opponent's armpit (gives them an easy finish).
- **Close and cross your legs** so that if they attack, they first have to spend time untangling you.

## The counter mechanism

1. As the opponent works to set up their heel hook, look for a **catch on their top heel** (the leg they've thrown over the top).
2. Unlike attacking a true heel hook (which requires separating the opponent's ankles — takes time and skill), here you only need to go **under the 50-50 leg and over the top leg.**
3. Hand on the training partner's knee → work to the half-foot position.
4. Once half-foot is achieved, close up the grip.
5. As the opponent tries to extricate themselves, **cross your ankles over the top.**

### Why this works even though the grip "doesn't look like much"
- This is a visually unimpressive grip (not much surface area to grab) but the half-foot position generates enormous tension regardless — even a **one-handed** version gets tight quickly once locked hands are raised and the elbow points into the opponent's hip.

## The two-way trap

When the opponent feels this pressure, they have two options, and you win either way:
1. **They hand-fight / try to pull the leg out** → your body blocks their hands from reaching yours → you transition to a **heel hook attack, going belly down** on the leg they just freed.
2. **They stay and accept the ankle lock pressure** → finish the ankle lock directly.

So: fight the ankle lock → finish it. Escape the ankle lock by pulling the leg → walk directly into a heel hook.

## Drilled Sequence

1. Opponent attacks 50-50 / goes after your legs — defend, cross your ankles.
2. Push in, interfere with opponent's hands, make grip fighting hard for them.
3. When opportunity appears, bind up their leg.
4. Achieve half-foot.
5. Lock hands, hold.
6. **Opposing forces for pressure:** elbow arches up/toward you, feet drive down/away from you. (Knees coming toward you kills the pressure — keep them driving away.)
7. If opponent frees the leg to escape, immediately transition: grab the now-exposed heel, go belly down, finish the heel hook.

## Key detail: opposing forces

Recurring theme throughout the course — pressure comes from creating opposing vectors: **elbow toward the opponent, knees/feet away from the opponent.** Knees drifting toward you collapses the leverage.

---

## Key Takeaways

- All Ashi Garami control positions expose some part of the attacker's leg — learn to spot and exploit it.
- 50-50 counter only requires going under the 50-50 leg / over the top leg — simpler than isolating a true heel hook target.
- The half-foot position makes even a visually unimpressive, low-contact grip extremely tight.
- You win the exchange either way: opponent fights the ankle lock → finish it; opponent escapes the ankle lock → walks into a heel hook.
- Finishing pressure = opposing forces (elbow toward opponent, feet/knees away). Knees drifting inward kills the pressure.
