# Proactive Inside Ashi Garami

Source: `7713668694114-06-01- Proactive Inside Ashi Garami.mp4`

## Overview

Shows how to fold cross ankle locks into a heel-hook game, using inside foot position across Ashi Garami — a very common heel-hooking position. The core insight: an Achilles grip is mechanically far more robust than a heel hook grip, and switching to it changes the whole exchange in your favor when an opponent defends the heel.

---

## The problem with fishing for the heel

- From inside foot position across Ashi Garami, the natural instinct is to chase the heel for a heel hook.
- Skilled opponents know this and **turn the heel away** from you — hard to get to against a good opponent.
- Worse: chasing the heel can actively backfire. An opponent can **time a misdirectional escape** against your heel-hunting attempt and end up taking your back.
- Root cause: a heel hook grip is great for generating breaking leverage, but it is **mechanically weak** — the opponent points his toes, slips the heel, and it's fairly easy to escape.

## The fix: switch to an Achilles grip

- Same starting position (inside foot, across Ashi Garami), but instead of fishing for the heel, take an **Achilles grip** on the opponent's ankle.
- Pointing the toes does **nothing** against an Achilles grip — it's stuck in place. This is a fundamentally more resolute, mechanically strong grip than any heel hook grip.
- Result: when you invert and the opponent tries the same misdirectional escape that worked against the heel hunt, **he can't even get started** — he'll break his own leg trying.

## Sequence

1. Get inside foot position across Ashi Garami (common heel-hook entry position).
2. Instead of chasing the heel, secure an **Achilles grip** on the training partner's ankle.
3. Invert.
4. Connect and lock your feet together.
5. Hollow your body, expose your training partner's heel/ankle.
6. Sync with your hips, pin the training partner's leg to the mat → strong inverted break.

## If the opponent rolls through

- If the opponent rolls through with momentum, transfer your feet to the outside — this is an easy transition from the inverted position.
- From there you land in a classic outside Achilles grip.
- Recall the finishing cues from earlier in the course: **bring the elbow down to your body, then bring the elbow to the ceiling** — this produces very strong cross ankle lock breaks.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Chasing the heel from inside foot Ashi Garami is high-risk against a skilled opponent — it can be timed and countered into a back-take.
- **Achilles grip > heel hook grip mechanically**: pointing the toes (the standard heel hook escape) does nothing against it.
- Same entry position (inside foot, across Ashi Garami) works for both heel hooks and cross ankle locks — the grip choice determines which attack you're actually threatening.
- If your opponent rolls through, transfer feet to the outside and finish with the elbow-down-then-up cue from the outside Achilles grip.
