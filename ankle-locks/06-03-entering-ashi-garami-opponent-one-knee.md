# Entering Ashi Garami Ankle Lock On Opponent On One Knee

## Overview

Second of the three required entry scenarios: the opponent balances on **one knee** instead of two. Covers why opponents do this and the two variants (knee out wide vs. knee in the middle) with a distinct entry for each.

---

## Why an opponent goes to one knee

- On two knees, mobility and height are limited.
- Standing one knee up raises the hips and gives much more mobility — faster lateral movement, ability to knee-drive forward, more options generally.
- Which knee position an opponent chooses tells you their intent:
  - **Knee out wide** → advantage of lateral mobility, likes to move side to side.
  - **Knee in the middle** → advantage of driving forward, likes to pass forward.
- You don't control which one they pick — you need both entries ready.

## Entry when the knee is out wide

1. Get a hold of the training partner's hand, lock a **two-on-one grip**.
2. Get both feet underneath the upright leg.
3. Use your heels dug into the mat to pull your body in toward the opponent.
4. Take both feet ("shoelaces") and put them underneath the training partner's knee — lift and elevate. Do not let go of the arm yet.
5. When his foot/knee come directly over your stomach, kick through — you're now in Ashi Garami.
6. Standard finish sequence from here: hand on the knee, forearm around the Achilles, foot played through underneath, forward shift takes the foot off the mat.
7. Note: this can also be done **without hands** — pulling the body through underneath and lifting so the knee passes over the stomach is enough to enter Ashi Garami on its own.

## Entry when the knee is in the middle

1. Create a barrier with your shoelace/foot directly in front of the training partner.
2. Grab the opponent's calf muscle with one hand; your second hand can go anywhere on the upper body.
3. With two feet in the inside position, pull and place your feet at two points: **one at the ankle, one at the hip**.
4. His hand will naturally go down to the mat on the opposite side (or he falls on his face).
5. Kick through into Ashi Garami — hand on the knee, hand around the ankle, foot as a butterfly hook (shoelace).
6. From here it's about getting the ankle up off the floor and into attacking position.
7. If the opponent tries to explosively rip the foot free, follow with your leg and take the **belly-down finish**.

---

## Key Takeaways

- An opponent standing one knee up is trading stability for mobility — read *which* knee position (wide vs. middle) to know what he's trying to do.
- Knee-out-wide entry: two-on-one grip on the arm, both feet under the knee, lift, kick through when the knee crosses your stomach. Can be done with no hands at all.
- Knee-in-middle entry: foot barrier in front + calf grip + feet planted at ankle and hip, kick through as his hand is forced to post.
- If the opponent explodes to rip the foot free from the middle-knee entry, follow with the leg into a belly-down finish rather than fighting the escape directly.
