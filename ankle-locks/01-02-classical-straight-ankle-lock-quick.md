# Straight Ankle Lock — Quick Reference

## Do this, in order

1. Get **elbow-deep** around the ankle (not wrist-deep).
2. Push partner's shin with free hand + hollow your body → bottom half of foot disappears.
3. Pull elbow **inside your own hip**.
4. Pinch knees around partner's leg.
5. Lock hands, figure-four.
6. Check the 4 cues below. All pass → finish. Any fail → reset.
7. Finish: wrist rises, elbow drives to opponent's centerline. **Not** an arch-back.

## 4 cues — check before finishing

- [ ] No daylight around the ankle
- [ ] You can see/feel the ankle bone
- [ ] Elbow is inside your hip (not outside)
- [ ] Partner can't kick the foot through AND can't limp the leg out

If any box is unchecked, you don't have the lock yet — don't crank on it.

## Skip the old way

Classic grip = wrist-deep, figure-four, pressure on Achilles. **Don't bother.**
- Achilles tendon doesn't tear from squeezing — only from jump/landing loads.
- Metal pipe test: full body weight on Achilles tendon, zero breaking effect.
- Wrist-deep = daylight = opponent kicks the leg free.
- Only produces discomfort, not a tap from a tough opponent.

## Two ways to blow the position

| You did this | Opponent does this |
|---|---|
| Left foot fully visible (too shallow) | "Puts the boot on" — pushes foot straight through |
| Pulled foot too far past centerline (too deep) | Limps the leg out, especially by yanking your forearms |

Target: bottom half of foot hidden, no more. That's "half foot."

## Grip ≠ grip: know which one you're using

- **Achilles grip** = elbow-deep, holding only. Good for control while partner squirms. Will NOT finish.
- **Ankle lock grip** = elbow-deep + half foot achieved. This one finishes.

Same starting position, different depth/foot control = different tool.

## Where the tap actually comes from

Elbow → opponent's centerline. Wrist rises and flexes.
Not: arching your back.

## New target vs old target

- Old (classic): back of ankle, Achilles tendon, straight-line pressure. Doesn't work.
- New (modern): front of ankle — anterior ligaments, joint capsule, anterior tibialis tendon. Twisting, not squeezing.
  - Toes pushed **in** → stress on anterior ligaments.
  - Toes pushed **out** → stress on anterior tibialis tendon.

## Milestone to hit before moving on

Get a training partner to tap **using your hands only** — no leaning, no extra body mechanics. Once you can do that consistently, move to next video.
