# If You Can't Get The Half Foot Position — Go Belly Down

## Overview

Second branch in the ankle lock decision tree. Covers what to do when the opponent successfully denies the preferred half-foot position (e.g., by turning toes in toward your spine). Core rule: **go belly down**, and use the orientation of the opponent's knee to decide which direction (inside or outside).

---

## The decision rule

If you cannot get the preferred half foot position despite your best effort → **turn belly down.**

You have two options:
- **Turn to the outside** — general default when given the choice.
- **Turn to the inside** — also highly effective, not just a fallback.

**Which one to pick is determined entirely by the orientation of the opponent's knee:**
- Knee facing/pushed **outside** (or even vertical) → fall to the **outside**.
- Knee facing/turned **inside** → pass your outside leg over and fall to the **inside**.

Just visually check the training partner's knee — that's your guide, live, in the moment.

## Belly-down entry — outside variant

1. Starting from an Achilles/holding grip, hollow out into the ankle lock grip / half foot position where possible.
2. Even without perfect half foot, the grip itself puts significant pressure on — the rib cage blocks the opponent kicking the leg through, and traction on the ankle makes limping out difficult.
3. If the knee is oriented outside, take your inside leg and pass it over the training partner's head.
4. Bring your two feet in close together.
5. Post your head and hand down on the floor, back onto both legs (belly down).
6. You can finish directly from here, or make the adjustment to half foot in the inverted position for an even stronger finish.

## Belly-down entry — inside variant

1. Same starting point: can't achieve half foot, opponent's toes turned in toward your spine.
2. Take the inside leg, pass it over the training partner's head.
3. Go in short with the second leg.
4. Rotate through to a belly-down position.
5. Spread your knees, roll/sprawl for a strong finish.

## If the opponent blocks even this: the "cut" counter

- Sometimes the opponent prevents you getting belly-down by keeping toes turned toward your spine.
- Counter: **cut the ankle** to make the opponent's hips move toward you (rather than away). Cutting here pulls him along the mat toward you.
- Pass your foot over the top and lock a triangle.
- Rotate through, maintain the triangle, and — even without half foot — spread your knees and go belly-down for a strong finish.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Rule of thumb: **can't get half foot → go belly down.** Don't fight for a stalled half-foot position indefinitely.
- Direction (inside vs. outside) is decided by **opponent's knee orientation**, not preference — knee out → go outside, knee in → go inside.
- Even without half foot, belly-down finishes generate real pressure via rib cage blocking and ankle traction.
- If the opponent also blocks the belly-down entry by keeping toes turned toward your spine, cut the ankle to drag their hips toward you, pass the foot, and lock a triangle before going belly-down.
