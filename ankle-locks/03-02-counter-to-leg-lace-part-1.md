# Ankle Lock As A Counter To A Leg Lace - Part 1

## Overview

Countering the **leg lace** — a common modern leg-locking control position using "double trouble" to bind both of your legs together, from which the opponent can attack heel hooks effectively. Even trapped in a leg lace, a straight ankle lock counterattack is available.

---

## Reading the position

Opponent has bound your legs together in a leg lace and controls you from there. Depending on how well he's controlling it, your bottom foot will be more or less exposed:

- **Foot exposed / opponent lazy about hiding it**: you barely have to work — just get your knee out and catch it.
- **Foot well hidden**: you have to dig for it.

## Digging for a hidden foot

1. Bring your foot to an inside position to expose it.
2. Get a hold of the knee.
3. Punch through — you'll see the **ankle bone become visible**, confirming you've reached the half-foot position.
4. If the opponent grabs at you defensively, that's actually good for you — his leg is bound up in his own leg lace/Ashi Garami, so he only has one hand free to fight your grip, which usually isn't enough.

## Finishing sequence

1. Draw the exposed foot out.
2. Get two hands on it — one hand, then reinforce with the other on the knee — to fix your grip and settle into the half-foot position.
3. Come up into a full figure-four.
4. Finish.

## What if the opponent escapes?

If your opponent is intelligent, he'll realize: **the only thing holding him in the ankle lock is himself** — you don't actually have Ashi Garami control over him, he has the entanglement on you. So he can simply abandon the leg lace/entanglement and get out.

That's fine — and expected. When he bails out of the entanglement to escape your counter, that transition creates **many opportunities to flow into the offensive ankle locks** covered earlier in the course. You go from counter-offense back into conventional offense smoothly.

### Sequence when opponent escapes
- He clears the Ashi Garami and lets go to get out.
- As he moves/turns to escape, follow with whichever leg positions correctly (e.g., throw the right leg over/under to reposition), and finish once he re-settles — often ending in a belly-down, one-hand finish.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Leg lace binds your legs together, but your bottom foot is still attackable — expose it (dig if needed), confirm half-foot via a visible ankle bone.
- Grabbing/hand-fighting from the opponent is often not enough to stop you, since his own leg is tied up in his lace.
- Two-part game plan: (1) if opponent stays and is naive, finish the straight ankle lock directly; (2) if he's smart and abandons the leg lace to escape, that abandonment is itself an opening — flow into your standard offensive ankle locks.
- This is a genuine counter-offense skill: it converts one of the scarier control positions in modern leg locking (being leg-laced) into your own offensive opportunity.
