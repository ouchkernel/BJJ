# Opponent Performs An Outside Spin: Following With An Inside Leg

## Overview

Companion decision-tree lesson to 02-08, covering the opponent's other major spin-based escape: the **outside spin**. This escape exploits an unclosed hip — the fix is the mirror image of the inside-spin counter: hook your **inside foot over the opponent's hip** to follow the roll to a finish.

---

## Why the outside spin works

- This escape is predicated on the loop around the opponent's hips **not being closed** — i.e., you have no Ashi Garami / leg control immobilizing his hips (no feet/triangle over the top of the hips).
- If the opponent's hips were pinned (e.g., a triangle-style lock over the hips), this spin would be impossible.
- With hips free, the opponent puts hands on the floor and spins away to the outside.

### The mechanical detail that makes it work
- The Achilles grip alone is holding the lock — nothing else.
- When the opponent's toes point up toward the back of your shoulder, the Achilles grip is very strong.
- The moment he **turns**, his toes invert to point downward — and from that inverted position, it becomes very easy for him to kick/rip the leg free, even from what felt like a strong grip.
- **Test/cue**: toes pointing up at your shoulder = hard to escape. Toes turned to point down = easy to escape. Watch for the toe-inversion moment.

## The fix: inside foot over the hip

1. As soon as the opponent begins the outside spin, use your **inside foot to hook over his hip** (a "back heel" style catch on the hip).
2. This connection means his own spinning motion carries **you** into a belly-down position.
3. From belly-down, finish with strong breaking pressure.
4. Sometimes one turn is enough; sometimes the opponent's spin has enough momentum that you'll be carried through 2, 3, even 4 rotations before you can finish — this is normal, ride it out.

## Rule of thumb

If your foot connects over the opponent's hip during his spin, you have a strong chance to finish him regardless of how many rotations it takes.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Outside spin escape only works when the opponent's hips are **not** pinned by your leg entanglement — closing that loop (e.g. triangle over the hips) removes the option entirely.
- Watch the toes: pointing up at your shoulder = strong grip; turning to point down = escape window opening.
- Counter: hook your **inside foot over the opponent's hip** as the spin starts — his own momentum carries you to belly-down.
- Multiple rotations before finishing is expected and fine — don't fight the spin, follow it.
- Contrast with the inside spin counter (02-08): there you use the **outside** leg; here you use the **inside** leg — the mirror-image response to the mirror-image escape.
