# Controlling & Immobilizing The Hips — Quick Reference

## The problem

Perfect upper body grip = still doesn't finish, if opponent can move his hips. He'll always relieve pressure by lifting/moving hips. This is how classic ankle locks got escaped constantly.

## The fix: opposing force

Ankle lock = force up and away.
You need a second force = down on the hips (or knee).
Up + down opposing forces = actual break.

## THE golden rule

- Opponent's knee points **out** → your **inside** leg controls.
- Opponent's knee points **in** → your **outside** leg controls.

Memorize this. It governs almost every leg configuration in this course.

## Do this (knee pointing outward)

1. Grip established. Test: try to break with hands only — watch him escape via hips. Confirms the problem.
2. Right foot into opponent's hamstring.
3. Left leg over the top.
4. Come down to shoulder, squeeze knees so his knee is trapped between yours.
5. Break.

Alt version: foot on the hip, cover it with your other foot, sandwich knees, rise and break.

## If he turns his knee inward to defend

1. Pass your **outside** leg over his leg.
2. Lock up, strong back-heel over his leg.
3. As he keeps turning, this creates down-pressure that forces him back into position.
4. Works even without the elbow fully locked.

## Butterfly Ashi Garami variant

1. Foot passes underneath, lands on/over the hip.
2. Back-heel one foot, push forward with the other — knee gets pinched between your knees.
3. Stack second foot on first foot for extra lock.

## Cue to chase

Make his heel "disappear" (half-foot). The more it disappears, the closer your elbow gets to your hip, the stronger the lock.

## Don't

Rely on standard/classic Ashi Garami for this — opponent just splits the feet and steps over the top. Doesn't constrain hips at all.
