# Transition From Achilles Grip To Ankle Lock Grip

Source: `7713668694114-01-03- Transition From Achilles Grip To Ankle Lock Grip.mp4`

## Overview

Recurring theme for the whole course: the transition from an **Achilles grip** (holds/controls only) to an **ankle lock grip** (breaks). These solve two different problems — a robust grip for controlling a leg in position, vs. leverage capable of breaking a joint. This lesson isolates the mechanics of that transition, done against real resistance.

---

## Why the Achilles grip alone doesn't finish

- The Achilles grip is excellent for **pulling** — holding someone in place.
- It cannot **push**. Against a strong, resisting leg, trying to push with this grip just makes it slide/ride up.
- To finish, you need to move the opponent's foot from **behind you to partially in front of you** — which requires a pushing force the Achilles grip can't generate on its own.

## The 3 mechanical keys to the transition

### 1. Pushing hand
- Only a hand can push — either an **open hand** or a **figure-four** grip, both using a palm on the training partner's leg.
- Many leg lock grips can only pull. The advantage of figure-four / one-handed grips specifically: **they leave a hand free to push.**
- Locking into a finishing grip (e.g. elbow-to-elbow) too early removes your ability to push — avoid committing to a full finishing lock before you've established position.

### 2. Pushing leg
- Your own leg can assist by pushing on the training partner's leg.
- Described as a butterfly-Ashi-style grip on the partner's leg — hands and feet work together to drive the opponent's foot into the correct breaking position.

### 3. Hollow body
- Even with a pushing hand and leg, a locked-out/rigid body makes it hard to get the foot into position.
- Fix: **hollow your body out** — scoot your hips back. This applies whether you're seated or on your side on the floor; the mechanics are the same either way.

**Combine all three** (push with hand, push with leg, hollow body) → the foot moves from a low-value position behind you to the high-value half-foot position in front of you.

---

## Drilled Sequence

1. Start in an Achilles grip — figure-four or one-handed, your choice, both work.
2. Note the opponent's foot is entirely **behind your elbow** (poor breaking position, good only for control).
3. Get a pushing hand on the training partner's leg/hamstring.
4. Simultaneously: scoot your hips back, hollow your body, push with your leg/foot ("shoelace" placement) into their leg.
5. This drives the foot into the **half-foot position**.
6. Lock the figure-four.
7. Squeeze knees, test: make your partner tap using your hands alone.

## Drilling notes from the roll

- Partners alternated roles and repeated the same sequence: Achilles grip → identify foot fully behind elbow (bad position) → push with hand + push with leg ("shoelace" placed into partner's leg) + hollow body simultaneously → half-foot achieved → lock figure-four → tap from hands alone.
- Emphasized: this transition should be fast, and achievable **even under resistance/pressure** — it's not a slow repositioning, it's a near-simultaneous combination of the three elements.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Achilles grip = control tool (pulls only). Ankle lock grip = finishing tool (needs pushing capability).
- Three simultaneous ingredients get you from one to the other: **pushing hand, pushing leg, hollow body.**
- Avoid committing to a finishing lock (e.g., elbow-to-elbow) before you've pushed the foot into half-foot position — you lose your pushing hand too early.
- Goal/benchmark: transition from Achilles grip to a hands-only tap, quickly, under resistance from a skilled opponent.
