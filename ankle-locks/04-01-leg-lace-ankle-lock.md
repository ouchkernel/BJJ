# Leg Lace Ankle Lock

Source: `7713668694114-04-01- Leg Lace Ankle Lock.mp4`

## Overview

Specialty topic: ankle locking out of a **double trouble** position (both of the opponent's legs controlled/laced). Normally this position is used to hunt heel hooks, but skilled opponents can make heel hooks difficult to finish — this lesson shows how to use the same controlling position to attack the ankle instead.

---

## The position: leg lace / double trouble

- Both of the opponent's legs are laced/controlled — staying true to the "double trouble" principle of controlling both legs at once.
- Standard game plan from here: control the primary leg, release the secondary leg, hunt heel hook on the freed leg.
- **Problem:** opponents know this. When you release a leg to hunt the heel hook, skilled opponents start turning/spinning, making the heel very hard to finish.

## The ankle lock alternative

Instead of releasing a leg to chase the heel hook, attack the ankle lock **directly from the laced position** — you already have an Achilles grip on the ankle, but the foot is too far behind you to break yet.

### Mechanism
1. Hand on the training partner's knee.
2. Hollow your body out.
3. Bring your elbow in (same principle covered earlier in the course).
4. Opponent trying to kick the leg through now finds it very difficult.
5. Bring your foot in, place it inside the training partner's ankle.
6. Close up your grips — now opponent can't pull the leg out *or* kick it through.
7. Head over your own shoulder, rotate to the outside → strong pressure.

This converts what looks like a mere holding position (Achilles grip, foot far behind you) into a genuine breaking position.

### Visual cue check (half-foot, same as always)
- Entire sole of the opponent's foot visible = holding position only, no breaking power, angling off has no effect.
- Hollow body, elbow-deep → **bottom half of the foot disappears** → that's your breaking position.

## Handling variations

### If opponent's primary leg slips free while you focus on the secondary leg
Transition into a different ankle lock variant: place your foot through, hollow out, get half-foot, take your foot over the training partner's hip, and come out to the same strong finishing position covered earlier in the course.

### If the opponent's knee points inward (instead of kicking the leg free)
- Place your foot on their calf muscle, second foot placed alongside.
- Turn onto your side, fix grips, lock up.
- Go directly to the **flat back finish** covered earlier.

### Reading the opponent's knee direction to choose the finish
- **Knee points up/outside:** throw your inside leg over the top and connect for a strong finish.
- **Knee points inside:** place your foot inside, pivot to bring your knee to face outside, then finish.
- **Can't change the knee direction:** go all the way around instead.

## The critical detail: winning the knee race

If the opponent's knee points inward, **whoever gets a knee to the mat first controls the exchange:**
- If the opponent's hip beats your knee/foot to the mat, they escape — every time.
- Fix: hook your foot over the top of their hip *before* going to the floor. This ensures your knee lands on the outside of their knee when you both go down, blocking their hip from reaching the mat.
- If your knee isn't there first, their hip reaching the mat = escape, guaranteed.

From this controlling position, **belly-down finishes are very strong.**

---

## Key Takeaways

- Double trouble / leg lace positions aren't just for heel hooks — the same control converts directly into an ankle lock attack when heel hooks are being defended well.
- Achilles grip (foot behind you) = holding only. Hollow body + elbow-deep + foot inside their ankle = breaking position (half-foot).
- If the primary leg slips while you focus on the secondary: transition to the hip-pass finishing variant already covered in the course.
- Read the opponent's knee direction to pick your finishing route (over the top vs. pivot-inside vs. go-around).
- **Race your knee to the mat before their hip gets there** — hooking your foot over their hip first is what guarantees this. Losing this race = they escape.
