# 3 Finishing Positions For Cross Ankle Locks

Source: `7713668694114-05-03- 3 Finishing Positions For Cross Ankle Locks.mp4`

## Overview

Straight ankle locks have 4 reliable finishing positions across 360° of rotation (outside hip, inside hip, belly-down, flat-back). Cross ankle locks are different — one of those four (inside hip) is unreliable with the standard entanglement, effectively leaving **3 dependable finishing positions**.

---

## The 3 positions that work well

With **outside Ashi Garami** in place and true half-foot achieved:

1. **Outside hip** — come down onto your shoulder, close your elbow. The elbow-closing action alone is enough to send significant pressure through the opponent's ankle. Works even one-handed.
2. **Flat back** — get the opponent's heel up, feet connected over their hip, then rotate through. As with straight ankle locks, don't go flat back in a straight line — get your body compact, drop the elbow, and rotate toward the other leg.
3. **Over-the-top position** — rotating over the top into a related finishing configuration is also strong (video demonstrates but treats as a variant of the same rotational family as outside hip/flat back).

## The one that disappoints: inside hip

- With an **outside** Ashi Garami, finishing from the **inside hip** feels weak — even with a true, complete half-foot grip.
- The opponent can post a hand and start limping the leg free relatively easily from this angle.
- Framed as an oversimplification but a useful one: **be wary of stalling out on the inside hip when your leg entanglement is an outside Ashi Garami** — the two don't match well.

## The fix when you land on the inside hip anyway

**Roll through.** If you're on the inside hip with an outside Ashi Garami and not getting real breaking pressure:

1. Recognize the position isn't going to finish (ankle exposed, half-foot present, but leg configuration kills the breaking force).
2. Roll across your training partner's body.
3. Come out the other side on the **outside hip** (or another strong position) — converting the failed inside-hip attempt into a winning outside-hip attempt.

This roll-through is presented as the default answer any time you feel yourself falling to the disappointing inside-hip finish with this entanglement.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Cross ankle locks: 3 reliable finishes (outside hip, flat back, belly-down/over-the-top rotation), not 4.
- **Inside hip + outside Ashi Garami = weak finish**, regardless of grip quality — treat it as a warning sign, not a finishing spot.
- Fix: roll your opponent through, come out on the outside hip (or other strong position) instead of grinding from the inside hip.
- (See next lesson — 05-04 — for how to actually win from the inside hip by switching entanglements instead of rolling away from it.)
