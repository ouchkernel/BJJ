# Belly Down Ankle Locks — Quick Reference

Source: `7713668694114-01-06- Introducing Belly Down Ankle Locks.mp4`

## Why this matters

~50% of all your ankle lock finishes will be belly down. Not optional — core skill.

## What it is

Go face-down/prone over the position instead of staying upright.
Result: body weight added + floor pins opponent's knee = way stronger opposing force than upright.

## When to use it

- Can't get perfect half-foot? Go belly down instead of forcing a bad upright finish.
- Tougher opponent = more belly down finishes. They'll fight, you follow, you end up belly down.
- Belly down is forgiving — even an imperfect grip often still finishes from here.

## Do this

1. Lock any hand grip already learned (fig-4 wrist / fig-4 bicep / own-elbow).
2. Hand on the floor, arch in like a wrestling sprawl.
3. Spread knees, press into the sprawl, finish.

## One-handed vs two-handed

| | One-handed | Two-handed |
|---|---|---|
| Head | Rises up | Comes down |
| Hips | Drop | Drop |
| Body | Sprawl shape | Curled/hollow around the leg |

Best single grip for this: grab your own elbow.

## If opponent turns knee inward to avoid half-foot

1. Lock a closed triangle/loop over both legs.
2. Separate your knees — same breaking force as the outward version.
3. Finish two-handed with a sprawl.

## Tell

If your partner is sweating and struggling way more in belly down drilling than upright drilling — that's the position working. More body weight, floor traps the knee, more pain to escape.
