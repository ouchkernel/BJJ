# Opponent Tries To Stand Up To A Knee To Get Foot To Floor: Flat Back Butterfly Ankle Lock

## Overview

Counter for one of the most common defensive reactions: the opponent curls their foot through and comes up to a knee, trying to plant the sole of the foot flat on the mat (which makes it very difficult to get to a desirable finishing position). This lesson gives the **butterfly Ashi** block plus a **flat-back finish**.

---

## Reading the defense

- Opponent's goal: get the whole sole of the foot down on the mat. Once it's flat on the floor, it's difficult to reposition into a finishing position.
- Watch for the opponent curling the foot through and starting to rise to a knee — that's your trigger to react.

## The counter sequence

1. As soon as you see the opponent curling the foot through/coming up to a knee, put your **foot underneath their hamstring**.
2. Put your **second foot on their calf muscle** — this pair of feet becomes a physical block/impediment locked in the opponent's path, so driving the foot to the mat becomes difficult.
3. Put a hand on the opponent's **knee**.
4. Use this control to achieve the **half foot position**.
5. Lock up your hands.
6. Put your **back flat on the mat**.
7. Rotate your **head to the outside** — this is the "flat back finish": head rotates outward while back stays flat, generating strong finishing pressure.

## Key mechanical notes

- The butterfly-Ashi foot placement (foot under hamstring + foot on calf) is specifically what denies the opponent's foot-to-mat plan — it's not just incidental leg control, it's the direct counter to this specific defense.
- The finish itself doesn't come from arching — it comes from **flat back + head rotating outward**.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Trigger: opponent curls foot through and rises to a knee, aiming to plant the sole flat on the mat.
- Counter: butterfly-Ashi block (foot under hamstring, foot on calf) prevents the foot from reaching the floor.
- From there: hand on knee → half foot → lock hands → back flat → head rotates outside → finish.
- This is specifically the tool for opponents whose knee ends up oriented in a way that still allows a flat-back/outside finish (contrast with 02-04, the short ankle lock, used when the knee points inward and blocks this option).
