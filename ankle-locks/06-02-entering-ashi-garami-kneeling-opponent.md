# Entering Ashi Garami On A Kneeling Opponent

Source: `7713668694114-06-02- Entering Ashi Garami On A Kneeling Opponent.mp4`

## Overview

Ankle locks don't happen by accident in sparring — you have to actively force your opponent into Ashi Garami. This lesson covers the first of three required entry scenarios (opponent on two knees, one knee, or standing): **getting a kneeling opponent into Ashi Garami**, plus a variant for a defensively-postured opponent who denies upper-body grips.

**Golden rule:** you cannot enter Ashi Garami while both of the opponent's knees are on the ground. You must get at least one knee off the mat first.

---

## Method 1: Opponent engages with an upper-body grip

1. Get a **pulling grip** high on the training partner's triceps/armpits — ear to ear with your partner. Don't grab low at the elbows; grip all the way up.
2. This grip makes it easy to slip underneath the opponent and force his hands down to the mat.
3. Once his hands touch the mat, **switch to a pushing grip** — turn your body underneath him, up to an ankle. You're now in Ashi Garami.
4. Once in Ashi Garami: push on the knee, hands on your own stomach, Achilles grip on the training partner's ankle.
5. Give a little pressure to force his hand further down to the mat — you're now ready for the ankle lock game.

### Alternate lift entry
1. Bind up the training partner, pull him in, and lift — force both knees off the mat.
2. Post on his knee, thread your foot through, and give a little lift to get that foot into ankle lock position.
3. When he tries to drive the knee back down to the mat, your leg blocks it.

## Method 2: Opponent doesn't grab you (no upper-body contact)

1. Push in and get a hold of a wrist or elbow.
2. Slide your body toward the opponent.
3. This makes it easy to lift him and start forcing his hands to the mat.
4. Come out to the side, hand on the knee, lift → you're in ankle lock territory, same finishes apply.

## Method 3: Defensive posture — opponent controls your arm

Used when the opponent is proactively defending (down on two knees, controlling one of your arms):

1. Grab the training partner's hand on one side, pull yourself down into position.
2. Put your knee **inside** the training partner's knee; point your foot down toward the floor (inverted foot).
3. This is a **safe defensive position** — hard for the opponent to leg lock you from either side:
   - Foot inverted on one side.
   - Arm controlled on the other side (if he goes for an ankle lock there, you can easily put your foot on his stomach and kick out).
4. To progress: release the controlled hand, scoot inside the training partner's leg.
5. Get his knee off the mat (visual cue: knee visibly off the mat).
6. Park your foot behind the training partner's leg (quasi-X-guard) → sets up the full ankle lock entry sequence.
7. Alternate finish: if the opponent's knee is already rotating outward as he defends, close over from the outside, lock a triangle, and finish belly down.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **No entry without a knee off the mat** — this is the prerequisite for every kneeling-opponent entry.
- Grip sequence for the standard entry: **pull grip high on the triceps → force hands to mat → switch to push grip → turn underneath into Ashi Garami.**
- If the opponent won't give you an upper-body grip, attack a wrist/elbow instead and slide in — same end result.
- The "knee inside, foot inverted, one arm controlled" position is a genuinely safe base while you wait for an opening — not just a transitional stance.
- Watch for the opponent's knee rotating outward as a defensive tell — it's a cue to go straight to a triangle/belly-down finish instead.
