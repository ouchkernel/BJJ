# Top Entry To Ashi Garami Controlling 2 Legs

Source: `7713668694114-06-06- Top Entry To Ashi Garami Controlling 2 Legs.mp4`

## Overview

All prior entries started from bottom position. This lesson covers entering Ashi Garami from **top position** — passing the opponent's guard while he's underneath you — with an emphasis on controlling both of his legs, not just the one you're attacking.

**Core piece of advice:** whenever entering from top position, control both of the opponent's legs to the best of your ability. This doesn't require an elaborate grip — often just a hand on the wrist/ankle area of the secondary leg is enough. You attack the primary leg while controlling the ankle of the secondary leg. You don't need this control at the exact moment of entry, but it's often worth acquiring as the opponent starts defensive movement.

---

## Entry 1: One-handed entry with secondary-leg control

1. Get between the training partner's knees.
2. Cup the training partner's knee.
3. Step up to the line of the training partner's hip with your leg.
4. This lets you simultaneously control an **ankle and a knee on the same side**.
5. Pass the foot up into position and sit straight back.
6. Result: you're controlling both of the training partner's legs — a wrist/hand grip on one ankle, two legs wrapped around the other.
7. As the partner starts to turn, this gives you good opportunities to connect your own legs.
8. Finish: transition to the favored **one-handed grip**, all the way inside; hip down for strong finishes.

## Entry 2: Two-handed entry with a triangle finish

1. Enter between the training partner's knees, hugging with the knee.
2. Bring your second foot up to touch just inside the training partner (a control point).
3. When the opponent tries to pull his other leg up, it runs into your hand — the hand serves as a blocker/checkpoint.
4. Pass that second foot over the top.
5. As he moves away, secure a **full triangle around the training partner's leg**, locked around the ankle.
6. Finish: tap straight down to the floor — described as a very strong finish.

### From a supine opponent (variant of Entry 2)
1. Move side to side until reaching the correct position.
2. Step up and secure your grips.
3. The foot comes up and catches in place.
4. As the opponent tries to move away, get your foot inside, lock the second triangle over.
5. Secure the one-handed grip.
6. Spread down, tap.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Golden rule for top-position entries: control both legs, not just the one you're attacking.** Minimal grip is fine — a hand on the secondary ankle/wrist is often enough.
- Two distinct top-position entries covered: a **one-handed entry** (ankle + knee control on the attacking side, one-handed finishing grip) and a **two-handed entry** (blocks the second leg, passes the foot over, finishes with a triangle around the ankle).
- The triangle-around-the-ankle finish is described as very strong and finishes with a straight tap down to the floor.
- This completes the full entry map for the course: bottom position against an opponent on two knees, one knee, or standing, plus top position controlling both legs, plus the ability to pass the foot side to side to switch between straight and cross ankle locks.
