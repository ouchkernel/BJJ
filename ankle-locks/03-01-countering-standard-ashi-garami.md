# Countering A Standard Ashi Garami With Ankle Locks

Source: `7713668694114-03-01- Countering A Standard Ashi Garami With Ankle Locks.mp4`

## Overview

Shifts from proactive ankle-lock attacks to **counter-offense**: your opponent takes a standard Ashi Garami (or Ruimi Ashi Garami) leg entanglement on you, and you use a straight ankle lock to counter-attack. Framed as one of the best uses of ankle locking — fast, efficient, and flips control back to you against an aggressive leg locker.

---

## Why standard Ashi Garami is vulnerable

Any Ashi Garami comes at a price: to some degree, the attacker's own legs become exposed to counterattack. The standard/Ruimi Ashi Garami is one of the easier variants to counter.

## Step 1: read the visual cue

Look at your opponent's Ashi Garami leg and check: **are his toes pointed outward or inward?**

### Toes pointed outward, naive positioning
- Sloppy toe positioning + foot brought across the centerline = easy entry into **palm-to-palm toe holds** for a quick finish.
- Mechanics: physically push the foot across, hold it, lock through underneath, connect hands palm-to-palm, bring head toward hands, pull elbow backward. Pressure comes from pushing (forearm makes his toes point back) combined with rotating your elbow back.

### Toes pointed hard outward, good positioning (expect this from a good opponent)
- The toe-hold entry becomes difficult — but this actually opens the ankle lock instead.
- There's always a gap/hole available — put your hand into it, lock your own hands together, and pull. This lands you directly in a **perfect half-foot position** with minimal effort ("it just lands right in place").
- Pass your second leg over the top (exact landing spot doesn't matter much), connect, and finish — the opponent's hip is already locked in place by his own Ashi Garami, which creates the counter-force needed for a strong finish.

## More sophisticated entry: knee inversion

1. From the same captured position, get an **inside foot position** on your arm/grip.
2. Invert your knee (rotate/bring it up and through).
3. Once the knee inverts, bring your second foot over the opponent's hip.
4. Sit down directly into the same ankle lock finishes covered earlier in the course.

### Drilled sequence (knee-inversion version)
1. Opponent shoots for a leg lock, kicks through into classic Ruimi Ashi Garami.
2. You capture his ankle as he enters.
3. Take an inside foot position, invert your knee.
4. Bring your foot over his hip, hand goes to his knee.
5. Slide your knee through and underneath — you're now in the pivot position for the standard ankle lock finishes.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Standard/Ruimi Ashi Garami always exposes the attacker's legs to some counter — read the opponent's toe direction to choose your response.
- **Toes outward + sloppy** → palm-to-palm toe hold (push foot across, lock underneath, pull elbow back).
- **Toes outward + tight** → straight ankle lock: find the gap, lock your hands, pull into half-foot, pass the second leg over, finish (opponent's own hip lock supplies the counter-force).
- Advanced variant: invert your knee once you have an inside foot position, bring the second foot over the hip, and sit into the same standard ankle lock finishes.
- This is a fast way to flip an aggressive leg-locking opponent from attacker to defender.
