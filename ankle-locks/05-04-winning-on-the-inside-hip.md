# Winning On The Inside Hip

## Overview

Follows directly from lesson 05-03: outside Ashi Garami creates a "dead zone" on the inside hip where cross ankle locks don't break reliably. This lesson shows how to actually finish from the inside hip instead of rolling away from it — by **switching the leg entanglement**.

---

## Why the inside hip fails with outside Ashi Garami

- Outside Ashi Garami = your foot passes over the opponent's hip on the same side as the leg you're attacking (e.g., attacking their right leg → your right foot over their right hip). Creates strong knee-to-knee connection.
- Reliable for flat-back and (to a lesser extent) other finishes, but creates the dead zone specifically on the inside hip.

## The fix: switch to an inside leg entanglement when you feel the position tilting toward inside hip

Two escalating options, both built the same way:

### Setup common to both
- Never leave a foot extended/exposed — bring your two feet back together immediately.
- Grab the training partner's knee, throw your ankles across so they land over the **far hip**.
- Back-heel — dig both feet into the hip.
- Expose the opponent's ankle, then lock in, folding the elbow down.

### Option 1 — ankles crossed over the far hip
- Cross your ankles over the opponent's far hip.
- Strong because it's **wedged against the floor**.
- When the opponent tries to limp the leg out, it's locked in place (contrast: without this entanglement, that's exactly when they escape).

### Option 2 (stronger) — triangle lock over the far hip
- Instead of just crossing ankles, lock your legs in a full **triangle over the opponent's hip**.
- Gives a much stronger back-heel on their hip.
- Lock your hands together — very tight.
- Best feature: if the opponent tries to limp the leg out, you simply **follow their body up and over** — their own escape attempt feeds the lock rather than beating it.

## Reading a resistant opponent (meta-strategy)

If your opponent knows cross ankle locks and how to defend them:
- Their first move will be to try to get you away from outside-hip/flat-back finishing setups — pulling you toward the inside hip.
- If you're stuck with a traditional outside Ashi Garami here, they can get the sole of the foot to the mat and hand-fight — hard to finish.
- Countermeasure: lock everything up as above (pass the foot across, lock the triangle/back-heel) — now when they try to limp free, they're stuck in place.
- From there: take a wrist-to-rib grip for a strong finish, **or, best of all, roll your training partner through** — extraordinarily strong once rolled.
- Conversely, if you triangle-lock your legs early, it becomes much harder for the opponent to open you back up, reach your wrists, or work escapes, and if they try to roll you over, you end up belly-down — a position where the described sensation is the opponent's ankle feels like "it's going to come right off the lower leg."

---

## Key Takeaways

- Outside Ashi Garami's dead zone (inside hip) is fixable by **switching entanglement** rather than only rolling away from it.
- Two versions: **ankles crossed over the far hip** (good, floor-wedged) or **full triangle over the far hip** (better — opponent's limp attempt feeds directly into a tighter lock).
- Never leave a foot extended/exposed while setting this up — feet come back together first.
- Against a knowledgeable opponent actively trying to escape to your dead zone: lock the triangle/back-heel early, then finish via wrist-to-rib grip or by rolling them through to belly-down.
