# Half Position In Cross Ankle Locks — Quick Reference

Source: `7713668694114-05-01- Getting The Half Position In Cross Ankle Locks.mp4`

## Straight vs cross

- Straight: their left foot → your right armpit.
- Cross: their right foot → your right armpit.

## Half-foot still applies

Sole of foot in front of you, only the ball of the foot visible. Same goal as straight ankle locks.

## Why cross hits harder

Targets the **anterior talofibular ligament** (the "rolled ankle" ligament) — rolls the foot inward like a toe hold. Often stronger than a straight ankle lock.

## Cue check

You must be able to **see the ankle bone** when you first snatch it. Can't see it → not deep enough yet.

## Sweet spot

Same as straight ankle locks: too far behind = no pressure, too far forward = they limp out.

## Method 1: Hollow Body

1. Post — heel of hand outside their knee, or thumb under knee.
2. Push forward, hollow your body out → ankle bone becomes visible.
3. Cover grip.
4. Test: push-through fails, limp-out fails.

**Block the knee-down escape:** pass your leg over the top, put your knee **inside** theirs. Stops them turning the knee down to limp out.

## Method 2: Hip Heist (better vs strong resistance)

1. Take the leg, come down.
2. **Turn your hips toward the floor** — this is what exposes the heel, not pushing.
3. Elbow too tight = can't see heel → loosen slightly, flare elbow forward.
4. Lock in, connect feet, fall to finish.

Use this one when opponent locks their leg out hard and pushing alone won't expose the ankle.

## Finish mechanics

1. Tightness first.
2. Then rotate — knee across, elbow down, rotate toward the hip.
3. Higher elbow = less pressure. Keep it down.

## Benchmark

Reliable half-foot in BOTH straight and cross variants — that's the foundational skill everything else builds on.
