# Opponent Tries To Stand Up To A Knee To Get Foot To Floor: Short Ankle Lock

Source: `7713668694114-02-04- Opponent Tries To Stand Up To A Knee To Get Foot To Floor- Short Ankle Lock.mp4`

## Overview

A second counter for the "opponent stands up to a knee, drives foot to the mat" defensive reaction — this time for when the opponent's **knee turns inward**, which blocks the flat-back/butterfly approach from 02-03. Introduces the **short ankle lock**.

---

## Reading the defense

- Same underlying opponent goal as 02-03: get the sole of the foot flat and weighted on the mat to escape.
- The key branching variable: **which way the opponent's knee points.**
  - Knee turns to the **outside** → flat-back / butterfly Ashi approach (02-03) works well.
  - Knee turns to the **inside** → flat-back finish becomes difficult. Use the **short ankle lock** instead.

## The short ankle lock sequence

1. Post on the training partner's knee.
2. Lift your elbow just enough to bring your leg **short over the training partner's knee** (not long over the hip, which is the 02-03 approach).
3. Lock a **triangle**, hooking your foot inside the training partner's hip.
4. This traps the leg — when the opponent tries to extract the leg or pull your knee out of position, it holds firm.
5. Finish: take your (in this case) right elbow to your right hip as the opponent defends/pulls — the elbow "goes around the corner" for a strong finish.

## Keeping the foot off the mat (the detail often missed)

- You still need to prevent the opponent's foot from reaching the mat, but the mechanism is different depending on knee orientation:
  - **Knee turned inward:** keep your elbow closed and your knee in front — this physically blocks the foot from driving down, which is what lets you pass your leg short over the top into the triangle.
  - **Knee turned outward:** straighten your legs out — in this orientation, letting the opponent's knee reach the mat doesn't actually hurt you, and the brake/finish comes on regardless.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Branch point: opponent's knee **outward** → flat-back/butterfly (02-03). Opponent's knee **inward** → short ankle lock (this lesson).
- Short ankle lock = pass leg **short over the knee** (not long over the hip) → lock triangle, foot hooked inside opponent's hip → finish by driving your elbow to your own hip/around the corner.
- Still have to deny the foot reaching the mat, but the technique for doing so depends on knee orientation: elbow+knee block (knee in) vs. straightened legs (knee out, doesn't matter if the foot touches down).
