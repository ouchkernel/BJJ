# Counter To The Short Ankle Lock — Quick Reference

## Why ankle locks counter everything

No perfect grip needed — snatch elbow-deep, go straight into your attack. Fast entry = good counter tool.

## The threat: short ankle lock

Opponent passes foot over the top, triangles around your outside leg. Very tight once locked.

## The exploit

Their triangle-forming foot is close and exposed. Every lock leaves something exposed — find it.

## Counter sequence

1. Catch/snatch the exposed foot — grab with your **elbow**, not your hand (faster).
2. It feeds straight into half-foot position.
3. Connect hands.
4. Bring your other foot under their hip/butt for base.
5. Ear over your own shoulder, finish.

## Why they can't escape

Their own legs (locked around you) trap them — they can't slip the heel free even though your grip looks minor.

## Progression to drill

1. Static entry.
2. Opponent standing / on one knee → throws leg into short ankle lock.
3. If they hook their foot under your hips: pass the foot hand-to-hand, post right hand on floor, slide shin to your butt, fall toward them, finish.
4. Full speed live.

## Benchmark

You are stronger in this exchange than they are — their own lock traps them. Snatch the exposed foot fast, elbow-first, straight to half-foot.
