# One-Handed Ankle Locks — Quick Reference

Source: `7713668694114-01-10- Special Topic- One Handed Ankle Locks.mp4`

## The split

- **Two-handed / belly-up** → power comes from **hand height**. Hands drift down = power gone.
- **One-handed / belly-down** → power comes from **pinning the knee to the mat**. Hand height doesn't matter.

Don't mix these up — they're different tools with different rules.

## Belly-up fix

Connect your hands (grab own gi / other arm) so the gripping hand can't be peeled down. If hands aren't connected, opponent pulls them down and pressure disappears.

## Belly-down sequence

1. Take grip (one-handed is fine).
2. Come down to elbow or hand.
3. Pin opponent's knee to the mat — best case: trap it between your shin/ankle and floor ("shoelace" behind knee). Worst case: just sprawl hips down, still works.
4. Spread knees, straighten spine, head up.
5. Lower hips + higher head = more pressure.

## Posting rule

- Two hands on the lock → post on your **forehead**.
- One hand free → post on your **hand or elbow**, head off the mat.

## Don't do this

Let your gripping hand slide from your belly button down toward your hip. Power vanishes. Keep the hand hooked into your own abdomen.

## Why use one hand at all

- [ ] Frees a hand to hand-fight / intercept opponent's defense
- [ ] Works even without perfect half-foot position
- [ ] Works even if opponent turns toes in toward your spine (strong defense)
- [ ] Lets you rotate across opponent's body to a hand-fight-proof position

## When to reach for this

Opponent is hand-fighting hard, or you can't get the ideal half-foot grip → whip over (inside or outside, doesn't matter) into belly-down, one-handed finish.
