# Opponent Stands Up To 2 Feet: Back Sweep And Overhead Roll Dilemma

Source: `7713668694114-02-06- Opponent Stands Up To 2 Feet- Back Sweep And Overhead Roll Dilemma.mp4`

## Overview

Answers the obvious follow-up question from the previous "opponent stands to one knee" material: what if the opponent stands all the way up onto both feet? Answer: still very attackable — this lesson covers getting both ankles from bottom Ashi Garami, then a **weight-distribution dilemma** (backward knock-down vs. forward roll) that gives you an answer no matter how the opponent balances.

---

## Why sweeping one leg fails

If you only control one of the opponent's legs and try to sweep, a talented opponent hops and preserves balance. **The fix is controlling both legs.**

## Getting both ankles (entry)

1. From bottom Ashi Garami, hook your foot behind the opponent's knee to draw the near leg closer.
2. Grab behind both of the opponent's ankles with a **no-thumb grip** — like gripping a pull-up bar. This gives a strong pulling connection.
3. Alternative detail for reach problems: if the far ankle is out of reach, drop your own left foot to the mat, plant your right foot against/behind the opponent's near butt cheek, and use it to lift/bump the far foot within reach before grabbing both ankles no-thumb.

## The weight dilemma — core decision point

Once you have both ankles, **read (feel, don't look) where the opponent's weight is**:

| Opponent's weight is on... | Your response |
|---|---|
| **Heels** | Knock him straight backward onto his butt (pulling grip on the ankles) |
| **Toes** (leaning into you to prevent the backward knock-down) | Switch to a pushing grip (thumbs forward instead of back), plant a foot/shin in his stomach, and roll him forward over your shoulder into a valley-down finish |

This is described as a **haptic (feel) cue, not visual** — you don't see the weight distribution, you feel it through the push/pull connection.

### Backward finish (weight on heels)
1. Get both ankles, no-thumb grip.
2. Knock opponent backward to his butt.
3. Adjust to half-foot position as he lands.
4. Connect hands, throw inside leg over, finish (flat-back style).

### Forward/overhead finish (weight on toes, resists backward knock-down)
1. Get both ankles.
2. Switch grip: thumbs move from behind (pulling) to in front (pushing) of the ankles.
3. Get your near foot into a butterfly hook; bring your other foot in tight — plant it in the pit of the opponent's stomach (close, "Tomonage"-style placement) for a stronger lifting/driving base.
4. Bump/drive the opponent forward and over your shoulder (roll him over your head).
5. Land in a strong finishing position — valley-down.

## Key detail: foot placement close together

Two feet close together (butterfly hook + foot in stomach) generates significantly more lifting/driving force than a wide base — explicitly demonstrated as effective even with a lighter-framed practitioner against a bigger partner.

## Visual + haptic cue recap (tie-in to earlier lessons)

- Visual: ankle bone visible, no daylight around the elbow-deep grip on the Achilles/ankle.
- **Haptic** (the new element here): feeling whether weight is on heels vs. toes through the push/pull connection on the ankles — this cannot be seen, only felt.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Opponent standing to **both** feet is not a dead end — get both ankles (no-thumb grip) from bottom Ashi Garami.
- Core dilemma you create: **weight on heels → knock backward; weight on toes → switch to pushing grip and roll forward over the shoulder.**
- Reading weight distribution is a haptic skill — feel it, don't look for it.
- Keep your base feet close together (butterfly hook + foot in stomach) for maximum forward driving power.
- This gives you an answer regardless of opponent athleticism or defensive skill — one of two responses always applies.
