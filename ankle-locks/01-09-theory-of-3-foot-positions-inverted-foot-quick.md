# 3 Foot Positions & The Inverted Foot — Quick Reference

## The 3 positions (reference: line between opponent's rear delt and tricep/lat)

1. **Ideal** — big toe points at that line. Easiest finish, snaps like a twig.
2. **Half turn** — toes point at their own spine. Harder, still finishable (short ankle lock or belly down).
3. **Fully inverted** — toes point the opposite way. Hardest. Direct finish = nearly impossible.

Defender's best position = fully inverted. That's their goal when escaping.

## Don't panic on fully inverted — it's recoverable

Not a dead lock. There's a fix.

## The fix

1. Throw your **outside leg** over their leg.
2. Turn **with** them, don't fight the rotation.
3. Within 1–3 turns, their foot rotates back through half-turn toward ideal.
4. Once at half-turn or better → **finish belly down** (doesn't need perfect half-foot).
5. If recovery is clean, work back to full half-foot from belly down for an even stronger finish.

## Works regardless of

Body orientation — underneath in Ashi Garami or on top with them standing/spinning. Same fix both ways.

## Mindset

Skilled partners won't sit still for this. They'll turn hard to escape. Matching their rotation (not fighting it, not bailing) is what keeps the submission alive. This is THE realistic live-rolling scenario.

## Drill order

1. Ideal position → easy half-foot, feel the pressure.
2. Half turn → short ankle lock or leg-over finish.
3. Full invert → chase with outside leg, 1-3 turns, recover to half-turn, finish belly down.
