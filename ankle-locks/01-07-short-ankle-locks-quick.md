# Short Ankle Locks — Quick Reference

## What it is

Instead of controlling the hip, control the area right in front of the knee. More direct opposing force, faster break. Shorter lever though — easier for a good opponent to defend.

## Use as an addition, not your main tool

Fast submission or nothing — doesn't grind like hip control does. Skilled opponents defend it more often than hip control. Expect to play back and forth between the two.

## Core move

1. Butterfly Ashi on their leg.
2. Inside leg over their ankle/leg, fall to the side.
3. Fast, strong pressure — but only holds up short-term against resistance.

## Opponent seated, coming at you

1. Classic grip, half-foot as usual.
2. Right foot behind their knee to control distance.
3. Cast leg over the lock, over their knee.
4. Strong direct pressure, fast.

## Opponent posts up to a knee

1. Foot stays behind hamstring.
2. Leg over the top, lock closed triangle, foot inside their hip.
3. **Works without half-foot** — big advantage here.
4. Second leg over too, quick triangle, foot behind head/leg.

## The hip-height rule

| Opponent's hips | Loop type |
|---|---|
| On the floor | Open loop around the knee (no triangle) |
| Off the floor | Closed triangle around the knee |

Why: hips-down = floor already constrains him, open loop is enough. Hips-up = he can rip a knee free from an open loop with both hands, so lock the triangle.

## Knee direction cue

- Knee outward + half-foot → finish over the top as usual.
- Knee inward, opponent posting up → triangle-the-knee finish, no half-foot needed.

## The real skill: know when to bail

1. Get grip, butterfly.
2. Opponent doesn't defend the knee → throw leg over, finish.
3. Opponent defends by pulling knee up short → **switch back to hip-control finishes from earlier lessons.**

## Don't

Treat this as your go-to. Good opponents will open the knee to kill it — you need hip control as the fallback every time.
