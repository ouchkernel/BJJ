# Ankle Lock As A Counter To A Leg Lace — Part 2

Source: `7713668694114-03-03- Ankle Lock As A Counter To A Leg Lace - Part 2.mp4`

## Overview

Continuation of countering an opponent's leg lock attempts with your own ankle lock. This lesson covers the case where the opponent hasn't fully laced your legs but is instead going directly after your heel (heel hook attempt), and how good defensive foot positioning creates the opening for a counter ankle lock.

---

## Defensive foot position (the foundation)

Whenever an opponent is hunting your ankle/heel:
- **Toes point away from their armpit.**
- **Heel stays close to your own hip**, ideally down near the mat.

This is explicitly the opposite of what makes you easy to heel hook: the closer your foot is to their armpit with toes pointing into it, the less work they have to do to finish you. Good defensive position buys time — it takes the opponent longer to work into your heel.

## How the counter develops

1. Opponent digs for your heel/ankle — because of good defensive foot position, this takes them time.
2. In that window, grab your **own ankle** and pass it into the gap inside your training partner's knee, getting a hold of their knee.
3. Pull up to get a hold of your training partner's ankle.
4. Close a loop around your training partner's hip; hand on their knee.
5. **Hollow your body out** to get the half-foot position.
6. Close up your arms/grips as usual.

### Defensive cross-ankle detail
- Once you've captured a leg and are locking your own feet together for base/control, **cross ankles with the outside leg on top**. If you cross naively (inside leg on top), the opponent can separate your feet and create problems.

## Grip refinement

- Basic connected grip is "adequate," but a stronger connection: roll your wrists over each other so they connect more tightly, pulling your elbow closer to the opponent's leg — this closes any remaining daylight around the ankle for a tighter lock.

## Drilled Sequence (from rolling)

1. Partner attacks — defend by pointing toes away from armpit, heel toward hip.
2. When opponent is occupied trying to get inside foot position, drag their leg up and lock it (capture their ankle).
3. Cross your own ankles (outside leg on top) for safety before finishing.
4. Move to half-foot position.
5. Connect arms/hands (roll wrists over each other for the tighter grip if daylight remains).
6. Finish.

## Key detail: "double trouble"

Once you've bound up both of the opponent's legs (your ankles crossed around them + your ankle lock grip), the opponent is in "double trouble" — even inverting doesn't help them escape, because both legs are controlled simultaneously.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Good heel-hook defense (toes away from armpit, heel to hip) is also the setup for a counter ankle lock — it buys the time needed to attack back.
- While the opponent digs for your heel, capture their ankle/knee and build the half-foot position.
- Always cross your own ankles **outside leg on top** for defensive safety once you've got a lock on their leg.
- Roll your wrists over each other to eliminate daylight and tighten the final grip.
- Controlling both of the opponent's legs simultaneously ("double trouble") removes their escape options, including inverting.
