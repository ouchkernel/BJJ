# The Best Starting Move In Your Ankle Lock Decision Tree: Half Foot To Outside Hip

## Overview

First entry in the ankle lock decision tree series. Once you have a good ankle lock, opponents go into a limited, predictable set of defensive reactions — the decision tree exists to give you a correct response for each. This lesson establishes the **default starting option**: half foot grip, then fall to the outside.

---

## The default sequence

1. Opponent seated in front of you.
2. Get an Achilles/holding grip on the leg.
3. Hollow your body out and convert to the **half foot position** (transition covered in 01-03).
4. Fall to the **outside** (as a general rule, outside finishes are easier and make it easier to maintain half foot than falling inside).
5. Pass your outside foot over the training partner's leg.
6. Put your foot underneath the training partner's hamstring, pass the inside leg over the top.
7. Fall down to an outside hip and outside shoulder — this is the finishing position.

## Why outside, and why this sequence

- Going from a bare holding grip (no half foot) to half foot creates an "astronomical" difference in stress on the ankle — tension in the anterior ligaments and ankle joint capsule is felt immediately once half foot is achieved.
- Falling to the outside is generally the **easier of the two options** (vs. falling inside) and helps maintain the half foot position through the finish.
- This sequence is described as **strong and efficient across a wide array of opponents** — not a niche counter, but the default/preferred starting move whenever you initiate an ankle lock attack.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Default starting option for any ankle lock attack:** get half foot, then fall to the outside hip/shoulder.
- Outside finishes > inside finishes as a general rule — easier to execute and easier to maintain half foot through the finish.
- This is a decision-tree entry point — later lessons cover what to do when the opponent prevents this default option.
