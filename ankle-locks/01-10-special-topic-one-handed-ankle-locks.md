# Special Topic: One-Handed Ankle Locks

## Overview

One-handed ankle locks almost always end up belly-down. This lesson covers why two-handed grips need high hands to work belly-up, why one-handed grips work on a completely different mechanic (knee-pinning, not hand height), and why they're a valuable tactical option — especially against a good hand-fighter or when you can't get the ideal foot position.

---

## Belly-up mechanics: hand height is everything

- In belly-up (chest-up) situations, breaking pressure comes from your upper body grip **combined with pinning the opponent's hip on the opposite side**.
- The higher your hands stay, the stronger the force delivered into the ankle.
- **Biggest way to ruin a belly-up ankle lock:** letting your hands drift downward as you arch back into the lock. The lower the hands go, the less pressure the opponent feels — power bleeds straight out of the lock.
- Fix for two-handed grips: connect your hands (grab your own gi, or your other arm/wrist in no-gi) so one hand can't be pulled down even under resistance.
- This is why one-handed locks generally **don't work well belly-up** — nothing anchors the hand at height once the opponent works to peel it down.

## Belly-down mechanics: a different tool entirely

- In belly-down situations, the pin isn't at the hip — **it's at the knee**. The opponent's knee gets pinned to the floor, and the mat itself supplies the opposing force.
- Because the floor provides the counter-force, hand height stops mattering. Instead:
  1. Take a one-handed (or classic) ankle lock grip.
  2. Come down to an elbow (or hand).
  3. Pin the opponent's knee to the mat — ideally trap it between your ankle/shin and the floor ("shoelace" behind the knee) for the strongest version; a simple sprawl with hips down still works even without a direct knee pin.
  4. Spread your knees, straighten your spine, bring your head up.
  5. The lower your hips and the higher your head rise, the stronger the pressure.

### Posting rules
- **Two-handed grip, belly-down:** post on your forehead (hands are both committed to the lock).
- **One-handed grip, belly-down:** post on your hand or elbow instead (you have a hand free) — take your head off the mat and let the elbow/hand be the post.
- Looking back at the opponent bleeds power **unless** your hands are connected — for a one-handed finish, keep your eyes away from the opponent and focus on straightening your spine.

### The critical hand rule for one-handed belly-down locks
- The gripping hand must stay hooked into your own **abdomen/belly button** — not slide down toward your hip.
- If the hand slides from stomach down to hip, pressure disappears almost entirely (demonstrated live: hand on inner thigh = "hopeless," hand hooked into own belly button = immediate tap).

---

## Why choose a one-handed grip at all

1. **Frees a hand to hand-fight.** Two hands committed to the leg = opponent can freely attack your grip with no resistance. One hand on the ankle, one hand free = you can intercept every attempt to peel your gripping hand down your body, control the opponent's other leg, or start rotating across their body toward the far side (where they lose hand-fighting access entirely).
2. **Doesn't require a perfect grip/foot position.** Even with the whole sole of the foot visible (i.e., no half-foot achieved) or the toes turned in toward your own spine (a strong defensive foot position), the sheer strength of pinning the knee to the mat can still get the tap — situations that wouldn't work belly-up.
3. **Good response when your opponent is turning toes in toward your spine** (a strong defense against half-foot) or actively hand-fighting — this is exactly when to whip over (inside or outside, doesn't matter which) into a belly-down, one-handed finish.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Two-handed/belly-up ankle locks live or die on **hand height** — connect your hands so they can't be pulled down.
- One-handed/belly-down ankle locks live or die on **pinning the knee to the mat**, not hand height — the floor supplies the counter-force.
- Posting: two hands committed → post on forehead. One hand free → post on hand/elbow.
- The free hand must stay hooked into your own belly button; letting it slide to your hip kills the pressure.
- One-handed belly-down finishes are a great fallback specifically when: opponent hand-fights hard, or you can't get an ideal half-foot grip (toes turned in, foot pushed through) — the power of the knee pin compensates for an imperfect foot position.
