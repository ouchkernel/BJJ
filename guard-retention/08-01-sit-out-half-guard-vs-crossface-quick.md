# Sit-Out vs Crossface — Quick Reference

## The golden rule (memorize this)

- Opponent on **two knees / prone** → control his **hip**.
- Opponent **sits out** → control switches 100% to his **head**. Hip is now unreachable — don't chase it.

## Do this, in order

1. While he's on two knees: knees pinched high around his hip, never let his knee outrank your hip.
2. He sits out and crossfaces (shoulder on your jaw) → drop hip-chasing immediately.
3. Thumb-post into his armpit(s) — lifts his shoulder off your jawline.
4. Slide your forehead/temple inside his armpit ("turtle head into shell").
5. Once your temple is in — turn freely into him, any knee/elbow escape is back on the table, guard restored.

## Why this works

- Shoulder-on-jaw = short lever, strong control.
- Temple-in-armpit = you're free. "Astronomical" difference between the two.

## Don't

Keep fighting for his hip after he's sat out. It's gone — he'll just hand-fight your hip and step out to pass while you're stuck chasing.

## Note

No-gi crossface = easiest of the 3 sit-out variants (reverse crossface and reverse underhook, covered next, are tougher). With a gi + lapel-reinforced crossface, this would be the *hardest* — but that's not this context.
