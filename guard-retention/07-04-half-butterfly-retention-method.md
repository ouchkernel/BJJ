# Half Butterfly Retention Method

## Overview

Deals with a half guard passer who has top head-and-arm control (underhook + crossface, hands locked) and lifts his knee off the floor — the trigger to transition from half guard into half butterfly guard. Covers the two ways this transition gets exploited if done carelessly, and how to do it safely.

---

## The setup problem

- Opponent is chest-to-chest, top head and arm (underhook + crossface, hands locked), and lifts his knee off the mat.
- **First danger:** if your leg is passive and his knee gets higher than your hips, he can drive the knee either direction to pass. You must keep your top leg on top of his quad (outside his thigh) so your hips rise with his knee — never let his knee gain a height advantage over your hips.
- **Second danger (the half-butterfly transition itself):** once you try to switch to half butterfly, if you break contact with his leg while pommeling your hook in, he can simply backstep and kick free — the attempted retention move actually hands him the pass.

## Building the half butterfly safely

1. Keep your top leg pinching around his upper leg (like a vice) — this holds his leg in place even while you move.
2. Maintain a back-heel (tension) against the back of his leg throughout — never fully release contact while pommeling.
3. Loop your hook out and pommel your foot in while maintaining that contact the whole time.

## The knee-direction problem (second layer)

Once half butterfly is achieved, if the opponent locks his top head-and-arm and tries to kick his leg free to the cross-side, you often can't follow — **because your knee is pointing the same direction as his intended pass.**

Fix:
1. Point your knee **against** the direction of his pass (if he's passing to his left, your knee points right).
2. Achieve this by pushing your legs over, or posting on the floor and switching your hips.
3. Your hook becomes a "sticky hook" on the back of his leg, and your foot/knee now oppose his kick-out direction — so when he tries to free his leg, you follow it instead of losing contact.
4. From there, actively move his hips away from the side he needs for his pass (lift him that direction), bring your other knee in, and you're back in guard.

## Full drilled sequence

1. Opponent gets chest-to-chest, top head and arm, knee lifts off the mat.
2. Keep top leg on his quad/outer thigh so hips match his knee height (never lackadaisical).
3. Hug your knee to his hip so when he tries to get up, your upper leg stays on top of his — his knee never emerges above your hips.
4. Move your hips out to the side and pommel your foot in without losing leg contact.
5. Check knee direction: if it's pointing the same way as his pass, swivel your hips underneath his — hook onto his leg with a sticky hook pointing against his pass direction.
6. Use that hook to move his body in the opposite direction of his pass, bring legs back in front, guard restored.

---

## Common Mistakes

- Letting your leg go passive when his knee lifts off the floor — his knee immediately gains a height advantage over your hips.
- Pommeling your butterfly hook in without maintaining constant leg contact — he backsteps and passes.
- Leaving your knee pointed in the same direction as his intended pass — you lose the ability to follow when he kicks his leg free.

## Decision Points

- **Opponent's knee comes off the floor in half guard** → top leg goes on his quad/outer thigh immediately, hips match his knee height.
- **Transitioning to half butterfly** → maintain constant leg contact (pinch + back-heel) throughout, never let go before the hook is set.
- **Opponent locks head-and-arm and tries to kick his leg free** → check your knee direction; if it matches his pass direction, swivel hips to point your knee (and sticky hook) against it.

## Terminology Introduced

- **Half butterfly guard** — a transition from half guard where one leg becomes a butterfly hook, used specifically as a response to an opponent freeing his knee off the mat.
- **Sticky hook** — a hook oriented against the opponent's kick-out direction so it follows his leg instead of losing contact.
