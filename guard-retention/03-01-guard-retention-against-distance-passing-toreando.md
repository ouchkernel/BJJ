# Guard Retention Against Distance Passing (Toreando)

## Overview

First-contact principles for when a standing opponent approaches a supine you and begins a distance/Toreando-style pass. The goal here isn't a specific escape yet — it's winning the initial grip fight and stance so you're not already behind before the pass even starts.

---

## The core problem: top hand vs. top foot position

- If the soles of your feet point straight down at the mat, it's easy for your opponent to get **top hand position** — his hands on top of your feet, pinning them down so you can't use them.
- Goal instead: **top foot position** — point the sole of your foot at the opponent's centerline/sternum, and get the **blade of your foot inside his wrist** before he grips. Now your foot is on top of his hand, not the reverse, and it's very difficult for him to push your foot down to your buttocks or throw your legs around.

### Foot setup
- Don't keep your two feet symmetrical or next to each other — an opponent can grab both together and throw them to one side easily.
- Keep feet **asymmetrical** (one higher, one lower) and toes pointing in **different directions**. This way he can only ever control one foot at a time, and the other is free to compensate.
- As he moves to an angle on one side, you can follow him with the working foot; if he switches sides, follow again.

## Denying him the choice of side

- If you present square/neutral to your opponent, he can fake one way and pass the other — you have no way to predict it.
- Instead, **present one side of your body to him** immediately once he gets a grip. If he wants to run all the way to the other side, it takes long enough that you can simply re-present the other side before he arrives.
- This lets you commit 100% of your defensive frames to one side at a time, since you know the other side isn't realistically reachable in the time available.
- Keep elbows and knees close together (tight stance) throughout — this is what makes single-side defense actually work.

## Putting it together

1. Opponent approaches — you present sole of foot toward his sternum, feet asymmetrical, toes pointing different directions.
2. On contact, pommel your foot inside and over his wrist (top foot position), denying him top hand position.
3. Present one side of your body to him — this forces him into a single, committed angle of attack you can see coming.
4. Keep elbows and knees tight, frame at the upper body as needed.
5. From here you can hold him off for extended periods and are set up to use the specific retention methods (high leg, inside leg, inside position) covered next.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Top foot position beats top hand position** — get the blade of your foot inside his wrist before he grips, not after.
- Keep feet **asymmetrical**, toes pointing in different directions — never let him control both feet with one motion.
- **Present one side of your body** so you always know which side he's committing to; the far side is too slow for him to reach in time.
- Elbows and knees tight together throughout — this is the stance that makes single-side defense work at all.
