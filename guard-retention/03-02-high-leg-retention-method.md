# High Leg Retention Method

## Overview

The first classical guard retention method covered in depth: using a thumb post at the opponent's elbow plus a high outside leg over his shoulder to correct your body angle and re-establish connection when he's cleared your legs and started moving to an angle.

---

## The thumb post

- As the opponent comes in and works to an angle, place a **thumb post inside his lead elbow** — the lead arm is the one closest to your head and the one that will eventually control your head if he pins you.
- This is directly analogous to controlling someone in a standing collar-tie: thumb inside the elbow means he can never reach your head or your legs without first working through that post.
- With the post in place, when he tries to manipulate your knee it becomes very difficult — he can't transfer to your head, and he can't get to your leg either.

## The high leg movement

1. As he works to get an angle and starts to pass, take your **outside leg**, bring it high over his body, and make contact with the **sole of your foot at his shoulder**.
2. Use that foot-to-shoulder contact to drag your entire body around in the opposite direction, squaring back up to him.
3. Bring your legs back in and make effective connection to his legs — this puts him in a position where he must react to you instead of continuing his pass.

## Defensive cycle → offensive cycle

- The moment he clears your legs, you are **100% defensive** — thumb post goes in immediately.
- High leg over, correct your body, and rather than settling for neutral position, actively create contact with his legs.
- Once you have that connection, you've flipped from defense to offense — he's now reacting to you.
- If you stop at "neutral" (corrected position but no connection), a good opponent will simply throw your base to the other side and resume passing from his offensive sequence — don't stop short of making contact.

## Drilling notes

- Practice both sides — you never know which side he'll pass toward, so the thumb post + high leg has to work off either arm/leg.
- The thumb "can only hold for so long," so the sequence relies on quickly using the strength of your legs (the high leg movement) to realign — don't expect the thumb post alone to hold indefinitely.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Thumb post inside the lead elbow** is the most efficient way to deny an opponent access to your head or leg — same principle as standing collar-tie control.
- **High leg to the shoulder** is what actually corrects your angle and squares you back up, not the thumb alone.
- Don't stop at "neutral" after re-squaring — actively connect to his legs to flip from defensive cycle to offensive cycle.
- Practice on both sides; the opponent's passing side is unpredictable.
