# Double Scoop Grips: Working Against Unlocked Hands

## Overview

The companion counter to the locked-hands double scoop lesson. More technically skilled opponents tend to avoid locking their hands under your legs — instead they go around with one arm and control the **calf muscle** of the opposite leg with the other hand. This requires a different counter than the locked-hands case.

---

## The sequence

1. Opponent comes around with one arm underneath your leg, controlling your body with unlocked hands (one arm wrapped, other hand gripping the calf).
2. Instead of targeting the wrist (as in the locked-hands version), **target and expose the elbow**.
3. Lift your hips off the floor and place the **heel of your hand behind his elbow joint**.
4. As he pushes into you, swing that leg **like a pendulum** — this rolls the opponent's elbow over and brings you up onto a hand.
5. This action exposes the opponent's back and makes it easy to bring your feet to your buttocks and scoot away.
6. Use the same basic scooting motion from earlier solo drills in the course to re-establish distance and return to open guard.

## Second application (both legs)

1. Straighten the leg and lift hips to expose the elbow to your hand.
2. Pendulum the second leg — this pushes the opponent away.
3. As he tries to turn and face you, create distance.
4. You're now in a strong position to hold him off.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Locked hands → attack the **wrist**. Unlocked hands (one arm wrapped, one hand on the calf) → attack the **elbow** instead — different grip, different target.
- Elbow attack mechanic: heel of hand behind the elbow joint + a pendulum leg swing rolls the opponent's arm/elbow over.
- Rolling the elbow exposes the opponent's back, which is what makes the scoot-to-open-guard escape possible.
- This reuses the basic scooting motion taught in earlier solo drills — worth having that motion drilled independently.
