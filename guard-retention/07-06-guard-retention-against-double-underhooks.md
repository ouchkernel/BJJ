# Guard Retention Against Double Underhooks

## Overview

Described as arguably the hardest guard retention problem in the whole system: opponent has **underhooks on both sides**, head and shoulders completely pinned, hip mobility severely limited. The upper body battle is already lost — the entire solution is winning the lower body battle by targeting the hip, not the knee.

---

## Accept the upper body loss

- With double underhooks, your elbows are jacked to the sides and the upper body battle is 100% lost — it's "not coming back." You cannot pommel your hands free.
- Therefore the whole fight has to be won with your legs/hips.

## The core principle (same golden rule as near-side underhook, doubled down)

- Focus knee control on his **hip**, not his knee.
- Do **not** try to lock your legs in a tight triangle/figure-four or cross your ankles — it doesn't help. If he gets a foot inside and his knee gets higher than your hips, "it's done" regardless of how tightly your legs are locked. Even a scorpion lock doesn't help — freeing the foot from a scorpion actually facilitates his pass.
- **What actually works:** put your foot on top of his calf muscle (controls his lower leg) AND pinch your knees at his hip (controls his upper leg). Don't lock as tight as possible or you'll exhaust yourself — a firm grip is enough.

## Why the foot-on-calf detail matters

- With just the knee pinch and no foot control of his lower leg, he can free his leg because he can still move his lower leg.
- Foot on calf + knee pinch at the hip together control both leg segments.

## The pommel-and-move sequence

1. With foot-on-calf + knee-pinch established, work to get your foot **inside**.
2. You don't need to move him far — the target distance is small: your knee moves from behind his butt cheek to inside his knee — "the width of your knee."
3. Put both hands on his lats (even with double underhooks locked, you can still reach his lats).
4. Wrestle/pinch to move him just that small distance, slip your knee back inside.
5. Once one knee is back inside, you can move him around freely — the double underhooks stop mattering.
6. Use a butterfly hook to pommel the foot inside and complete the transition; once two knees are in front of his hips, push and break his grips.

---

## Common Mistakes

- Trying to control his **knee** instead of his hip — losing battle, leads to mount or cross-side passes.
- Locking legs as tightly as possible (crossed ankles, figure-four triangle, scorpion) — none of it helps once he frees a foot and gets his knee above your hips; a scorpion in particular backfires and facilitates his pass.
- Exhausting yourself trying to hold a maximally tight knee pinch instead of a firm-but-sustainable one.
- Only pinching with the knees and neglecting foot control of his lower leg — he escapes because his lower leg is still free to move.

## Decision Points

- **Double underhooks locked, head/shoulders fully pinned** → stop fighting upper body, shift 100% focus to hip control with the legs.
- **Establishing leg control** → foot on his calf (lower leg) + knee pinch at his hip (upper leg), together, not knee-pinch alone.
- **Working to reverse the position** → don't try to move him far; the needed distance is only the width of your knee, from behind his butt cheek to inside his knee.
- **Getting hands active despite double underhooks** → hands still reach his lats even with both underhooks locked — use them to help wrestle the small distance needed.

## Terminology Introduced

- **Double underhooks** — opponent has underhooks on both sides simultaneously; considered the single hardest control to retain guard against in this system.
- **Foot on calf + knee pinch at hip** — the two-part lower-body control that substitutes for the lost upper-body battle.
