# Late Defense Against Body Lock Passes: Chin Post And Shrimp

## Overview

A "worst case, it's already happened" response: your opponent got a full, deep body lock (long arm/short arm, knees pinched tight) despite your best efforts. This lesson covers the first late-defense tool — attacking his head position with a chin/jaw post, then shrimping the grip apart.

---

## Why head position matters

- Body locking depends on the opponent keeping his head in a **strong, low, driving position** — ear/head jammed into your chest and shoulders.
- Take his head **up and away from your chest** and the whole position weakens — this sets up a strong shrimp.

## Safety note on hand placement

- **Keep fingers together, use the heels of your hands** to push on the head/face — never let fingers or thumbs stray into the eyes. This is repeatedly emphasized: careless finger placement here is how people injure training partners and run out of partners to train with.

## The sequence

1. Don't panic, but don't stall either — opponent will start walking over your knees the longer you wait.
2. Get both hands to the opponent's head, heels of hands doing the pushing (not fingers).
3. Lift your hips slightly and work your hands underneath/up along the head.
4. Slide hand down to the **chin**. If you can't reach the chin, go for the **jaw**. If not the jaw, at minimum get the **ear**.
5. Push the head away (to the side) while **shrimping your hips in the opposite direction simultaneously** — this is what actually breaks the lock; the opposing push/shrimp motion pulls his hands apart.
6. As the grip breaks and you create space, bring a knee inside — either the **top knee or bottom knee** work (top knee is the stated general preference).
7. Continue the shimmy/pulse motion outward until you're fully out to the side.

## Finishing options once free

Any of these three re-establishes control and shuts down the body lock:
- Closed guard
- Knee inside (top or bottom knee)
- Omoplata

## Key detail on grip defense

Keep active, "sticky" hooks with your feet at all times rather than relaxed/passive legs — passive legs let the opponent step over them freely while you're working the head-control sequence.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Body lock passing depends on the opponent's head staying jammed low into your chest — removing that head position is the key that unlocks the shrimp.
- Always push with the **heel of the hand**, fingers together, never near the eyes.
- Priority order for hand placement: chin → jaw → ear (take whatever's available).
- The finishing mechanic is a **simultaneous push-the-head / shrimp-the-opposite-way** action, not sequential.
- End state options: closed guard, knee-in, or omoplata — all three shut the body lock down.
