# North-South Toreando Leg Frames — Quick Reference

## Why north-south is dangerous

- Passer's effort: front-of-hips (easy, like decline bench) → knee line (moderate, like bench press) → north-south (hard, like shoulder press — you always lift less overhead).
- Your workload roughly doubles once they reach north-south. It becomes a fatigue race that favors them.
- Stop the transition. Don't just defend after they arrive.

## Do this as they start walking around

1. Watch the stages: in front of you (safe) → angle forming (danger starting) → fully around (high danger).
2. The moment they start the walk: frame their **lead leg**, below the knee, solid grip.
3. That hand normally tracks their lead arm — now it can't. Compensate: other hand goes to a **thumb post on their elbow**.
4. Now you're connected to both leg (blocks the walk) and elbow (blocks head control).

## If they abandon and drop to knees

Get hands back in place, re-establish top knee + feet up front, standard inside position.

## Mindset

They'll get north-south sometimes if they're good. Fine — just don't hand it to them for free.
