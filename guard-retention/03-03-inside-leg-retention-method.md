# Inside Leg Retention Method

## Overview

Second classical retention method, used specifically when the opponent's control of your outside leg is too strong to use the high leg method. Instead of going over the top, you hook with your inside leg to switch him from the side he intended to pass on to the opposite side.

---

## The mechanism

- When your opponent's grip/control on your outside leg is too dominant to high-leg over, use the **inside leg** instead.
- Even with a hand on your knee, a **thumb post** on the training partner lets you get the sole of your foot onto his lead leg at the thigh — this forms an effective hook.
- From that hook, pull your whole body across to the **other side**, switching your opponent from the side he was passing toward to the opposite side.

## Why switching sides works

- A passer is making continuous second-by-second calibrations based on which side he believes he's passing toward. Every grip, angle, and weight shift is committed to that side.
- If you switch him to the opposite side, all of those calculations become invalid — he has to stop, recalibrate, and start his passing sequence over from scratch.
- Analogy used: a boxer switching from orthodox to southpaw stance mid-exchange — everything about the opponent's approach (punch selection, angle, exit) has to change instantly.

## Drilled sequence

1. Opponent passes toward a given side, controlling your outside leg with hand position — you can't use the high leg method here.
2. Bring your **inside leg** in, make contact with your shoelaces on his thigh — this is the hook.
3. Use the hook to drag your body back across the centerline, presenting the **opposite** side of your body to him.
4. He now has to re-figure the entire pass — this window gives you the opportunity to make effective contact with his legs and put him into a defensive/reactive cycle.
5. Practice both directions — same idea works whichever side he initially commits to.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Use inside leg specifically **when outside leg control is too strong** for the high leg method — it's the answer when high leg is shut down.
- The **thumb post** is what lets you still get your foot to his thigh even while he controls your knee.
- Switching your opponent's committed side forces a full recalibration of his pass — like a boxer switching stances mid-fight.
- The goal isn't just escaping — it's using the disruption window to go on the offensive.
