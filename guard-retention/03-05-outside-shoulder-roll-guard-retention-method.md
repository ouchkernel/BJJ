# Outside Shoulder Roll Guard Retention Method

## Overview

A retention method for the more dangerous stage of a Toreando-style pass: the opponent has already gotten contact, distance, and angle, and is now level-changing down toward chest-to-chest. This is "heavier artillery" than the earlier framing methods — a rolling movement that puts you back in guard using leg strength and body momentum rather than arm strength.

---

## Why this stage is more dangerous

- Earlier stages (contact, distance, angle) are handled with frames — thumb post, high leg, inside leg, inside position.
- Once the opponent is level-changing down toward chest-to-chest, those frames alone aren't enough — you need a bigger, structurally efficient movement to reset.

## Setup: knee-elbow connection, whichever side is available

- The standard goal is knee-elbow connection on the **near side** of your body. If the opponent clears your near-side knee, establish knee-elbow connection on the **far/outside side** instead — don't fight for a connection that's already lost, switch sides.
- Stop his elbow specifically, since that's what would otherwise control your head.
- Bring your outside knee through to form the knee-elbow connection.

## The roll mechanics

1. Pass your hand through **underneath his armpit**.
2. Push your foot into the mat and come up into a **knee prop** (same motion as the solo knee-prop drill).
3. With knee-elbow connection plus the knee prop, you now have a knee shield on one leg and a cross frame on the other arm — enough structure to sustain his weight without collapsing.
4. Push off the floor with that planted foot and **roll all the way through** — this returns you to guard.
5. Reset: knees and elbows together, elbows outside knees, ready to counter-attack.

## Why this method specifically

- **Mechanically efficient** — built around inside position, not raw strength.
- **Minimal back exposure** compared to other resets that involve turning away.
- Works well against bigger, stronger opponents specifically because it uses full-body rolling momentum, which is very hard for someone to stop compared to resisting an isolated frame.
- **Drillable in isolation** — the same footwork (foot down → knee prop → roll through) can be repeated every class as a standalone movement, building the base skill quickly, then applied live.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Use this once the opponent has cleared frames and is closing to chest-to-chest — it's a step up in urgency from the earlier framing-only methods.
- Establish knee-elbow connection on **whichever side is still available**, not rigidly the near side.
- Core mechanics: hand under the armpit → foot to the mat → knee prop → roll through, using leg drive rather than arm strength.
- Low back exposure, high effectiveness against bigger/stronger opponents, and easy to drill daily as an isolated movement.
