# Negating Body Lock Guard Passing — Quick Reference

## 3 things the opponent needs for a body lock (deny any one)

1. Connected hands (locked around your waist)
2. Elbows controlling outside of your hips
3. Head below your shoulder line — **if their chin stays on your shoulder, you can always counter with a sweep**

## Base stance (do this by default)

- Elbows close to your own sides
- Head in front of opponent's head

This alone forces them to work past you and expose their wrists.

## Negation toolkit — cycle through these

1. **Hand control → knee post**: grip one hand (palm-to-palm / inside wrist / feed-a-wrist-for-2-on-1) just long enough to plant your knee in front of their shoulder. Then release the hand — the knee alone kills the lock.
2. **Elbow/thumb post**: thumb inside their elbow. Stay open like a scarecrow — they have to beat the post first, and retreating from it gives you an off-balancing chance.
3. **Feet on the hips**: if hand control keeps failing, put a foot on their hip. Foot on the head also works — forces them to remove it, usually trading you wrist control.
4. **Cross shoulder post**: one hand controls both their shoulders + head. Frees your other hand for anything. Single best method if you only get one.
5. **Side neck post**: V-grip on the side of their neck. Blocks head-below-shoulder. Easier to get than cross-shoulder, not as strong. Usable from supine on a sit-up too.

## Critical danger zone

Sitting up from supine into range **without** a frame already in place = instant flattening. Always come up with a hand across (cross frame) or another tool already active.

## Mindset

No single tool stops a good body-locker. Combine them — as they adapt to one, switch to the next.
