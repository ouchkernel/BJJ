# Leg Pommeling Vs Double Scoops

## Overview

A more general recovery method against double scoops (locked or unlocked), focused on repositioning your legs so the opponent can't lift/stack you, then pommeling your feet back to a controlling position on his hips.

---

## Why positioning matters first

- If your hips line up directly on the opponent's centerline, he can bring your knees to your chest and **stack** you easily.
- If the opponent gets your leg all the way up onto his shoulder, his hand can cross your centerline and he starts passing — this is the position he wants.

## The sequence

1. Opponent scoops underneath both legs. First priority: **find an angle** relative to him — don't stay lined up on his centerline.
2. **Move your legs off his shoulders/trapezius and down to his biceps/elbows.** This makes your legs feel heavier to him — much harder to lift and stack you. Also keep your **spine straight** (a rounded spine is what makes stacking easy).
3. As he tries to lift/move you, **turn your hips offline** rather than staying square to him.
4. From this angled position, **pommel your feet inside** — bring a foot inside his bicep/shoulder area. Either foot works.
5. Once a foot is pommeled in, it becomes very difficult for him to lift that leg.
6. Get feet back to his hips and you're back in your standard guard game.

## Second application (isolating the mechanism)

1. Priority #1: prevent the leg from getting all the way up onto his shoulder (which lets his hand cross the centerline).
2. Bring hamstrings down to his elbows/biceps immediately.
3. Turn an angle, get a foot inside his shoulder/bicep.
4. Lifting that leg is now much harder for him.
5. Pommel the legs back to inside position, feet on hips, resume your guard game.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Two things happen together to defeat the stack: get an **angle** off his centerline, and get your **legs down to his elbows/biceps** (not up on his shoulders) with a **straight spine**.
- Heavy legs beat light legs here — biceps-level leg placement plus straight spine is what makes your legs "heavy" and hard to lift.
- The recovery move itself is **pommeling a foot inside** the opponent's bicep/shoulder area — either leg works.
- Preventing the leg from reaching his shoulder in the first place denies him the centerline hand-cross that drives his pass.
