# Guard Retention Against A Near Side Underhook

## Overview

One of the hardest half guard retention problems: opponent gets chest-to-chest with a **near side underhook** (as opposed to the far-side underhook + crossface covered previously). Described as one of the strongest passing positions in the whole system — a genuinely difficult mission, but recoverable with the right focus (hip control, not knee control).

---

## What a near side underhook is and why it's dangerous

- Opponent has half guard top, with an underhook on the **near** side (not the far side as in the previous crossface scenario).
- He puts his head on the floor on that side, using the underhook to jam his shoulder into your jaw — a very effective crossface, because his head jams your head, pinning it between his head and his near-side underhook.
- With head and shoulders totally pinned, he then works his hand onto your knees and walks them out, sometimes using his own shoelace on the inside of your thigh to help. He dominates the space between your knees and can pass to mount, cross-side, wherever he wants.

## The core principle: control the hip, not the knee

- **Hands:** Place both hands on his lats (lap muscles), elbow jammed low to your side (limits how deep he can crossface).
- **Biggest problem:** if your hips stay inactive on the ground and his knee comes up (often via his hand pushing your knee), his knee gets higher than your hips and dominates inside position — from here he passes almost every time.
- **Fix:** the instant you see his knee rising, pinch your two knees around **his hip** (not his knee). This denies him the height advantage regardless of what his hand does to your knee.
- If he puts a hand on your knee to push it down, **peel the hand off** — don't tolerate it. This is safe here because you're controlling his other arm with an overhook, so he has no easy kimura option.

## Building the escape

1. Never let his knee get higher than your hips — pinch knees around his hip the moment his knee lifts.
2. Get a butterfly hook, but do it correctly: stand your foot on his calf muscle first (not just a lazy hook), then pinch knees together. This resists both his kick-out attempt and his knee-push attempt.
3. Pommel your foot inside. As soon as that foot goes in, check your knee — it should move from being **behind** his butt cheek to being **in front of** his hip.
4. Put both hands on his lats, lift, and bring your knee in front of his hip.
5. From there you can push and move him around — back in business.

## Drilled Sequence

1. Opponent gets near side underhook, pins head and shoulders.
2. Hands on his lats, elbow low and jammed to your side.
3. Pinch knees around his hip (never his knee) the moment his knee tries to rise.
4. Stand your foot on his calf muscle for a solid butterfly hook base.
5. He tests by trying to kick out / push your knee down — hold the position (it won't be immediately easy for him either way).
6. Pommel your foot in — knee moves from behind his butt cheek to in front of his hip.
7. Hands on lats, lift, bring knee to front of hip — guard restored.

---

## Common Mistakes

- Pinching your knees around **his knee** instead of his hip — he simply pops the knee and spreads straight over the top (described as a "terrible" mistake, repeated for emphasis).
- Staying passive with your hips while his knee climbs — this alone is usually enough for a skilled opponent to pass.
- Tolerating a hand on your knee instead of peeling it off.
- Attempting a lazy butterfly hook without first standing your foot on his calf muscle for control.

## Decision Points

- **His knee starts to rise off the floor** → immediately pinch knees around his hip, not his knee.
- **He puts a hand on your knee to push it down** → peel it off; safe to do because your overhook denies his kimura.
- **Setting a butterfly hook** → foot on calf muscle first, then pinch knees, then pommel in.
- **Foot pommels inside** → target is knee in front of his hip (not behind his butt cheek).

## Terminology Introduced

- **Near side underhook** — opponent's underhook on the same side as his head-control crossface (vs. far-side underhook + crossface). Considered one of the strongest half guard top control positions.
- **Overhook** — your own arm control on his far arm, used here to shut down his kimura options while you peel his hand off your knee.
