# Guard Retention Against A Reverse Underhook Sit-Out

## Overview

Third and final sit-out variant: the reverse underhook. Unlike the crossface and reverse-crossface variants (both primarily head-control problems), the reverse underhook gives the opponent strong **shoulder** control with comparatively weaker head control — a different problem requiring a different solution, built around getting your shoulders off the mat rather than freeing your head.

---

## What a reverse underhook is

- A standard underhook: your right arm goes under the opponent's left arm (or the mirror) — your elbow ends up inside his elbow, a form of inside control.
- A **reverse underhook**: same idea but crossed — your left arm under his left arm (same-side-to-same-side rather than opposite). You end up on your elbow, your hand, or (most common) a compromise with the elbow just barely off the mat.
- Danger: if your leg comes free while he holds this and he turns into you, it exposes your back — people get their back taken from this position, so care is needed.
- Compared to crossface/reverse crossface: gives him excellent shoulder control but **weaker head control**, meaning it's relatively easier to move your head away — but it introduces the back-exposure risk instead.

## The escape sequence

1. Hand to his lower back (with a gi, thumb inside the belt — illegal to grip the drawstring no-gi, so lower back is the substitute grip).
2. Take your trapped-side elbow and focus on moving it — and your head — away from his body.
3. Knee goes right into his hip (same hip-control principle as the two-knees/prone family — reverse underhook, despite being a sit-out variant, still allows some hip access because of the grip's geometry). Feet come **close together**, not crossed, not triangled — this alone buys time; he can still eventually free his leg, but not immediately.
4. Open your trapped elbow and post up onto it — critical detail: **the instant your shoulder is off the mat, it is no longer a passable position** (guard-pass criteria requires hip and shoulder down for a 3-count) — so there's no risk in un-weighting your legs at this stage.
5. With shoulder off the mat and hips now mobile, bring your top-side knee up underneath yourself.
6. Reach the handle on his oblique muscles, scoot around the corner and pull.
7. He'll defend by pulling his head back out (to protect his own back exposure) — this puts you back in a strong position to reset guard from.

## Common mistake to avoid

- Don't try to force your knee directly through the gap between your body and his — there's no space, and people get stuck trying. Instead, come up to a knee first (per the sequence above), then reset from there.

---

## Common Mistakes

- Panicking and dropping the leg / trying to force a knee through a gap that isn't there.
- Forgetting that once your shoulder is off the mat, there is no pass risk — hesitating to move your legs freely out of habit.
- Not bringing your feet close together early (crossing or triangling instead) — close-together feet is what buys the time needed to build the elbow post.

## Decision Points

- **Opponent takes a reverse underhook** → hand to lower back, trapped elbow/head move away from his body, knee to his hip, feet close together (not locked).
- **You need mobility** → post up onto your elbow — from this instant, no guard-pass criteria can be met, so legs can move freely.
- **Building the recovery** → knee comes up, grab his oblique handle, scoot and pull; he pulls his head back to protect his own back — guard restored from there.
- **Common failure point (gap-diving)** → don't force the knee through the gap; come up to a knee properly instead.

## Terminology Introduced

- **Reverse underhook** — an underhook where the same-side arm goes under the opponent's same-side arm (vs. the standard opposite-arm underhook); strong shoulder control, weaker head control, carries back-exposure risk if mishandled.
- **Guard-pass criteria (referenced)** — hip and shoulder both on the mat for a 3-count; posting a shoulder off the mat at any point resets the clock and removes pass risk entirely.
