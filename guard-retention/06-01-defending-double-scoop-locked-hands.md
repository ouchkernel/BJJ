# Defending Against Double Scoop Grips: Working Against Locked Hands

## Overview

Introduces defense against **double scoop grips** — a classic, long-used method of guard passing where the opponent gets underneath both of your legs (a "soup line" position) and controls both legs from below. This lesson covers the specific case where the opponent locks his hands together underneath your legs.

---

## Context: why double scoops matter

- This is a time-honored passing method, in use since the earliest days of jiu-jitsu.
- Some opponents prefer to lock their hands together in this underneath position. It's not a technically wrong choice for them, but it creates a specific vulnerability to exploit.

## The problem with staying bent over

- If you stay bent over (knees pulled up toward your chin), your wrists/thumbs can't reach the opponent's wrists — the position is closed off.

## The sequence

1. Recognize the opponent has worked underneath both legs and locked his hands together (soup line / double scoop with locked hands).
2. **Straighten your body out.** This is the key unlock — straightening exposes the opponent's wrists to your thumbs (staying curled up keeps them out of reach).
3. As he holds on tight with his locked hands, begin a **shimmying action** — described as the same motion as "taking off a tight pair of pants."
4. The shimmy works one knee through the gap. The instant one knee is through, the position is broken ("the cat's out of the bag").
5. Bring your whole foot through as well.
6. Use that foot to post on the opponent's body and create an angle to work back to guard.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Straightening your body (not staying curled/bent) is what exposes the opponent's wrists to your thumbs in the first place — this is the prerequisite, not the shimmy itself.
- The finishing mechanic is the same "shimmy like removing tight pants" motion used elsewhere in the guard retention system.
- Getting **one knee through** is the tipping point — everything unravels for the opponent's grip after that.
- Once a knee is through, bring the whole foot through and post it to create an angle back toward guard.
