# Inside Position Leg Scissor

## Overview

A refinement of the last-battle defense (head/shoulder control) built around two specific hand positions and the "leg scissor" body-movement — a method to flip from a pinnable supine position to a much safer prone position without the opponent completing a 3-second pin.

---

## The two-hand defensive position

When the opponent has cleared your legs, level-changed down, and is closing in for head/shoulder control:

1. **Hand 1 (head guard):** place it on your own forehead, like blocking a punch.
2. **Hand 2 (chest guard):** place it on your own chest, like saluting a flag.

**Why these specific spots:**
- Hand on the forehead means when the opponent reaches for your head, they only ever get your forearm — never your head directly.
- Hand on the chest means when the opponent completes their pin attempt, that hand becomes your underhook — giving you inside position on the upper body instead of being shut out.

Three seconds sounds short but is a long time in a combat sport — this position needs to survive that window, not indefinitely.

## The leg scissor — escaping the position

You can't hold the two-hand defensive frame forever; the opponent can step over your body into mount. From the frame:

1. Bring your hands/elbows back to yourself.
2. Grab hold of the training partner's leg.
3. As the opponent works around your head, scissor your legs to get to your base — this is the movement of going from supine (chest up) to prone (chest down): turn onto your side, scissor the legs, arrive prone.
4. From prone, you are safe — the guard is not passed from here.

## What prone position gives you

Once prone with the leg scissor completed, you have multiple options:
- Sit back to guard.
- Grab the opponent's legs directly.
- Simply back away and stand up.

The specific choice matters less than the fact that your guard has not been passed.

## Live sequence example

Opponent clears legs, establishes chest contact, wrestles for head. Because the defensive hand position means they never get direct head control, you can pass a hand through into an underhook. From there, use the legs to scissor onto your side, grab the opponent's calf, and scissor up to your base — from which you can either drive into a takedown attempt or conservatively sit back into guard.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Two-hand frame: forehead (blocks head control, gives them only your forearm) + chest (becomes an underhook when they pin).
- Don't put your hands anywhere else — protecting the temple specifically matters.
- You cannot hold the frame forever — the opponent can step over into mount, so transition out via the leg scissor.
- Leg scissor = turn to your side, scissor legs, go from supine to prone. From prone, the guard is not passable.
- Once prone, you have several safe follow-ups: sit back to guard, grab the legs, or stand and back away.
