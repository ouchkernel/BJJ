# 12 Essential Body Movements — Quick Reference

Drill all 12 daily until automatic, before applying anything else in this course.

## The 2 key lines

- **Hip line passed** → you're now in a defensive cycle.
- **Shoulder line passed** → bigger recovery movement needed (inverted spin territory).

## Pass criteria (what you're preventing)

Opponent needs **hip + shoulder pinned simultaneously for 3 seconds**. Elbow prop and knee prop both deny this by keeping one half off the mat.

## The 12 movements

1. **Pommeling legs** — knees to chest, circular feet + linear hip shift, toes out. Creates space/mobility.
2. **Shrimping** — foot outside the knee, frame, push hips away. Costs knee-elbow connection on that side.
3. **Scooting** — seated version of shrimping. Feet in, push hips back.
4. **Hip kip** — feet kip upward, hips go light, knees snap back to face opponent. Powered by elbows braced on mat. Use when knees get turned the wrong way and you don't have time to shrimp.
5. **Crossover** — cross one foot over the other knee to pull around and re-face opponent. Use *before* he passes your hip line.
6. **High leg** — outside leg goes high, sole of foot anywhere on opponent's body, pull yourself back to guard. Use once he's past your hip line.
7. **Inside leg hook** — inside leg hooks opponent's lead quad, pull body around. Alternative to high leg.
8. **Inverted spin** — asymmetric leg extension, invert, touch floor, rotate through, finish elbows+knees tight. Use once he's past your *shoulder* line.
9. **Elbow prop** — come up to elbow, shoulder off mat. Upper-body strength dependent.
10. **Knee prop** — toes dig in, prop on knee, hip comes off mat. **Preferred over elbow prop** (legs > arms in strength). Critical: knee must land **outside your own ankle** or you get pushed over.
11. **Shoulder roll** — continuation of knee prop: trace foot along mat, roll through back to guard. One of the best retention moves in the sport.
12. **Leg scissor** — supine → prone via scissoring the legs. Sets up prone retention options.

## Non-negotiable detail

Knee prop only works if the propping knee is **outside** your ankle (inside of knee on the mat). Knee inside the ankle = you get pushed over immediately.

## Preference order

Knee prop > elbow prop, whenever your flexibility allows it.
