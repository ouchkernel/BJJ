# Guard Retention Vs Knee Cut: Knee Prop / Shield Method

## Overview

Introduces the knee cut pass — a hybrid pass that controls the hips through the legs but also usually needs upper-body control (head/shoulders, typically via an underhook in no-gi). Covers how the gi and no-gi versions of the knee cut differ mechanically, identifies the exact area of the hip that gets contested, and gives the knee-shield-based retention answer for the no-gi version.

---

## Gi vs. No-Gi Knee Cut — Why the Emphasis Differs

### Gi version
- Opponent uses **collar and sleeve grip** to pin your shoulders (or better: a tricep grip).
- He cuts **away** from your knee, not over it, then uses the jacket grip to prevent you countering: he can push your shoulder down and pull up on your sleeve, pinning you so you can't turn to elbow-escape.
- The elbow escape requires turning toward the escape direction and bringing elbow + knee together — the jacket grip on your tricep/sleeve directly blocks that turn, while he scoots his own knee far away from you where you can't reach it.
- Net effect: with the gi, going "long" (knee far out) works very well for the passer because the jacket grip neutralizes your recovery.

### No-gi version
- No jacket grip available, so if the opponent tries the same "long" cut with lots of space, you can just elbow-escape freely — nothing stops your elbow.
- Instead, in no-gi he must stabilize your shoulders with an **underhook** (not a collar grip) and land his knee **in the pocket of your hip** — tight, not far out.
- This creates **diagonal control**: underhook on one side (upper body), knee on the opposite side (lower body). Even if you bring elbow and knee together, he simply steps his knee forward and pins you.
- **The contested area**: the pocket of your hip. If he wins that space — knee to the mat there, underhook on the opposite side — he wins the exchange the large majority of the time. From there he just controls your head and drives forward to finish the pass.

## The Retention Answer (No-Gi): Knee Shield

Core idea: since the danger area is the pocket of your hip, occupy it first with your own knee before he gets there.

### Setup
1. Identify the danger: opponent's knee driving between your knees toward your armpit — this is the "emerging knee cut" signal.
2. Place your knee (the one nearer his approaching knee) across as a **shallow knee shield** directly in the pocket of your hip, ahead of his knee arriving.
3. Keep a hand/frame in close so when he reaches for head control it's difficult to establish.

### Mechanism
- As he drives forward to get his underhook and complete the pass, the knee shield creates just enough space that you can **misdirect his hips out to the side** rather than let him land squarely in your hip pocket.
- If your knee shield is inactive/not there, he cuts straight into that space and passes. If it's active, his knee lands out wide instead of in the pocket — and once it's out wide, it's an easy elbow escape with your bottom knee.
- Once he's misdirected outward, bring your **bottom foot to his hip** on the opposite side, then turn off that foot to rotate your body away and recover guard.

### Drilled sequence (from multiple angles/entries — starting seated, opponent standing, opponent already driving in)
1. See the danger cue: opponent's knee punching between your knees toward your armpit.
2. Place knee shield in the pocket of your hip immediately.
3. Frame a hand/arm across to control his approach and slow head control.
4. As he drives forward, use the knee shield to **misdirect him out to the side** (not straight through).
5. Space opens for your bottom foot — plant it on his hip (opposite side from the shield).
6. Turn your body off that foot, rotating all the way out, back into a guard/retention position.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Knee cut passing differs meaningfully between gi and no-gi: gi version goes "long" and uses jacket grips to prevent your recovery; no-gi version stays tight and fights for **the pocket of your hip** using an underhook + knee combination.
- The single most important piece of real estate to defend in the no-gi knee cut is **the pocket of your hip** — if the opponent's knee lands there with an opposite-side underhook, he wins the exchange almost every time.
- **Knee shield** placed proactively in that pocket is the retention tool — it doesn't block the pass outright, it **misdirects** the opponent's knee out wide, which both prevents the pin and opens space for your own recovery.
- Sequence is always: identify danger → shield the hip pocket → misdirect out to the side → foot to opposite hip → turn out to recover guard.
