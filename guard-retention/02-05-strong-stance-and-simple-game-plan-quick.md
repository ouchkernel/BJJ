# Strong Stance + Simple Game Plan — Quick Reference

## Stance checklist (5 elements)

1. Stay compact (like a boxer)
2. Spine rounded (rocking-chair shape) — never flat
3. Knees close to chest
4. Elbows close to knees
5. Elbows **outside** knees (supine especially)

## Self-framing — how to hold that stance under pressure

- **Pulling method:** wrists hook inside knees, pull knees to chest. (Prefer wrists over gripping shins — keeps hands free.) Use it only when he's actively attacking your stance, release otherwise.
- **Pushing method:** elbows push off mat, hands push into outside of your own knees. Widens your base from hip-width to elbow-width — bounds how far he can throw your legs.
- **Floor framing:** frame the floor itself (no contact with opponent) to reorient your knees back toward him when they end up pointing the wrong way.

## Opening game plan: two-on-one grip

1. Check opponent's hand height.
2. **Hands high** → two-on-one wrist, thumbs **up**.
3. **Hands low** → two-on-one wrist, thumbs **down**, or two-on-one elbow.
4. Either wrist grip can upgrade to two-on-one elbow (stronger).
5. He can't really start passing until he breaks this grip — you're now leading the exchange (offensive cycle), not reacting to him (defensive cycle).

## The #1 mistake to avoid

Going supine = he gets a big mobility advantage. **Don't reach/chase to compensate.** Stay compact, self-frame, let him come to you — same as a boxer countering instead of lunging.

## Full game plan

- Seated → open with two-on-one immediately.
- Supine → stance first, self-frame, don't chase. Everything else is secondary to holding the stance.
