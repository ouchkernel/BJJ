# The 3 Great Safety Positions Against A Body Lock

## Overview

Companion to the body-lock negation lesson. Where that lesson covered active framing/hand-fighting methods to prevent the lock, this one covers three static positions that make a body lock structurally impossible to apply — useful as a reset when fatigued or when facing a body-lock specialist whose speed makes active negation risky.

---

## Why safety positions matter

Body locking can come on very quickly — you can defend well for minutes and then lose focus for a few seconds and get locked up. Against a genuine body-lock specialist, having positions where a lock is simply not possible gives you a place to recover, rewire strategy, or just slow the pace down without relying on split-second hand-fighting.

## The three positions

### 1. Knee shield
- Put a knee in front of the opponent's shoulder — described as "kryptonite" to a body-lock pass, stops it immediately.
- Works from either your top knee or bottom knee, and from either full guard or half guard.
- Top knee is the generally preferred version, but bottom knee is also effective.

### 2. Closed guard
- Locking a closed guard around the opponent completely stops any body lock attempt.
- Forces the opponent to abandon what they're doing, open your guard, and restart their entire passing sequence from zero.

### 3. Omoplata
- Requires only a small amount of angle to set up.
- The instant the opponent is in the omoplata, there is no body lock risk whatsoever — they must withdraw from the submission threat before even thinking about resuming a body-lock pass.

## How to use them together

These three positions work in concert with the active negation methods (hand control, thumb posts, feet on hips, cross shoulder, side neck post) from the companion lesson — cycling between active framing and one of these three static safety positions is what makes it genuinely difficult for a specialist to ever complete a body-lock pass.

**Live example flow:** starting from a live roll, use thumb posts and a knee in front of the opponent's shoulder to control them as they try to advance; the combination denies each of the three things they need (head below shoulders, connected hands, elbows around the hips) and instead gives you the critical inside-knee position that kills the lock outright.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Three positions make a body lock structurally impossible: knee shield (either knee, either guard type), closed guard, and omoplata.
- Knee shield in front of the shoulder is the single most repeatable one — usable from nearly any scramble.
- Closed guard and omoplata both force a full restart of the opponent's passing sequence.
- Use these as a "safe harbor" to fall back to when tired or up against a body-lock specialist, combined with the active hand-fighting/framing negation methods from the previous lesson.
