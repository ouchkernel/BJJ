# Elbow Prop Method

## Overview

A method for the same "emergency response" stage as the outside shoulder roll — opponent has contact, distance, angle, and is level-changing down toward completing the pass. Built around the legal requirement for a pass: the opponent must pin **both a hip and a shoulder** to the mat for three seconds. Getting either off the mat defeats the pass by definition.

---

## The rule this exploits

- To complete a guard pass, the opponent must pin your hip **and** shoulder to the mat simultaneously for three seconds.
- Earlier lesson covered **knee/hip propping** (getting the hip off the mat). This lesson covers the shoulder side: **elbow propping**.
- As long as your shoulder is off the mat, there is no guard pass — regardless of what else is happening — and you also gain extra mobility from that elevated position.

## The elbow prop sequence

1. As he comes around the corner and starts to level-change down, switch your framing hand from an inside-arm frame to a **cross frame** — forearm across his collarbones, thumb posting inside his elbow so he can never reach your head.
2. With your other hand now free, **prop up onto your elbow**.
3. Once propped, your shoulder is off the mat — even if he completes his positional adjustments, there's no pass, because the shoulder criterion isn't met.

## Two ways to finish from the prop

### Method 1 — Scoot (simpler)
- Bring your feet back toward your own buttocks and scoot away from the opponent, re-establishing guard. This is the simpler of the two options.

### Method 2 — Combine with the shoulder roll
- Against a strong opponent who's pushing hard into you (scooting is difficult), use the shoulder roll instead: hand goes underneath, prop up, and as he comes in to complete his post, roll all the way through and put him back in guard.
- Preferred against physically strong, pressuring opponents — uses leg strength rather than trying to out-scoot someone stronger.

---

## Key Takeaways

- A guard pass legally requires hip **and** shoulder pinned — getting your shoulder off the mat via an elbow prop defeats the pass outright, independent of anything else happening.
- Cross frame (forearm on collarbones, thumb inside elbow) is what denies head control and frees your other hand to prop.
- **Scoot** is the simple finish; **shoulder roll** is the finish to use against strong, pressuring opponents where scooting is impractical.
- This method pairs directly with the knee/hip-prop method from the previous lesson — same underlying rule, opposite anchor point (shoulder vs. hip).
