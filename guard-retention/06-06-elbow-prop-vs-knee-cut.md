# Elbow Prop Vs Knee Cut

## Overview

A refinement of the no-gi knee cut defense: adds thumb/hand control to slow the opponent's underhook, and introduces the **elbow prop** as the recovery mechanism after the knee shield redirects the pass attempt.

---

## Setup: Denying the Underhook

- If the opponent already has sleeve/lapel control (or the no-gi equivalent), his knee can go far out and shoulder control stops you from elbow-escaping or recovering guard — going far out with no-gi is a much easier fix (see the previous lesson).
- In no-gi, the opponent's knee has to land in the **pocket of your hip**, or he needs to dominate an underhook on the other side.
- **Preemptive hand control**: take a hand and control your opponent's hand/wrist as he reaches for the underhook (grip on his hand, or even better, inside his elbow). This makes it much harder for him to establish the underhook — he has to beat your hand first.

## The Sequence

1. Establish a **shallow knee shield** on your training partner, right in the hip-pocket danger zone, as he comes in.
2. Control his approaching hand/wrist (or hook inside his elbow) to deny an easy underhook — with no gi to grip, it's a thumb-fight you can usually win, and you can always peel a hand off (working against a thumb is easy to break).
3. As he presses in to pass, **direct him out with the knee shield** — he gets misdirected rather than landing square.
4. Use that redirected momentum to **come up to an elbow prop** — post yourself up onto your elbow as part of the recovery.
5. Retract your feet back toward your own buttocks, then **scoot away** to create space.
6. Face the opponent, guard re-established, ready to play again.

## Recovering the Underhook If Already Lost

- If you see the opponent starting to straighten up and pass your wrist/hand away, don't let it go uncontested — you can always put your hand back, or double up (place a second hand inside his elbow) so if he tries to wrist-pass you he has to beat that inside hand first.
- Staying circumspect about hand position (constantly re-attaching/controlling) prevents the underhook from ever being fully secured, even mid-exchange.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Knee shield + active hand/wrist control on the passing side = harder for opponent to establish the underhook that the no-gi knee cut depends on.
- Once the knee shield redirects the opponent's forward pressure, the recovery move is to **prop up to an elbow**, retract the feet, and scoot away to reset distance and face the opponent again.
- Constant, low-effort hand fighting (re-attaching to his wrist/elbow) denies the underhook piecemeal instead of needing one big defensive movement.
