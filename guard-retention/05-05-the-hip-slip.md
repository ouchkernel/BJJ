# The Hip Slip (Late Defense vs. Body Lock)

## Overview

A third late-defense option against a fully locked-on body lock, used when the opponent hasn't yet passed your legs (contrast with the outside shoulder roll lesson, which handles the case where he has). Exploits a structural weakness common to most body lockers: they grip with a **long arm and a short arm**, not symmetrically.

---

## The core insight

- Most body lockers lock one arm long (reaching far around) and one arm short. This asymmetry creates **wrist exposure on the short-arm side** — that wrist sits right in front of you and is easy to grab, whereas the long-arm wrist is too far behind you to reach.
- This asymmetric grip is the entry point for the whole technique.

## The sequence

1. As the opponent locks the body lock, identify which arm is short (e.g., his right arm short if he's right-handed and locking that way).
2. Grab the **short-arm wrist**.
3. **Thumb-post the opposite shoulder** (just where the shoulder meets the bicep) with your other hand — so you now control one wrist and the opposite shoulder.
4. As he holds on tight, **shimmy your hips like you're taking off a tight pair of pants** — a lateral hip-sliding action.
5. This shimmy moves his lock **down from your waist to around your legs**. The instant it's around your legs instead of your waist, the hip control (the entire point of a body lock) is gone.
6. From here, collect the opponent's arms/elbow as he holds onto your legs and shift your body out to a good angle.
7. With his hands occupied holding your legs, you're now free to throw a leg over the top and set up an **omoplata** — he has to deal with that before any guard pass is possible.

## Handling the opponent's reaction once his lock slips to your legs

- **If he holds on tight (less experienced reaction):** push his head to a given side — this sets up throwing a leg over into omoplata.
- **If he's more experienced and unlocks his hands to jump over your legs:** the moment his hands unlock, **all of your standard guard retention tools apply again** (this is described as the whole point of the body lock's grip — once it's broken, everything else in the guard retention system kicks back in). Get your forearms inside his elbow/neck, bring your legs back in, and rebuild guard from there.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Structural flaw exploited: body lockers almost always grip long-arm/short-arm, not symmetrically — the short-arm wrist is exposed and reachable.
- Control sequence: grab short-arm wrist + thumb-post opposite shoulder, then shimmy hips laterally (like removing tight pants).
- The shimmy's purpose is singular: move his lock from your **waist** (controls hips = dangerous) to your **legs** (doesn't control hips = safe).
- Once his lock is on your legs, either he holds on (→ push head, throw leg for omoplata) or he unlocks to chase (→ every normal guard retention tool works again, since locked hands were the only thing making the body lock special).
