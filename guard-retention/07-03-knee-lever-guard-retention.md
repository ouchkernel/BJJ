# Knee Lever Guard Retention

## Overview

A third answer for half guard bottom under chest-to-chest cross-face control — instead of the knee/elbow escapes, this lesson **off-balances the opponent directly** with a knee lever, using it to tip him over and buy the space needed to recover guard. Covers both the standard version (opponent's knee already on the mat) and a variation (double knee lever) for when it isn't.

---

## Setup

- Same starting problem as prior half-guard-bottom lessons: opponent chest-to-chest, cross-face, in the driver's seat.
- **Prerequisite for the standard knee lever: the opponent's knee must be on the mat.** This is the single condition that makes the technique available.

## Standard Knee Lever (Opponent's Knee on the Mat)

1. Bring your foot up to the opponent's knee. Two feet come together **crossed at the toes, not the ankles** (don't cross ankles).
2. With the foot placed this way, the opponent's knee is effectively pinned to stay on the ground.
3. Pinch both knees around the opponent's hip.
4. Lift your **butt slightly off the floor**.
5. As your butt lifts, **crimp/trap his elbow** against his side — this removes his ability to base out with that arm. (His other hand is typically pinned under your head, so it isn't available as a base either.) This leaves his leg as his only remaining base option.
6. Point your knees in the lever direction and **hit the lever like a punch** — a sharp, immediate off-balancing action, not a slow push. This forces him to base out with his leg (his only remaining option) or tip over entirely.
7. As he steps/bases to recover, put your thumb into his armpit and bring your knee up to your chest — this plants a knee in front of his hip.
8. Shift your hips outside his knee and lock up closed guard.

## Double Knee Lever (Opponent's Knee NOT on the Mat)

Used when the prerequisite (opponent's knee on the mat) isn't met.

1. Pinch both legs around the opponent's **thigh** (not the lower leg/calf as in the standard version).
2. Knee-lever from this position to force his knee down toward the mat.
3. Once it's down, **stand your feet on top of his calf muscle** — this makes it hard for him to lift the knee back up off the mat.
4. From that pinned position, hit the same knee lever as before (off-balance, force him to base/tip) as he tries to climb back on top.

## Key Mechanical Details

- **Crossing toes, not ankles** for the foot placement against the opponent's knee.
- **Elbow crimp** (trapping his posting arm to his side) as your hips lift is what removes his base options and makes the lever effective — without it he can simply post through the lever attempt.
- The off-balancing action should be sharp/sudden ("like a punch"), not a gradual push — described as generating a surprising amount of force for a fairly small movement.
- Once he's destabilized and tries to recover, that's the window to insert your knee in front of his hip and shift to closed guard.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Knee lever needs the opponent's knee on the mat to work in its standard form; if it's not on the mat, pin it there first with the double-knee-lever (thigh pinch + stand on calf) before hitting the same lever action.
- The lever itself only works because you've simultaneously **removed his base options** — crimping the elbow to his side is what makes the off-balance actually land, not just the leg positioning.
- Deliver the lever sharply, not gradually — it's described as a "punch," and it generates more force than the small movement would suggest.
- After destabilizing him, the recovery follows the same pattern as the other half-guard-bottom escapes: knee in front of his hip → hips outside his knee → closed guard.
