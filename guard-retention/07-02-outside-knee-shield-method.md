# Outside Knee Shield Method (Half Guard Top Head & Arm)

## Overview

Follow-up to the inside knee/elbow escape: what to do when the opponent's control is tight enough that you **can't** get the inside knee through. The answer is to use the **outside** knee as a shield instead, generating the same pushing force from a different angle.

---

## Why You Need This Second Option

- Half guard bottom under chest-to-chest, underhook + control on top is a fundamentally difficult position because the top player can create pushing force against you while you can only pull with your legs.
- The inside knee/elbow escape solves this by getting a knee in front of the opponent's hip — but if a high-level opponent has his knee jammed too tight to your hip, you may not be able to bring the inside knee through at all.
- When that happens: use the **outside** knee to create a shield in front of his hip instead. Same underlying goal (a knee in front of his hip to push from), different leg.

## Setup

1. Shift onto your side, attempt to invert the knee as usual — if you discover you can't bring the knee through, switch to the outside knee.
2. Angle your **outside knee downward** and place it as a **shallow** knee shield in front of the opponent's hip.
   - Don't go in too far/deep — you want your kneecap (specifically the bottom of the kneecap) against his hip, not foot-deep.
   - Angle the knee **slightly downward** — angling upward tends to let it slip out.
3. **Feet placement detail:** top foot pushes forward, bottom foot retracts, so your two feet end up roughly stacked one over the other (not spread far apart). Far-apart feet = a weak shield. Stacked feet = you'll feel real tension transmitted into the opponent's hip when they line up correctly.

## Using the Shield

1. Once the shield is engaged (feel the tension), the opponent's cross-face pressure becomes much less effective — he can't drive it home.
2. Take one hand inside the opponent's shoulder to control it (single-hand shoulder control).
3. **Come up to an elbow prop** — push and post onto your elbow.
4. Use the knee shield to extract your bottom foot and place it on the opponent's hip (this denies any body-lock attempt).
5. Shrimp out — with the shield, the elbow prop, and the extracted foot in place, guard recovery follows quickly.

## Troubleshooting: "Kipping" the Knee In

- Sometimes the opponent is tied in tight enough that the knee shield is hard to insert directly.
- Fix: use a small **kipping motion** (rapid small hip movements) to "tip" the knee into place rather than forcing it in one motion.
- Once the knee is in, bring the two feet over each other (per the stacked-feet detail above) so the opponent feels the tension/pinch through his hip.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Outside knee shield is the **backup** to the inside knee/elbow escape — use it when the opponent's control is too tight to bring the inside knee through.
- Shield depth and angle both matter: shallow (kneecap, not foot-deep) and angled **slightly downward** (upward angling lets it slip).
- Feet should end up stacked (one over the other), not spread apart — spread feet = weak shield, no tension transmitted.
- If the knee won't insert cleanly, use a small kipping motion to work it into place rather than forcing it.
- Full recovery sequence: knee shield engaged → single-hand shoulder control → elbow prop → extract bottom foot to opponent's hip → shrimp out → guard restored.
