# Wrist Control Vs Double Scoops

## Overview

A hand-control-focused counter to double scoops, complementing the leg-pommeling method. The core danger being defended against: the opponent getting a hand across your centerline to control your hip or shoulder, which significantly boosts his passing potential.

---

## The danger being defended

- With double scoops, the opponent is always looking to get a hand across your centerline — controlling either your hip or your shoulder from there. If he secures this, his passing potential goes up substantially.

## The sequence

1. Opponent gets underneath both your legs (double scoop position).
2. **Priority #1: get your legs down toward his elbows** (same repositioning idea as the leg-pommeling lesson) — this makes it harder for him to lift and reach across your centerline in the first place.
3. **Follow up immediately with hand control** — control his wrists/hands (whichever grip feels natural). This matters even if he already got the leg back up on his shoulder: controlling his wrists prevents him from actually bringing that hand across your centerline to finish the grip.
4. With his hands controlled, work your hips out to the side.
5. This sets up a **palm-laying action** — using your controlled grip on his hands to drag your body/legs back into a winning position and return to guard.
6. If he tries to switch his grips while you have wrist control, it's very difficult for him to do so — maintain the control and keep turning.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The specific threat here is the opponent's hand crossing your **centerline** to control your hip or shoulder — that's what meaningfully increases his passing potential, so it's the thing to deny.
- Sequence priority: legs down to his elbows first, then immediately layer on **hand/wrist control** — the two work together.
- Wrist control also locks him out of **switching grips**, not just stops the current pass attempt.
- The "palm-laying" turning action, done while maintaining wrist control, is what returns you to a winning position from here.
