# Sit-Out Half Guard Variations: Guard Retention Against A Sit-Out Crossface

## Overview

Introduces the sit-out family of half guard passing positions — where the opponent, instead of staying on two knees or prone chest-to-chest, drops his hip down and sits out to one side. This changes the geometry completely and requires a different control focus than everything covered previously. States the master rule for the rest of this sub-series: **control the hip when he's on two knees/prone; control the head when he sits out.** Demonstrates the sit-out crossface variation specifically, described as the easiest of the sit-out variants to deal with (no-gi).

---

## What changes when an opponent sits out

- Previously (two knees / prone chest-to-chest), your head faced his head/chest, and all retention methods focused on controlling his **hip** (knee shields, elbow escapes, pinching knees around the hip, half butterfly).
- When he sits out to a hip, the angle changes completely: now your head faces his **hips**, not his head.
- This makes elbow escapes and half-butterfly entries from earlier lessons far easier to hit, because you're no longer fighting an opponent square on top of you.
- Sit-out variants: crossface, reverse crossface, or reverse underhook (this lesson covers crossface; the other two are covered in following lessons).

## The golden rule (central concept of the whole sit-out sub-series)

> When someone is on two knees / prone in half guard: focus of control is **their hip**.
> The moment they sit out: focus of control switches entirely to **their head**.

- With sit-out, their hip is now too far away for you to access with your knees — chasing it is a losing move (he just puts a hand on your hip, bobs the other, and steps out to pass).
- Instead, all their control in a sit-out comes through your head — specifically, the crossface locks onto your **jaw**, using their shoulder as a lever on a "relatively short lever" ending at your chin.
- **The fix:** get your temple inside their armpit instead of letting their shoulder sit on your jawline. This single detail is described as making an "astronomical" difference — once your temple is inside the armpit, you can turn freely into the opponent, and any elbow/knee escape becomes available again.

## Mechanics of freeing the head (crossface variant)

1. While they're still on two knees, keep both knees pinched high around their hip (never let their knee get higher than your hip) — same principle as prior lessons.
2. The moment they sit out and take a crossface (shoulder on your jaw), switch focus entirely off the hip.
3. Thumb-post: get thumbs into their armpit(s) — this lifts their shoulder off your jaw and frees their chin/jaw from controlling you.
4. Continue the motion until your forehead/temple slides inside their armpit — "like a turtle putting its head back into its shell."
5. Once your head is free, you can turn into the opponent freely; any guard pass they attempt fails because you can always bring knee and elbow together and slot your legs back in front.

## Why crossface (no-gi) is the easiest sit-out variant

- Without a gi, the opponent's crossface grip is only holding your lat muscle — a big, strong "handle" but with nothing reinforcing it.
- With a gi, a crossface reinforced by the lapel would be far harder to escape (arguably the hardest of the three variants) — but that's out of scope here since this is a no-gi context.
- Recommended training order: drill against the crossface first (easiest), then reverse crossface (harder, covered next), then reverse underhook.

---

## Common Mistakes

- Continuing to fight for hip control after the opponent has sat out — the hip is no longer reachable and chasing it just gives him an easy pass.
- Letting the opponent's shoulder stay on your jawline instead of thumb-posting to get your temple into his armpit.
- Not recognizing the transition moment — failing to switch your entire focus from hip to head the instant you feel him change from prone to sit-out.

## Decision Points

- **Opponent stays on two knees / prone** → focus all control on his hip (knee shields, pinch knees around hip, etc., per earlier lessons).
- **Opponent sits out to a hip (any grip)** → immediately abandon hip control, switch fully to freeing your head via thumb-post into his armpit(s).
- **No-gi crossface specifically** → easiest sit-out variant to escape; good starting drill.

## Terminology Introduced

- **Sit-out** — opponent drops his hip down to one side instead of staying on two knees/prone, changing your head-to-hip orientation.
- **Thumb post** — inserting thumbs into the opponent's armpit(s) to lift the controlling shoulder off your jaw.
- **Temple-in-armpit** — the target head position that frees you from crossface-style head control; contrasted with "shoulder on jawline" (bad for you).
