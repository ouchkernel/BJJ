# The Trimura: Triangle-Kimura Combination — Quick Reference

## Core idea

Use senkaku (triangle) to trap the opponent's HEAD — removes wiggle room, stacks a strangle + two kimura options into one position. "Trimura" = half triangle, half kimura.

## Entry (version 1)

1. Knee inside opponent's leg, push/separate.
2. He turns, re-locks hands underneath to block kimura.
3. Thread hand through, come to elbow.
4. Legs close together, lift under his armpit.
5. Second leg passes through.
6. Return to kimura grip, lock down.
7. Turn body so legs go in a straight line, relock the triangle.

## What you get from the locked triangle

1. Tight stranglehold (triangle itself).
2. Push kimura: thumb post on wrist + push arm (head trapped = very strong).
3. Push-pull kimura (even tighter): breaks at wrist alone, or wrist + elbow.

## If he defends by gripping his own hands

1. Pull the "wrong way" — drive hands to the floor.
2. Unlock triangle, switch base.
3. Land on the power line → quick breaking finish.

## Entry (version 2 — tighter triangle)

1. Dorsal position, knee inside, separate hands.
2. Leg over his leg; he locks hands defensively.
3. Elbow on floor, leg in his armpit, slide through under his head.
4. Initial kimura grip to hold him in place.
5. Unlock legs (hold elbow/wrist), pop up, thread both legs through, relock triangle tight.

## Kimura options from the tight version

- Simple push on far arm → devastating kimura.
- Double push-pull on far arm + separate hand behind back → even more devastating.
- Come up on the NEAR arm, land on power line → strongest kimura finish of all.

## Big picture

- 3 leg-incorporation methods total: cross kimura, kimura plata, trimura.
- All exist to make kimura a legs-vs-arm fight, not arms-vs-arms — lets smaller/weaker athletes win.
