# Trap Triangle Kimura: Full Repetition

## Overview

A complete, start-to-finish repetition of the trap triangle Kimura from a seated guard against an opponent in seated butterfly guard. This chapter shows the whole move run together — grip, entry, leg placement, and the finishing alignment — before the following chapters break the entry down into a full menu of grip-fighting options.

---

## Starting Position and Grip

- Opponent is in a seated butterfly guard.
- First priority: take the hand to the center line.
- Take a pronated grip on that hand.
- Go in on the training partner and connect everything to the hip — this grip/connection is strong enough that a yank-away is very difficult to execute.

## Entry Into the Trap Triangle

- From the strong hip connection, pull the training partner into the trap triangle.
- Even if the opponent keeps his hand glued to your chest, it's easy to slide two thumbs underneath and push the wrist to the outside.
- From there, turn the corner around the training partner and catch the head.
- Shimmy the hips so the knee points down toward the floor — this is the **inverted knee**.
- Lock the feet (cross the ankles) around the opponent's head and trapped arm.
- With the ankles locked, it's very difficult for the opponent to posture up and escape.

## The Finishing Alignment

- Lock up fully: the opponent's far shoulder, near shoulder, elbow, and your own head should all line up in a straight line.
- This straight line is the **bottom power line** — the preferred bracing/finishing alignment from bottom position.
- Once aligned on the power line, the Kimura pressure becomes very strong.

## What Comes Next

- This repetition shows the finish, but the real work is having a large number of effective ways to *get into* this position against different grips and reactions.
- The following material runs through a short, concise sequence of favorite grips so that from any seated butterfly guard, the legs can be fired into the trap triangle position and the Kimura game can begin.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Sequence skeleton: center-line hand control → pronated grip connected to the hip → pull into trap triangle → thumbs under to push wrist outside → catch the head → invert the knee → lock ankles → align on the power line.
- The trap triangle is defined by simply crossing the ankles around the opponent's head and one trapped arm.
- The finish depends on getting the opponent's far shoulder, near shoulder, elbow, and your own head into a straight line — the bottom power line.
- A strong finish is only half the picture; a wide set of grip-fighting entries into this same position is the other half.
