# Pull-Dominant Versus Push-Dominant Kimura — Quick Reference

## The problem

- Pushing on a kimura lets the force bleed into the opponent's mobile shoulder joint instead of the elbow.
- Result: a weak, "feeble looking" lock — lots of arm movement, no break.

## The fix — pull dominant

1. Immobilize opponent's hand with your push hand.
2. Sweep your elbow to his elbow.
3. Move your head back and away to generate a strong **pull through the elbow** (not a push through the wrist).

## Target state

- Hand: immobilized.
- Shoulder/head: immobilized.
- Elbow: the only thing still moving — being pulled.

## Payoff

- Break happens in millimeters of elbow travel vs. many inches of wasted arm movement with a push-dominant kimura.
- More consistent breaking success across all kimura positions.
