# Power Prop Counter — Snake Through To T-Kimura And Strangle Finish

## Overview

This is the answer to the power prop problem: rather than attacking the underhook arm directly (risky — exposes the back), the target becomes the **propping arm** itself. A knee-freeing step, a head-control detail, and a snaking hand motion turn the opponent's own elbow post into a kimura, which is then converted through a rolling transition into the T-Kimura position and finished with a strangle.

---

## Freeing The Knee

- Once the opponent has switched to the power prop (posting up on the elbow), the propping arm — not the underhook arm — becomes the target.
- Nothing can be done to that arm until the trail knee is freed.
- Post a hand on the training partner's hip, then bring the trail knee up — turn the knee toward the opponent's opposite hip and come up into a position where the leg is out, similar to a knee-on-belly type posture.

## Locking The Head-Trapped Kimura

- Seize the training partner at the wrist.
- Bring your armpit over the training partner's head — this cannot be skipped: if his head is up, the move doesn't work. Use your own head to press his head down first, then get the armpit over the top of it.
- In one continuous motion, snake the hand through and confidently roll the body across the opponent's body.
- Result: the kimura grip is now in place, with the head trapped as part of the control.

## Transition To T-Kimura

- Point the toes and scoot the hips so the foot can be placed on the training partner's leg, then push out.
- This transition drops you right into the **T-Kimura** position.
- As the opponent tries to turn up and into you, his body direction is easy to control from here.

## Turning The Opponent And Finishing With The Strangle

- From T-Kimura, use a bottom-knee finish: as he comes up, bring the bottom knee in underneath.
- Hook the foot over the training partner's shoulder and turn the opponent over.
- As you fall onto your side, draw the training partner's knuckles in toward your shoulder.
- This sets up an initial figure-of-four kimura grip (hand-assisted if needed) — but the actual finish comes from a strangle applied from this same position, not the joint break.

## Full Sequence Recap

1. Opponent props on one elbow in half guard, threatening sweeps and single legs.
2. Get hold of his wrist; use your head to press his head down while posting to free your knee.
3. Once the knee is free, go over the top of his head and snake your hand through.
4. Forward-roll past his body — this carries you past the opponent while keeping the kimura grip.
5. Slide your hips up to his shoulder line; place your foot on his knee and kick out — this delivers T-Kimura.
6. If he fights to get up, keep manipulating him at the elbow throughout.
7. Go in with the bottom knee; once it's in, hook the foot over his shoulder and turn his body over.
8. This gives perfect control to pull his knuckles into your chest.
9. Bring the legs up high, lock an initial figure-of-four (hand-assisted if necessary), then finish with the strangle.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The counter to the power prop is to redirect the kimura attack onto the propping arm itself, not the underhook arm.
- Freeing the trail knee (post on the hip, knee up and across) is the prerequisite step — nothing else works until it's done.
- Head control (pressing his head down before the armpit goes over it) is mandatory — the whole sequence stalls if his head is up.
- The snake-through-and-roll delivers a head-trapped kimura that transitions directly into T-Kimura by pointing the toes and kicking the foot off his leg.
- The finishing sequence — bottom knee in, hook foot over shoulder, turn opponent over, knuckles to chest, figure-of-four, strangle — converts the opponent's own strong half-guard prop into a controlled path to a strangle finish.
