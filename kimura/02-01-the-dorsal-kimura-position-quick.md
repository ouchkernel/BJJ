# The Dorsal Kimura Position — Quick Reference

## What it is

Opponent on his side, arm up in a kimura position — shape resembles a shark's dorsal fin.

## Core principle

1. Primary goal in ANY kimura situation: get the opponent's hand **behind his back**.
2. Anterior/posterior dichotomy: you always want his hand posterior (behind); he always wants it anterior (in front). Hand behind back = weak opponent. Hand in front = strong opponent.
3. Central problem of the whole kimura system: keep his hands separated, stop him reinforcing the threatened arm with his other hand, his body, or his clothing.

## Why dorsal position alone isn't enough

- No access to the power line (the diagonal line of power) — limits how much breaking force you can apply.
- Opponent can post hands on your knee, push out, turn in, and even threaten arm locks on you.
- Weak head control, even with knees squeezed together.
- Forces you into **push-dominant** kimuras (mechanically weak) instead of **pull-dominant** ones (mechanically strong).

## Takeaway

Dorsal kimura is a starting point, not a finishing position — convert it (see next lesson).
