# Central Problem Restated & Defeating Locked Hands — Quick Reference

## Central problem

Get the opponent's hand behind his back (mechanically weak) against his effort to keep it reinforced in front (mechanically strong).

## 4 ways opponents reinforce the hand

1. Lock own hands together
2. Grab own clothing
3. Hand inside own thigh
4. Hand on own opposite hip

Unsupported arm = easy to get behind the back. Supported arm = hard. Against strong opponents you often can't force it at all — see later methods.

## Defeating locked hands

**Step 1 — diagnose the bottom elbow:**
- Tight to the body (no gap) → Method A
- Exposed (gap between elbow and ribs) → Method B

### Method A — elbow tight to body

1. Hold elbow-to-elbow one-handed; pinch his elbow to your armpit/chest.
2. Release push hand only if pull hand locks/frames — never leave space.
3. Post free hand on floor, knee in and over his bicep.
4. Relock; shin wedge lifts his elbow over the shoulder line.
5. Knuckles behind the back → elbow to floor, beat the shoulder → finish.

### Method B — elbow tucked, no knee room

1. Unlock hand, thread through and around to his thigh.
2. Lever his arm out to expose the bicep.
3. Same shin-wedge mechanism as Method A: push down/pull up → hand behind scapula.
4. Elbow down, sit through → finish.

## Watch for

- Naive opponent (arm exposed): just unlock, knee in, go.
- Aware opponent (everything tight): thread hand through the kimura grip itself to create space.
