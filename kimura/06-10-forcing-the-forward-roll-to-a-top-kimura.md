# Forcing The Forward Roll To A Top Kimura

## Overview

A continuation of the bottom half guard kimura sequence: once the hand is trapped behind the back, this lesson shows how to actively force the opponent into a forward roll that carries the attacker on top — converting a bottom kimura attempt into a top kimura finish.

---

## Forcing the roll

1. From the hand-behind-the-back position, turn the hips and bring the training partner's elbow right into your own armpit, applying a pulling kimura.
2. This creates the credible threat of a broken arm if the opponent does nothing.
3. **The opponent must roll over the shoulder on the same side as the kimura.** This is the only way they can roll out of the pressure.
   - If they roll over the *wrong* (opposite) shoulder, they break their own arm and don't escape at all — the attacker can simply come up on top of them.
4. As the opponent rolls over the correct (same-side) shoulder, put both legs in the air. The opponent's own rolling motion carries the attacker over on top of them.

## Working the top position

1. Once on top, return to the one-arm kimura grip used earlier in the entry.
2. Key control detail: if the opponent controls your leg above the knee, they are in the driver's seat. To prevent this:
   - Put a hand on the bottom knee, bring that knee in as a wedge, and post the top leg.
   - This lets you free your leg and pass the training partner's guard from the top-kimura position.
3. Come up and put pressure toward the opponent's shoulder line.
4. Transition to the full two-handed kimura: step over the training partner's head, ending back on the diagonal line of power, ready to finish.

## Full sequence recap

1. Bottom half guard, shallow knee shield, dominate the shoulder line.
2. Come up, control the wrist, lock the initial (one-arm) kimura.
3. Bring the hips to the opponent's hand rather than falling backward — this pins the hand in the time available.
4. The opponent is forced into a roll over the same side as the kimura; legs go up and carry the attacker over the top.
5. Underneath the wrist, lock the one-hand kimura; bring everything up to the shoulder line.
6. Push the bottom knee, bring the knee in, push the top knee, and extract the leg.
7. Come up and threaten the opponent's shoulder line.
8. Return to the classic figure-four hold; bring everything up to the traditional power line for the finish from top position.

## Practical notes

- The entire sequence hinges on the roll being *involuntary* from the opponent's point of view — they aren't choosing to roll for tactical reasons, they're being forced into it by the threat of a broken arm on the only shoulder that offers relief.
- Leg control (not letting the opponent control above your knee) is the detail that keeps the top position safe while extracting your trapped leg.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Once the hand is behind the back, turning the hips and pulling the elbow into your armpit creates a genuine arm-break threat that forces a roll.
- The opponent can only escape by rolling over the same shoulder as the kimura — rolling the other way breaks their own arm.
- Put both legs in the air as they roll; their own motion carries you into top position.
- On top, avoid letting the opponent control your leg above the knee — wedge the bottom knee in and post the top leg to extract your leg and pass.
- Finish by returning to the classic figure-four grip and bringing everything up to the shoulder/power line.
