# Basement Training — One-Handed Kimura When The Power Line Is Blocked — Quick Reference

## Trigger

Opponent denies the power line — e.g., hugs the threatened arm against his own hip.

## One-handed kimura steps

1. Grip the **blade of the opponent's hand** (not the wrist).
2. Ignore his free-arm block attempt.
3. Rotate into a **high tripod position**.
4. Rotate your whole body — head turns into the hips, free hand blocks his hip.
5. Finish: head moves almost across his stomach, legs walk around his head → tremendous breaking pressure.

## Conventional kimura mechanics (for comparison)

- Elbow-to-elbow contact = maximizes lever and fulcrum.
- Head moves away in an arc, driving with the legs.
- This is the "power line" — the thing the one-handed version compensates for when it's blocked.

## When to switch

| Opponent does this | You do this |
|---|---|
| Arm hugs his own hip (denies power line) | Switch to one-handed grip on the blade of the hand |
| Blocks his own hips with a hand | Block his hip in return, tripod, rotate for one-handed finish |

## Key detail

Don't fight to re-establish the two-hand grip once it's genuinely blocked — go one-handed and finish through rotation instead.
