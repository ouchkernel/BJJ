# The Head And Arm Kimura — Quick Reference

## The one rule that drives the whole system

- Opponent's hand **behind** his back → go for the finish.
- Opponent's hand **in front** → use kimura to control/pin, or use legs to force it behind the back.

## When to use head and arm kimura

- Can't get the hand behind the back: can't control the head, opponent has a death grip you can't separate, or you lack flexibility to step over into a leg kimura.
- Bonus: generates back exposure — bridges kimura system into back system.

## Entry steps (from side control, basic kimura stalled)

1. Reverse grip on the arm — shoulder directly behind his elbow (closer = harder for him to slip out).
2. Hand on floor, rotate around, bicep behind his head.
3. Drive forward toward the opposite elbow — locks head + arm together.
4. Keep elbow AND head driving forward (don't sit back on heels, or he turns in and you lose the head).

## Getting to unweighted legs

1. Sit behind him even with his legs up defensively — irrelevant.
2. Shift weight, pass foot through, hook far hip.
3. Lift, bring second leg out → **unweighted legs** (both feet free).
4. Feet around his wrist to separate locked hands; foot into armpit.
5. Bring his hand overhead, lock rear triangle (ushiro senkaku) around head + arm.

## Rear-triangle-kimura finish

- Looks like a strangle — is also a kimura: knuckles outside your quad, lift the elbow.
- Head can't move → huge breaking effect in a short distance.

## Alternate recap (stalled kimura → trimura)

1. Bicep behind head, lock figure-four, sit to the mat.
2. Pass foot across, second leg in → two unweighted feet.
3. Use leg strength to separate his locked hands.
4. Fall to your side, lock figure four → half-triangle/half-kimura ("trimura").
5. Hands outside his quad → strong kimura attacks.

## Big picture

Failed kimura → head and arm kimura → back system triangle → back to kimura (now leg-controlled). Legs for power, head control throughout.
