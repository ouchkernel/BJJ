# Linear Versus Twisting Locks And Push/Pull Hands — Quick Reference

## Two categories of joint lock

| Type | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Linear | Straight-line force against the joint | Armbar (juji gatame), ude gatami, kneebar, achilles lock |
| Twisting | Twist applied around the joint | Figure-four toehold, heel hook, americana, **kimura** |

Kimura = twisting lock. Always keep that in mind when applying it.

## Push hand / pull hand

1. Every twisting lock uses a push hand and a pull hand.
2. Kimura: one hand pushes/immobilizes the **wrist** ("stopper hand" — old catch-wrestling term), the other hand pulls the **elbow**.
3. In practice: hold and immobilize the wrist; do the real work by pulling the elbow.
4. Arm bend is variable — can work >90° or <90°; personal preference is <90°.

## Why it works

- Opposing pull (elbow) + stop (wrist) = twisting pressure on the joint.
- Extreme cases: pressure can take the elbow fully out of its socket.

## Where to start

- Side kimura = recommended entry point for learning the whole system (simplest, clearest starting position).
