# Outside-Position Kimura — Beating The Ashi Garami Entry

## Overview

The answer to the outside-position problem: secure the opponent's wrist first, then commit to the springboard leg decisively and immediately, using your own rollover speed to beat his Ashi Garami entry before he can use it against you. Three live demonstrations show the same core idea from slightly different starting positions — standing outside position, working down on two knees, and a riskier variation — all finishing in a pin with the arm trapped.

---

## Step one: secure the wrist first

- Whenever you're up in outside position, take an initial hold of your training partner and secure the wrist first.
- This is usually easy to get, because your opponent thinks he already has dominant inside position with his feet and isn't worried about his hand.
- Just by working head to head in the position, you can come up and score the wrist without much resistance.

## Step two: commit fully once you put the leg up

- Once you put up your springboard leg, the onus is on you to go — no hesitation.
- Putting the leg up is a calculated risk: it hands your opponent an entrance into Ashi Garami (single leg X).
- This is not the time to delay. The instant your knee comes off the mat, you've accepted that risk — come up and push the hand to the mat immediately.
- The movement: leg comes up, opposite hand goes to the mat.

## Step three: beat the entry with your roll

- Even as your opponent goes for the Ashi Garami, your rollover will always beat his entrance into it if you commit without delay.
- This puts you in perfect position to finish through a different route than a straight kimura finish.
- Even if he manages to get all the way up to his knees, physically throw him back down, bring your head into the tackle position, and secure a pin with his arm trapped.

## Variations demonstrated

- **From standing outside position:** secure the wrist, springboard leg up, opposite hand to mat, roll beats the Ashi Garami attempt, throw him down and pin with trapped arm.
- **From two knees, close and tight:** go back and secure the wrist, leg comes up, he flies into Ashi Garami but the rollover beats his leg; as he comes back up to his knees, use a full knockdown, put your hand on his opposite hip, and high-step into a strong attacking position.
- **The riskier method:** move in toward the opponent, come down onto two knees, work until you secure the wrist, he goes for Ashi Garami, but your rollover takes you up and over the top; put him back down to his back, push, pop up into a pin with the trapped arm.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Secure the wrist first — it's usually easy since the opponent is focused on his dominant foot position, not his hand.
- Once the springboard leg goes up, there is no room to hesitate — commit and go immediately.
- Your rollover speed is what beats his Ashi Garami entry, not avoiding the entry altogether.
- All three variations end the same way: opponent thrown down, pinned with his arm trapped.
- This is an effective way to keep attacking when you're completely shut out from inside position.
