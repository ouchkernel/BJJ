# Elbow Penetration Method From North-South

## Overview

The full step-by-step mechanics of the elbow penetration entry against an opponent with sound defensive arm position. The method requires north-south (it fails from perpendicular side control), and it's built around a repeatable sequence: penetrate the thumb under the tricep, drive the elbow forward, raise the opponent's elbow, then switch wrist position and thread the hand through to lock the figure-four.

---

## Positional requirement

- Elbow penetration cannot be done from a perpendicular side position — it doesn't work.
- You must move to a **north-south** position first.

## Step-by-step sequence

1. From across the training partner's side, take the thumb and go underneath the training partner's tricep — hand on one side, arm on the other.
2. Bring the thumb underneath as far as it will go (may not be far if the opponent's arms are in very tight).
3. Use the hand on the floor to move around into a quasi-north-south position.
4. Work the arm underneath the opponent's elbow. Since the opponent knows what's coming, they keep their arms in very tight, so bring the hand up as far as it'll go — the thumb will only go so far before the opponent's strong arm stops further movement.
5. Once you've gone as far as you can, bring the hand across and drive the **elbow forward** (not back toward the opponent's shoulder — see mechanics note below).
6. Start a drive toward the opposite hip: the whole body goes forward, and the legs get the opponent moving.
7. Goal of the drive: take the opponent's elbow as high as possible. The lower the opponent's elbow sits toward the floor, the harder it is to get underneath and penetrate through it.
8. Once the elbow is raised, change wrist position — go to palm up.
9. From the palm-up position, take the knees off the mat.
10. From here, physically push the opponent's wrist over your hand.
11. If penetration is still hard, take the heel of the hand and push the opponent's hand forward — no matter how strong they make their arm, the heel of the hand can always push it forward. As the opponent's hand goes forward, space opens to bring your hand through.
12. Once the hand is through, you're in position to lock the figure-four.
13. As always, the middle finger controls the bend of the wrist — on both your own wrist and the opponent's.
14. From here, come around the corner, cover the head, and go into any of the finishing sequences (including against a locked-hands defense).

## Key mechanics notes

- **Elbow position matters**: the further your driving elbow is back toward the opponent's shoulder, the less effective the penetration. If the opponent makes a strong defensive arm and you try to lift from that back position, it's just your bicep working against them. Bringing the whole elbow forward lets you drive with your whole body instead.
- **Opponent's elbow height is the whole game**: elevated elbow = penetration is possible; elbow low toward the floor = very hard to get underneath.

## Recap from side control

1. Start in side control.
2. Move off the near side, then the far side, and move around.
3. Get to north-south: wrist comes in, elbow goes forward, then change wrist position.
4. Take the knees off the opponent until a good compressive force is achieved.
5. Push with the heel of the hand so the hand can go inside.
6. Once inside, lock the figure-four.
7. Block the training partner's head.
8. From here, any of the previously covered finishing sequences can be employed successfully.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Elbow penetration only works from north-south, not from perpendicular side control.
- Thumb goes under the tricep first, then the elbow drives forward (never back toward the opponent's shoulder) to power the penetration with the whole body, not just the bicep.
- Everything depends on getting the opponent's elbow elevated — low elbow kills the entry.
- Once the elbow is raised, switch to a palm-up wrist position, lift the knees off the mat for compressive drive, and use the heel of the hand to push the opponent's hand forward and create space to thread through.
- The middle finger controls wrist bend on both your own wrist and the opponent's once the figure-four is locked.
- Full sequence: side control → north-south → elbow forward/drive → elbow elevated → wrist flips palm-up → knees off mat → heel-of-hand push → hand threads through → figure-four locked → cover head → finish.
