# Standing Kimura Vs. The Single Leg (Head Outside) — Quick Reference

## Head inside vs. outside

| | Head inside | Head outside |
|---|---|---|
| Kimura access | Blocked — must stuff head first | Easy — no barrier |
| Risk | Lower (opponent less mobile) | Opponent can easily switch to double leg |

## Sequence

1. Control the head AND block the wrist (prevents the double-leg switch).
2. Punch hand in, step all the way up to his feet.
3. High-amplitude sumi-gaeshi-style throw.
4. Scissor legs — even if he fights back to his base, the kimura leverage sends him back down.
5. Legs spread 90 degrees on both sides of his body, elbow in → strong finish.

## Repeat run-through

1. Block head + wrist first.
2. Secure kimura grip, lower level, step up to his feet.
3. High-amplitude throw.
4. He turns into you → lift the trapped elbow.
5. Scissor legs, elbow down to floor → perfect kimura attacking position.

## Key points

- No head barrier = easier kimura access, but always block the wrist or he switches to a double leg.
- Step all the way to his feet before throwing.
- Kimura leverage is strong enough to send him back down even if he regains his base.
