# Connecting The Figure-Four Grip Under Resistance

## Overview

Addresses a common, frustrating problem: you've passed the guard, trapped the wrist, and want the kimura, but your training partner keeps his arm straight enough that you physically can't reach your own wrist to lock the figure-four. Two practical methods are given for closing that grip under resistance, plus a grip-choice principle (thumb vs. no-thumb) that determines whether the setup even survives the opponent's counters.

---

## Grip choice: thumb vs. no-thumb

- Optimal grab point on the opponent's wrist is established (from earlier material); for breaking mechanics, thumb or no-thumb makes little difference — but it makes a real difference for setups.
- If your hands are not yet locked and you grip with no thumb, the opponent can simply pommel his wrist out and escape, or take his hand inside yours and recover his arm position.
- Rule: whenever your hands are unlocked, use a thumb grip. Lock in the usual fashion. This makes it significantly harder for him to pommel his hand free.

## Problem: can't reach your own wrist

- Situation: everything else is in place (guard passed, opponent's wrist trapped, arms in) but the opponent keeps his arm straight and you can't physically close the figure-four.
- Tempting bad option: bring the hand up to the wrist and grab — can work if done well, but done badly it's a disaster because you end up gripping the short end of the lever.
- Don't try to claw for your own wrist — change your body position instead.

## Method 1: step over and pull to connect

1. Step over your opponent's body.
2. Bring your elbow down to his elbow.
3. With hands separated (not yet locked), lift — once his elbow comes off the mat, throttle the hands together and connect the figure-four.
4. Bring the wrist into a traditional bent-arm finishing position.
5. Step over the head and you're in position to break.

## Method 2: shorten the lever temporarily

1. Grab your own wrist halfway down the forearm — a leverage disaster as a finishing grip, but a legitimate way to hold someone: since it's a genuine figure-four, twisting the arm out isn't easy for him.
2. Bring everything down and lift.
3. Off the lift, work your grip down the wrist in two steps to the proper hold.
4. Sweep the hand in behind, drive forward, foot goes over — elbow to elbow, ready to break.

## Result

- Both tactics get you around the "can't connect the figure-four" problem and into a secure, standard figure-four hold from which the arm can be attacked normally.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Whenever your hands are unlocked in a kimura setup, grip with your thumb — no-thumb lets the opponent pommel free or recover his arm.
- Don't claw for your own wrist when the opponent keeps his arm straight — change your body position instead.
- Method 1: step over his body, elbow to his elbow, lift to get his elbow off the mat, then throttle the hands together into the figure-four.
- Method 2: temporarily grab your own forearm halfway down (short lever, but still a real figure-four), lift, then work your grip down the wrist in stages to the standard hold.
- Both methods end the same way: wrist into bent-arm position, step over the head, ready to break.
