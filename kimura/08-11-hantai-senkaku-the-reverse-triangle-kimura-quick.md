# Hantai Senkaku: The Reverse Triangle Kimura — Quick Reference

## Two ways to use the trap triangle position

1. **Trap triangle** — just cross the ankles.
2. **Hantai senkaku** — lock a real figure-four triangle, "wrong way," no strangle intent — purely to control head + arm.

## Entry steps

1. Enter trap triangle as usual from any grip-fighting sequence.
2. Force hand outside, grab the head.
3. Shimmy hips to the OPPOSITE side from the normal trap triangle finish.
4. Grab your own shin, pull ankle forward over his shoulder.
5. Lock the figure-four triangle.

## Critical detail #1 — heel placement

- Heel must lock **in front** of the shoulder.
- Heel behind the shoulder = he can still posture up and escape.

## Critical detail #2 — exposing the hidden wrist

1. Points of shoulders + elbows go back down to the mat (opposite of normal triangle reach).
2. Drive hips up toward the ceiling.
3. Wrist gets exposed → thumb in, lock it.
4. Reverse direction again (go back the way you came) → good arm bend, full figure-four Kimura grip.

## Finish

- Draw his elbow all the way to your armpit (further away = weaker finish).
- No need to cover the shoulder with the leg — you're not strangling.
- Works because his head literally cannot move — the standard Kimura escape (moving the head) is taken away.
- Break can happen in millimeters once locked.

## Notable application

- This exact chain (elbow two-on-one → trap triangle → hantai senkaku → exposed wrist Kimura) is what Ethan Crowenstein used for a famous ADCC trials Kimura break.
