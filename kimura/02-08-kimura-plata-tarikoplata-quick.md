# Kimura Plata (Tarikoplata) — Quick Reference

## Setup

1. Same start as cross kimura, come up and over, step in.
2. Release the figure-four grip — your hand holds your own thigh instead.
3. Second hand posts on the floor for stability.
4. Do NOT sit back.

## Finish (extension version)

1. Turn hips around the corner — this separates even the strongest locked hands.
2. Close one leg, extend the other.
3. Extension alone finishes most people.
4. For flexible ("gumby") opponents: after extending, turn hips in a circle toward their head for extra pressure.

## Finish (push/turn version)

1. Get hold of the arm, turn slight angle, step foot over top.
2. Withdraw pushing hand — leg replaces it as the pusher; former pushing hand becomes post.
3. Keep legs contracted (NOT overextended — disaster if overextended).
4. Push separates the hands; the hip TURN does the actual breaking.
5. Pull-dominant finish, not push-dominant — bring everything in, turn, lift elbow.

## vs. Cross Kimura

| Cross Kimura | Kimura Plata |
|---|---|
| Keeps figure-four grip | Releases grip, holds own thigh |
| Sits to buttocks | Stays upright |
| Leg strength separates | Hip turn separates + breaks |
| — | More stable (extra post hand), easier head position |

## Notes

- Nicknamed after Tarik, a Norwegian competitor who popularized it in competition.
- Danaher personally prefers cross kimura, but both are highly effective.
