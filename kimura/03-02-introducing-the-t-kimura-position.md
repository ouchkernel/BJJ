# Introducing The T-Kimura Position

## Overview

Introduces the T-kimura as "a system within a system" — one of the most important structural ideas in the whole kimura approach. It's the shape the kimura almost always takes the moment you first enter it out of an uncontrolled scramble, and understanding its nuances turns positions most people treat as neutral into an immediate attacking opportunity.

---

## Why the T-kimura matters

- It's an absolutely critical part of the overall approach to kimura — described as a system within the larger system.
- It almost always occurs in any uncontrolled scramble situation the moment you first enter a kimura.
- Many of the most innovative and useful methods for entering a kimura result directly in this T-kimura position.
- Once you understand its nuances, you can take what most people see as a neutral, uncontrolled situation and go immediately on the attack, creating real danger for your opponent.

## Restraint, not submission

- The T-kimura is a restraining position, not a submission position.
- To finish a kimura, your chest must be facing your opponent's chest — chest to chest. This works whether you're on top or on bottom, as long as the two chests face each other.
- In a T-kimura, the two chests don't face each other — they both point in the same direction, because the two bodies are perpendicular to one another. So there is no finish available from here.

## The halfway house

- Because it can't finish anyone, the T-kimura's value is as a halfway house: the position you land in first, in the middle of a scramble, when you get initial contact with a kimura.
- From there, the plan is to restrain your opponent with the T-kimura, then work to gain full control and transition either toward the finish or into another attack.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The T-kimura is a system within the system — it's the shape most kimura entries land in during a scramble, so its details matter enormously.
- It is a restraining hold only, never a finishing hold — finishing kimura requires chest-to-chest alignment, and in T-kimura the chests point the same direction, not at each other.
- Treat it as a halfway house: restrain first with the T-kimura, then work to convert that control into either a finish or a different attack.
