# Recovering To Turtle When The Double Leg Succeeds

## Overview

A contingency for when the long-arm kimura attack against the double leg doesn't get there in time — the opponent's double leg is too strong or too fast, and he bumps you down to the mat before the kimura is locked. Rather than losing the position, you recover into turtle and use the inside-arm/outside-arm concept from the earlier turtle-position kimura material to get right back into the attack.

---

## The problem scenario

- Opponent is driving hard on a double leg — long arm and short arm present, head in good driving position.
- You feel him lifting your leg and your hands are forced to the mat before you can get the kimura grip fully locked.
- Rather than fighting a losing position, drop down into turtle.

## Recovery using the inside-arm concept

- Coming down to turtle recreates a position already covered: the inside arm / outside arm framework from the turtle-position kimura chapters.
- Once in turtle, you have a clean inside-arm opportunity — lock the opponent's inside arm.
- Put your knee up, the same mechanic used in the earlier turtle work.
- This lands you back across your training partner's body in the same perfect attacking position seen before.
- Step over into the winning position.

## Alternate demonstration: opponent gets deep and lifts fast

- If the opponent gets deep on the double leg and the lift is too strong and too fast, you get taken straight down before he can transfer up to a seatbelt or other back control.
- Come in and lock — get the same grip over the opponent's forearm used throughout the kimura system.
- Put your leg up and take the opponent through by rolling underneath your own leg.
- This lands you in a good attacking position.
- From there: separate his hands, bring the hand down, switch, step over his head — this puts you right on his power line, ready for a strong attack.

---

## Key Takeaways

- If the double leg is too fast/strong to stop with the long-arm kimura, don't fight the takedown — drop to turtle instead of getting flattened.
- Turtle recovery reuses the inside-arm/outside-arm framework already established: lock the inside arm, post the knee, and you land back in the same attacking position as the standing dedicated turtle-kimura work.
- If the opponent gets deep and lifts before he can secure a seatbelt/back control, lock the forearm grip, post the leg, and roll underneath your own leg to take him through into the attacking position.
- The finishing sequence from there is consistent with the rest of the system: separate the hands, switch, step over the head, and arrive on the power line for the kimura finish.
