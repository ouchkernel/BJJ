# The Olympic Bar Variation For Turtle Position — Quick Reference

## Setup: building the Olympic bar

1. Head forward, score one-on-one grip.
2. Convert to two-on-one.
3. Cover his head, knee over the top.
4. Lever his arm upward.
5. Come in elbow to elbow; hand crosses to opposite shoulder — forearm + upper arm form a bar across his back.
6. Keep the barring hand up by the shoulder — never let it sit low.
7. Result: he cannot get his hand back down through your arm to base defensively.

## Finishing sequence (two phrasings of the same idea)

**Version A — turn to dorsal:**
1. Hand on floor, pin/break him down to shoulder + hip, turn to dorsal position.
2. Swim hands through, back to elbow-to-elbow.
3. Break his grip, get his hand behind his back.
4. Elbow down on floor.
5. Step boldly across his body.
6. Arrive on the power line, kimura ready.

**Version B — draw elbow, punch second arm:**
1. Pin down to shoulder + hip.
2. Draw elbow up, punch in second arm.
3. Classic frame position, kimura in place (elbow to shoulder line, his hand behind scapula).
4. Elbow leads, step boldly across, head moves back.
5. Power line — strong breaking position.

## Why use it

- Very stable, mechanically efficient, hard for opponent to balance-break or resist.

## Bonus note

- If opponent's hands are too far away to reach at all: good news, not bad — he's balled up tight to avoid back attacks, which makes the one-on-one grip easy to get.
