# Udigatami Mechanics In Detail

## Overview

Udigatami (a straight armlock) is a move people attempt constantly but very rarely finish. This chapter breaks down why the conventional teaching method underperforms and lays out a modified leg and elbow configuration that closes its two biggest holes: the opponent bringing his head in to rotate the elbow free, and the opponent simply sliding out the far side once the classic knee-pinch is abandoned.

---

## The problem with the conventional udigatami

- Most instruction shows two knees pinched around the training partner's head and shoulders, with a variety of hand/arm-fold methods to control the trapped arm.
- The opponent's primary defense against this: bring his head toward you. If he can do that, he can rotate his elbow and free himself — a very common failure point.

## The modified leg configuration

1. Instead of focusing on pinching the knees together, place the near knee as a **wedge in front of the opponent's shoulder** — a cross-face wedge. This directly blocks the head-toward-you escape that a conventional pinch doesn't stop.
2. Problem this creates: with two knees now both in front instead of pinched around, there's nothing stopping the opponent sliding away sideways ("like spaghetti").
3. Fix: hook the bottom foot behind the training partner's armpit. This foot actively hooks and controls, replacing the missing knee-pinch function.
4. Often the second leg comes across the body as well, reinforcing the position.
5. Result: with the legs alone (cross-face knee + armpit-hooking foot), the opponent finds it surprisingly difficult to either yank out or push in.

## Elbow and finishing mechanics

- Point your elbow at the opponent's ear rather than the two common weaker options:
  - Locking straight across, which lets his elbow easily rotate inside your arms.
  - A slightly adjusted version that's "not much of an improvement."
- Going elbow to elbow and reinforcing your own elbow produces the strongest breaking mechanics available for this hold.
- Instead of merely compressing down with the arms, actively **move your hips away from the opponent while moving your head toward him** — this is the true breaking point, and there's still further range to move beyond it for a deeper finish.

## Summary of the configuration

- Knee: cross-face wedge (not pinched).
- Foot: hooked behind the armpit.
- Second leg: often thrown across.
- Elbow: pointed at the opponent's ear, reinforced elbow-to-elbow.
- Finishing motion: hips away, head toward opponent.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The conventional two-knee pinch fails because it doesn't stop the opponent bringing his head in to rotate the elbow free — that's the primary escape it needs to address.
- Trade the pinch for a cross-face knee wedge, but you must add a foot hook behind the armpit or the opponent slides straight out the open side.
- Elbow-to-ear (not locking straight or at a shallow angle) plus elbow-to-elbow reinforcement gives the strongest breaking leverage.
- The actual finish comes from moving your hips away and your head toward the opponent, not from squeezing down with the arms alone.
