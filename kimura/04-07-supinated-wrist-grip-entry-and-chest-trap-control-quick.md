# Supinated Wrist Grip Entry And Chest-Trap Control — Quick Reference

## The problem it solves

- North-south elbow-penetration entries stall: wrist is too thick to fit under the elbow, opponent turns elbows down and scrambles to guard.

## Core idea

- Fingers are thinner than a wrist → dig fingers under his **wrist** instead of forcing your wrist under his **elbow**.
- Grip alone is weak (he can just lift his fist free) → pin it with your **chest**.

## Steps (from across the side, opponent has two arms in tight)

1. Take a palm-up (supinated) grip.
2. Break your arm back, punch it through under his wrist, grip it.
3. Wedge/pin that wrist under your chest.
4. He yanks — arm is stuck, can't come free.
5. Draw him up and pull into the winning Kimura position.

## Finish options once trapped

- Hand straight behind the back.
- Throw legs over.
- Bicep-slicer position.

All effective — pick based on what's available.

## Repeat drill cue

- Palm-up grip → fight through → lock wrist → wedge under chest → pull to expose space under elbow → hand into winning position → leg across body → finish.
