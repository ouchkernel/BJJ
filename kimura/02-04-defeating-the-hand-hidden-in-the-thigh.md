# Defeating The Hand Hidden In The Thigh

## Overview

Addresses a second, more dangerous way an opponent reinforces the kimura arm: tucking the endangered hand inside his own thigh. This is called one of the safest things an opponent can do, because it doesn't just reinforce the hand — it straightens the whole arm, removing the bend that a kimura needs. The counter relies on a legal all-four-knuckle grip that lets you strip the hand out regardless of how tightly it's held.

---

## Why the thigh hide is a bigger problem than locked hands

- Tucking the hand inside the thigh accomplishes two things at once: it reinforces the hand, and it straightens the entire arm.
- When an opponent instead grabs his own hand (locked hands), there's still a good bend in the arm, which actually facilitates the kimura.
- When the opponent reinforces by tucking into his own thigh, the arm gets straightened out — a much harder shape to work with.

## The knuckle grip — legality

- It is illegal in jiu-jitsu to grab one, two, or three fingers (single or double digits) and pull.
- It is fully legal to grab all four knuckles together as a line.
- Grabbing all four knuckles lets you strip the hand out no matter how tightly the opponent holds it against his thigh.

## The sequence

1. Start in the dorsal position; hold on one-handed with confidence by bringing your chest down to his elbow for control.
2. With your free hand palm up, find and grip the knuckle line — all four knuckles together.
3. Withdraw the training partner's arm out from his thigh.
4. Take the hand behind the back; your other (reinforcing) hand supports it.
5. If the opponent tries to straighten his arm back out and escape, climb over the wrist — don't let go — and lock it down.
6. Bring your elbow down to the floor, cover the training partner's head, and you're in the perfect position for the break.

## Practical notes

- The chest-to-elbow control is what lets you hold the position confidently with only one hand while the other hand works the knuckle-grip extraction.
- Climbing over the wrist is specifically the answer to the opponent trying to straighten his arm free after you've already extracted it — don't release your grip during that scramble.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Hand-inside-thigh is more dangerous than locked hands because it straightens the whole arm, removing the bend the kimura needs.
- Never grab one, two, or three fingers — that's illegal. Always grab all four knuckles as a line — that's legal and strips the hand regardless of grip strength.
- Sequence: chest-to-elbow one-handed control → palm-up four-knuckle grip → withdraw the hand from the thigh → hand behind the back, reinforced by the other hand → climb the wrist if he tries to straighten back out → elbow down, cover the head, finish.
