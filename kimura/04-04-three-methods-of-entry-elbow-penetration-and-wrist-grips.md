# Three Methods Of Entry — Elbow Penetration And Wrist Grips

## Overview

The three main methods for getting into a kimura hold against an opponent who has good, sound defensive arm position (tombstone defense): elbow penetration, and two wrist-grip variations distinguished by supination/pronation. This lesson lays out the choice criteria and the basic wrist-control rule that underlies all three.

---

## The three methods

1. **Elbow penetration** — take the hand and penetrate underneath the opponent's elbow, getting the hand into position to immediately lock up a kimura. Any method that goes under the elbow and penetrates through it counts as an elbow penetration method.
2. **Supinated wrist grip** — starting not at the elbow but at the wrist, with the gripping hand palm up.
3. **Pronated wrist grip** — starting at the wrist, with the gripping hand palm down.

## Choosing supinated vs. pronated

- General rule, based on where the opponent's hand sits relative to their chest:
  - Hand **above** the chest → use a **supinated** grip.
  - Hand **below** the chest → use a **pronated** grip.

## Wrist control mechanics

- When first securing someone's wrist, grip with the thumb.
- How you choose to finish is a personal preference — some prefer no thumb at finish, some prefer thumb. Arms have been broken successfully both ways, so it doesn't make a significant difference at the finish.
- It does matter, however, at the moment of first controlling the wrist — use the thumb to secure the grip, whether the grip is pronated or supinated.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Kimura entries against a defended opponent break down into three methods: elbow penetration, supinated wrist grip, pronated wrist grip.
- Choose supinated vs. pronated based on hand height relative to the opponent's chest — above the chest, go supinated; below the chest, go pronated.
- Secure the wrist initially with the thumb regardless of grip type; the finishing grip (thumb or no thumb) is a matter of preference and doesn't meaningfully change effectiveness.
- Mastery of kimura requires competence in all three entry methods, not just one.
