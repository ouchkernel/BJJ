# Bottom Knee To Three-Quarter Juji Gatame

## Overview

A third option out of the T-kimura bottom-knee attack: instead of finishing with a rear triangle (ushiro sankaku), pass the foot all the way through your training partner's head and lock a three-quarter juji gatame. The preference for the three-quarter version over a traditional armbar is explicit — it controls the head and shoulders so well that the opponent can't spin out of it, creating a very strong finishing position.

---

## Sequence

1. From T-kimura, opponent works his way up; slow him down at the elbow.
2. As the knee comes through underneath him, bring your leg over your training partner's shoulder and start correcting his body over to the side.
3. Instead of continuing into ushiro sankaku (the rear triangle), simply slip your foot over your training partner's head.
4. Come up so your head is over your training partner's hands.
5. Pass the foot through to lock up the three-quarter juji gatame rather than a traditional juji.

## Why the three-quarter version

- When your opponent tries to spin out of the lock, he finds it very difficult to get past your control of his head and shoulders.
- That head-and-shoulder control is what makes this a strong finishing position, favored over the traditional juji gatame in this situation.

## Second demonstration (different angle)

1. Opponent explodes up from bottom; bring your knee underneath him.
2. Pass your foot over the top of his shoulder and return your training partner down to the mat as he fights to defend his arms.
3. Pass your foot through and bring the second leg over.
4. Always look to pass your foot inside and underneath your training partner's arm to lock up the three-quarter juji gatame.
5. Begin straightening out the arm.
6. When he tries to turn out of the lock, the three-quarter juji is very strong at controlling his head and shoulders, creating strong breaks.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Out of T-kimura bottom-knee control, once your leg is over the opponent's shoulder, you have a choice: continue to ushiro sankaku (rear triangle), or slip the foot over his head and pass it through to a three-quarter juji gatame.
- Head position matters — come up so your head is over your training partner's hands before passing the foot through.
- The three-quarter juji is favored over a traditional juji because it controls head and shoulders so tightly that the opponent can't spin past it to escape.
- Always pass the foot inside and underneath the opponent's arm, not around the outside, to set up this variation.
- This is a clean way to convert a T-kimura control situation directly into a strong arm-lock attack.
