# Bending The Arm And Exposing The Back — Quick Reference

## Elbow angle preference

- Both >90° and <90° bends can break the arm.
- Preference: **<90°** — more catastrophic breaks, better control, lowers his ability to resist.

## The problem

- Flat back on the ground = no way to pull his arm in behind his back. Must expose the back first.

## Step-by-step: bending the arm behind the back

1. **Post a support leg** (hard to do with both knees down).
2. **Point your elbow at the ceiling**, elbow-to-elbow with him.
   - This lifts his back off the ground.
   - Also puts his wrist behind his own elbow.
3. **Throttle your hand over the top of his wrist** — motorcycle-throttle motion, arm-wrestling style (get your hand ON TOP of his).
4. Draw his hand all the way in behind his back → mechanically weak position, nearly impossible for him to pull free.

## Arm-wrestling analogy

- Whoever gets hand-on-top in arm wrestling dominates the other's arm to the table.
- Same "get on top" principle bends the training partner's arm behind his back here.

## Key point

- Works against a strong, resistant arm — this is leverage, not strength. A weaker person can fully control a much stronger opponent with correct mechanics.
