# Half Guard To Closed Guard Kimura Conversion — Quick Reference

## The logic

- Closed guard kimura = safe to hold, hard to enter.
- Half guard kimura = easy to enter, risky to hold.
- Fix: enter from half guard, convert to closed guard before finishing.

## Starting control

1. Shallow knee shield to prevent chest-to-chest.
2. Bar across opponent's shoulder line (elbow + hand) to hold off crowding/cross-face.
3. Go outside the elbow — want hand behind back, but opponent may hide it inside his own thigh.

## Conversion A — one-handed kimura frame

1. Hand hidden? Come out onto elbow, curl wrist around his forearm (one-handed kimura frame).
2. Second foot to his knee, sliding action, near knee comes in and blocks.
3. Sit down, shimmy hips out — reopens the kimura even with hand hidden.
4. Keep head up, body tucked, weight on opponent.
5. Invert far leg, cross feet (closed guard) — shoulder line, finish.

## Conversion B — arm extraction

1. Move opponent down the body; if hand can't be extracted, come up into one-handed elbow frame.
2. Foot to his knee, shimmy hips back, knee to outside position, lock over his leg.
3. Lift pelvis off floor over him — arm extraction.
4. Shoot hips out, invert second leg, up to shoulder line — closed guard, finish.

## Remember

- Never force the direct hand extraction if it's failing — switch to the one-handed elbow frame first.
- The finish always ends in closed guard (crossed/inverted legs) on the shoulder line.
