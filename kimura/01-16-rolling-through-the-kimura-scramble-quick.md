# Rolling Through The Kimura Scramble — Quick Reference

## Core principle

- **Hand behind the back > the pin.** As long as the hand stays trapped behind his back, you dominate the scramble even if you lose the positional pin.
- Don't panic and scramble back to "neutral" when the pin is lost — that just trades a winning position for an even one (a head-height battle you might lose).

## Don't do this

- Don't let go of the arm to fight for the pin (unstable, usually ends back in neutral anyway).
- Don't stay on the same side of his body as he rises (lets his hand swing back to the front — disaster).

## Roll-through steps

1. Feel the pin slipping as he rises — don't fight it.
2. Exaggerate the loss: head to his elbow, jackknife your hips, head down. Let him come up easily.
3. Go around the corner, out of his path (not staying on his side).
4. Turn everything to your guiding direction and take him over.
5. Come up with his head covered by your leg (stops him continuing the roll).
6. Land back in the same pin, on his diagonal power line, kimura intact.
7. Apply pull-dominant finishing mechanics.

## Why it's safe to "lose" position

- Trapped hand behind the back neutralizes normal jiu-jitsu positional hierarchy.
- OK to willingly give up bottom side control (even give up your back) mid-roll.
- If he tries to pin/consolidate instead of rolling through, break the arm from underneath.

## Mat-side reminder

- This exact scramble recurs across many kimura positions — drill it, it's not a one-off.
