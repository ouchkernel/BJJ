# Kimura Plata (Tarikoplata)

## Overview

The sixth Kimura variant, and the odd one out — a hybrid between an omoplata and a Kimura, commonly called a Kimura plata or Tarikoplata after the talented Norwegian athlete famous for using it. The classic version simply throws the leg over from a figure-four grip; the version taught here modifies the grip first to open more space and create a more secure hold before the leg ever moves.

---

## What It Is

- A hybrid move sitting somewhere between an omoplata and a Kimura — jokingly called a "Kimura plata."
- Famously used by a talented Norwegian athlete, and often named after him — Tarikoplata.
- Classic version: start with a figure-four grip, then simply throw the leg over.

## The Coached Variation

The version taught here differs from the classic in the grip itself:

- Start with a **partial** figure-four grip rather than the full one.
- Instead of gripping the wrist, put the hand flat on the training partner's back.
- This exposes more of the hip for the leg to step over.
- Benefits of this modification:
  - Greatly facilitates the action for anyone less flexible (or, as noted, anyone who's had a hip replacement).
  - Creates more space for the leg to travel through.
  - Makes for a more secure grip and prevents hand slippage at the last minute — a real risk with the classic full figure-four grip.

## Execution Sequence

1. Start with the modified partial figure-four grip: hand flat on the training partner's back.
2. Throw the leg across — everything tucks into the crook of the knee.
3. Put your head over your own hand and turn your hips out to the side.
4. As the right leg advances forward and the left leg comes around, the hips travel across the opponent's hips.
5. Move out to the side, taking your hip all the way through onto your own buttock — you should have the sensation of "falling off" your opponent.
6. This hip travel produces strong pulling pressure and a very strong finish.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Kimura plata / Tarikoplata is the sixth Kimura variant — a hybrid with the omoplata, not derived from the standard figure-four Kimura grip.
- Classic version: full figure-four grip, then throw the leg over.
- Coached version: partial figure-four with the hand flat on the opponent's back instead of gripping the wrist — this opens more hip space for the leg and creates a more secure, slip-resistant grip.
- Key hip mechanic: as the legs travel across the opponent's body, drive your own hip through onto your buttock, aiming for the sensation of falling off your opponent — that hip travel is what generates the strong pulling finish.
- The hand-on-back modification is specifically recommended for less flexible athletes (explicitly including post-hip-replacement grapplers).
