# Kimura Recovery From Single Leg On The Knees — Quick Reference

## Situation

Single-leg attack, both on knees. Opponent successfully angles off, bumps your hand, peeks his head out toward your waist — danger of him taking your back.

## Recovery steps

1. Hand stays inside his forearm, hugging his elbow.
2. Bring outside leg up.
3. Pull elbow down to the mat, look under your own armpit, travel across his body.
4. Lock the initial kimura grip.
5. Read his reaction:
   - Aggressive turn-up into you → foot into his hip → sumi gaeshi–style roll → elevate with the kimura arm → send him through.
6. Land in the **T-kimura position** (perpendicular control, elbow locked).

## If he tries an aggressive top hold instead

- Same elevating (sumi gaeshi) foot action — push off other foot, elevate with the arm, send him up into a pin on top.

## From the T-kimura

- Knock him back down to hip/tip, scissor legs, re-enter the kimura — repeatable if he keeps trying to stand.
- Many further kimura attacks are available from here once he's grounded.

## Key point

Losing the head-trap (opponent peeking his head out) is not a disaster — hand-hug-elbow + outside leg + pull-elbow-under-armpit converts the scramble straight back into a kimura and a dominant T-position.
