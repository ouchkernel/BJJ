# Standing Kimura — Flanking Tactics To Get The Foot Inside

## Overview

Extends the Kimura-vs-butterfly-guard game to a standing posture. The core problem addressed here is purely positional: against a well-structured butterfly guard, simply stepping a foot inside runs straight into the opponent's defense (grabs, foot-in-front-of-foot, etc.). The solution is a family of flanking tactics — lateral movement combined with hand/shoulder control — that force the opponent's feet to react and open the inside lane needed to start the Kimura game.

---

## The problem: getting a foot inside against good structure

- When attacking a strong butterfly guard from standing, the first requirement is getting a foot to the inside position — but this isn't easy against an opponent like Mateus, whose feet are well positioned.
- If you just try to step in, you run into his offense: he can grab your legs, or put his foot in front of yours to block the entry.
- A strong defensive structure (feet close together, active hands and legs) makes a direct step-in risky — you can run into his hands, his legs, "all kinds of business."

## Flanking tactic: forcing the opponent to react

1. Stand in front of your opponent, start posting and moving to take a flank position — force him to turn and face you.
2. Generate lateral movement: every time you move to a side, one of his feet has to follow you, since his feet are close together.
3. That lateral requirement inevitably opens up space as he adjusts to stay square to you.
4. Compound the effect with your hands: control one of his hands and the opposite shoulder to actively move him around, creating more openings.
5. As he turns/resets to face you again, step into the space that opens up.
6. A common finishing touch: fake a wrist grip and pull one of his legs as he sits back up, capturing the inside position.

## Additional tactical tools

- Flanking (lateral) movements.
- Lifting movements.
- Push-pull movements.
- Described as "a thousand tactical tricks" available between these categories — the goal of all of them is the same: get a foot to the inside position.
- Once a foot is inside, you can start playing the Kimura game from there.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Against a well-structured standing butterfly guard, don't try to force a foot inside directly — you'll run into grabs or foot-blocking.
- Flanking is the core mechanism: lateral movement forces a foot to follow, which opens space; combine it with hand/opposite-shoulder control to actively move the opponent.
- A fake wrist grip paired with pulling a leg as he sits back up is a reliable way to capture the inside foot position.
- Flanking, lifting, and push-pull movements are three named categories of tools for the same job — getting the foot inside — treat them as a toolkit, not a single fixed sequence.
- Getting the foot to the inside position is the prerequisite that unlocks the standing Kimura game covered next.
