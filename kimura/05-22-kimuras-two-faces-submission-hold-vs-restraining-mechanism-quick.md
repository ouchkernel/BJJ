# Kimura's Two Faces — Submission Hold Vs Restraining Mechanism — Quick Reference

## The two faces

| Face | Hand position | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Submission hold | Driven behind the back | Attack the shoulder, finish |
| Restraining mechanism | Stays anterior (front of body) | Control opponent into dominant position — no finish intended |

## Restraining-mechanism entry (same drill, different intent)

1. Partner shoots leg, gets initial angle.
2. Hand on his head for control; shoot other hand through, wrist-deep.
3. If he snaps your head forward — use the trapping hand as a base, stay stable.
4. Trust the frame: he cannot take your back, lift you, or switch to a double leg (second leg already posted).
5. Cross wrists to lock; easy to put him to the floor.
6. Branch: commit back to T-Kimura, OR snake hand through + hip-heist as he stands → end up on top.

## Why drill this specific version

- Builds rapid, smooth, efficient entries — great single-leg defense on the mat.
- Directly counters strong underhook players coming up from bottom: he stands up successfully but still can't take you down.
- Drill loop: sit him through, bring him over, return to start, repeat.
- Works even when opponent gets deep on the leg and forms a real angle.
