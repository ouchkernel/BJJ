# Preventing The Back Take During The Inside Arm Roll — Quick Reference

## The risk

- Bad roll → you end up between his legs → he shuffles up, you slide down → he gets two hooks in → you're now defending.

## The rule that prevents it

- Good news: controlling his **elbow** stops him from getting his hips above yours — the one thing he needs to exploit the back.
- He may still "have your back," but he can't do much with it if you're tight on the elbow.

## During the roll

1. Leg may get tangled in his — fine, as long as your **knee stays high above him**.
2. Keep hips above his belly button, toward his chest → perpendicular angle.
3. Result: he gets at most one hook, minimal real control.
4. Pull his feet apart, knees to chest, if he tries to lock in.

## Clearing tangled legs

1. Actively free legs — don't let him hold position to shimmy up.
2. If he gets crab-ride hooks and tries to lift you: extract, move legs to the side.
3. Bring legs over the top, scissor → back to good attacking position, head over his hips.

## The one rule that decides the scramble

- His hips higher than yours = he wins.
- Your hips higher than his = you win.
- Ride high. Don't let your hips sink toward his.

## Recap checklist

- [ ] Expose elbow, fight hand inside.
- [ ] Roll while staying over his chest with your hips.
- [ ] Keep knees toward chest; clear his legs with your hands if needed.
- [ ] Hook your foot onto his foot to clear his legs (works regardless of his leg strength).
- [ ] Pendulum the legs across, scissor to a good side position.
- [ ] Ready to apply kimura.
