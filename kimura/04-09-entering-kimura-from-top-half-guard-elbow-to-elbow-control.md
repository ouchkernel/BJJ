# Entering Kimura From Top Half Guard — Elbow-To-Elbow Control

## Overview

A short foundational chapter that sets up the rest of the half-guard Kimura material: the general rules for attacking Kimura from top half guard against an opponent who has trapped your leg and is fighting with an underhook. The key idea is that the opponent's underhook is only as dangerous as your head position allows it to be, and the fix — as in prior chapters — is elbow-to-elbow control.

---

## The situation

- You're on top in half guard; the opponent (Matthias, in the demonstration) has your leg well trapped.
- The bottom player is commonly advised to take an underhook in this position, and does so in one of two ways:
  - Staying down with the shoulder on the mat.
  - Propping up on the elbow — referred to as a **power prop**.
- It doesn't matter which variation the opponent uses: as long as you can access his top arm, Kimura attacks from here are very successful.

## The head-position warning

- The opponent's ability to make his underhook effective is directly proportional to the relative head position between the two of you.
- If your head drifts above (north of) the opponent's head, he can easily use the underhook to expose your back and take you out of position.
- As your head drifts north, the underhook becomes progressively more disruptive to your position.
- Conclusion: you must manage both the opponent's underhook and, more importantly, your own head position.

## The fix: elbow-to-elbow control

1. Take your near arm (left arm in the demo) across over the opponent's near arm.
2. Maintain a strong elbow-to-elbow position.
3. Keep your head over the opponent's hips (not drifting north).
4. With elbow-to-elbow control and correct head position held together, it becomes very hard for the opponent to hike you up with the underhook.
5. From here, it becomes relatively simple to access the wrist and begin working Kimura attacks from half guard.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Whether the opponent's underhook comes with the shoulder down or propped up on the elbow (power prop) doesn't matter — access to the top arm is what determines success.
- The underhook's effectiveness scales with your head position: head drifting north of the opponent's head makes the underhook dangerous; head kept low and over his hips neutralizes it.
- The core fix is elbow-to-elbow control, paired with keeping your head over the opponent's hips — together these shut the underhook down and open the path to the wrist.
- This elbow-to-elbow foundation is the setup used across the following half-guard Kimura entries (one-handed restraint into T-Kimura, freeing the trapped knee, and chin-position underhook negation).
