# Hand And Elbow Placement: Lever And Fulcrum — Quick Reference

## The lever

- Runs from opponent's **elbow** to your own **smallest (pinky) knuckle**.
- Grabbing the wrist = short end of lever (weak). Grabbing the hand = crosses a flexing joint (weak).
- **Optimal**: middle finger hooked over the fold of his wrist (works thumb or thumbless).

## Pull-hand (second hand) checklist

1. Cover your own knuckles with your grip.
2. Bring your wrist all the way up — **no daylight** between your wrist and his wrist.
3. Hook your middle finger over your own wrist too.
4. Daylight = he can slip his hand back toward his body and escape; also loosens the lock for the finish.

## Elbow placement

- Sweep **elbow to elbow** — as close as possible.
- Elbow drifting toward his armpit → weaker lever.
- Elbow drifting away from his elbow → kimura degenerates into a push instead of a pull.

## Core principle

- Kimura = lever and fulcrum in action. Power comes from legs/hips but is *transmitted* through correct hand/elbow placement (car engine + transmission analogy).
- Aim for a **pull-dominant** kimura — the pull arm should do the primary work, not the push/stopper hand.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Middle finger over his wrist fold
- [ ] No daylight between your wrist and his
- [ ] Elbow swept to his elbow
- [ ] Force is pull-dominant, not push-dominant
