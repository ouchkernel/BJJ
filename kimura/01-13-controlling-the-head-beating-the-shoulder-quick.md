# Controlling The Head: Beating The Shoulder — Quick Reference

## Trinity of power (kimura strength factors)

1. Legs incorporated into the kimura.
2. Elbow brought up to the shoulder line.
3. Head controlled.

Get 1 of 3 strong = strong kimura. Get all 3 = devastating kimura.

Why head control matters: a free head lets the opponent weaken, then escape, the lock.

## Beating the shoulder — steps

1. Get hip height over training partner's shoulder (past his framing hand/forearm).
2. Turn hips in to collapse his arm.
3. Knee into his ribs.
4. Back-step — weight moves directly over his shoulder line.
5. Lift the elbow joint, step your leg directly over his head.

## Leg placement rule

- Do NOT drop the knee down for a tighter trap — you lose your ability to pull.
- Keep foot up near his shoulder line — leg stays free to act as your **drive leg**.

## Finishing mechanics

- Drive leg pushes off the floor.
- Head/spine/back pull away from opponent.
- Result: strong pull on the elbow; he can't follow because your leg caps his head rise.

## Common mistake

- Just pushing with the arms → weak, anemic kimura. Fix: trap head + drive leg + pull with the back, not the arms.
