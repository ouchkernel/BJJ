# Wrist Control Kimura — Mirrored Repeat And Recap — Quick Reference

## Diagnosis (recap)

Closed guard kimura is a rare finish at elite level — not because kimura is weak, but because the traditional setup fails, and the traditional finish is usually a weak push instead of a pull.

## Full sequence (recap)

1. Get wrist control instead of using momentum: two-on-one grip, drive down to the hip.
2. Thumb grip on the wrist.
3. Bring opponent forward with your legs over your shoulder, make him base out.
4. Turn the corner, lock the kimura (no sit-up needed).
5. As he recovers: shimmy hips out, invert near knee so it points **down** (never up — up lets him posture and pressure your arm).
6. Cross feet, build a strong resistant shape as he posts and pushes.
7. Turn until far shoulder + near shoulder + elbow line up = the shoulder line.
8. Pull his elbow to your armpit.
9. Finish.

## Finishing refinements

- **One hand** is enough once the shoulder line + armpit connection are set — short pull.
- **Two hands, arched body**: lift the hips off the floor (hips down + bent body = you're pushing, not pulling). Arched, hip-lifted, two-handed pull = devastating.

## Mirrored side

Same sequence works identically on the other side — drilled here to confirm symmetry.

## Core rule

Hand controlled → bicep controlled (same arm) → head pulled out of your path over your shoulder. All three traditional stoppers (hands, biceps, head) die at once from wrist control — no speed required. Elbow-to-armpit + shoulder line + hips lifted = the actual break.
