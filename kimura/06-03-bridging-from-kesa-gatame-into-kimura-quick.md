# Bridging From Kesa Gatame Into Kimura — Quick Reference

## Trigger

Holding kizu (kimura) kesa gatame, opponent swims through and threatens your back with an underhook.

## Bridge-and-scissor entry

1. Surrender the head (never the real control point).
2. Step forward, commit down toward the mat.
3. Elevate your hips above his hips — legs become weightless.
4. Start locking the kimura grip.
5. Scissor legs across his body to the far side.

## Getting from lock to break (diagonal line of force)

1. Initial lock = position only, not a break.
2. If he blocks your hip with a hand: sit down, beat his shoulder.
3. Come back the other way — elbow to the floor.
4. Beat the shoulder again, step over his head.
5. Move up to the idealized (diagonal) line of force.
6. Legs separate ~90 degrees, elbow to his elbow → even a small pull finishes it.

## Alternate entries into the same finish

- **From standard side control**: he punches a hand through → switch to kizu kesa gatame (shoulder/elbow focus) → elevate hips, split legs, balance on ribcage → scissor → beat shoulder, step over head → line of force.
- **From framing/underhook fight**: he frames and pushes your face → pick up the underhook → back to kizu kesa gatame → if he shocks your head forward, let the head go (control is elbow/shoulder, so his back-take is slowed, not immediate) → as he drives forward, expose the arm, grip forearm, walk the corner, elbow to floor, body up to head/shoulders, step over head, finish.

## Key point

Kesa gatame here is a bridge, not a hold to sit in — control shoulder/elbow just long enough to scissor into a pull-dominant kimura on the diagonal shoulder line.
