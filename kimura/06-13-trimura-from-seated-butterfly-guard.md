# Trimura From Seated Butterfly Guard

## Overview

Introduces the "trimura" — a hybrid attack combining triangle and kimura mechanics — and shows the grip entry from seated butterfly guard, the preferred setup position for this family of attacks. The entry is built on winning a two-on-one grip battle for a single arm before kicking the legs into a trap triangle.

---

## Why seated butterfly guard

- Seated guard is described as almost always the starting point for triangle-family action in both training and competition.
- Closed guard entries into this family exist too, but seated guard entries are generally favored because the greater mobility of a seated guard makes it very easy to get into trap triangles and work either kimuras or strangles from there.

## Grip entry mechanics

1. The opponent takes an inside tie, gripping with one hand (e.g., a grip inside your bicep).
2. Control the opponent at the elbow with one hand.
3. Bring the opposite hand across to control the top of the forearm at the pit of the elbow.
4. This produces a **two-on-one control** over the opponent's arm — when the opponent tries to pull away, it becomes very difficult for them to free the arm.

## From grip to trap triangle to kimura

1. With the arm trapped by the two-on-one grip, it becomes relatively easy to kick the legs into place and lock an initial trap triangle.
2. Make the adjustment out to the side, inverting the far leg — this converts the position into a strong kimura attack rather than a pure triangle/strangle.
3. Cross the ankles: this gives strong control of the opponent's head and shoulders, and the opponent finds it very difficult to defend from here.
4. Everything is lined up on the power line — the opponent's elbow is directly in line with their shoulders — producing tremendous finishing pressure.

## Practical notes

- A basic triangle strangle is available directly from the trap-triangle position, but this lesson is specifically about diverting that entry into the kimura (trimura) finish instead.
- The name "trimura" reflects that this is a genuine hybrid: the leg configuration is a triangle/trap-triangle, but the actual submission mechanism applied is the kimura shoulder lock.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Trimura = triangle leg configuration + kimura submission mechanic, entered from seated butterfly guard.
- The entry depends on winning a two-on-one grip on a single arm: control the elbow with one hand, the forearm/pit of the elbow with the other.
- Once the arm is trapped two-on-one, kicking into a trap triangle is straightforward.
- Adjusting out to the side and inverting the far leg converts the trap triangle into the kimura attack; crossing the ankles locks strong head/shoulder control.
- Seated guard is preferred over closed guard for this entry because its mobility makes trap-triangle entries much easier.
