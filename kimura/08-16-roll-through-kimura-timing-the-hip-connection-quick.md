# Roll-Through Kimura: Timing The Hip Connection — Quick Reference

## Why you need this

You won't always be able to control the head finishing a kimura from side control top. If the hand stays behind the back, you're still in control even when it looks chaotic.

## The situation

- Bottom side control, kimura grip secured, but head is uncontrolled and opponent sits up.
- Bad: head uncontrolled, risk of going backwards.
- Good: hand still behind his back = you still own him.

## Rule of thumb

Don't go backward when he rises — roll **forward** so he follows you. You're still in the driver's seat as long as the hand is behind his back.

## Correct execution

1. Pitch across his body — hips directly on his hips.
2. Maintain hip-to-hip contact through the roll.
3. Your leg follows him through the roll (don't let it lag).
4. Land in braking position, right on the power line.
5. **Critical**: quadricep stays glued to his stomach/abdominals the entire time.

## Common failure

- You come up too slowly, lose hip/quad contact with his stomach.
- He seats up, turns, faces you, frees his arm — position lost, back to guard.
- Cause: your leg/hips didn't time to his roll — space opened up.

## Checklist while drilling

- [ ] Hips pitched onto his before the roll starts
- [ ] Leg tracks him continuously, no lag
- [ ] Quad stays connected to stomach/abs throughout
- [ ] Land on the power line, head controlled
- [ ] Hand still behind the back at landing
