# Turtle Entry: Leg-Control Variant

## Overview

The conventional counterpart to the wrist-control roll: instead of grabbing the opponent's wrist, this version hooks the opponent's near-side leg. Trading a bit of arm security for control of the opponent's lower body, which strongly limits his ability to sit up and facilitates the roll into the leg-controlled Kimura.

---

## Setup (shared with the wrist-control entry)

1. From turtle, go in with the wedge (knee behind the tricep).
2. Pass the foot inside and hook onto the training partner's arm.
3. Apply pressure on the arm to motivate the opponent to make the switch, then come back in.

## The leg-control grip

- Instead of grabbing the wrist, reach through and get a hook on the training partner's near-side leg.
- **Disadvantage**: if the opponent extends his arm, the hook on the leg can conceivably be lost. To prevent this, the foot must stay cocked back nice and tight throughout.

## Executing the roll

1. Use the other leg as a pendulum to throw the training partner forward.
2. A key advantage of this method: controlling the opponent's leg strongly limits his ability to sit up.
3. Pass the leg and post across the face.
4. Come up into a very strong finishing position.

## Full sequence, repeated for clarity

1. Turtle situation — wedge in place, leg comes around the corner, makes the initial hook.
2. Sit on the leg, catch the training partner's near-side leg.
3. Come up, use the outside leg to start a rolling action.
4. Takes the opponent over; pass the leg, post, hand across the thighs, turn up.
5. Very strong finishing position.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Leg-control variant swaps the wrist grip for a hook on the opponent's near-side leg.
- Trade-off: controlling the leg limits his ability to sit up during the roll, but the hook can be lost if he extends his arm — keep the foot cocked back tight.
- Same wedge-and-hook entry as the wrist-control version; the divergence happens at the point where the hand would normally reach for the wrist.
- Finish is the same posted-hand-across-thighs, turn-up mechanic used elsewhere in the leg-controlled Kimura system.
