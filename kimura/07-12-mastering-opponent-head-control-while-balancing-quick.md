# Mastering Opponent Head Control While Balancing — Quick Reference

## Why it matters

- Head to the inside = barrier to the kimura (must stuff it).
- Head to the outside = no barrier, kimura easier.
- You must be able to actively move the head, not just react to it.

## Base position

1. Compressed for time: frame/wizard hand near opponent's wrist (works whether his hands are locked or gripping your ankle/calf).
2. More time available: drop the frame, post both hands on his shoulders.
3. As he circles/drives to knock you down, hop with him to stay balanced.

## Moving the head

1. Stuff down/inside: forearm drives head down; as he fights back up, post inside and post the elbow.
2. Post back around: physically post the head back down on the other side.
3. Goal: move the head left/right at will while balancing on one leg — works against opponent on two feet or two knees.
4. Vs. kneeling single leg: keep free leg positioned so he can't switch to a double leg while you work the head.

## Chaining into the kimura

1. Control/move the head to access the arm.
2. If he pushes his head up into you, push it back to the outside and take the kimura.
3. Lock the kimura once head is controlled.
4. Finish the takeover with a hip throw (sumi gaeshi) into the finishing position.

## Watch for

- Losing balance while forcing the head — hop with the opponent's movement rather than fighting it statically.
