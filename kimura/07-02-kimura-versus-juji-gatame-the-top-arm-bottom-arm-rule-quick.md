# Kimura Versus Juji-Gatame — The Top Arm / Bottom Arm Rule — Quick Reference

## The rule

- Hand **behind his back** (posterior) → no armbar possible, ever. Roll him over, done.
- Hand **in front** (anterior) and he passes your legs → live juji-gatame threat, always targeting your **top arm**.

## Why it goes wrong

1. You hold kimura on your side = a top arm and a bottom arm.
2. He steps his leg around your head to attack the top arm.
3. A simple east-to-west turn on his part drops your kimura into his juji-gatame.
4. Both people "have a lock" — his (armbar) is worth far more than yours. If you cling to the kimura, he separates your hands and finishes.

## The fix: convert top arm to bottom arm

1. As he steps around your head, plant your foot on the floor and switch your legs.
2. This flips you from facing one way (one arm up) to facing the other way (opposite arm up).
3. Do this actively, not statically — staying still loses the exchange most of the time.

## Full sequence

1. Half guard bottom, lock kimura.
2. He dances past your legs. If hand goes behind back here — skip everything below, just roll him.
3. If hand stays in front: as he turns around your head, plant foot, push on him as he crosses over, drop his shoulders to the mat.
4. Turn in the opposite direction as he steps over; push his hands + pull with your other arm → his own momentum rolls him through to his back.
5. Come up on top, elbow to the floor.
6. Surround his head, step across onto the power line, finish.

## Timing checklist

- [ ] Match his pace — don't be late with hand placement or getting to top.
- [ ] If he comes back up, expect a T-kimura scramble (same as covered before).
- [ ] Transition to top via **high tripod**: switch legs, step over his head, elbow to mat, sweep elbow-to-elbow and pull.
- High tripod = covers both sides of his body fast enough to beat his movement.
