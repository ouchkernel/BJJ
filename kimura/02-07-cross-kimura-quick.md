# Cross Kimura — Quick Reference

## When to use

Opponent locks his own hands together in a dorsal kimura position — you can't hand-fight his hand behind his back.

## Entry

1. Post a leg on the opponent.
2. Throw that foot from one side of his body across to the other — leg goes OVER the top of your own hand.
3. Sit down: two legs go across his body (looks similar to an armbar shape).

## Finish

1. Cross your feet.
2. Keep his trapped hand inside your own thigh.
3. Use leg pressure to reinforce the hand and break.

## If he escapes up

- Comes up to his knees: don't panic, keep feet crossed, keep hand pinned in thigh, keep reinforcing with the leg.
- Rolls over: follow the roll — same lock still applies for the finish.

## Alternate entry (pop-up version)

1. Pop up, take leg right over the top.
2. Hide your own hands inside the crook of your own knee.
3. Sit down and fall across the training partner's body — tremendous pressure on the arm.
4. If he rolls out, follow and stay in position — same pull finishes.

## Why it works

- Converts kimura from arms-vs-arms (strength dependent) to legs-vs-arm (technique dependent).
- First of three leg-incorporated kimura variations in this system.
