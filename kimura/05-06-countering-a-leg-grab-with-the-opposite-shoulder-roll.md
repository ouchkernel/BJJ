# Countering A Leg Grab With The Opposite-Shoulder Roll

## Overview

The practical payoff of the opposite-shoulder roll: countering the common defensive reaction where the opponent, seeing you take inside position, grabs your leg to try to work into an Ashi Garami-style leg entanglement game. Instead of fighting the grip directly, you move the opponent's head with your forearm to expose his other elbow and roll the opposite direction into T-Kimura.

---

## The defensive reaction being countered

- As you post and come into an inside position on the opponent (from standing, working toward Kimura), a very common reaction is for him to reach in and grip your leg.
- This is a good strategy on the opponent's part: from there he can bring a foot to the inside position and start working into Ashi Garami-type entanglement games.
- Once the leg is tied up this way, the conventional (same-side) Kimura entry to that arm is no longer available.

## The counter: chuck the head, roll the opposite way

1. The opponent's head is usually tucked tight to your leg in this position — that's part of what gives him good controlling position on the leg.
2. Post two hands on him, then use your **forearm** — not your hand — to move his head over to the side.
3. Moving the head this way exposes his *other* elbow (the one you couldn't access with the leg tied up).
4. Catch that arm all the way over the top.
5. This requires what looks like an awkward roll: tuck your elbow through and roll to the opposite side.
6. Result: a T-Kimura on the other side — the mechanics rely on the opposite-shoulder roll and break-fall principle covered previously; it can feel awkward, but it does work once you commit to a well-performed roll.

## Follow-ups from the resulting T-Kimura

- As the opponent goes to come up, use a bottom-knee entry.
- As he comes up to his base, you can throw a butterfly hook in, landing in a good position for sweeps.
- These follow-ups are the same general attacking options seen earlier in the Kimura-vs-butterfly-guard material.

## Second run-through

1. Step into the inside space against the opponent.
2. He does a good job tying up your leg.
3. Move his head to expose the second (previously inaccessible) Kimura.
4. Reach across his body.
5. The reach puts you in position to make the roll across.
6. End up in T-Kimura, with many attacking options from there and a good final attacking position.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Grabbing your leg to set up Ashi Garami is a common, sound defensive reaction to your inside-position entry — expect it.
- The counter is not to fight the grip but to move the opponent's head with your forearm (not your hand) to expose the elbow on the far side.
- Moving the head this way opens up the arm you couldn't otherwise reach because the leg tie-up blocked the same-side Kimura.
- The resulting roll goes to the opposite shoulder (the awkward-feeling roll from the previous lesson) and lands in a T-Kimura on the other side.
- From that T-Kimura, the same follow-up toolbox applies: bottom-knee entry, butterfly hook, and sweep options as the opponent tries to recover.
