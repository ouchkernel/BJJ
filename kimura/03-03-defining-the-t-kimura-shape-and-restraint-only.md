# Defining The T-Kimura: Shape And Restraint Only

## Overview

A precise definition of the T-kimura's physical shape, and a clear statement of what it can and cannot do: it cannot finish anyone, but it takes away your opponent's options so completely that turning into you becomes his only realistic move.

---

## The shape

- Your opponent lies down; his body forms the **stem** of a T.
- Your body crosses over his and forms the **hat** of the T — you are perpendicular to him.
- You have the kimura locked across his body in this T shape, and both of your chests face up toward the ceiling.
- Because your chest and his chest don't face each other, there is no submission hole available from this position — it's impossible. The only way to submit someone with a kimura is chest facing chest.

## What it restrains

- If your opponent tries to turn *away* from you, the kimura makes that impossible.
- If he just stays where he is and doesn't move, it's easy for you to use your leg like a pendulum, come up on top, and restrain him further.
- The only direction that makes sense for him to go is to turn *into* you.
- If he succeeds in turning into you, there's a real chance he could get all the way up and even escape the hold, ending up on top of you.

## The battle lines

- The T-kimura has no value as a submission hold, but tremendous value as a restraining hold — it lets you hold someone down long enough for your other attacks to work.
- Your opponent's options are structurally limited: he can't turn away, and he can't stay still (or you'll pin him). His only real option is to turn into you.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Physically, the T-kimura is a perpendicular cross: your opponent's body is the stem, yours is the hat of the T, with both chests facing the ceiling.
- No submission is possible from this shape — kimura only finishes chest to chest.
- The opponent's escape options are boxed in: turning away is blocked by the kimura, staying still invites you to pin him from on top, so turning into you is his only real move.
- Once you understand this basic element, you're set up to exploit the elbow battle that follows when he tries to turn in.
