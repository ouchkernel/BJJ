# The Diagonal Power Line — Quick Reference

## The mistake

- Applying kimura perpendicular to opponent's body — limits your ability to lift his back and get a hand behind him.

## The fix: diagonal power line

- Line your body up on the diagonal from his **one hip to the opposite shoulder** (e.g., left hip → right shoulder).
- Your hip dominates his shoulder; your shoulder dominates his hip.
- Legs at ~90°, eventually step over his head.

## Sequence

1. Lift elbow off floor, roll his wrist inward.
2. Walk your hips up to his shoulder.
3. Step over his head; your head moves down toward his hips.
4. Sweep elbow to elbow, legs separate to 90°.
5. Take your head back and away → strong pulling force.

## Head position rule

- Head crowded in close to his = limited pull.
- Head back, spine straight, aligned on the power line = maximum pull. (Pull-dominant kimura.)

## "Beat the shoulder"

- Bring your hip up onto his shoulder line → sets up stepping over his head for real head control.
- Head control = part of the "trinity of power," central to kimura success.

## Self-test: no-hands kimura

- Sit to his hip, step over his head, apply the position with control hand off him and only the blade of your wrist resting on his wrist (no grip).
- If positioned correctly on the power line, you still get strong breaking pressure — proves you're pulling via position, not pushing with hands.

## Checklist

- [ ] Body on the diagonal (opposite hip to opposite shoulder), not perpendicular
- [ ] Hip beats his shoulder
- [ ] Stepped over his head for head control
- [ ] Head pulled back, not crowded in
- [ ] Passes the no-hands test
