# The Power Prop Problem — Quick Reference

## The setup

From half guard top, bottom player works an underhook to escape/sweep.

## Basic underhook fix

1. Danger sign: chin over his shoulder + he hikes the underhook = you get destabilized to head-over-head (bad).
2. Fix: drop chin down to his elbow — now much harder for him to hike the arm.
3. Reinforce: cross to good elbow position, sit through. His underhook becomes nearly useless, you're head-over-hips.

## The power prop (the problem)

- Well-defended underhook often makes him switch: posts up on the **elbow** instead of the shoulder = power prop.
- Effect: you feel unstable; he can come up with single-leg attacks and scoop inside your leg.
- **Do not** go straight for a kimura on the underhook/prop arm here — it's risky and exposes your back.

## Watch for

- Instability + multiple live threats (sweeps, single legs) = signal you're facing the power prop, not the basic underhook.
