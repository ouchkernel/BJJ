# The Kimura Vs Single-Leg Entry Drill — Quick Reference

## Setup

- Both partners on two knees, standard wrestling tie, hands inside. (Artificial start — fine for drilling reps, not for live rolling.)

## Core sequence (memorize this order)

1. Surrender head position (head moves away) → partner drops into single leg.
2. Quasi-whizzer on the near arm — first response, always.
3. Turn side-on to partner off the whizzer.
4. Second hand controls his head — keep downward pressure; if his head rises, Kimura gets much harder.
5. Open hand punches through, wrist over his forearm — go deep, never a shallow/superficial grip.
6. Base hand to the floor.
7. As he tries to circle further behind, the Kimura frame limits him — walk with him to hold position.
8. Lock the figure-of-four (reach to your own buttocks — it locks easily).
9. Finish: touch his back to the ground (T-Kimura), or stay seated.

## Safety

- Bring the hand through carefully — do NOT poke eye/nose/mouth going through to the forearm. Be deliberate, not clumsy.

## Two finishing branches once Kimura is locked

| Branch | Action | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Commit down | Extend leg through, put him to a hip, go back to T-Kimura | Classic T-Kimura attacking position |
| Stay on top | Punch hand through, as he tries to stand hit a hip-heist | End up on top of opponent instead |

## Key point

You don't have to finish in T-Kimura every time — the Kimura's job is to kill the single-leg attempt and start the sequence; multiple good positions follow from there.
