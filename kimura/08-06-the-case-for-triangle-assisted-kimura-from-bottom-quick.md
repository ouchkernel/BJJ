# The Case For Triangle-Assisted Kimura From Bottom — Quick Reference

## The problem

- Kimura from closed/half guard rarely controls the head.
- Without head control, opponent can: posture up, lock hands together, tuck hand into own thigh, or bring his head over yours (kills the lock, pressure flips onto you).
- Root cause: legs wrapped around the waist (standard guard) leave the head completely free.
- One of several reasons Kimura from closed guard is low-percentage at the top level.

## The "strong man's move" trap

- Kimura only becomes a pure strength contest when it's two hands vs. two hands.
- Fix: always bring the legs into the attack — same principle as the top-position leg-controlled Kimura material.

## The fix: triangle as control, not submission

1. Use a triangle-style leg lock to immobilize the opponent's head + one arm.
2. This is NOT the triangle strangle — purely a control mechanism.
3. The actual finish is still the Kimura.
4. Result: hybrid of triangle control + Kimura finish.

## Why it's high percentage

- Legs (not hands) control the head → opponent can't posture up, can't bring head over yours, can't easily defend.
- Rated as one of the single best ways to raise Kimura-from-bottom success rate.

## Two variants

| Variant | Mechanic |
|---|---|
| Trap triangle | Feet simply crossed in a triangle shape around head + arm |
| Hantai sankaku (reverse triangle) | Triangle locked the "wrong" way for tremendous pressure |

Both work well — choice depends on setup (trap triangle mechanics covered in detail in the next lesson).
