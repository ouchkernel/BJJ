# Trimura: Seated Guard Grip Entry — Quick Reference

## Why trap triangle > closed guard for kimura

Trap triangle controls the opponent's head directly — closed guard doesn't. That head control is what makes the kimura actually finishable.

## Entry steps (from seated position)

1. Reach for a **pronated grip** on his wrist (palm away) — use a pass-off: left hand takes his wrist toward your center line, hands it off to your right hand. Don't reach directly with the right hand.
2. Go to elbow two-on-one.
3. Trap his hand across your center line onto your hip (stops him pulling it free).
4. Kick your legs over into a trap triangle — arm is now fully isolated.

## Decision point

- Head controlled, knees pulling in → strangle is available.
- If he forces his arm to the **outside** to kill the strangle → that's your kimura trigger.

## Kimura steps once he defends outside

1. Push the hand even further outside than he wanted.
2. Control wrist + elbow, pull elbow down to your sternum.
3. Invert your working knee: foot up to ceiling, knee down to mat (ankles cross).
4. Draw his elbow up to the shoulder line → **bottom power line**: your head / his elbow / both his shoulders in a straight line.
5. Pull in with the knees, lock the kimura.

## Checklist

- [ ] Pass-off grip used (not a direct naive reach)
- [ ] Opponent's hand trapped against your hip
- [ ] Trap triangle locked before going for strangle or kimura
- [ ] Knee inverted (foot up / knee down) when he defends outside
- [ ] Head, his elbow, both his shoulders aligned (bottom power line) before finishing

## Rule of thumb

If your head drifts off the bottom power line, the finish gets much less reliable — get back on the line before forcing the break.
