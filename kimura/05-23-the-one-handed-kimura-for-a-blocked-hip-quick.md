# The One-Handed Kimura For A Blocked Hip — Quick Reference

## Standard two-handed finish (baseline)

1. Separate opponent's hands; establish the power line (his shoulder → his opposite hip).
2. Lead with the elbow, cover his head, position along the power line — elbow blocks the hip as you step over the head.
3. He flattens out, head moves away → rotate for the finish. Most popular/effective Kimura finish in the sport.
4. Hands locked too strong to separate? Use your knee to pry them apart, then lead with the elbow behind the back.

## Problem: opponent blocks your hip with his arm

- Denies you the ability to cover his head → standard finish stalls.

## Fix: One-handed Kimura

1. Grip the **blade of the hand**, never the wrist — maximizes leverage.
2. Block his hip in return with your free hand.
3. His arm blocking the power line → use a **tripod action**: come up high to get past his blocking arm.
4. Walk in a circle around his head while continuing to block his hips.
5. Result: strong one-handed breaking effect even without head control — his blocking arm can't stop the tripod action.

## Key point

Blocked hip = switch tools, not force through. One-handed Kimura via tripod + circular walk is the direct counter to a hip-blocking defense.
