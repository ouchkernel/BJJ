# The One-Handed Kimura

## Overview

A specialized addition to the kimura arsenal for a specific defensive problem: an opponent who over-wraps your hip to deny you the power line. Instead of fighting through the over-wrap with the standard two-handed figure four, this method connects to the opponent's hand with just one hand and lets the legs and body rotation do the actual breaking work.

---

## The problem: over-wrapping the hip

- The kimura system wants to align on the **power line** — head to the opponent's far hip, hips to his near shoulder, forming a diagonal line from one hip across to the near shoulder. The body wants to be aligned on that line.
- A smart opponent knows this and defends by **over-wrapping your hip**: when you get a kimura across side control, he reaches over your hip and hugs it.
- Consequence: this stops you from doing the classic move of stepping over his head to control it, since your hip is now anchored to his wrapping arm.

## The one-handed kimura solution

1. Take what would normally be your pulling (control) hand and bring it through to grip the **blade of the opponent's hand** — not the wrist, the blade of the hand.
2. With this one-handed grip in place, come down. As the opponent tries to stop your hips from coming around by over-wrapping and reaching around your lower back, place your free hand down by his hip as a blocker.
3. Even as he wraps his arm tight around your waist, come up and tripod, walking all the way around until your ear touches his hip.
4. From here, drive forward. The legs come around the corner, then rotate all the way through.
5. As your ear crosses the opponent's stomach, that's where tremendous pressure is generated. A drive leg pushes off the mat as you go forward, producing a very strong finish with only one hand connected to the arm.

## Why it works despite only one hand

- It's an illusion that the break is happening with one hand — really it's the legs and the rotation doing the breaking.
- The one hand is just the connector linking you to the opponent's arm; the mechanical work comes from walking around, driving forward, and rotating through.

## Where it fits in the system

- This move, together with the other kimura variations already covered, forms a **family of kimuras** that cover each other well — when one method fails, another steps up.
- Using this multiplicity lets you break through strong defenses that would stop you cold if you only had one way of getting through.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Use the one-handed kimura specifically when the opponent over-wraps your hip to deny you the power line and the head-step-over.
- Grip the blade of the hand, not the wrist.
- Block the opponent's hip with your free hand, tripod up, and walk around until your ear touches his hip.
- The finish comes from legs and rotation driving forward, not from hand strength — the one-hand grip is purely a connector.
- Always have more than one way of expressing a given move; that's the only way to cover contingencies when an opponent defends intelligently.
