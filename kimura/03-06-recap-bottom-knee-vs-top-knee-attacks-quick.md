# Recap: Bottom Knee Vs. Top Knee Attacks — Quick Reference

## Recap

- T-kimura = restraint only, buys time for your attacks.
- Opponent must turn into you (can't turn away, can't stay still).
- His elbow position decides whether he gets up.
- Use his elbow/forearm as a lever to keep putting him down and set up your attack.

## The fundamental choice

| If opponent... | Then attack with... |
|---|---|
| Gets up to a single elbow/hip base | Bottom knee — penetrate the space he creates |
| Gets flattened onto back + shoulders (via lever/fulcrum) | Top knee — head to the floor, knee comes across his body |

## Bottom knee route

1. Control his elbow as he rises.
2. He comes up to a single elbow/hip.
3. Penetrate with bottom knee into the gap.
4. Opens a whole host of follow-up attacks.

## Top knee route

1. Use lever and fulcrum to flatten him onto back and shoulders.
2. Put your head to the floor.
3. Bring top knee across his body.
4. Come up into attacking position; top leg can cross-face to block his head from rising.

## Bottom line

Bottom-knee vs. top-knee is the core split that organizes every T-kimura attack going forward.
