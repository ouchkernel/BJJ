# Kimura Vs. Bicep Slicer: Relationship And Mechanics — Quick Reference

## When to use it

- Kimura grip secured but can't get opponent's back off the floor (hands locked in front) → switch to bicep slicer instead of forcing the pull.

## Grip mechanics

1. Pulling/frame-arm wrist goes **OVER the top** of opponent's forearm — never lower.
2. Curl wrist so it sits on top of his forearm (creates pressure on its own).
3. Flip hand through — do NOT grab his wrist (conventional push-hand).
4. Grab your own **shoulder** (not just your elbow) — significantly tighter than the elbow grip.

## Positioning

1. Elbow forward = elbow-to-elbow with opponent (same key as kimura success).
2. Reach his shoulder line.
3. Walk your hips around the corner to the OTHER side of his head.
4. Pressure now goes straight onto the forearm bones — hard to stop even if he locks his hands.

## Leverage principle

| Position | Result |
|---|---|
| Stay on near side | Good pressure, finishes many opponents |
| Walk around corner, elbow-to-elbow, hand on shoulder | Much closer to end of the lever |
| Walk PAST his centerline | Maximum pressure — severe load on forearm bones + muscle |

## No-release entry (from locked-hands scramble)

1. Punch through, lock over the top onto your own bicep — same wrist-over position.
2. NEVER let go of his arm mid-transition (don't let him yank arms to the floor and escape).
3. Thread straight through without releasing.
4. Post hand on floor, walk around the corner, elbow to his elbow, grab shoulder.
5. Breaking pressure comes quickly at the shoulder line — more so past centerline.

## Checklist

- [ ] Wrist over (not under) his forearm
- [ ] Grip own shoulder, not just elbow
- [ ] Elbow-to-elbow contact
- [ ] Walk hips around the corner / past centerline
- [ ] Never release grip during the transition
