# Kesa Gatame (Scarf Hold) — Strengths And Limitations — Quick Reference

## What it is

Side pin: underneath opponent's arm, controlling head/shoulders, wide base on two separated legs.

## Key correction

- Real control target = **shoulder and elbow**, not the head. It looks like a headlock but isn't one functionally.
- Done well: punishing, long-lasting pin.
- Done poorly (lose elbow control): opponent escapes toward your back.

## Two variations

| Variation | Control | Back exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional kesa gatame | Near shoulder + wraps head | Higher if elbow control lost |
| Kizu (kimura) kesa gatame | Underhook on far shoulder | Low — preferred version |

## Why it's rare in modern elite BJJ

- Limits high-percentage submission options vs. other side pins.
- BJJ has far fewer standing-throw entries than judo/wrestling, where kesa gatame naturally lands you after a throw.
- Not a "big strong man's move" — works for lightweights who understand the shoulder/elbow focus.

## Practical use in this system

- Not a primary pin choice — a **reactive, transitory** control.
- Trigger: opponent swims through for an underhook while you hold side control.
- Catch into kesa gatame just long enough to stall him, then transition to kimura (next lesson).
- Freely surrender the head if he threatens your back from here — the head was never the real control point, so giving it up costs little.
