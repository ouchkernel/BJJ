# Top Knee To Cross Kimura And Trap Triangle — Quick Reference

## Skip this

- Scissoring-leg strangle from top-knee cross-face — works, but low percentage. Not the priority.

## Entry: top-knee cross-face → cross kimura

1. Cross-face leg keeps his shoulders pinned as he struggles up.
2. Head to the floor.
3. Bottom foot posts, pulls you over — his hand ends up inside your knee.
4. Use opponent's elbow as a lever to lift his back off the mat.
5. Curl foot to outside, use kimura leverage to bring him over.
6. Throw leg over his fist — arm now caught inside your leg.
7. Lock a triangle or just close your legs; pull for finishing pressure.

## If he rolls through: don't fight it

1. Let the roll happen, continue attacking.
2. Take your leg over his head, roll through with him.
3. As he comes back to base, he's locked in a **trap triangle** (feet locked, NO figure-four).
4. Bring his elbow up to the shoulder line, hand trapped inside your leg.
5. Result: very strong finish, second roll-through blocked.

## Full-detail run-through

1. T-kimura → opponent turns up → elbow lever + top-knee cross-face hold him down.
2. Head to mat, pop up, knee over his fist.
3. Kimura drags him up off floor → **cross kimura** (two legs across his back, elbow to shoulder line).
4. Triangle / crossed feet / pull the elbow — pressure builds.
5. He pitches forward to roll — accept it.
6. Back-step, go over his shoulder, commit to a second roll-through.
7. Catches him in trap triangle — no third roll possible.
8. Elbow to shoulder line again → tremendous breaking pressure from bottom.

## Why it's so strong

- Kimura + triangle = head control = one leg of the "trinity of power" → dramatically amplified kimura pressure.
