# Trap Triangle Kimura: Setup And Finish — Quick Reference

## Starting position

- Seated butterfly guard (favorite setup position for this attack).

## Step 1 — Pronated wrist grip

1. Take opponent's hand to the center line.
2. Pass to a pronated grip (palm away from you).
3. Hand up to elbow, take his hand across to your hip.
4. Everything closes around his arm — very hard to yank free even if he posts on your shoulder and pushes.
- Why it works: mechanical, not strength-based — hip covers the one weak zone of the grip.

## Step 2 — Close the trap triangle

1. As he yanks away, foot to his hip.
2. Second leg locks around his head.
3. Ankles crossed like closed guard, but around head + one arm = **trap triangle** (not a real triangle yet — no strangle, pure control).

## Step 3 — Break the arm outside

1. Two-on-one grip on his wrist.
2. Use hips as a lever to work his hand outside (works even if he resists).
3. Thumb slides underneath his wrist.

## Step 4 — Lock the finish

1. **Key detail**: invert your knee so the foot points at the ceiling, not the floor.
2. This turns off his shoulders and hips.
3. Lock feet over on the other side.
4. Now aligned on his power line, elbow to your armpit.
5. Pull to finish the Kimura.

## Checklist

- [ ] Hand to center line → pronated grip → hand to hip
- [ ] Foot to hip → second leg around head (trap triangle closed)
- [ ] Two-on-one wrist, hips as lever, hand pushed outside
- [ ] Thumb under wrist
- [ ] Knee inverted — foot to ceiling
- [ ] Feet locked over, power line aligned, pull to finish

## Watch for

- Opponent instinctively moving his arm across his body and raising his head (thinking it's a real triangle threat) — expected reaction, keep working the sequence.
- Foot pointing down at the floor instead of up at the ceiling — this is the detail that actually turns off his shoulders/hips; get it wrong and he can posture out.
