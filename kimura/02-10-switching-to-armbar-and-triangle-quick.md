# Switching To Armbar And Triangle — Quick Reference

## Core idea

Kimura has two faces: submission hold AND restraining hold. When you can't force the kimura submission, use it to restrain and switch to juji gatame (armbar) or triangle instead.

## Route 1: Three-quarter juji gatame

1. From resisted dorsal kimura: switch arms elbow-to-elbow (left, then right).
2. Hand to floor in line with his hips; swivel feet outside.
3. Knees up, walk in, RE-LOCK the kimura grip immediately.
4. Keep kimura locked (prevents arm extension = prevents turning escape — even if he gets to his knees, kimura pulls him back).
5. Once he locks hands defensively: thread foot through under his neck → three-quarter juji gatame.
6. Separate hands; head/shoulder control lets you straighten and finish even if he turns partway up.

### If he comes up hard (counter to the counter)

1. Draw in second arm.
2. Knee behind his head as a wedge.
3. As he rises to knees: lock figure-of-four → senkaku (triangle) from the front, finish.

## Route 2: Front triangle direct

1. From front kimura position, can't secure arm: switching-elbow method to separate — hands stay UP (low hands = he swims free).
2. Thumb to sternum, hand to floor, knee up, sit down with control.
3. Elbow to elbow, pin his hand to his own ribs (restraining hold, no submission yet).
4. Pass foot through → prefer three-quarter juji gatame: feed leg through, feet drawn to neck (not extended).
5. He can't turn out — head/shoulders constrained (unlike regular armbar).
6. If he locks hands defensively: hold opposite elbow, LET him come up on top — tight senkaku wedges him in → lock figure-of-four → finish.

## Watch for

- Never re-lock the kimura loosely before threading the foot — a free arm = turning escape.
- Low hands during elbow-switching lets opponent's arms swim free.
