# Trimura From An Over-Tie Against A Collar Tie

## Overview

A specific trimura entry that starts from defending an opponent's collar tie. Rather than simply resisting the tie, the response is an "over-tie" that neutralizes the collar tie and simultaneously wins control of the opponent's elbow, opening a fast path into a trap triangle and, from there, the kimura finish.

---

## The over-tie response

1. The opponent takes a collar tie — e.g., a right-handed collar tie.
2. Respond with an **over-tie**: go outside and over the opponent's collar-tie arm, holding it with the opposite hand (left hand versus his right-hand tie).
3. At the same time, the other hand (right hand) comes through underneath and seizes the opponent at the elbow.
4. Result: one of the opponent's arms is now well controlled — both at the tie and at the elbow.

## From over-tie to trap triangle

1. With the arm controlled this way, it's an easy kick-through into a triangle entry.
2. A basic triangle strangle is available directly from here, but the lesson's focus is diverting into the kimura.
3. As the opponent starts defending against the initial triangle/strangle threat, shift to the trimura — apply kimura pressure using a trap triangle locked around the opponent's head and arm.

## Second demonstration (with distance control)

1. Opponent takes a collar tie; respond with the over-tie.
2. Control the training partner's elbow.
3. Post on the hip to control distance.
4. Kick the legs over into a trap triangle.
5. Force the opponent's hand to the outside, then immediately bring everything up to the bottom power line — taking the opponent's elbow up in line with the shoulders.
6. The opponent's head is driven down and a very strong finish results.

## Practical notes

- The over-tie is a dual-purpose response: it neutralizes the opponent's collar tie grip and simultaneously establishes the elbow control needed for the trap-triangle entry — one motion solves both problems.
- As with the seated butterfly entry, the triangle threat is used as a lever to make the opponent defend, creating the opening to convert to kimura rather than finishing the strangle directly.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Against a collar tie, respond with an over-tie: go outside/over the tying arm with one hand while the other hand seizes the elbow underneath — this both defuses the tie and controls the arm.
- That elbow control gives an easy kick-through into a trap triangle.
- Use the triangle/strangle threat to make the opponent defend, then convert to the trimura kimura finish around the head and trapped arm.
- Posting on the hip to control distance and forcing the opponent's hand to the outside sets up bringing the elbow up to the power line for the finish.
