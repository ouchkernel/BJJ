# Standing Kimura Vs. The Single Leg (Head Inside) — Quick Reference

## Why this matters

- Kimura works standing top/bottom/ground, gi and no-gi — but the #1 standing application is as a counter to the single leg takedown.
- Works against high single leg, sweep single leg, and single leg tackle — because most finishes require the opponent to stand back up, which is when the counter fires.

## First question every time

**Is his head inside or outside?** Head inside = more common, covered here.

## Head-inside sequence

1. Head tucked to his chest = kimura is very hard to lock (head is a barrier).
2. Use forearm to stuff the head down and out of the way.
3. Fight for wrist control / head position first if needed.
4. Stuff head, go over the top, lock the kimura.
5. Step and slide toward his far foot — his head moves out or gets trapped in your hips.
6. High-amplitude sumi-gaeshi-style throw puts him down.
7. As he tries to lock hands, triangle your legs around his arms, split them apart.
8. Transition into kimura (ude garami) breaking position.
9. As he comes up: lift elbow + come through with bottom knee, OR return him to mat + come over top knee.

## Before you throw

- Hold your balance first — don't let him rush a switch to double leg.
- Get a controlling grip (far wrist) before stuffing the head and stepping to his feet.
- Let your own head fall forward so his head naturally clears to the side.
- After the throw: he turns into you → keep lifting and putting him back down, scissor legs, elbows, step over head → breaking position.

## Key points

- Head inside = barrier, must stuff it first.
- Step to the far foot for the throw.
- Leg-triangle to split his arms is the bridge from the throw into the kimura finish.
