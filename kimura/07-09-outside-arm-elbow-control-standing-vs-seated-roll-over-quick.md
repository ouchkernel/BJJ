# Outside Arm Elbow Control — Standing Vs. Seated Roll-Over — Quick Reference

## Setup

1. Reach back with both hands, catch above the elbow (strong grip, hard to yank free).
2. Lock in and catch the wrist too.
3. Choose standing or seated finish from here.

## Standing roll-over — ADVANCED ONLY

1. Inside leg stand up (easy — he can't lock hands, you already have the kimura).
2. Lock kimura, turn in — underneath him or over the top.
3. Stand up faster than he can follow, pressure him down to the mat.
4. Elbows down, switch elbows, up to shoulder line, foot over, power line, break.
- **Warning**: real injury risk (falling body weight + locked arm lock). Two very advanced athletes only. Never teach to beginners.

## Seated roll-over — DEFAULT / SAFER

1. Rise, score the elbow, catch it.
2. Step inside leg up, **fold down into a seated position** (not standing).
3. Curl foot underneath body, catch opponent's leg.
4. Fall back into attacking position.
5. No roll → finish right there.
6. He rolls (most common) → come up tripod, legs across, bone-breaking finish.

## If elbow is too far back to reach (shallow grip)

1. Abandon the elbow plan — put hand in his back pocket instead.
2. Turn him to face you (his arm must commit around your waist).
3. As grip comes in, score the elbow as you rise.
4. Keep turning in — danger he circles back to your waist.
5. Have him sit, throw leg over top → strong break. If he rolls, tripod up, elbow down, finish.

## Key points

- Reach-back above-elbow grip = direct access, no need to roll him first.
- Seated version is the safe, default classroom teach; standing version is powerful but injury-prone.
- Shallow tight waist → can't reach elbow → use hand-in-pocket to turn him instead.
