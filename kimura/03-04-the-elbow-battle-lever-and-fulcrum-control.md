# The Elbow Battle: Lever And Fulcrum Control

## Overview

Once you know the opponent's only real option out of the T-kimura is to turn into you, the next question is how to make that as hard as possible. The answer is elbow position: whoever controls the opponent's elbow controls whether he can stand up at all, and this single control point turns into a lever-and-fulcrum system powerful enough to sit him back down even after he's fought all the way up to his knees.

---

## The elbow battle

- As your opponent turns into you from T-kimura, there's a fascinating battle for elbow position.
- If his elbow comes inside your elbow, he wins every single time.
- To get up, he needs to get his elbow to the mat.
  - If you hold his elbow up and don't let him move it, he can't get up — he looks "like a 90-year-old man."
  - If he manages to pull his elbow in toward his own body and down to the floor, he can whip up like an athlete very quickly.
- The whole battle is elbow position: he wants his elbow inside your bicep/elbow; you want your elbow underneath his and on the outside of his bicep/elbow.
  - Rule: always want your elbow underneath and inside his, and his elbow elevated off the mat.
  - If you maintain this, even if he goes hard, it's very difficult for him to get up.
  - If your elbow bends/folds and he gets his elbow to the mat, he can get up on you.
  - Worse still: if his elbow gets fully inside yours, he'll get all the way up — "I've got nothing" from there.

## Lever and fulcrum

- Even if the opponent does get up — even all the way to his knees — lifting his elbow lets you put him back down. Don't underestimate the power of a lever and a fulcrum, described as "essentially the magic of jiu-jitsu."
- As the opponent rises and faces you, dominating the end of the lever (his elbow) lets you build a strong base with your hand and turn him back down to the mat with a simple application of lever and fulcrum.
- Have confidence in this mechanism — don't panic and abandon the position (e.g., letting go and retreating to guard) just because he's gotten up.

## The one condition that breaks it

- This all works **as long as his elbow doesn't get inside yours**.
- If his elbow gets inside, that kills your fulcrum and lever — you're done, and you need to switch to something else.
- As long as your elbow keeps dominating the inside position, you can always return him to the mat with the lever-and-fulcrum extension, even if he got all the way up.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Whoever controls the opponent's elbow controls whether he can stand up out of T-kimura — hold his elbow off the mat and he can't rise.
- Your elbow must stay underneath and inside his, with his elbow kept elevated; if your elbow folds or his gets inside yours, you lose the position.
- The lever-and-fulcrum mechanism (dominating his elbow as the lever) lets you sit him back down even after he's stood all the way up — trust it instead of bailing to guard.
- The single failure condition is his elbow getting fully inside yours — once that happens, the lever/fulcrum is dead and you must switch attacks.
