# Turtle Position Kimura — Identifying The Roll & Exposing The Elbow — Quick Reference

## Step 1 — Read the grip

- [ ] Seat belt grip (over one arm, under other) → **not** a kimura opportunity. Do something else.
- [ ] Body lock (both hands under both your arms) → kimura is live, both inside and outside arm.
- [ ] Opponent switches seat belt → body lock mid-scramble → treat as risky, not an invitation.

## Step 2 — Identify the arm

- **Inside arm** = between the two bodies.
- **Outside arm** = nothing between you two.
- Preference: attack the inside arm.

## Step 3 — Expose the elbow

1. Move — don't stay static. Head coming up + motion = elbow exposure.
2. Use the blade of the wrist to draw the elbow forward (same idea as exposing a heel for a heel hook).
3. Keep monitoring/controlling his wrist throughout — slows his switch to seat belt.

## Step 4 — Lock safely (avoid the face-plant)

1. Monitor wrist.
2. Lock kimura hand in one-handed.
3. Free hand becomes a base of support as he throws weight forward.
4. Open leg, pitch him over.

## Step 5 — Roll him (the core skill)

1. Confirm body lock, not seat belt.
2. Monitor inside wrist, come up, expose elbow, second hand as base.
3. Don't throw straight down at his base — take him **forward**, toward his weak base.
4. Legs as a pendulum, scissor across his body.
5. Stay high — don't sit to the floor early, or he pops back on top.

## Watch for

- Static throws vs. a strong base rarely work — always redirect him forward/off-base.
- Head stays up the whole time; slouching = he recovers top position.
