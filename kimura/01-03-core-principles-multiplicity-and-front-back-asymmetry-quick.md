# Core Principles: Multiplicity And Front/Back Asymmetry — Quick Reference

## Multiplicity principle

- Never rely on one variation of a submission.
- System uses 7 kimura varieties on purpose — covers the many unexpected lines of resistance opponents give you.
- One tool vs. every line of resistance = hard. Multiple tools = easy.
- Rule: study the whole group of kimuras, not a single version.

## Front/back asymmetry principle

- Hands in front of the body = strong, coordinated.
- Hands forced behind the back = dramatically weaker, less coordinated.
- This is why "kimura = strong man's move" is wrong — hand-behind-back neutralizes even strong opponents.
- First goal after locking figure four: get the hand behind the back ASAP, every time.
