# Butterfly Hook Elevation To Side Kimura

## Overview

A method for using the feet to elevate and tilt the opponent from bottom half guard, converting a stalled kimura attempt (where the opponent has successfully hidden the hand) into a side-control-style kimura. The key mechanism is a butterfly hook under the far leg used to lift and tip the opponent to the mat.

---

## The problem this solves

- From the knee-in half guard position, when the opponent crowds forward and the attacker comes out to lock a kimura, the opponent will typically reinforce the hand in a strong defensive position — often braced inside their own thigh — making it very hard to extract.
- The underlying battle in every kimura: the attacker wants the hand behind the back to maximize effectiveness; the defender wants the hand in front of the body to maximize safety.
- Insight used to beat the defense: if the opponent's outside leg is out wide for base, the hand becomes easy to extract — so a good defender keeps that knee closed down. This closed-knee defensive posture is exactly what the butterfly hook exploits.

## The butterfly hook elevation

1. Shimmy the hips out and bring the second leg in as a butterfly hook (in addition to the near foot, which may already be locked over the opponent's heel).
2. Once the butterfly hook is in, start a lifting action. As the opponent tries to hold their base, it becomes very difficult for them to resist.
3. Drive off the hooking foot, look down at the floor, and tilt the training partner down to the mat.
4. This transition lands the attacker in a strong position for a side kimura.

## Finishing mechanics (side kimura)

- The basics of any side kimura come down to the same principle used throughout the system: chase the line of the opponent's shoulder and beat the shoulder.
- Once up on the opponent's shoulder line, it's an easy matter to control the head and push everything down onto the power line for a strong finish.

## Full sequence recap (mirrored side)

1. Start in the knee-in half guard position with control over the opponent's upper body as they drive forward.
2. Come up and lock the kimura grip; the opponent hides and reinforces the hand.
3. Shimmy the hips out; take the second foot in as a butterfly hook (the first foot may start locked over the opponent's heel).
4. Drop the hooking foot, begin the lifting action, free the second foot.
5. Focus on a simple lift that drives the opponent over from their own driving posture.
6. Come across to the far side, give a quick pull, put the opponent's hand down to the floor, and correct position with the elbow on the mat.
7. Walk around to the opponent's shoulder line, step over the opponent's head, and reposition directly over the power line.
8. Finish with a strong kimura.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The kimura hand-fighting battle is: attacker wants the hand behind the back; defender wants it in front, braced with the knee closed down for base.
- If the opponent's outside leg goes wide, the hand becomes easy to extract — since a good opponent won't allow that, use the butterfly hook to force the issue instead.
- Shimmy the hips out, hook the far leg as a butterfly hook, then lift and tilt the opponent down to the mat — this converts a stalled kimura into a side-kimura opportunity.
- Every side kimura finish follows the same rule: chase and beat the line of the opponent's shoulder, then push everything down onto the power line.
- After the tilt, walk around to the shoulder line and step over the opponent's head to reset directly over the power line before finishing.
