# Negating The Underhook Via Chin Position Into T-Kimura

## Overview

A repeat of the half-guard climb into T-Kimura, but with the missing piece named explicitly: the single best way to control an opponent's underhook is head position, specifically getting your chin over his elbow. This chapter walks the full sequence again — chin negation, elbow-to-elbow, Kimura lock, freeing the trapped knee, and the finish — from that added starting point.

---

## Starting point

- Half guard, opponent (Mateus) doing a good job controlling with an underhook.
- The single best way to control that underhook is to get over the shoulder.

## Chin position negates the underhook

- The more your chin drifts up toward the opponent's shoulder, the more effective his underhook becomes.
- Fix: your first move is to put your chin over his elbow.
- Once the chin is over the elbow, when he tries to lift/hike you, it becomes much more difficult.
- With the underhook neutralized this way, you can sweep across into an elbow-to-elbow position.
- Core principle: the beginning of negating the opponent's underhook is head position, specifically through chin placement.

## Getting the Kimura lock

1. Chin over his elbow.
2. Sweep across to elbow-to-elbow.
3. Secure the initial Kimura lock.

## Freeing the trapped knee (repeat of the mechanics)

1. Off the initial Kimura lock, come down and post on the head.
2. Come up into a high tripod.
3. Fit your foot inside the opponent's knee.
4. Use that foot placement to free the knee of the trapped leg.
5. Once the foot comes through, it becomes possible to roll across the opponent's body.

## Handling the opponent turning up into you

- As the opponent turns up into you, play the same game as before: keep his elbow elevated.
- This lets you return him to the mat.
- Even if the opponent manages to get all the way up to his knees, you can still lever him back down and return him face-down, because the elbow stays controlled.

## Finishing into cross-Kimura

1. Shoot your leg over the top.
2. Come up into a tripod again.
3. Put your knee over the opponent's hand.
4. Come out the back door — this makes it possible to lock up a very tight cross-Kimura position.
5. Bring the elbow up to the shoulder line.
6. Get the strong finish.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The single best way to control (negate) an opponent's underhook from half guard is head position — specifically, getting your chin over his elbow before he can hike it.
- Chin over the elbow → sweep to elbow-to-elbow → secure the initial Kimura lock.
- With the Kimura locked, free the trapped knee the same way as before: post on head, high tripod, foot inside the opponent's knee, roll across his body.
- If the opponent turns up into you, keep his elbow elevated throughout — this lets you return him to the mat even if he gets as far as his knees.
- Finish by shooting the leg over, tripoding again, kneeing over his hand, coming out the back door into a tight cross-Kimura, and hiking the elbow to the shoulder line for the finish.
