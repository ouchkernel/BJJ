# The Right-Right-Right Rule — Standing Kimura To T-Kimura

## Overview

Once the foot is inside from standing (see the flanking-tactics lesson), this covers the conventional way to convert that into a Kimura attack and names the pattern behind it: a right-side lead attacks the opponent's right arm and rolls over the right shoulder — the "right-right-right rule." Two run-throughs of the entry are given, both ending in T-Kimura with a path to the back if the opponent scrambles up.

---

## The conventional (right-right-right) entry

- Convention: if your right side is leading, most Kimura attacks go to the opponent's right arm.
- Sequence, first version:
  1. Move in on the opponent from a head-to-head position.
  2. Lift/pull to get your foot to the inside position.
  3. Score the opponent's wrist.
  4. Put your right knee on his right hip.
  5. Because you're standing, it's easy to go directly over his hip from here — you land straight in the **T-Kimura position**.
- From T-Kimura, as the opponent tries to come up, there are many attacking options.
- If he scrambles all the way up to his knees, use your feet to push away his base and go directly to attacking the back.

## Second run-through (same mechanics, different finish path)

1. Tie up with the opponent from standing; generate motion (push-pull) to find the inside position.
2. As he comes in, the right leg goes across his right hip.
3. Score the wrist; the leg goes high.
4. Cartwheel through his guard as he tries to come up and face you.
5. If he gets all the way to his knees, get your feet on his knees and stretch him around into good attacking position on the back.

## Naming the pattern: the right-right-right rule

- General rule: standing right side forward with a right lead → roll over your right shoulder → attack the opponent's right arm. This same-side pattern is called the **right-right-right rule** (right lead, right shoulder roll, right arm attacked).
- There are also ways to roll over the *opposite* shoulder instead — flagged here as a useful expedient and addition to the game (developed in the following lessons on the opposite-shoulder roll).

---

## Key Takeaways

- The default standing Kimura pattern is same-sided all the way through: right lead → right knee to right hip → roll over the right shoulder → attack the opponent's right arm. That's the right-right-right rule.
- Once a foot is inside from standing, going over the opponent's hip lands you directly in T-Kimura — no extra steps needed.
- From T-Kimura, if the opponent scrambles to his knees, push his base away with your feet (or stretch him around off his knees) and go straight to back attacks.
- The right-right-right rule is presented as the conventional/default option — the instructor immediately flags that rolling over the opposite shoulder is also viable and worth adding to your game.
