# The Opposite-Shoulder Roll And Break Fall Principle — Quick Reference

## The concept

- Standard pattern (right-right-right): right lead → roll over right shoulder → attack right arm.
- New option: roll over the **opposite** shoulder instead — feels awkward at first, but the awkwardness is the point.

## Why it feels awkward — break fall reference

1. Beginner break fall: right side forward → left (opposite) hand posts on mat → triangle (hand = apex, feet = base) → roll over **right** (forward) shoulder → slap out.
2. Rolling over the **left** (non-forward) shoulder as a beginner breaks the triangle → rolling against your own body → bad landing. Never taught this way.

## How the Kimura version works

1. Right side forward.
2. Instead of posting a hand at the triangle base, lock the Kimura grip.
3. Roll through off the Kimura into a winning position.
4. Advanced athletes can roll over the *opposite* shoulder because the Kimura grip supports the roll — a beginner's unsupported break fall can't do this safely.

## Why use it

- Pure surprise value: opponents expect right-right-right. Rolling opposite breaks the pattern they're braced for.
- Becomes especially useful when the opponent defends your inside position by grabbing your legs (next lesson's counter).
