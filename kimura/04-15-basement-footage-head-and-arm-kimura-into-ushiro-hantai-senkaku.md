# Basement Footage — Head-And-Arm Kimura Into Ushiro/Hantai Senkaku

## Overview

A live classroom (basement) session introducing the concept of using the legs to set up kimuras in an unconventional way: the seated head-and-arm kimura. Instead of controlling only the opponent's arm as in a conventional kimura, this approach also traps the head, opening a direct route to the back and into triangle positions — Ushiro Senkaku (triangle from the back) and Hantai Senkaku (the "wrong way" triangle) — from which the kimura becomes far easier to finish.

---

## The Core Concept

- Danaher, working with Nicky Ryan, introduces the idea of using the legs to set up kimuras in a special way: the **seated kimura** — specifically a **seated head-and-arm kimura**.
- The key difference from a conventional kimura: instead of controlling only the opponent's arm, the head is controlled too.
- This head-and-arm control is described as one of the greatest ways to access an opponent's back.
- From the back, the attack used is **Ushiro Senkaku** — the triangle from the back — which opens up many opportunities into various forms of triangle.
- Once a triangle is locked, it becomes an easy matter to transition into a kimura from it.

## The Demonstration

- Danaher is coaching Nicky Ryan through this concept; his training partner is Ethan Crowlinson.
- Starting point: Ethan fails to finish a conventional kimura — he can't get the hand behind the opponent's back.
- Instead of abandoning the attack, he traps the head and the arm together — now it's a kimura plus head control.
- This lets him take a **seated position**, which unweights his feet.
- With unweighted feet, he can use his legs to separate the opponent's hands and lock up the strength of his legs through a triangle — in this example, Ushiro Senkaku — to control the opponent.
- From that triangle, he goes back to the kimura for the finish.

## Two Ways To Lock The Triangle

- The triangle in this setup can be locked either from the front or from the back of the opponent.
- The footage highlights **Hantai Senkaku** — the "wrong way" triangle — as one of Ethan Crowlinson's strongest moves, one he has famously used in competition to produce a legendarily powerful break in ADCC.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The seated head-and-arm kimura controls both the arm and the head, not just the arm — this is what opens the door to the back.
- Taking a seated position with unweighted feet lets the legs do the work of separating the opponent's hands and locking a triangle.
- Ushiro Senkaku (triangle from the back) and Hantai Senkaku (triangle locked the "wrong way") are the two triangle entries shown from this position.
- Once a triangle is locked from here, going back to the kimura for the finish becomes straightforward — a triangle greatly facilitates any kimura attack.
- Hantai Senkaku, in particular, is highlighted as an extremely powerful attack — used to produce a notable break in ADCC competition.
