# Finishing After The Inside Arm Roll

## Overview

Where the previous lessons covered rolling the opponent from turtle bottom and surviving the resulting scramble, this lesson closes the loop: how to actually finish once you've won the roll. The core idea is that the opponent's attempt to scramble back to top position is itself the mechanism that delivers the finish — his own explosive movement forces him into a forward roll that lands you directly on the power line.

---

## Setup recap

- Kimura from turtle bottom via the inside arm roll puts the opponent from top position down to bottom, letting you finagle your body around him until you can get through to a finish.
- Having already covered rolling the opponent and winning the scramble, this lesson focuses on the finish itself.

## Sequence

1. Opponent is out to your side; you start from a body lock situation.
2. As he moves to try to recover, focus on small motion until you can expose his elbow.
3. Take the one-handed grip, put the leg up, and start a confident roll that puts your body across the opponent — head up over his hips, his arm right in front of you.
4. Secure his wrist and give a small push to get hold of the full kimura grip.
5. If the opponent stays static here, you can finish directly from this position.
6. Almost all opponents, however, will try to explode up on top of you instead of staying still.
7. As he comes up on top, keep your head over his hips.
8. As he explodes up, you're in a perfect position to throw your legs on top of him and maneuver your body over — working the hand behind his back.
9. Once the hand goes behind the back, you have a strong finish available.
10. This forces the opponent into a physical forward roll over his own shoulder.
11. Follow the roll: get your elbow to the mat and end up directly on his power line, ready to finish.

## Second demonstration

1. Opponent locks up behind you; expose his elbow and work through as before.
2. Even if he puts a leg out to base, it doesn't matter — always roll away from his base, never into it.
3. Turn away from his base and end up on top of him, hips directly over his.
4. Even as he tries to come up on top, throw your leg over the top of his and focus on getting his elbow behind his back, forcing the forward roll.
5. Follow the roll through — end up on the power line, ready for a strong finish.

## Key principle

- His own attempt to explode back to the top position is what powers the finish — you don't fight his scramble directly, you redirect it. Getting the hand behind the back at the right moment converts his explosive recovery attempt into a forced forward roll that delivers him straight onto your power line.
- Never roll into the opponent's base of support; always roll away from it.

---

## Key Takeaways

- If the opponent stays static after you secure the roll and the wrist, you can finish right there.
- Most opponents will instead try to explode up on top — that explosive energy is what you redirect into the finish, not what you fight against.
- The finishing trigger is getting the hand behind the back at the moment he's exploding upward — this forces him into a forward roll over his own shoulder.
- Always roll away from wherever the opponent has placed his base, never into it.
- Follow the forced forward roll all the way through: elbow to the mat, land on the power line, finish.
