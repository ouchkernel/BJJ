# Kimura Vs. The Kneeling Single Leg — Three Attacking Options

## Overview

The hybrid standing-vs-kneeling scenario: you're on your feet and your opponent is down on two knees, having shot a tackle or sweep single leg. This is extremely common — it's the position that results the instant a standing single leg attempt gets caught. Three distinct attacking options are demonstrated for turning this into a kimura.

---

## The base problem

- You can't simply stand upright over a kneeling opponent who has your leg — he'll switch to a double leg and put you straight down.
- Correct posture: an athletic stance putting weight down onto the opponent, not standing tall and passive.
- His best option from his knees is to explode up to his feet and take you down — your job is to make that difficult.
- The advantage: you have a height advantage over a kneeling opponent, which makes it comparatively easy to either stuff his head or shuck it to the outside. Both are viable; you still have to actively control the head to make either work.

## Option 1: Stuff the head

- Take the training partner's head down and stuff it.
- With the head stuffed, catch and lock the kimura grip.
- Bring your feet in close together — this is now safe because holding his hand prevents him from switching to a double leg.
- Pitch down and bring him over into a good attacking position (whether or not the leg gets hooked in the process).

## Option 2: Shuck the head to the outside

- Start with a hip bump to move his head away, then pass the head to the outside.
- Go in on the opponent and use your foot as a hook — step the foot all the way around the corner.
- From the floor, lift him over and into a good attacking position.
- Follow him down: switch your legs, bring your body up, place your elbow on the floor — this lands you in the breaking position on the mat.

## Recovering when the opponent holds on tight

- If the opponent recovers and holds the single leg tightly after you've stuffed the head and come down to a shoelace grip, switch across into the position covered earlier: sweep the leg through and put him down.
- As you separate his hands, he will typically try to explode back up into you — use the kimura itself to consistently sit him back down to the floor.
- Once he's down on the mat, look to come over the top and into a strong kimura finish.

## Option 3: Physically move the head (summary demonstration)

Danaher restates the full range as three clean options against a kneeling opponent attempting the single leg:

1. **Stuff the head** — a good stuff on the head makes it straightforward to recover your leg; if he pulls the leg in to try to angle off, snake your hand in and sweep through into a good attacking position.
2. **Stand and lock, foot to foot** — with the head to the inside, stay standing on two feet, lock the kimura, go foot to foot with the opponent, and use the kimura itself to drag him over.
3. **Physically move the head** — start with a hip bump to bring the head over, go in and lock, step your foot all the way around the corner, and take him over with a hip throw (sumi gaeshi); follow by bringing your body over the top into the spine finishing position.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Vs. a kneeling single leg you must post an athletic, weighted stance — standing tall/passive invites the double leg switchdown.
- Your height advantage over a kneeling opponent makes it comparatively easy to control his head, which is the prerequisite for either attacking option.
- Three attacking options: (1) stuff the head and lock the kimura; (2) shuck the head to the outside and hook the foot around to lift him over; (3) physically walk the head over with a hip bump and finish with a sumi-gaeshi-style takeover.
- Holding his hand/wrist for the kimura simultaneously denies him the switch to a double leg, so it's safe to bring your feet in close once the lock is on.
- If he holds the single leg tight after a head stuff, switch across into the sweep-the-leg-through recovery and use the kimura to sit him back down whenever he tries to explode back up.
