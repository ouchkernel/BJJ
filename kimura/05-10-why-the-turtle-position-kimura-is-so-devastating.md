# Why The Turtle-Position Kimura Is So Devastating

## Overview

An explanation of why the kimura against turtle position deserves far more attention than it currently gets. It's a very strong and effective attack, yet strangely underused in contemporary jiu-jitsu — most likely because back attacks are so well developed in the sport that fighters default to conventional strangleholds when they get behind a turtled opponent. This chapter argues the kimura against turtle is an outstanding alternative and, per the available historical evidence, was probably the primary setup the historical man Kimura used for his own attacks.

---

## An underused weapon

- The kimura lock against turtle is a very strong and effective form of attack.
- Strangely, it isn't used much in contemporary jiu-jitsu.
- The likely reason: back attacks in general are so well developed in the sport that when most people get behind someone in turtle position, they default to more conventional back attacks — usually going for strangleholds — rather than the kimura.

## Why it's worth prioritizing

- Personally, tremendous and long-standing respect for the efficiency of the kimura as an attack against turtle position.
- When on top with an opponent in a defensive turtle position, the kimura is an incredible method of opening the opponent up to attacks and getting a devastating submission hold.
- The available (if not fully certain) historical evidence suggests this was, in fact, the main setup the historical man Kimura mostly used when hunting his own kimura attacks.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Kimura vs. turtle is very strong and effective but underused — the sport's focus on conventional back attacks (strangles) has crowded it out.
- It's an excellent way to open a turtled opponent up to attack and to get a devastating submission.
- Historically, this appears to be the setup the man Kimura himself relied on most — reason enough to give it serious attention here.
