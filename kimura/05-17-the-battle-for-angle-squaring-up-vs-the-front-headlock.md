# The Battle For Angle — Squaring Up Vs The Front Headlock

## Overview

Once a single leg down on the mat is established (both athletes on their knees), the first exchange is a battle for angle: the attacker wants to work around behind the defender, and the defender's job is to keep the attacker directly in front and square up, sprawling down toward the front-headlock system. This lesson lays out both sides of that battle and what winning or losing it leads to.

---

## The attacker's goal (Mateus in on the single leg)

- Clear goal: form an angle and move around behind the defender.
- The further around behind he gets, the more he weakens the defender's whizzer (over-hook) activity, and the more he can reach across with his free hand to tackle the defender's second leg.
- Once he gets to that second leg, he pulls and drops the defender down to the hip, completing the takedown.
- Practical path: barrel into the defender's whizzer, come around further and further, gaining the ability to sit the defender down and complete the takedown as he goes down to the hip.
- Summary: the attacker is always looking for angle toward the defender's back. If he can't get to his feet and complete a standing takedown, going around behind is always his number-one priority.

## The defender's response

- Goal is the exact opposite: keep the attacker right in front at all times.
- As the attacker starts coming around and forming an angle:
  1. Punch a hand through.
  2. Take the other hand and stuff the attacker's head.
  3. Start sprawling down toward the floor so your hips end up directly over the opponent.
- With that arm in place and hips sprawled over the opponent, it becomes very difficult for him to complete the takedown from that position.
- From here you can go directly into the front headlock system (covered as a separate video/lesson).

## The battle dynamic

- This is described as the most common situation encountered when working single legs down on the mat.
- There's a real battle for supremacy: the attacker wants to go around behind; the defender wants to keep him in front.
- Two outcomes:
  - **Defender wins:** typically ends up in a front headlock and moves into the front headlock system.
  - **Attacker wins:** either completes the takedown outright, or — in many cases — gets the defender's back as he runs around behind.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Single leg down on the mat opens with a battle for angle: attacker wants around behind you, defender wants to stay squared up in front.
- Attacker's path to the back: weaken your whizzer, reach the second leg with the free hand, pull you down to the hip.
- Defender's counter: punch a hand through, stuff the head with the other hand, sprawl hips down directly over the opponent to shut down the takedown.
- Winning the angle battle as the defender leads to a front headlock and the front headlock system; losing it lets the attacker complete the takedown or take your back.
- This sets up the following lesson's response — using the kimura when you're losing the angle battle.
