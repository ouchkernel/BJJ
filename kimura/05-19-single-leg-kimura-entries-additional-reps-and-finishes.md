# Single-Leg Kimura Entries — Additional Reps And Finishes

## Overview

Two additional live reps of entering the Kimura against a single-leg shot, both finishing in the T-Kimura position. The first rep walks through the entry in careful detail — elbow elevation, foot placement, and stretching the opponent's body out to set up a back attack. The second rep shows what to do when the opponent keeps his grip locked on the legs: a triangle is used as a backup tool to separate his hands and turn his face into the same T-Kimura outcome.

---

## Rep One: taking the angle into T-Kimura

- Training partner shoots the single leg and starts forming an angle, reaching the point where he can no longer be kept in front — a normal moment where control of "squaring up" is lost.
- Response: punch the hand inside. The near hand initially serves as a base of support while the frame arm gets in place, then the Kimura is locked up.
- Leg mechanics: the outstretched leg goes out first, then the second leg sweeps through inside the first.
- The Kimura is then used from a seated position — even if the opponent bases his free hand out, it doesn't matter. One good strong pull and he goes over.
- Once down, everything is clamped in tight. If he tries to move, the elbow is kept elevated — even if he manages to come all the way back up to his feet, it means nothing because the frame holds.
- Feet are placed inside one after the other (first foot, then second foot) to stretch his body out fully.
- Result: perfect position to come up and start attacking the back — any of the back attacks covered earlier in the system will work from the T-Kimura position.
- The overarching goal: take an opponent trying to work an angle behind you and use the Kimura to take him from his knees straight to his back, landing in the T-Kimura position — the ideal launch point for counterattacks.

## Rep Two: locked grip on the legs — triangle backup

- Training partner does a good job shooting for the leg; second leg comes up; attempts to square up fail and he gets in and locks up the Kimura.
- He makes a strong grip on the legs to resist. No problem — sweep the first leg through and tilt the man over.
- If his hands remain locked despite the tilt, lock a triangle around the arm to separate his two hands, then turn his face — this converts the position into the classic T-Kimura.
- Once he comes up on top from there, there are countless ways to keep working: the priority is simply anything that keeps the arm trapped in place.
- From that trapped-arm position, lift the opponent up into any of the various finishing attacks demonstrated earlier in the system.

---

## Key Takeaways

- These reps are entry drills into T-Kimura, not new techniques — the emphasis is on the details (elbow elevation, sequential foot placement, stretching the body) that make the entry reliable.
- The Kimura frame holds even if the opponent bases out, keeps a strong grip on the legs, or scrambles back to his feet — none of those reactions defeat it if the fundamentals are held.
- When an opponent's grip stays locked through the tilt, a triangle is the backup tool: it separates the hands and turns his face, converting the scramble into the same classic T-Kimura outcome.
- T-Kimura is treated as a universal launch point — once there, any of the system's established back/arm attacks apply.
