# Bottom Knee To Side Triangle (Yoko Sankaku)

## Overview

A fourth conversion from the T-kimura bottom-knee attack: instead of the rear triangle or three-quarter juji gatame, the leg is passed underneath the opponent's armpit to build a side triangle (yoko sankaku). The initial lock is described as unimpressive on its own — the key is a deliberate adjustment period that tightens it into a genuine finishing triangle, after which the strangle is completed either directly or by returning to a kimura grip.

---

## First demonstration

1. From T-kimura, opponent explodes up on top of you; get your knee in as a wedge underneath his hip and lat muscle to hold and control him.
2. Instead of passing the foot over the shoulder, go over your training partner's near-side head.
3. As you hook, his arm ends up in a position where you can slip your arm through underneath his elbow.
4. Push off your bottom leg to push your opponent back down.
5. Use this side-triangle position to lock an initial lock with your feet.
6. Reinforce it: hold the elbow joint, unlock your feet, and straighten your whole body out around his head.
7. Come back and relock — this produces a devastating strangle position.

## Second demonstration (other side, shows the legs)

1. T-kimura, opponent explodes up on top; focus on getting your knee inside.
2. Pass your leg across so it fits underneath your training partner's armpit.
3. Push off your bottom leg and send your legs through.
4. The initial lock is not very impressive — there has to be a period of adjustment.
5. Adjustment: lock the elbow, put your bottom foot on the floor, and turn your body belly-down.
6. Relock for good, tight coverage of the triangle.
7. From here, go back to a kimura grip and strangle for the finish.

## Why it works

- The T-kimura can easily be converted into a yoko sankaku (side triangle), which is a fine way to strangle an opponent.
- The initial triangle lock alone isn't tight enough to finish — the belly-down adjustment is what converts it into a real finishing shape.

---

## Key Takeaways

- From T-kimura bottom-knee control, passing the leg underneath the opponent's armpit (rather than over the shoulder or through the head) builds a yoko sankaku (side triangle).
- The knee-as-wedge under the hip/lat is what lets you hold and control the opponent enough to set this up.
- The initial triangle lock is deliberately loose/unimpressive — don't expect to finish from it immediately.
- Adjustment sequence to tighten it: lock the elbow, post the bottom foot, turn belly-down, then relock.
- Once tight, you can return to a kimura grip and strangle for the finish, or finish the strangle directly from the triangle.
