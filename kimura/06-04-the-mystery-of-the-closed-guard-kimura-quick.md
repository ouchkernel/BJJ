# The Mystery Of The Closed Guard Kimura — Quick Reference

## The mystery

- Every other beginner move (elbow escape, upa, armbar from guard, back strangles) stays a lifelong staple, used constantly at every level up to world championships.
- Closed guard kimura: taught day one, but almost never finished at elite level (roughly 5-10 times in 25 years, vs. tens of thousands of successes for other basics).
- Notable example: Jacaré finished Marcelo Garcia with it — remembered precisely because it's so rare.
- Kimura itself works fine everywhere else (MMA, gi, no-gi) — the problem is isolated to this specific setup.

## Traditional (failing) setup

1. Pommel past his hands on your biceps.
2. Bring his hands to the floor.
3. Open guard, fake his head one way, use body momentum to move up the other way.
4. Lock kimura, taking advantage of hands being on the floor.
5. Move out to the side, attempt finish.

## Why it fails against good opponents — 3 independent stoppers

| Element | How it stops you |
|---|---|
| Hands | Hand back on your chest = you can't get up at all |
| Biceps | He walks around your waist, returns you flat to the mat |
| Head | His head follows yours and physically blocks you sitting up |

Speed/momentum can't beat all three at once against an aware opponent — small movements from him counter a fast attacker every time.

## Diagnosis

- Not a kimura problem — a **setup** problem (and a push-vs-pull finishing problem).
- Fix = better setup (wrist control, power probing) — covered in the next lessons, not a different submission.
