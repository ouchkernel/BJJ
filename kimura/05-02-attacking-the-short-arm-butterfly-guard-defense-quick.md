# Attacking The Short-Arm Butterfly Guard Defense — Quick Reference

## When to use this

- Opponent keeps hands back / won't reach ("short arms") because he's been caught by Kimura before.
- If arms are long instead → skip this, go straight to the conventional Kimura entry.

## Head-throw tactic (hand-base reaction)

1. Can't grab the wrist directly — it's guarded. Move his head instead.
2. Throw/force the head toward the mat.
3. He bases a hand out to catch himself.
4. Seize that base hand by the wrist.
5. Post on his hand, go up and over the top.
6. Land in the standard butterfly Kimura attacking position.

## From the attacking position

1. Bottom knee under, or
2. Top leg over/top knee control
3. Either leads to finishes — e.g., cross Kimura.

## Variant: shoulder-drop reaction

1. Opponent lets the shoulder go to the mat instead of basing a hand.
2. Step to inside position, score the wrist directly.
3. Go over the top → same attacking position as the hand-base version.

## Core principle

- Force the hand to the mat (via head or shoulder) → cover it → body over the top → same attacking position every time.
