# Kimura As The Response To Losing The Angle Battle

## Overview

When the defender is clearly losing the battle for angle against a single leg down on the mat — the attacker has formed a perpendicular angle and squaring up is no longer working — the kimura becomes the go-to response. This lesson walks through the mechanics of converting a losing angle battle into a kimura control position (the "T kimura"), with a strong finish and a triangle-kimura ("trimura") backup if the opponent's head rises.

---

## Recognizing the trigger

- Mateus gets in deep on the single leg; the defender tries to square up and fails.
- Mateus succeeds in forming a perpendicular (right) angle on the defender.
- This loss of the angle battle is the trigger for switching to the kimura response.

## Step one: never leave two knees on the mat

- With two knees down, the attacker has an easy path to a double leg and will immediately put the defender down to a hip.
- The instant the defender sees the attacker has formed a right angle, the first priority is to post the second leg up (get off two knees).

## Building the kimura grip

1. Normal instinct is to try to square back up to the man — but if his hand-fighting makes this difficult, seize the wrist instead.
2. Bring your head over his head (a reversal of the usual head-toward-the-hips instinct).
3. Punch through with the frame arm.
4. As the attacker tries to spin around behind, control him with that grip.
5. Use the kimura grip and sweep your leg through on him.
6. This lets you easily pull him over, landing directly in the T-kimura control position.

## Working the T-kimura

- Once in the T-kimura, there are countless ways to proceed as the opponent fights to get back up.
- Standard priorities: penetrate with the bottom knee, or cross-face with the top knee.
- From here, a strong finish is available directly.

## Backup: the triangle-kimura ("trimura")

- If the opponent's head rises during the fight to escape, there's no need to abandon the position — come around the corner, lock up the triangle, and finish with the combined triangle-kimura ("trimura") on the training partner.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Trigger for the kimura response: you're losing the angle battle and the attacker has formed a perpendicular angle on you.
- Golden rule: never leave both knees on the mat once he's squared to a right angle — post the second leg up immediately or he gets an easy double leg and drops you to the hip.
- Grip sequence: seize the wrist, bring your head over his head (not toward the hips), punch the frame arm through, control him as he tries to spin behind, sweep the kimura-side leg through, and pull him over into the T-kimura.
- From the T-kimura: fight for bottom-knee penetration or top-knee cross-face, then finish.
- If his head rises during the scramble, come around the corner and lock the triangle for a triangle-kimura ("trimura") finish instead of losing the position.
