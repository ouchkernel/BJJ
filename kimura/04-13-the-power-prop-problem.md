# The Power Prop Problem

## Overview

From half guard top, the bottom player's most natural counter-attack is the underhook. This lesson first shows how simple head/chin positioning neutralizes a basic underhook, then introduces a more serious escalation — the bottom player posting up on the elbow instead of staying flat on the shoulder (the "power prop"). The power prop destabilizes the top player and opens up sweeps and leg attacks, and it makes attacking the underhook arm with a kimura directly a genuinely risky idea.

---

## The Basic Underhook Threat

- From half guard, the bottom player will very often work for an underhook.
- Danger: if your chin is up over your training partner's shoulder and he hikes that underhook up, he can scoop inside and start rolling you across — you start giving up back exposure.
- There's a wide range of bad things that can follow once he gets that underhook hiked and your chin is high.
- The whole problem comes back to head position, manifested through where your chin is.

## Neutralizing It With Head Position

- If your chin stays over his shoulder while he hikes the underhook, you get destabilized into a head-over-head position — that's a losing spot to be in.
- The target instead is **head over hips**.
- Fix: drop your chin down to his elbow. Once your chin is at elbow level, it becomes significantly harder for him to hike that arm up.
- Reinforce it further by coming across and getting good elbow position on your opponent, then sitting through. This makes his underhook almost useless and leaves you head-over-hips — a good platform to start using his own underhook against him.

## The Power Prop Escalation

- A well-defended basic underhook often inspires the bottom player to switch strategies entirely: instead of staying down on the shoulder with the underhook, he comes up onto the elbow — the **power prop**.
- This significantly complicates the top player's situation — it creates a real feeling of instability.
- From the power prop, the bottom player can come up with single-leg attacks and scoop inside the top player's leg. There's a wide range of dangerous options available to him from here.
- Given that instability and the number of live threats, it becomes very difficult and risky to go straight for a kimura in this position — attacking carelessly here exposes your back to your opponent.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Half guard underhooks are neutralized through head/chin position: chin over the shoulder invites destabilization, chin down at the elbow (reinforced by crossing to good elbow position and sitting through) shuts it down.
- A well-defended underhook often provokes the opponent to switch to the power prop — posting up on the elbow instead of staying flat.
- The power prop is dangerous: it destabilizes the top player and opens the door to sweeps and single-leg attacks.
- Attacking directly for a kimura here is risky — it exposes your back to a live, actively-attacking opponent.
