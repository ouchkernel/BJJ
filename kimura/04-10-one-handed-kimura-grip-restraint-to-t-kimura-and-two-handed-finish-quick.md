# One-Handed Kimura Grip — Restraint To T-Kimura And Two-Handed Finish — Quick Reference

## Core concept

Kimura has two jobs here: **restrain** (one-handed grip, clear the legs) then **submit** (two-handed figure-four, finish).

## One-handed grip — setup

1. From a figure-four, release and lift your palm off his hand.
2. Pass off: controlling hand moves from over-your-wrist to under-your-wrist.
3. Grip target = **blade of the hand**, 4 fingers, no thumb. NEVER the wrist (he straightens and escapes).
4. Free hand to the floor, hike his hand toward his armpit → elbow to shoulder line.

## Clearing the legs

1. Shoot hips out, hand on his near-side knee.
2. As he locks legs, scoot hips out, knee in as a wedge.
3. Trust the grip — position looks unstable but isn't dangerous.
4. Post on his leg, extract your trapped leg.
5. If he explodes up: post arm underneath, hold the Kimura — his own arm stops him.
6. Even without leg control, one-handed Kimura alone restrains a base-recovery attempt.

## Converting to the finish

1. Post hand on floor, switch base back in.
2. Return to two-handed figure-four.
3. Crowd his shoulder line, step over the head.
4. Move to the diagonal line of power.
5. Elbow-to-elbow, pull-dominant Kimura → finish.

## Base leg rule

- Keep support leg **bent and on the toes** — never straight/relaxed (bridge risk).

## Reading his legs during extraction

- Knees up high → scoop underneath them.
- Legs extended away → post on bottom knee, knee in, post and slip leg out.

## Checklist

- [ ] Grip is on the blade, not the wrist
- [ ] Elbow hiked to shoulder line
- [ ] Base leg bent, on toes
- [ ] Leg extracted using knee wedge / post
- [ ] Converted back to two-handed figure-four before finishing
- [ ] On diagonal line of power before pulling for the finish
