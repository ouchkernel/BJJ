# Practical Application Roadmap — Top, Bottom, And Standing — Quick Reference

## Three practical categories

1. Top position
2. Bottom position
3. Standing position

(Starting point for the material: top position.)

## Where to start learning

- Best starting point for mechanics: side kimura on top.
- Real problem now: not finishing a kimura, but **getting to one** — once an opponent knows your kimura is dangerous, they defend hard against it.

## Neutral position = opportunity

1. In a neutral exchange (e.g., seated open guard), opponent doesn't feel threatened — legs still in play.
2. They reach for your body freely.
3. The moment your head is higher than their elbow, and your elbow is higher than theirs → easy kimura entry.
4. This only works because the position is neutral — a threatened opponent wouldn't reach this way.
