# One System, Many Kimuras: The Grip Variations — Quick Reference

## Core principle

Every finishing hold needs multiple variants (same logic as using many heel hooks, not just one) — small contingencies during application favor different versions. This system has 5 Kimura variants off a basic figure-four, plus a 6th that doesn't stem from the figure-four at all (more Kimura-like than omoplata-like, per Danaher).

## Variant 1 — Classic figure-four Kimura

- Push hand at his wrist, pull hand at your own wrist.
- Works from any position, top or bottom.

## Variant 2 — Bicep figure-four

- Still figure-four, but different hands: one to his shoulder, one to your own shoulder.
- No push/pull wrist hands.
- Same Kimura action on the arm, different pressure mechanics.

## Variant 3 — Cross Kimura

1. Start from a conventional figure-four Kimura hand grip.
2. Bring both legs across the opponent's body.
3. Feet locked or unlocked — either works.
4. Legs reinforce the grip for very strong breaks.

## When to use legs (cross Kimura)

Whenever you can't get your hand quickly/efficiently behind the opponent's back — use the legs to reinforce instead.
