# Turtle Kimura Finish — The Diagonal Power Line And Breaking Mechanics — Quick Reference

## From bar arm to kimura

1. Secure one-on-one tight; come around the corner into two hands on one.
2. Knee over head, pinch, elevate, throw in the bar arm.
3. Partner's arm is exposed from turtle base — go elbow to elbow, catch the kimura.

## Taking it to the mat

1. Take the trapped hand to his spine ("hand behind the back" / posterior hand position).
2. Bring him down to his shoulder: step foot up, knee to mat, turn, elbow down.
3. Spread feet 90 degrees.
4. Dominate the power line: head down to his hips, elbow drives to his elbow.
5. = punishing kimura, landed exactly on the power line.

## Alternate entry (facing the back)

1. Two arms in behind his arms — NOT a seatbelt/strangle grip.
2. Head over his head; commit with the head to snatch the wrist, feed it in (initial one-on-one).
3. Around the corner as he bases out; knee over head, lock the first bar arm (now almost impossible for him to get his hand back to the floor).
4. Elbow to elbow, lock the kimura, drive him down to the mat.
5. Landing: knee forward, elbow down, legs step across his body, head over his hips, elbow to elbow — pull dominant kimura.

## The one rule that matters

- **Diagonal line of power:** head, hips, and locked elbow all in one line = maximum breaking force.
- Correct alignment lets your body keep extending well past the opponent's actual breaking point — this is why the position can snap an arm with minimal apparent effort.

## Why it works historically

- Matches the historical man Kimura's reputed method for breaking arms: controlling entry landed squarely on the diagonal power line.
