# Power Probing — Consolidating The Elbow-Prop Setup — Quick Reference

## Core idea

Lead with the **pushing hand** onto his wrist (not the traditional pulling/framing arm) — force his hand to the mat first ("power probing"), then enter.

## Why the classic method fails (recap)

| No wrist control means... | Result |
|---|---|
| He can lock hands, bicep-to-chest | Crushed back flat, kimura lost |
| Fast opponent hand-checks your chest | You're stopped instantly |
| Nothing pins his hand to the floor | He posts and throws you back down anytime |
| His head never moves | Blocks your sit-up directly |

## Power-probing entry steps

1. Force his hand down to the mat (steal inside position + bump forward with legs; or collar-tie + pull hand down; many small variations work).
2. Grip the wrist.
3. Come up to a **propped elbow** — elbow must stay **behind** your head (elbow in front = you collapse under his shoulder push).
4. Keep everything in front, go around his body into the kimura grip.
5. As he tries to bring his head back up: shoot hips out, invert far knee, come up to the shoulder line.
6. Shoulders + elbow + your head aligned on the shoulder line → small pull finishes it.

## Full checklist

1. Create space → get a hand down → grip wrist.
2. Prop elbow (behind head) even as he fights you.
3. Secure figure-four grip.
4. Invert far knee.
5. Feet positioned, hips off the floor.
6. Align shoulder line.
7. Pull to finish.

## Key point

The rare elite successes of closed guard kimura have always involved wrist control enabling an elbow prop — power probing makes that repeatable instead of accidental.
