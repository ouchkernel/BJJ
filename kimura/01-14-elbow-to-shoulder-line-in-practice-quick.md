# Elbow To Shoulder Line In Practice — Quick Reference

## Concept

- Shoulder line = line joining opponent's two shoulders.
- Elbow far from shoulder line → opponent feels little pressure, you do more work.
- Elbow ON the shoulder line → break comes cheap.

## Rule

- You **pull** the elbow to the shoulder line. You cannot push it there.

## Steps

1. Head goes over the opponent's elbow first (opposite of your finishing head position).
2. Find his elbow with your elbow.
3. Move your head away — pull his elbow up to the shoulder line.
4. If he holds his hand down at the hip, don't yank haphazardly — do a controlled "armed test" lift first.
5. Once elbow is on the shoulder line: bring elbow to elbow, trap the head, pull backward to finish.

## Why bother

- Arm left down at the hip = hard break, lots of mechanical work.
- Elbow brought to shoulder line first = same or more devastating break for much less work.
- Works even on a straight arm (bent arm still preferred when available).
