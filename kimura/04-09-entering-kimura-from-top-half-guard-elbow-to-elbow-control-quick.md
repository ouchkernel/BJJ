# Entering Kimura From Top Half Guard — Elbow-To-Elbow Control — Quick Reference

## Situation

- Top half guard, opponent has your leg trapped and is working an underhook (shoulder-down or propped on elbow — "power prop"; either is fine as long as you can reach the top arm).

## The key rule

- Underhook danger is proportional to your head position, NOT just the underhook itself.
- Head drifts north (above his head) → underhook becomes very effective, exposes your back.
- Head stays low, over his hips → underhook is neutralized.

## Fix — elbow-to-elbow control

1. Bring your near arm across over his near arm.
2. Lock elbow-to-elbow.
3. Keep your head over his hips (not drifting north).
4. Result: underhook can't hike you up.
5. Now it's easy to access the wrist and start Kimura attacks from half guard.

## Checklist before attacking the wrist

- [ ] Elbow-to-elbow established?
- [ ] Head kept low, over his hips?
- [ ] Underhook confirmed neutralized (he can't hike you)?

This is the foundation for the follow-up half-guard Kimura entries (one-handed restraint/T-Kimura, freeing the trapped knee, chin-position underhook negation).
