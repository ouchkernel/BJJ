# Thumb Or No Thumb — Quick Reference

## The question

Should you lock your thumb over the opponent's wrist in kimura, or grip thumbless?

## The theory (why people say "use the thumb")

1. Turning the forearm with the thumb creates tension and pushes the elbow forward.
2. Kimura is a twisting lock, so a turn should add breaking power — in theory.

## Why it breaks down

- Only works if the opponent is relaxed and lets his wrist turn.
- Against a strong resistant arm: your thumb just spins around his wrist — no turning effect at all.

## The evidence

- Countless arms broken in competition/training with **both** thumb and thumbless grips, roughly even split.
- Some kimuras use zero hand-on-wrist contact (leg-based) — no thumb, no possible wrist twist — and still produce devastating breaks.
- Conclusion: **thumb position is not what breaks the arm.**

## Rule of thumb (grip choice)

| Opponent's wrist | Grip |
|---|---|
| Smaller | Use the thumb |
| Much larger / heavier ("gargantuan wrists") | Skip the thumb |

## Bottom line

- Don't obsess over thumb vs. no thumb for **breaking power** — personal preference, both work.
- It DOES matter for **entering/setting up** the kimura (covered later) — just not for the finish.
