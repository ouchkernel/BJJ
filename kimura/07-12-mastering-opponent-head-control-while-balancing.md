# Mastering Opponent Head Control While Balancing

## Overview

A foundational skill chapter for standing kimura work against the single leg: the ability to physically move an opponent's head left and right while maintaining balance, whether he's on two feet or two knees. Head position was already established as the deciding factor for whether the kimura is easy or blocked — this chapter drills the skill of actively controlling it rather than just reacting to wherever it happens to be.

---

## Why head position matters

- If the opponent's head is to the inside, it's a physical barrier to the kimura — you have to stuff it out of the way.
- If the opponent's head is to the outside, there's no barrier and the kimura is generally easier to access.
- Because head position isn't fixed during a live single leg exchange, the ability to actively move it — not just wait for a favorable position — is treated as a core combat skill for anyone who wants to call themselves a master of the kimura in the standing position.

## Building the base position

- Starting point: training partner has shot a high single leg with the head to the inside.
- Initial defensive posture: a frame/wizard grip in with a hand on or near the opponent's wrist — this works regardless of whether the opponent has his hands locked, or is holding the ankle/calf instead.
- When there's time to work the head, drop the wizard frame and instead post both hands on the opponent's shoulders, using that post to keep balance.
- As the opponent tries to circle you down to the floor, use a hopping action to stay balanced and travel with him rather than getting stacked or dumped — e.g., if he "runs the pipe" to try to knock you down, hop with him.

## Moving the head at will

- From the balanced posted position, you should be able to move the opponent's head in either direction:
  - **Stuff it down/inside**: drive a forearm into the head and physically push it down; as he fights to bring it back up, post on the inside and post the elbow.
  - **Bring it back around the other side**: physically post the head back down on that side.
- Goal: be able to move the opponent's head left and right at will while balancing on one leg — described as a very important combat skill.
- The same principle applies with the opponent down on two knees on the single leg: keep your free leg positioned so he can't immediately switch to a double leg, then bring his head down to the inside or move it to the outside using the forearm.

## Applying it to access the kimura

- Once you can control the head, accessing the arm for the kimura becomes much easier.
- Example sequence: opponent scores the single leg; post initially and post the head so you can access the kimura. If he brings his head up and pushes into you, push the head to the outside and take the kimura.
- Example sequence from standing: opponent picks up a high single leg; hop with him to keep balance as he tries to move you; bring his head down into a stuck position where you can access the kimura; move his head to the outside, lock the kimura, and use a hip throw (sumi gaeshi) to bring him over into the finishing position.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Head position determines kimura access: head to the inside blocks it, head to the outside opens it — so actively moving the head is the skill, not waiting for luck.
- Base posture: frame/wizard hand on the wrist when compressed for time; posted hands on both shoulders with a hopping balance action when there's room to work.
- You should be able to move an opponent's head in either direction — stuff it down/inside or post it back around — while balancing on one leg, against an opponent on either two feet or two knees.
- Keep the free leg positioned so a kneeling opponent can't switch straight to a double leg while you work the head.
- Once the head is controlled, lock the kimura and finish the takeover with a hip throw (sumi gaeshi) into the standard finishing position.
