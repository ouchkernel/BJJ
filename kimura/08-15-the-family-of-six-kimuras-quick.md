# The Family Of Six Kimuras — Quick Reference

## The principle

Five figure-four-based kimuras cover each other — when one fails, switch to another. Always have more than one way to express a move.

## The 6th kimura (leg-entry, no figure four)

- Typically from **turtle position**.
- Sometimes called "reverse omoplata," but plays more like a kimura.

Steps:
1. Knee comes in, wedges on training partner.
2. Lock the opponent's arm in place.
3. Pass the arm across to the other side — grab his wrist or a leg.
4. Put his hand behind his back.
5. Rotate through, send him across.
6. Come up to your knees, catch the hips, rotate back.
7. Result: same kimura breaking pressure, generated by the legs.

## The six as a team

One of the six will always be appropriate — whether resistance shows up at entry or at the finish.

## Beyond the six

- Other kimura variations exist (e.g., bahata plata, a leg-based kimura) but aren't part of this system.
- Reason: judged either unnecessary or already better covered by one of the six or by other moves.
