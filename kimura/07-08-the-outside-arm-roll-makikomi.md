# The Outside Arm Roll (Makikomi)

## Overview

A companion to the inside arm roll from bottom turtle position: when an opponent behind you has *not* given you the inside arm (hands not locked, working a tight waist grip or a hand on your bicep instead), you switch to the outside arm and use a standard makikomi-style roll instead. The mechanics differ from the inside arm roll but the destination is the same — good attacking position across the opponent's side, with a strong kimura opportunity that appears naturally as he tries to fight back to face you.

---

## Reading which roll to use

- Whenever an opponent is behind you, he'll be slightly out to one side. His arm between the two of you is the **inside arm**; his other arm, positioned on the outside, is the **outside arm**.
- If his hands are locked: favor the inside arm roll (covered separately).
- If his hands are **not** locked — e.g., he's controlling with a tight waist grip or has a hand on your bicep — you can't attack the inside arm, because he hasn't given it to you. Work the outside arm instead.

## Outside arm roll (makikomi) mechanics

1. Control his arm **wrist to wrist** — do not try to reach up above the elbow.
2. Lock wrist to wrist so his body ties into your body.
3. Do **not** roll into his base. Instead, roll in a forwards diagonal.
4. Locking his arm in this way makes it very difficult for him to yank it back out — it's anchored around your rib cage.
5. Extend your inside leg, then turn your body open, rolling him through.
6. You end up in good attacking position across your training partner's side.

## The follow-up kimura

- As your opponent tries to swim back into you (to re-face you), his arm inevitably comes underneath your arm — this is exactly the opportunity you're looking for.
- Catch the arm here and lock up a quick kimura.
- Your frame hand comes through for a good grip on his arm.
- He'll want to tuck his hand inside his own thigh for protection — don't let him; keep his hand down behind his back.
- Switch your base, come up, and work to beat his shoulder line: get your elbow down to the floor and walk around the line of his shoulders.
- Bring everything over into the diagonal line of power for a strong breaking finish.

## Variation — opponent doesn't lock hands

1. Go in and catch wrist to wrist as before.
2. Bring your leg out and turn underneath your training partner, ending in a good pin.
3. If he pushes his hand through and underneath to try to escape, catch it there.
4. Lock the hands in a figure-of-four grip.
5. Bring your hips up, walk past the line of his shoulders.
6. Finish on the diagonal power line for a strong, bone-breaking kimura.

## Key Takeaways

- Choice of roll depends on whether hands are locked: inside arm roll when his hands are locked, outside arm roll (makikomi) when they aren't and you can't access the inside arm.
- Control the outside arm wrist to wrist only — reaching above the elbow is unnecessary and weaker.
- Roll in a forwards diagonal, never into the opponent's own base of support.
- The best kimura opportunity often appears *after* the roll: when the opponent tries to swim back and face you, his arm slides under yours — catch it there.
- Keep the opponent's trapped hand pinned behind his back, not tucked under his own thigh for protection.
- Finish by walking your base past his shoulder line and driving into the diagonal power line.
