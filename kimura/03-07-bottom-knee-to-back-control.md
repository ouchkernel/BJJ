# Bottom Knee To Back Control

## Overview

Shows several closely related ways to convert the bottom-knee penetration out of T-kimura directly into back control, once the opponent turns into you and tries to rise. All variations share the same core mechanics: punch/bring the bottom knee inside, get a second hook in, win the head-height battle, and use the arm and lever control already built to swing around onto the back.

---

## Method 1 — bottom knee, hook, and head-height battle

- Starting locked up in T-kimura, as the opponent starts turning up into you, punch the bottom knee inside.
- As he goes to get up, throw the second leg over as a hook; as he fights to base, extend that second leg through — you now have two hooks in.
- Take the top hand off and throw it across so both hooks and both hands are working together. You don't want the opponent getting up inside your legs and into your guard — that's the failure state to avoid.
- Get your hand in, take the second hand back.
- As the opponent rises, the whole battle is now for **head height**: you must get your head higher than his. If his head ends up higher than yours as he turns, he lands in your guard instead.
- To win head height: bring the hand in with the thumb leading, and build a base. On elbows only, both heads are the same height — to truly build a base, put weight onto one arm and come up to a hand. That gives you a height advantage.
- As the opponent goes to get up, get your elbow to the floor and come across — this lands you in a good back-control position.

## Method 2 — knee through, hook over the top

- From T-kimura, opponent turns into you; bring your knee through inside, hook your second foot over the top.
- Come up and hook — both partners on an elbow, and as the opponent continues rising, you're in the perfect position to thread both hooks in.

## Method 3 — lift and turn across the body

- Opponent comes up; bring the knee inside, pass the second foot over the top.
- As he goes to get up on top of you, use your elbow to lift him and your leg to turn him across the body.
- This leaves you in position to hug his arm, bring your arm around, and go straight into the usual back attacks.

## Method 4 — lever over the hip

- Opponent does a good job coming up on top; bring the knee through and take your foot over his hip.
- As he fights to get up on top, use that leg as a lever to lift him over.
- Hug the arm, bring your arm around the corner — feet land in perfect position to lock up the back.

---

## Key Takeaways

- All bottom-knee entries share the same shape: knee punches/threads inside, second leg follows as a hook, and the whole sequence turns the T-kimura scramble directly into back control.
- The decisive detail in the primary method is head height — losing that battle puts the opponent in your guard instead of you on his back, so actively build a base (weight onto one arm, up to a hand) to win it.
- Avoid letting the opponent get up inside your legs into guard — that's the specific failure to watch for while threading hooks.
- Several variations exist (hook-over-top, lift-and-turn, lever-over-the-hip) but all end the same way: hug the arm, bring it around, and lock up the back.
