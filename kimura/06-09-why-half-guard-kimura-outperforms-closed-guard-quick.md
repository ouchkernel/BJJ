# Why Half Guard Kimura Outperforms Closed Guard — Quick Reference

## Why half guard beats closed guard for kimura

1. **Angle** — closed guard = square to opponent, long distance to travel. Half guard = favorable angle, opponent's lead arm is close.
2. **Opponent psychology** — a half guard passer who's beaten one leg feels confident, reaches with the lead arm to control the head → gives up easy kimura access.

Danaher now teaches kimura-from-bottom starting in half guard, saves closed guard entries for advanced athletes.

## Entry checklist

1. **Prevent chest-to-chest.** Knee shield in the hip pocket, shallow/conservative (no gi) or higher with lapel + reinforced elbow/knee (gi).
2. **Dominate inside space.** Elbow on one shoulder, hand on the other (forearm across shoulder line) + second hand inside — ties up all inside space, holds opponent off. Monitor distance with the knee.
3. **Come up for the grip.** Establish wrist control, come up and around (away from the head, not over the shoulder toward it) into the power prop, lock the beginning of the kimura.
4. **Do NOT fall backward.** That lets the hand go straight back inside his own thigh.
5. **Bring hips to his hand instead.** Makes retracting the hand very hard → get the hand behind the back.

## Why hand-behind-back matters

- Posterior kimura >> anterior kimura for finishing.
- Once the hand is behind the back, even strong opponents become weak.
