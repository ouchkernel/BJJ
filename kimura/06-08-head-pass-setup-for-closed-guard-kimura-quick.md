# Head Pass Setup For Closed Guard Kimura — Quick Reference

## The problem

Kimura from bottom closed guard has historically underperformed. Three obstacles block it:

1. Opponent's head
2. Opponent's hands
3. Opponent's arm wrapping around your body and flattening you

## The fix: pass the head

1. Bottom closed guard — secure head/shoulders: collar tie one hand, other hand near the ear (not face/eyes).
2. Open the closed guard with confidence.
3. Push the opponent's head to one side; bring your same-side arm across to the other hand — head is now out of the way.
4. Elbow inside opponent's elbow, arm over the top — lock a basic figure-four (elbow control only, no wrist yet).
5. Move out to the side, invert near knee, lock.
6. Come down to the wrist conventionally — opponent's hand inside your thigh, thigh over the top.
7. Bring everything up to the shoulder/power line — finish.

## Alternate entry (elbows-across)

1. Inside position, pull opponent forward, secure and pass the head.
2. Bring your two elbows across each other.
3. One elbow inside, come up, lock the figure-four (elbow only).
4. Turn body out to the side, cross legs, everything up to shoulder line, finish.

## Key points

- Elbow-only grip (no wrist) is already very hard to escape — don't rush the wrist.
- Passing the head is the single move that removes the biggest obstacle to closed guard kimura.
- Finish always aligns on the shoulder line — small pull, big effect.
