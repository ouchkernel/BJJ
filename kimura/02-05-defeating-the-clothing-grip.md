# Defeating The Clothing Grip

## Overview

Covers the third common reinforcement tactic: the opponent grabbing his own pants or clothing to protect the kimura arm. Since grabbing an opponent's own clothing is legal (only grabbing the *other* person's clothing is illegal), this defense is available even in no-gi. The counter works by attacking the grip's weak direction rather than fighting its strong, robust straight-line pull.

---

## The problem

- The opponent reinforces his hand by grabbing his own pants or clothing.
- No-gi grappling rules prohibit grabbing the opponent's clothing — but there's nothing preventing the opponent from grabbing his own clothing.
- Opponents using this tactic typically play everything in tight.
- If you simply try to pull the hand away in a straight line, the clothing grip is quite robust and strong — a straight-line pull loses to it.

## The solution

- Work in the opposite direction of the clothing grip: take the hand in what appears to be the "wrong" direction rather than fighting the grip head-on.
- Follow by taking the elbow up above the shoulder line.
- Because you moved the hand away in the opposite direction to where the grip was set, when the opponent tries to recover his grip it's very difficult for him to get back to it. If you instead brought the hand back the "normal" way, he would sweep right back into the same grip he just had.

## Sequence

1. Take the hand away from the clothing, moving it out in the opposite/"wrong" direction relative to the grip.
2. Take the elbow up above the shoulder line.
3. Put the knuckles in behind the scapula.
4. Shrug the elbow over the opponent's hip.
5. Come down to an elbow, sit on the power line, and get a strong finish.

## Limits of these expedients

- These are simple expedients for common problems, not a universal solution — some reinforced hands will be locked down to a degree these tricks won't overcome (other tactics are addressed separately).
- Even so, in a surprising number of cases, decisive action along these lines is enough to take what looks like a stalled situation and convert it into a kimura submission win.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Own-clothing grips are legal even in no-gi (only grabbing the *opponent's* clothing is illegal) and are quite strong against a straight-line pull.
- Counter by moving the hand in the opposite direction from the grip, not fighting it head-on — this also denies the opponent an easy re-grip.
- Sequence: hand away in the wrong direction → elbow above the shoulder line → knuckles behind the scapula → elbow shrug over the hip → elbow down, sit on the power line, finish.
- These are simple expedients, not a complete answer to a deeply reinforced hand — but they resolve a surprising number of stalled situations decisively.
