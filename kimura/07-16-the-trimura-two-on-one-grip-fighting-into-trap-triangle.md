# The Trimura — Two-On-One Grip Fighting Into Trap Triangle

## Overview

Introduces the "trimura" — a hybrid attack that starts as grip fighting toward a trap triangle and ends locked as a kimura, half triangle and half kimura. The mechanism is a supinated elbow two-on-one grip: how to take it, why its one weakness has to be covered with the chest, and how to convert the same control from a leg-based trap triangle entry into a finished kimura lock.

---

## The grip: supinated elbow two-on-one

- **Pronated grip**: palm faces away from you.
- **Supinated grip**: palm faces back toward you — this is the version used here.
- The weakness of the supinated two-on-one is toward you: if the opponent drives his hand toward you, he can break the grip.
- Fix: cover that weakness with your chest.

## Taking and securing the grip

1. Take the opponent's hand with a two-on-one.
2. Hug his knuckles in toward your chest — this closes off the weak direction and leaves the hand with nowhere to escape to.
3. Use a full five-finger grip; the thumb isn't needed and doesn't help.
4. Result: with one arm completely controlled this way, it's very difficult for the opponent to pull free.

## From grip control into the trap triangle

1. With the arm controlled, plant a foot on the opponent's hip.
2. Kick over into the trap triangle.
3. Push the wrist to the outside.
4. Drive the hips up.
5. Pass the hand off and take control of the elbow.
6. Bring the elbow to your sternum and lock up the kimura.

## Why "trimura"

- The finished position combines a trap triangle leg configuration with a locked kimura grip — half triangle, half kimura, hence "trimura."
- Repeating the core mechanics: elbow two-on-one, supinated grip, everything pulled in toward the chest so the opponent can't pull away.
- Foot roles: the near/controlling foot manages distance, the far foot kicks over into the triangle configuration.
- End state: excellent control of both the head and one arm.
- Force the hand to the outside, pass it across, and take control of the elbow — as the opponent's head is driven down, the kimura becomes progressively easier to finish.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Supinated two-on-one (palm toward you) is the grip of choice for the trimura entry; its only weakness — being broken toward you — is neutralized by hugging the opponent's knuckles into your chest.
- Use a full five-finger grip; the thumb contributes nothing to this particular hold.
- Entry sequence: control the arm at the chest → foot on hip → kick into the trap triangle → push wrist outside → hips up → pass hand off → control elbow → elbow to sternum → lock the kimura.
- The trimura name reflects the finished shape: a trap-triangle leg configuration combined with a kimura arm lock, run simultaneously off the same controlled arm.
- As the opponent's head is forced down during the sequence, the kimura lock becomes progressively easier to finish.
