# T-Kimura To Russian Arm Control And Pin

## Overview

The technical entry into the Russian arm out of T-kimura, including the fallback plan for when a strong or explosive opponent fights all the way up to his knees despite your best efforts. Even then, you still have a Russian arm — and from there this chapter shows how to convert it into a full pin, using an ear-to-chest head position and a two-leg lift to bring the opponent down and settle on top.

---

## The fundamental rules of the T-kimura engagement

- If your opponent turns away from you, his own kimura stops him.
- If he stays still, you simply come up and pin him.
- So his only real option is to turn into you.
- As he turns in, there's a battle for elbow position: you want to lift and frustrate him at the elbow, which always creates space to get your knee inside.
- But there will always be an explosive opponent who manages to get his elbow to the floor and, despite your best efforts, fights all the way up to his knees.

## The fallback: you still have a Russian arm

- Even if he gets all the way up, his arm is still pinned across the centerline — in other words, you still have a Russian arm.

## Entry sequence

1. As your knee comes in, take your second foot and place it in as a butterfly hook.
2. Lift up on his elbow and bring your second knee across, catching your training partner's arm with your hand.
3. As he goes to pull away, sit up and take the Russian arm — your head ends up on one side and the Russian arm on the other.

## Attacking from the Russian arm — control details

- Maintain the grip on the lat muscle and the grip on the bicep, keeping your head in close, so that when he pulls away you can simply hold at the forearm — this makes it surprisingly difficult for him to yank the arm free.
- Put your foot (e.g. left foot) into his near thigh.
- Start an action of falling back and lifting so he goes up into the air.
- Keep your ear on his chest — if your head comes upright, he can quickly spin into you and cause problems; ear-on-chest prevents that turn-in.
- Put up a post leg, bring the second leg in, and settle into a good pin across your training partner's side.

## Second demonstration

1. T-kimura, opponent explodes up to his base; knee comes in, but he does a good job of getting up to his knees anyway.
2. Thread your foot in as a butterfly hook, bring the second leg around the corner, and as your hand comes back, sit up and hook.
3. Slide in and lift your training partner up in the air — initially with two legs.
4. Once his hips come over yours, the outside leg comes out and the inside leg sustains the position.
5. Pull with your arm on that side and he falls.
6. Use the sustaining leg as a pendulum to get your ear to his chest.
7. Your head pins him long enough to bring your arm to the other side, lift, and put a wedge underneath to settle into a good pin.

---

## Key Takeaways

- T-kimura's engagement rules force the opponent into only one real option: turning into you — turning away or staying still both lose to your kimura/pin.
- Even if he powers all the way up to his knees against your best effort, you still retain a Russian arm because his arm is still trapped across the centerline.
- Entry: knee in, second foot as a butterfly hook, lift the elbow, bring the second knee across, catch the arm, sit up into the Russian arm as he pulls away (head one side, arm the other).
- Grip on lat + bicep, head in close, hold at the forearm — this is what stops him yanking the arm free.
- Ear-on-chest is a load-bearing detail: it prevents him from spinning back into you while you lift and turn him.
- Finish the sequence with a post leg and a second leg wedged underneath to settle into a full pin across his side.
