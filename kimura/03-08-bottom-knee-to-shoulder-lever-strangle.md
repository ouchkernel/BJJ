# Bottom Knee To Shoulder-Lever Strangle

## Overview

A second bottom-knee attack out of the T-kimura position, used when your opponent's up-and-forward knee blocks the standard hook-inside-the-hip entry. Instead of fighting for the hip, you take your foot over your training partner's shoulder and use the shoulder itself as a long lever to keep him down, converting the position directly into a strangle. Two demonstrations of the same idea are shown, approached from slightly different entries.

---

## The problem this solves

- Starting point is the usual T-kimura bottom-knee attack: your opponent fights his way up, you use your elbow on his elbow to slow him and try to throw your hook in.
- Sometimes his knee comes up and forward, which makes it hard to get your foot inside his hip to hook in the usual way.
- **Solution**: instead of hooking the hip, take your foot over your training partner's shoulder.

## First demonstration — shoulder lever into strangle

1. From T-kimura, opponent fights up; slow him at the elbow.
2. When his knee-up posture blocks the hip hook, take your foot over his shoulder instead.
3. As he turns into you, open your knee — this puts you in the perfect position to turn him over.
4. Release your kimura grip and connect your knuckles to his chest. This knuckles-to-chest connection is what makes it surprisingly difficult for him to spit back out of the lock.
5. Lock an initial triangle, hand-assist it, and bring your shoelaces to your training partner's back.
6. From there you can quickly lock up a strangle hold.
7. Concept: the shoulder is being used as a long lever to keep your opponent down while you work the strangle.

## Second demonstration — knee-behind-head entry

1. Same start: T-kimura, opponent turns up hard into you.
2. Focus on getting your knee underneath him and keeping his elbow off the floor.
3. Bring your knee up **behind his head** — don't try to throw the leg over from a low-elbow, low-knee position, you'll never get the knee over that way.
4. From behind the head, hook your leg over his pectoral muscle.
5. As he spins into you, use that leg to turn him over.
6. Come up and connect your knuckles to his chest.
7. Fall and lock your legs (the initial lock), hand-assist, and bring your shoelaces behind his back.
8. Focus on connecting knuckles to chest — this is what brings the strangle on immediately.

## Common mistakes

- Trying to throw the leg over from a low elbow / low knee position — it won't clear. Get the knee behind the head first, then hook over the pectoral muscle.

---

## Key Takeaways

- When the standard hip hook is blocked by an opponent's up-and-forward knee, take your foot over his shoulder instead — the shoulder becomes a long lever holding him down.
- The trigger to turn him over is his own turn into you: open your knee as he turns in.
- Release the kimura grip and connect knuckles to chest before locking the triangle — that connection is what stops him spitting back out of the lock.
- Sequence to finish: initial triangle lock → hand-assist → shoelaces to the back → strangle.
- Don't try to throw the leg over from a low elbow/low knee position — bring the knee behind the head first, then hook over the pectoral muscle.
- This is a second, complementary bottom-knee attack out of T-kimura alongside the other bottom-knee options.
