# Wrist Control Setup — Two-On-One To Thumb Grip — Quick Reference

## Why wrist control

Traditional momentum setup asks you to beat hands + biceps + head at once with speed — a good opponent always counters. Wrist control neutralizes all three at once, no speed required.

## Entry steps

1. Opponent covers your biceps in closed guard.
2. Pommel one hand at a time inside — forces him off your biceps.
3. If no reaction, start bringing his hands to the floor.
4. Two-on-one grip on one hand, straighten body, push his hand down toward your hips.
5. At your hip: take a thumb grip on the wrist. (A hand back on your bicep now is irrelevant.)
6. Pull him forward with your legs, block his wrist against your hip — off-balances him.
7. His only save: post his free hand on the floor.

## Why it beats hands/biceps/head at once

- Posting hand goes over your shoulder → his head ends up out of your path.
- Posting-side hand + bicep are both controlled/neutralized by default (same arm).
- Open guard, shoot out to the side, come up to elbow — nothing blocks you.
- As he tries to recover, slip the free hand in → kimura locked.

## Extracting a hidden/trapped hand

1. Don't fight the hand directly — disrupt balance instead.
2. Feet inside his legs (grapevine).
3. Grapevine pressure to destabilize as he holds tight.
4. Lift hips off floor → hand comes free.

## Finish: pull, not push

1. Shimmy hips out to the side.
2. Invert near knee — sole of foot to ceiling (knee-to-ceiling = failure).
3. Cross/lock feet.
4. Move your head away from his head.
5. Line up his far shoulder, near shoulder, elbow = the shoulder line.
6. Pull his elbow to your armpit.
7. Pull (not push) — under an inch of pull now breaks the arm.

## Common mistake to avoid

Head/chin in front of his elbow → any pressure degenerates into a weak push as his head rises and he escapes.
