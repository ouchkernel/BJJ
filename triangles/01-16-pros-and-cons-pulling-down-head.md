# Pros And Cons Pulling Down Head With Hands

## Overview

Detailed treatment of the "head pull method" mentioned back in the Overview chapter as one of the three posture-problem solutions. Framed as a fallback option, used specifically when you can't get an angle on the opponent and can't eliminate their shoulder from the lock — plus one small mechanical detail described as the actual difference between a triangle that finishes and one that doesn't.

---

## When to use it

- Your first goal on every triangle attempt is to get an angle on the opponent.
- Pulling down on the head is the next option specifically for the times you can't successfully get that angle.
- It's used when the opponent's shoulder is still inside the lock of your triangle (i.e., the shoulder-elimination principle hasn't been achieved) — in that case, you have to lock the triangle behind them and manage from there.

## The critical mechanical detail

- Whenever you're in this situation, your shoelaces (the top of your foot) must go into the **pocket of your own knee** — not on top of your own calf muscle.
- This looks like a tiny, insignificant difference from the sidelines. It isn't. Described as "the difference between someone tapping and championship level."
- Every time your shoelaces end up on top of your calf muscle instead of tucked into the knee pocket, you're dramatically lessening the strangle power of the triangle.
- This has to become a reflex — check it the instant you get hold of the opponent in these situations, not as an afterthought once you're already mid-finish.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Use head-pulling as your fallback specifically when you can't get an angle and the opponent's shoulder is still trapped inside your triangle.
- The shoelaces-in-the-knee-pocket detail (vs. shoelaces on the calf) is presented as the single mechanical difference between a finishing triangle and a non-finishing one in this position.
- Make the shoelace check a reflex, not something you remember to fix only after noticing the finish isn't working.
