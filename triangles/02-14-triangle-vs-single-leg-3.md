# Triangle Vs Single Leg 3 — Countering A Deep, Elbow-Deep Single Leg

## Overview

Addresses a specific problem case: the opponent gets an unusually strong, elbow-deep grip on the leg (above the knee) rather than the shallower wrist-deep grip assumed in the previous two chapters. The tight waist stops being effective here, so a different entry is used — including a "wrong-side" triangle variant.

## The problem

- If the opponent is elbow-deep on your leg (lock sitting above your knee), the tight waist grip loses its effectiveness and you can start to feel trapped.
- Solution: abandon the tight waist/whizzer entirely for this scenario.

## Entry: seize the tricep, foot over the head

1. Seize the opponent's tricep with your hand.
2. Bring your own elbow to touch theirs and go around the corner.
3. Put your foot directly over their head.
4. As they posture up into this, you come around the corner into position to apply the triangle.
5. Lock the figure-four as usual for the strangle.

### Variant: foot to the back of the neck

- Same tricep/elbow seizure, but instead of coming around one way, rotate with the intention of putting your foot on the **back of the opponent's neck**, then take everything across to the other side.
- The foot wedges behind the neck and produces a clean strangulation.
- Recommended order: seize the elbow/tricep first, then place the foot on the back of the neck — this creates the connection needed to work from.

## Variant: hantai sankaku (the "wrong-side" triangle)

- In the same elbow-deep scenario, instead of a standard front triangle, lock a **hantai sankaku** — a triangle locked in what looks like the "wrong" direction.
- Setup: seize the opponent elbow-to-elbow, foot on the back of their neck. As they posture, notice their arm is off to one side rather than centered — this off-center positioning is what makes the hantai sankaku available.
- Turn around the corner into the hantai sankaku for a very strong strangulation. Either the standard front triangle or the hantai sankaku work well here — it comes down to preference.

## Key Takeaways

- When the opponent gets unusually deep (elbow-deep, above the knee) on your leg, drop the tight waist — it stops working.
- Replacement entry: seize the tricep/elbow, foot goes either over the head or to the back of the neck, then turn the corner into the triangle.
- The same elbow-deep scenario also opens up the hantai sankaku (wrong-side triangle) as an equally valid finish — pick whichever the opponent's arm position favors.
