# Managing Transition From Trap Triangle To Figure Four Triangle 2 — The Scoop Arm

## Overview

Second lesson on managing the trap-to-figure-four transition. Introduces the four-limb framework (strangle leg, support leg, posture arm, scoop arm) and covers the three places the scoop arm can be used to generate the angle needed to finish.

---

## The four-limb framework

- **Strangle leg** — does the choking.
- **Support leg** — locks the strangle leg in place.
- **Posture arm** — controls the opponent's posture (from the previous lesson).
- **Scoop arm** — reaches inside the opponent's leg (or elsewhere) to create angle.

The scoop arm's entire job is to generate angle on the opponent. Front triangles finished directly from square-in-front-of-the-opponent are easily countered — the best triangles are almost always finished **perpendicular** to the opponent, and most major triangle counters are shut down simply by achieving that perpendicular angle.

## The three scoop arm placements

1. **Inside the near leg (favorite)** — scoop elbow-deep inside the opponent's leg, pull your ear around toward their knee, both feet point forward. When the opponent yanks out, the position holds; from here it's easy to cover the shoulder and lock feet directly above the head.
2. **Outside the far leg (second favorite)** — used when the opponent pulls the near leg back/away to deny the inside scoop. Switch to an outside grip on the far leg instead; produces the same effect (feet point forward, same finish).
3. **Under the opponent's arm (fallback)** — if neither leg is available, scoop the arm instead. Not as strong as a leg scoop, but sufficient to turn the body and reach the same finishing position.

**Key defensive logic:** the opponent can only pull one leg away at a time. If they defend the near leg, take the far leg. If they leave the near leg exposed, use it directly. If both legs are defended, fall back to the arm scoop.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Four limbs, four jobs: strangle leg (choke), support leg (lock the strangle leg), posture arm (control the head), scoop arm (create angle).
- Perpendicular angle to the opponent is the target orientation for nearly every triangle finish — most standard counters fail once you have it.
- Three scoop arm options, in priority order: inside the near leg → outside the far leg → under the arm. The opponent can only take away one option at a time, so there's always a next option available.
