# Story Of Every Triangle Is The Same

## Overview

A conceptual reset before moving into entries. Reduces every triangle application — front, back, reverse, whatever the type — to a single two-step story, so the entry material that follows can be understood as one repeating pattern rather than a pile of unrelated setups.

---

## The two-chapter story

**Chapter one — get "one arm in, one arm out."**
Create a situation where one of the opponent's arms is caught inside your triangle and the other is locked outside of it. From there you lock up what's called a **trap triangle** — the opponent's head and one arm caught inside your locked legs (ankles crossed, not yet a figure-four).

**Chapter two — manage the transition from trap triangle to figure-four triangle.**
Pull the opponent in, adjust your body, and employ the mechanical elements covered in the earlier lessons (shoulder-line, shoulder-elimination, knee-below-ear, inner-thigh-to-tricep, posture control, scoop arm, unified legs, shoulders-up) to actually close the finish.

## Applied example (seated, opponent on two knees)

1. Establish "one in, one out": one arm inside the legs, one outside.
2. Lock the trap triangle (ankles crossed).
3. Opponent's whole goal now is to pull away / posture up — counter this with a **knee pull**: pull the knees to your chest (this is exactly why the earlier solo body-folding drills matter — they build this specific motion).
4. This knee pull brings the opponent's head close enough that you can get your wrist around it (not just your hand).
5. Once the head is controlled, safely unlock the trap triangle, get the scoop hand in, form the angle, and close the wedges behind the head.
6. Lower legs point the same direction (unified) → lock done.

## Why this framing matters

- Every triangle you'll ever hit — front, back, reverse — resolves into this same two-chapter pattern: get one-in-one-out, then manage trap-to-figure-four.
- Recognizing this pattern is what lets the specific entries covered in upcoming lessons feel like variations on one theme rather than a new system each time.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Every triangle = the same two-step story:** (1) get one arm in / one arm out and lock a trap triangle, (2) manage the transition to figure-four using the mechanics already covered.
- The **knee pull** (knees to chest) is the primary tool for closing distance and controlling the head once the trap triangle is on — and it's the direct payoff of the solo body-folding drills from earlier in the course.
- This two-chapter pattern applies universally across triangle types (front, back, reverse) — it's the unifying structure behind everything else in the course.
