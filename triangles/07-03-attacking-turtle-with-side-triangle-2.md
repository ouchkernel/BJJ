# Attacking Turtle With Side Triangle 2

## Overview

A second, seated version of Yoko Sankaku — also very prominent in modern competition — entered from a front headlock rather than the standing/kneeling entry covered previously. Also covers a critical error to avoid when transitioning down to the mat.

## Setup — Front Headlock

Front headlock situations (commonly arising while attacking a turtled/scrambling opponent) sometimes present a little space to work a different move rather than the usual arm-based front-headlock system.

## Sequence

1. Knee forward so your thigh/hip flexor presses against the opponent's trapezius.
2. Post on the opponent to keep their head down — if the head rises, they can shoot a double leg on you.
3. Foot through, hook on the opponent, set a tight waist grip, second hand down on the floor.
4. Pull back on their arm and slide down to the mat, **staying over** the opponent (hand on the floor keeps you positioned above them, not flopped to the side).
5. Their attempt to pull the arm back works against your leg — hard for them to succeed.
6. Use the tight waist grip to come up and seize the opponent, foot behind their tricep — eliminates the shoulder, same principle as always.
7. Lock the figure-four, hand on the waist, squeeze for the finish.

## Handling A Weak Front-Headlock Grip

If the standard front-headlock hand game isn't there: knee in deep, but keep your **second leg out and away** with a hand posting on the head — two knees down on the floor gives the opponent an easy double leg, so don't offer that. Take away their double-leg option by putting your leg behind their reaching arm, tight waist with the other hand, pull on the trapped arm. **Don't flop down to your side** — that gives up top position for nothing. Keep your head over their waist as you sit through with control, pull your foot **over your own knee** (a slippage-prevention detail — foot below the knee is easy to lose), then lock the figure-four and retract to strangle.

## Common Error To Avoid

The recurring mistake across all reps of this entry: **flopping down to the side** when transitioning to the mat instead of sitting through with control, head staying over the opponent. Flopping lets the opponent's head come free and gives up top position for nothing.

## Difficulty Of Defense

This is described as one of the hardest triangles in the system to defend — it's not obvious to the opponent where to turn or what to hook, making it a genuinely scary position to be caught in.

## Key Takeaways

- The seated Yoko Sankaku comes from a front headlock, using thigh-on-trapezius pressure and a tight waist grip rather than the standing kimura-threat entry.
- Take away the opponent's double-leg option deliberately (leg behind their reaching arm) before committing to the transition down.
- The single biggest execution error: flopping to the side instead of sitting through with control and keeping your head over the opponent's waist.
- Pull your foot over your own knee before locking the figure-four — prevents slippage during the finish.
