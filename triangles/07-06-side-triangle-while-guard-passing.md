# Side Triangle While Guard Passing

## Overview

Shows how to reach Yoko Sankaku while passing a seated guard, by first linking into the kimura system — the same "opponent can only turn into you" dilemma used elsewhere in the course to set up triangles.

## Setup Logic

Anytime an opponent is in a seated guard, there's guaranteed back exposure on the far side, and both athletes will be reaching during the hand-fighting — that reaching is the opening.

## Sequence

1. From in front of a seated opponent, engage the usual hand fights, step your foot just inside their body.
2. Take everything across the opponent's body and lock a kimura, coming around into a **T-kimura** position.
3. The opponent's options: stay static (you pin/attack from the kimura), or try to turn away (physically blocked by the kimura) — so their only real option is to **turn in**.
4. As they turn in: lift their elbow to slow them down, bring your knee underneath, take your foot/leg over their head.
5. As they fight to come up on top of you, kick through — this lands you in the Yoko Sankaku position.
6. From here, standard side-triangle procedure: belly-down shift, punch the leg through, come back to lock an incredibly tight figure-four.

## Second Demonstrated Rep

Same shape: post/move, step inside, lock across the body. As the opponent turns in, knee in front elevates their elbow, leg comes forward over their head and passes through underneath. **Don't try to lock the triangle directly from this cramped position** — foot to the mat, turn through so the quads point down, come to two straightened legs, bend one then the other for a very tight strangulation.

## Key Takeaways

- The kimura's "turn away is impossible, turn in is the only option" dilemma is the actual engine of this entry — the same mechanism used to reach the rear triangle from guard passing elsewhere in the system.
- Reaching Yoko Sankaku from guard passing is really just applying the standard entry (belly-down shift, one leg extends/one retracts) once the kimura forces the opponent into position.
- Don't try to force the triangle lock directly out of the cramped position the kimura creates — always run the belly-down shift first.
