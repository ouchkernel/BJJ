# Omoplata To Front Triangle

## Overview

An entry that starts from bottom position, uses the threat of an omoplata to force a roll, and converts the resulting top position into a front-triangle finish — either from top or by coming back underneath into a strangle.

---

## The standard omoplata problem

Normally, finishing an omoplata means extracting your bottom leg, aligning your body flat with your opponent's, back on the ground. But with your back flat, there's very little weight on the opponent's head, so it can rise and cause escape problems. This is why the omoplata is easy to enter but often hard to actually finish.

## The fix: stay perpendicular

1. From guard, enter the clamp position, taking your foot over the top and hooking onto your opponent's far shoulder as usual.
2. Instead of lying down and aligning with your opponent, stay roughly **perpendicular** to them and come up onto an **elbow**.
3. Put your bottom foot's toes on the mat and hip-heist, using your knee to force your opponent's head down toward the mat.
4. This lets you post up onto both hands.
5. Continue driving your hips forward — this puts extreme stress on your opponent's trapped arm, similar to a kimura applied with the legs.
6. The opponent now has a simple choice: a broken arm, or a forward roll. Given that choice, almost everyone rolls.
7. As they roll down to their shoulder/back, hook your foot inside theirs — you're now in the classic one-in-one-out position, one of their arms trapped inside your legs, one outside.
8. Hug the head, knee to the floor, turn your body across so only the head and one arm remain trapped inside the triangle.
9. Head down to the mat, legs come off the floor, lock the figure-four.
10. Finish from top position, or punch your arm through and come up into bottom position to strangle from underneath — either works.
11. If the opponent stands or moves at any point, hook inside their leg as usual to keep the position tight.

## Variant: rolling straight into a mounted triangle

A closely related sequence: enter the clamp, form the angle, push the bottom foot to the floor, come up to an elbow, use the knees to force the head down and heist across into a position where you can hook around the opponent's head, then bring your body weight forward and lock the figure-four — finishing from either top or bottom. This is described as a good introduction to finishing front triangles specifically from top position.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The key fix to the classic omoplata finishing problem is staying **perpendicular**, not lying flat and aligned — flat alignment removes weight from the opponent's head and lets them escape.
- The roll is forced by a genuine dilemma: broken arm vs. forward roll. Most opponents choose to roll, and that's exactly what sets up the triangle.
- As they roll, the one-in-one-out position is already there — you just need to hug the head, turn your body across, and lock the figure-four.
- This entry can finish from either top or bottom position, and doubles as an introduction to top-position triangle finishing.
