# Wrist Controls To Triangles 2

## Overview

Continues from the previous lesson by addressing the interface between the clamp and wrist-control entries — specifically, the counter a knowledgeable opponent uses against the clamp (grabbing your extracting leg) and how to fall back on collar tie + wrist control to solve it, working around the rule against grabbing individual fingers.

---

## The clamp counter

A knowledgeable opponent knows that to finish from the clamp, you eventually have to extract your bottom leg to throw it over into the triangle. So instead of letting that happen, they simply **hold your knee/leg in place** the moment you enter the clamp, denying the extraction.

## The fix: collar tie + all-four-knuckle grip

Because the rules of competitive jiu-jitsu make it illegal to grab one, two, or three individual fingers, you can't just rip their hand off your leg finger-by-finger. Instead:

1. Fall back on the collar tie.
2. Grip **all four knuckles** together (legal) and turn your training partner's hand outward.
3. This makes it easy to kick your leg over the top and go straight into the triangle series.
4. As they try to move away, make the usual turn — knee to the crotch, lower legs all pointing the same direction, lower back lifted up off the ground — everything locks into place for a strong finish.

## Worked example: wrist blocked by the elbow

In some passing/gripping situations, when you try to push the opponent's wrist in, they block you with their own elbow.

1. Plant your foot on the mat.
2. Shrimp out to the same side, beating their elbow.
3. Throw your leg over the top and lock as usual.
4. Bring your body around the corner, catch the head, complete the turn to get your wedges in place.
5. If the opponent yanks out or tries to stand, hook inside their leg and keep it tight — this keeps the minimum possible "volume" inside your triangle, which is what produces a very tight strangulation.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Anticipate the clamp counter: a smart opponent grabs your extracting leg the moment you enter the clamp.
- Since individual-finger grips are illegal, the legal workaround is gripping **all four knuckles together** and turning the hand outward — functionally similar control without breaking the rule.
- When an opponent blocks your wrist-push with their elbow, plant and shrimp to the same side to beat the elbow rather than forcing through it.
- If the opponent tries to stand or move once you're set, hook inside their leg to minimize the "volume" inside the triangle — tighter triangle, faster tap.
