# Wrist Controls To Triangles 2

## Overview

Shows the interface between the clamp and wrist/collar control — specifically what to do when a knowledgeable opponent defends the clamp correctly by grabbing your bottom leg to prevent extraction. Also covers a legal-grip detail (the four-knuckle grip) that solves the problem.

## The Counter Being Solved

An opponent familiar with the clamp knows you ultimately need to extract your bottom leg to finish into a triangle. A smart opponent will simply **hold your knee in place** at that moment, denying the extraction.

## The Fix: Four-Knuckle Grip

- The sport doesn't allow grabbing one, two, or three individual fingers — but grabbing **all four knuckles together** is legal, and that's all you actually need.
- Take all four knuckles, hold, and turn the opponent's hand **outward**.
- With that grip in place, it becomes easy to kick over the top and proceed into the triangle series even though the leg-extraction route is blocked.

## Sequence

1. Enter the clamp as usual (close guard: pull hands in, get them to the mat, into the clamp).
2. Opponent intelligently grabs your lower leg to prevent extraction.
3. Switch to a collar tie plus the four-knuckle grip, extracting the opponent's trapped hand via the knuckle turn instead of fighting the leg.
4. Turn the corner, knee toward the groin, lower legs pointing the same direction, lower back lifted off the ground — the same inverted setup used elsewhere.
5. Lock the figure-four for the finish.

## Second Application — Elbow Blocks During A Wrist Push

When pushing a wrist inward and the opponent blocks with their elbow (can't get past it): plant your foot, shrimp to the **same side**, beat the elbow, throw over the top and lock. From there, catch the head, turn the corner to set the wedges. Even if the opponent stands up or yanks out, hooking inside their leg keeps the triangle's internal volume minimal, producing a very tight strangle.

## Key Takeaways

- The four-knuckle grip is the legal, effective substitute for individual finger grips when an opponent locks down your leg extraction from the clamp.
- This chapter is explicitly about the **interface** between two entry methods (clamp + wrist/collar control) — when one gets shut down, the other picks up the slack rather than abandoning the attempt.
- Beating an elbow block during a wrist push is a shrimp-to-the-same-side motion, not a strength battle.
