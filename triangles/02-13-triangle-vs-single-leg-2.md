# Triangle Vs Single Leg 2 — The Quarter Nelson Entry

## Overview

Second method for countering the single leg down on the mat, using a quarter nelson to break the opponent down to a hip instead of the step-over used previously. Two variants of the quarter nelson are shown.

## Setup

Same starting scenario as the previous chapter: opponent deep in on a single leg, head on the inside, both knees down.

## Quarter nelson — variant 1 (head post)

1. Take the second leg up (don't leave both legs on the mat — same reasoning as before, avoids an easy switch to a double leg).
2. Stuff down on the opponent's head.
3. Use the head-post method to put their shoulder, then hip, down on the floor.

## Quarter nelson — variant 2 (chin strap, slightly stronger)

1. Instead of posting on the opponent's head, grab the jaw — a chin strap.
2. Grab your own forearm (not your wrist) for a stronger lever action straight down into the mat.
3. Both variants work; the chin strap is described as slightly stronger.

## From hip-down to triangle

1. Once the opponent is down on the hip, transition your grip to the back of their head, then down to one hand.
2. With that one hand, block the opponent's hip and walk around their body.
3. As you reach the far side, catch the opponent's wrist and head together.
4. Throw everything across your body, head to the mat — you're now locked up for the triangle.
5. Lock the elbow to your own hip so the trapped arm can't be yanked free, then step over and lock the figure-four.

## Key Takeaways

- The quarter nelson is a second reliable way to break a single-leg opponent down to a hip, as an alternative to the step-over.
- Two variants: head-post (standard) and chin-strap/forearm grab (slightly stronger lever).
- Once down on the hip, the path into the triangle is: walk around the body → catch wrist and head together → throw across your body, head to the mat → lock the elbow to your hip → figure-four.
