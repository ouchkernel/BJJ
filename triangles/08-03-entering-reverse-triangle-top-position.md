# Entering Reverse Triangle 2 (From Top Position / Side Crucifix)

## Overview

A second entry into the reverse triangle, this time from top position — specifically from a side crucifix built with a **scissor** action of the legs, rather than a pinch.

---

## Prerequisite: side crucifix via scissoring, not pinching

Starting from top side control on an opponent pinning their arm:
1. Sit through, pulling the training partner's elbow up.
2. This creates a partial sit-through position.
3. As the opponent retracts and keeps their arm in, hip forward and push their wrist down and forward.
4. Step over their arm into a scissor action with your legs — this is the side crucifix.

**Critical distinction:** most people pinch their knees together here. Pinching does **not** control the opponent's arm — even though legs are generally strong, pinched knees have no real strength in this application, and the opponent can simply limp the arm out and escape.

Instead: **scissor** your legs (one knee forward, one knee back). This:
- Takes the opponent's shoulder up into a vulnerable position.
- Forces their head to rise (with a pinch, they can just keep their head flat on the floor and escape; the scissor denies them that).

## Entry sequence into the triangle

1. From the scissored side crucifix, pass a hand with a bent wrist over the opponent's head.
2. Come forward and up, lift.
3. Pass your leg over the training partner's head, planting it as a wedge underneath them.
4. Post your other hand on the floor for base.
5. Drive your legs through and begin locking up.
6. Take your hands out for a wider, more stable base as you adjust position.

## Securing the leg (key trick)

One of the instructor's favorite details in this position: reach across and seize the opponent's far leg, feeding it to your arm.
- This locks in a secure base of support.
- Prevents the opponent from bridging — if their leg is left free on the floor, they can bridge and disrupt the triangle.
- With the leg controlled, an opponent trying to shrimp or turn is shut down by the leg control alone.

## Finish

Look toward the opponent's legs; as you do, both your legs come up and the strangle applies — described as "enormously tight" from top position. The same sequence is demonstrated a second time (control the arm → step over → side scissor crucifix → catch the far leg and lock it in → lock the figure-four → turn/look toward the legs → strangle) to reinforce the pattern.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Entry starts from top side control, using a **partial sit-through** to free the opponent's arm into the crucifix.
- **Scissor the legs, don't pinch them** — pinching fails to control the arm and lets the opponent keep their head down and escape; scissoring lifts the shoulder and forces the head up.
- Pass a hand over the head with a bent wrist, lift, and thread your leg over as a wedge to build the position.
- **Catch and control the opponent's far leg** — this is what prevents bridging and secures your base; leaving that leg free is a bridging risk.
- Finish by looking toward the opponent's legs as both your own legs rise into the strangle.
