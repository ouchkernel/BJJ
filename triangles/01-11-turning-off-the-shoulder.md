# Turning Off The Shoulder

## Overview

One of the instructor's favorite daily drills. Trains the specific skill of forming an advantageous angle on the opponent from bottom position by turning off (pivoting around) their shoulder — without using your hands at all. This is the mechanical foundation for the trap-to-figure-four transition covered in later chapters.

---

## The drill

1. Start square in front of a kneeling training partner, legs simply crossed — a trap triangle, not yet a real triangle.
2. Place your calf muscle over the partner's shoulder.
3. Turn off the shoulder: move your hips offline while your lower legs come around to point in the same direction — this is what allows the figure-four to lock.
4. Repeat from crossed feet each time: cross feet → calf over shoulder → turn off the shoulder → lock figure-four.

## Why train it without hands first

- The point of doing this drill hands-free is to develop the ability to draw an opponent forward and into a triangle using body mechanics alone. Hands and arms get added to this same movement later in the course, but building the base skill without them first makes the eventual hands-included version far more reliable under resistance.
- Being able to turn and form an advantageous angle on your opponent is described as a big part of whether triangle attempts succeed or fail in competition and sparring.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Sequence: crossed feet (trap triangle) → calf over the opponent's shoulder → hips go offline, legs turn to point the same direction → figure-four locks.
- Train this hands-free first — it builds the ability to draw an opponent into a triangle using body mechanics alone, before adding hand/arm assistance later.
- This is the direct mechanical precursor to the "fundamental transition from trap triangle to figure-four triangle" covered later in the course.
