# Getting Lower Back Off Ground And Onto Points Of Shoulders

## Overview

Addresses a foundational body-positioning error: working triangles with the lower back flat on the mat. Explains the cascade of problems this causes and the fix — weight on the points of the shoulders instead.

---

## The problem with a flat lower back

Working from a triangle attempt with the lower back and buttocks on the mat causes three compounding issues:

1. **Stacking becomes trivially effective** — any forward movement of the opponent's head toward yours overloads and collapses the triangle immediately. Not competitive; it "just blows the legs apart."
2. **Forming an angle becomes very difficult** — too much of your back is in contact with the floor, creating drag. Movement/turning is severely limited.
3. **Posture control is lost** — if your lower back is on the floor, your body doesn't follow when the opponent postures up. The triangle can't track the opponent's movement.

Net effect: your two biggest defensive vulnerabilities to a triangle (stacking, posturing) both become highly effective against you, and the one thing you need most — mobility to form angle — becomes very hard to access.

## The fix: come up onto the points of your shoulders

- Getting the lower back off the ground and shifting weight onto the points of the shoulders produces several good effects:
  - Very little friction against the mat → you can turn underneath the opponent freely.
  - When the opponent tries to stack you, they're driving forward directly into the strangle instead of breaking it — if they kept driving forward, they'd get reversed.
  - When the opponent postures up, you stay hooked into the leg regardless — even if they reach standing, the lock (positioned behind the head) holds and it becomes very hard for them to get away.
- This connects back to the solo body-folding/pummeling drills covered earlier in the course — those drills specifically build the ability to operate with weight on the shoulders, hips light, and legs mobile.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Lower back flat on the mat = the single biggest structural error** underlying both easy stacking and easy posturing defenses.
- **Fix: weight onto the points of your shoulders, lower back off the ground.** This kills friction, enables the turn, and makes both stacking and posturing work in your favor instead of against you.
- This is a direct practical payoff of the earlier solo drilling work (body folding, shoulder-weight mobility) — it's not just theory, it's the trained physical capacity to operate this way under resistance.
