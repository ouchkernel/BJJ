# Mechanics Of Rear Triangle

## Overview

The core mechanical breakdown of Ushiro Sankaku. Goal: a tight tourniquet-like strangle where only the opponent's neck and arm are caught inside your legs — everything else eliminated.

## Roles Of Each Leg

- **Strangle leg** — makes contact with the opponent's carotid artery.
- **Support leg** — comes in second to complete the lock.

## Step-By-Step Mechanics

1. Put the **hollow of your knee directly on the carotid artery** — this is the first priority.
2. Get your **hips north of the opponent's shoulder line** — back away from the opponent as needed.
3. Bring in the support leg. The initial lock is almost always a **trap triangle** (feet crossed, not yet a full figure-four).
4. **Hand-assist**: pull your heel, drag everything north so your knee rises toward the opponent's elbow/armpit. The lower your knee sits on their body, the weaker the strangle — you want it up high, near the armpit.
5. Wedge your foot behind the opponent's back, set against their **lat muscle**.
6. Work against an **extended arm**. If the opponent's hands are locked with the elbow forward, they can push against your knee and reduce strangulation pressure — take their elbow over their own head and lock it in place so the neck stays exposed.
7. Finish by bringing your **support knee toward your strangle knee** — turn everything down, knee pressing inward (not flat/open), and squeeze.

## Recurring Principle: Unified Legs

Both lower legs point in the same direction throughout the entry — this appears in every triangle in the system, not just the rear triangle. Set the strangle leg on the carotid, lock a trap triangle, hand-assist north so the knees come up toward your own chest (this lifts the opponent's chin and exposes the neck), set the shoelaces against the lats, then bring support knee to strangle knee for the finish.

## Key Takeaways

- Strangle leg = carotid contact. Support leg = completes the lock.
- Priority order: knee on carotid → hips north of shoulder line → trap triangle → hand-assist north (knee toward armpit, not low on the body) → foot wedged behind the lat → support knee to strangle knee to finish.
- Keep the opponent's arm extended (elbow over their own head) — a bent, locked-in arm lets them push your knee and bleed off pressure.
- Legs stay unified (pointing the same direction) throughout — this is a system-wide rule, not specific to this triangle.
