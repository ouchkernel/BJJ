# Reverse Triangle → American Lock — Quick Reference

## The 4-step system

1. Working grip.
2. One-in-one-out → trap triangle.
3. Trap triangle → figure-four (strangle).
4. Strangle fails → **American lock** (the go-to joint lock for this triangle).

## The trigger

Opponent knows which knee is the strangle knee → they push it off with their hand. **That push is your cue.**

## Sequence

1. They push the knee → score their wrist, go over the top, lock the **American lock**.
2. Connect their elbow to your armpit — this is non-negotiable, no strength in the lock otherwise.
3. Their only counter (thrusting the arm forward) makes it **easier** for you to go back to the strangle.

## Why this American lock is unusually tight

Their head can't move (trapped by the triangle position) — and head movement is normally how people relieve American lock pressure. Not available here.

## The dilemma

Defend the American lock → strangle reopens. Defend the strangle (push the knee) → American lock opens. They can't defend both.
