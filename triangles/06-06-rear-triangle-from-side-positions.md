# Rear Triangle From Side Positions

## Overview

Entries into Ushiro Sankaku from a side pin (across-side control) — using simple body movement to expose the opponent's back and go straight into a leg strangle, without needing to fully pass to the back first.

## Setup

Opponent flat on their back, arms in a standard defensive position, legs up, you're pinning across their side.

## Sequence

1. Post your near hand at the opponent's hip — blocks the hip from moving.
2. Bring your second hand/arm over the opponent.
3. Move around the opponent's body toward a **north-south** position.
4. Work your wrist underneath their elbow. Even a tight defensive arm always leaves *some* degree of gap — take your thumb back underneath, bring your hand through, and drive up to get it inside their forearm. This sometimes takes some "finagling" — changing your hand angle, moving slightly across their body — to fit it in.
5. Once past the forearm, turn and drive on the opponent, bring your hand underneath their neck, and secure their arm with a **figure-four grip** (your wrist to your own wrist).
6. Knee down right underneath their shoulder, sit down into position. Expect their hands locked defensively, knees up by their chest.
7. Use your foot to clear their legs out of the way — hook your first foot actively into their legs, then extract your second foot. This creates **two unweighted legs** that move freely.
8. Even if the opponent grabs your foot and holds on tight, it doesn't matter — it's your legs versus their hands. Push through and set your ankle inside their armpit.
9. Lean forward, bring your hand across to the other side, go wrist-to-wrist.
10. Bring their knuckles up to your chest, line up your legs (unified) → trap triangle → hand-assist → figure-four → strangle.

## Handling A Strong Push

If the opponent resists hard by pushing on your knee, it makes little difference — the strangle goes through regardless. If they're strong enough to genuinely stall you, reach through with your second hand, bring both of their arms overhead, and strangle from there.

## Key Takeaways

- The whole entry hinges on getting your wrist/hand underneath the opponent's elbow despite a tight defensive arm — treat it as a small technical puzzle (thumb back, hand through, angle change), not a strength contest.
- "Two unweighted legs" is the goal once your foot clears their legs — free legs that move regardless of what the opponent's hands are doing.
- It's legs versus hands: an opponent gripping your foot cannot actually stop you from setting up the triangle.
