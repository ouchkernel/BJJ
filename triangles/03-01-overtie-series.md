# Overtie Series

## Overview

Introduces the overtie grip as a triangle setup from seated guard — an alternative/upgrade to a standard collar tie that grips over and around the opponent's own collar tie, giving outside position and strong head control right from the start.

## What Is An Overtie

- Standard collar tie: two opponents on their knees, each gripping the other's collar.
- **Overtie**: instead of matching grips, you reach over and around the opponent's collar tie and take your own collar tie grip outside and over theirs, on the same side. Result: they have the inside position on the grip exchange, you have the **outside position**.
- Useful in multiple contexts — in standing grappling it sets up slide-bys and back takes; from seated guard, it controls the opponent's posture and traps an arm.

## Sequence

1. Opponent comes in with a collar tie. Play wrists/tricep initially (fine as a guard-pass deterrent).
2. Sneak on the overtie — it doesn't feel especially threatening to the opponent from their perspective.
3. Second hand goes through to control the opponent's **elbow**.
4. Place your foot on the **same side as the trapped arm** — not the opposite side, which would expose you to an Ashi Garami counter-attack while you're trying to work an upper-body submission.
5. When the opponent tries to yank their arm free, it's shockingly difficult — both head and elbow are controlled.
6. Place the foot in, creating an unweighted foot (same principle as other entries), fall around the corner.
7. Because the overtie already controls the head, feeding a hand inside the opponent's legs to start the inversion/turn is easy.
8. Lock the figure-four directly over the opponent's head for the finish.

## Realistic Application (Live Pommeling)

In live rolling, expect a lot of hand-fighting before this grip lands — wrist ties, arm drags, pulling out and re-gripping side to side. The overtie is just one of the grips you're cycling toward during that scramble; once it lands, the same turn-the-corner, lock-the-triangle sequence applies.

## Key Takeaways

- The overtie gives outside position and head control simultaneously — a meaningful upgrade over a standard, symmetric collar tie for triangle purposes.
- Foot placement must go on the same side as the trapped arm, or you invite a leg-lock counter mid-attack.
- Treat this as one tool among several you're cycling through during realistic hand-fighting, not a grip you expect to land cleanly on the first attempt.
