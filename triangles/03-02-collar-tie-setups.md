# Collar Tie Setups

## Overview

Shows how a standard (non-over) collar tie sets up the front triangle from seated guard — the collar tie's core value is direct head control, since all opponent posturing begins at the head.

## Why Collar Ties Work For This

A collar tie directly controls the opponent's head, and posturing (the main defense against triangles) always starts at the head. Starting with a collar tie gives you a strong chance of holding the opponent in place long enough to build a triangle — the remaining question is how to manage their arms while you do it.

## Sequence

1. From a hand-fight with the opponent on two knees, catch their wrist and secure the **elbow joint** — grip **underhand**, not overhand (overhand has its uses elsewhere, but not for this specific triangle entry).
2. Second hand comes up and locks onto the opponent's collar.
3. Place your foot on the **same side as the elbow control** — going to the opposite side invites an Ashi Garami counter-attack.
4. Foot on the hip, then just inside the opponent's bicep.
5. Go around the corner, kick through, lock an initial trap triangle.
6. Get control of the opponent's head, grip their leg, make the turn so both feet point forward.
7. Lock the figure-four cleanly on top of the opponent's head and neck.

## Key Takeaways

- Collar tie's core value for triangles is head control — the opponent's posture-based defenses start and end at the head.
- Grip the elbow underhand, not overhand, for this specific entry.
- Foot placement must match the side of your elbow control — wrong side invites a leg-lock counter mid-entry.
