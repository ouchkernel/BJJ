# Entering Reverse Triangle 3 (Top/Bottom Is Irrelevant — Handling Reversals)

## Overview

A core axiom of triangle mastery, applied specifically to the reverse triangle: **once the triangle is set, top and bottom position become irrelevant.** This lesson explains why that matters especially here, and shows how to adjust when the opponent reverses you mid-entry.

---

## The axiom

"Once you set the triangle, top and bottom position become irrelevant." This is true for all triangles in the system, but it requires faith — if you lose top position **before** the triangle is fully set, bad things can genuinely happen. Once it's set, though, the strangle works identically whether you end up on top or bottom.

## Why the reverse triangle specifically carries reversal risk

Unlike some other triangles where you end up perpendicular to the opponent (the hardest orientation to roll), the reverse triangle lines your body up **with** the opponent's — and someone lined up with your body is the *easiest* person to roll over. So there's a real chance of being reversed while entering this particular triangle.

## Adjusting when you get reversed

Starting from the same top-position entry as before (control elbow, step forward, lock up the one-in-one-out, set the knee on the carotid), if the opponent turns up into you while you're hunting for their foot, you can end up in bottom position. When that happens:

1. Punch your hand through and form a **tight waist grip**.
2. Face the camera / reorient and check your knee-to-ear relationship — it's likely now **above** the opponent's ear (bad — this is the same failure condition covered in the mechanics lesson).
3. Go underneath and hold the opponent's shoulder, so you now have two control points: a shoulder grip and a waist grip.
4. Have the confidence to **unlock your triangle** temporarily if the opponent tries to posture up — your arm position (shoulder + waist control) makes posturing up impossible anyway.
5. Shift your hips and move your knee from above the ear to **below** the ear. This subtle shift is described as *the* key to the strangle.
6. Bring your leg around the triceps to eliminate the opponent's shoulder again.
7. Re-lock the figure-four.
8. Apply the strangle — insanely tight, even from bottom position.

The sequence is demonstrated a second time from a side-pin start (arms controlled tight, lift into a good pin, cross the body, lock up) with the same reversal occurring and the same fix applied: recognize the knee is above the ear, put a foot on the floor, shift the hips, drop the knee below the ear, then finish.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Once a triangle is set, top/bottom position doesn't matter** — this holds for the whole system, not just the reverse triangle.
- The reverse triangle carries extra reversal risk because your body ends up lined up with the opponent's (easiest orientation to roll), unlike other triangles where you're perpendicular.
- If reversed while entering: punch a hand through for a tight waist grip, add a shoulder grip, don't panic about unlocking the triangle temporarily (your grips prevent posturing anyway).
- The fix is always the same: **shift your hips so the knee moves from above the ear to below it** — that single adjustment is what restores the strangle.
- With this adjustment you can move effortlessly between top and bottom position with no loss of finishing effectiveness.
