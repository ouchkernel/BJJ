# Transition To Inside Heel Hook — Quick Reference

## The principle

Attacks alternate ends: opponent down on you → attack upper body. Opponent postures away (his standard defense) → lower body opens up. Repeat.

## Sequence

1. Push his hand to centerline, elbow two-on-one grip.
2. He yanks away → body in close, elbows to your side, his hand near your hip.
3. Regulate distance: foot on hip (same side as trapped arm) vs. pull-away, upper-body grip vs. push-in.
4. Result: an **unweighted leg** — hard for him to track. Attack an initial triangle with it.
5. He postures to break it → pull with knees, hand behind his head (kills his last posture option — now he has to stand).
6. He stands, starts defending → bring your **outside leg** through first (keeps you aligned; inside leg first = he passes).
7. Feel the triangle slipping → foot to your own buttocks (minimize distance), shift into **X-guard**.
8. X-guard: drive his hands to the mat.
9. Switch to **reverse X-guard**, bring him into the air, figure-four around his legs.
10. Finish: **inside heel hook**.

## Key cue

Outside leg goes in first whenever you're losing a top-position triangle to a standing opponent — it's what keeps the connection if he tries to throw your legs by.

## Big picture

A defended/lost triangle against a standing opponent isn't a dead end — it flows straight into X-guard → reverse X-guard → inside heel hook. Triangle game and leg-lock game are one connected system.
