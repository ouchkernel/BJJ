# Solo Pummeling Drills For Triangle Development

## Overview

"Movement before moves" — before drilling actual triangle entries, build the specific foot dexterity those entries require. Pommeling is the repetitive circular foot movement that lets you thread a foot into a small space with either foot, in either direction, on demand. Four progressively harder solo variations.

---

## The four pommeling progressions

1. **Supine pommeling** — flat on your back. Takes all weight off the legs, keeps the abdomen relaxed. Recommended as a daily warm-up before class.
2. **Seated pommeling** — hands on the floor for support, same circular foot motion. Engages the core more; knees have to come up higher toward the shoulders than in the supine version.
3. **Hands-off pommeling** — same seated position, but hands come off the mat. Now only your core supports your legs through the movement — more coordination and core strength required.
4. **Elbow-propped (vertical) pommeling** — propped up on your elbows, pommeling in a more vertical, shoulder-supported position. Closest to positions you'll actually be in during live sparring.

## Why this matters

- The goal is to be able to move your feet with the same coordination and confidence you already have with your hands, in two different directions and circular motions.
- This dexterity is what lets you "swim" a foot into good attacking position even against a resisting opponent — directly enabling the one-in-one-out position that starts every triangle.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Progression order: supine → seated (hands down) → seated (hands off) → elbow-propped vertical. Each step removes a support and adds core/coordination demand.
- This is presented as ideal daily warm-up material, not a one-time drill.
- The end goal is foot dexterity good enough to pommel into position under resistance, not just in isolation.
