# Partner Inversion And Rotational Drill

## Overview

Builds on the pummeling drills by adding inversion — getting comfortably up onto the points of your shoulders with a partner, then rotating around their body to catch a triangle. This is the essential prerequisite movement for most bottom-position triangle entries covered later in the course.

---

## The spinning inversion drill

1. Training partner kneels behind you; grab wrists.
2. Come up so your weight is on your shoulders, body inverted, knees to chest — the same core position needed for most triangles.
3. Bring your feet to the partner's armpit: right foot brings the left foot across so the **heel** locks into the armpit (not the sole of the foot — you want the heel to hook, not just rest there).
4. Partner keeps their body strong/resistant, doesn't get pulled forward. The point isn't to yank them — it's to practice rotating around the corner and throwing your leg over their shoulder.
5. Once you catch the shoulder, release the second hand and come through into position to turn off the shoulder and continue into the various triangle setups covered later.

## Key mechanical detail: catching with the hollow of the knee

- The catch on the opponent's shoulders has to come from the **hollow of your knee**, not your calf muscle. Contact should land on the opponent's trapezius.
- Demonstrated poorly: catching with the calf gives a weak connection to the head/shoulders, and the finishing attempt turns into a scramble.
- Done correctly: hook the Achilles-tendon-area behind the opponent's shoulder as you rotate, bring the hollow of your knee to their armpit, then bring the hollow of your second knee over their trapezius as you turn — this sets up a clean figure-four lock.
- Because you're balanced on the points of your shoulders (minimal mat friction), you can rotate easily once the first hook is set.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Grab wrists, invert onto your shoulders, hook a heel (not the sole) into the partner's armpit, rotate around the corner, lock figure-four.
- The single biggest quality difference: catching the opponent's shoulder/trapezius with the **hollow of the knee**, not the calf. Calf contact = weak connection = scramble instead of a clean lock.
- This drill is what later lets you turn, balance, and penetrate with the knee-hollow to the trapezius under live conditions.
