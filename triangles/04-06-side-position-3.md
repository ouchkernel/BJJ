# Front Triangle Side Position 3

## Overview

Continues and extends the knee-penetration entry, then addresses the single most common failure point across all these top-side triangle variations: **losing the trapped near arm**. Offers a specific recovery method that finishes with a different triangle variant — the rear triangle (Ushiro Sankaku).

---

## Knee-penetration entry (extended)

1. Near arm trapped as before.
2. Punch your hand through to dominate the **inside position at the bicep** — this blocks the opponent from bringing the hand back.
3. Drive forward, bring your knee to your own hand.
4. Bring your elbow out and use it as a prop to turn the corner and extract your foot.
5. Penetrate knee-first, foot-second.
6. Lock the trap triangle, control the head, turn the corner, figure-four.

Framed as a good option for a "slower moving athlete" — it gives up much less space than the foot-penetration route.

## The common problem: losing the near arm

If the opponent turns *into* you, he can extract his elbow to a safe position on the inside of your hip — then turn back to face you and use his regular escapes. This is the smart, expected defense.

## The fix

1. The moment you see the opponent turning away (to free the arm), take inside thumb position on his wrist.
2. As he completes the turn, push through and put your hand/forearm inside his armpit (can be doubled up briefly for more force).
3. Even after his elbow is now safely on the inside of your hip, that forearm **prevents him from turning back to face you**.
4. Walk down his hips, recapture the elbow.
5. Place your foot next to his elbow, sit through, pass the foot inside his armpit — this re-establishes one-in-one-out.
6. Control his chin, grip your own shin with the second leg, come around the corner.

## Finish: a different triangle

From this recovered position, lock up **Ushiro Sankaku (rear triangle)** instead of the front triangle. As the opponent pulls away the strangle comes on immediately — the recovery still results in a fast finish, just via a different variant.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Knee penetration (extended version) uses an elbow-as-prop to turn the corner and free the foot, and is well suited to slower athletes since it minimizes escape space.
- The single biggest failure mode in top-side triangles is the opponent turning into you to extract the near arm to a safe inside position, then turning back to escape.
- The fix isn't to prevent the elbow extraction — it's to block the opponent's ability to turn back and face you afterward, using inside thumb position → forearm in the armpit.
- This recovery naturally leads to a rear triangle (Ushiro Sankaku) finish rather than the front triangle — still a fast, tight submission, just a different lock.
