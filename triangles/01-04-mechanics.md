# Mechanics

## Overview

Third of the four critical themes. The claim: almost every common problem people run into applying triangles in competition and sparring traces back to mechanics, not physical gifts — and this course puts heavy emphasis on precise mechanics specifically to counter the widespread belief that triangles are only for people with long legs.

---

## The core argument

- There's nothing more frustrating than doing all the hard work of setup, entry, and controlling a resisting opponent, only to have the finish "run away" because the mechanics weren't there at the last step.
- A large part of the course is spent on the precise mechanics needed to overcome resistance across all five triangle types — this isn't optional detail, it's presented as the actual determinant of your success rate.

## Body type vs. mechanics

- Common belief: triangles are for people with long, thin legs; short-legged people will never have a "noteworthy" triangle.
- Counter-claim: with mechanics well understood, even short-legged athletes can develop a triangle their opponents respect and fear.
- Body type does still play a role (as it does in every jiu-jitsu technique) — this isn't a claim that it's irrelevant. But a large part of what's commonly attributed to body type is actually attributable to mechanics.

---

## Key Takeaways

- If your triangle attempts keep "running away" right at the finish, the diagnosis offered here is mechanics, not lack of flexibility or leg length.
- Short legs are a real constraint but not disqualifying — good mechanics compensate for it.
- This theme is the throughline justification for why the rest of the course drills mechanical detail (grip depth, angle, knee/foot placement) as heavily as it does.
