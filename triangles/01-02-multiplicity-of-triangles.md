# Multiplicity Of Triangles

## Overview

The first of four critical themes running through the whole series. Most jiu-jitsu athletes, when they say "triangle," mean only the front triangle. The multiplicity principle says: that's leaving performance on the table.

---

## The core idea

- The sport of jiu-jitsu overwhelmingly emphasizes one triangle — the front triangle — to the point that "show me a triangle" almost always produces a front triangle demonstration.
- The front triangle genuinely is the highest-percentage of the five and will account for the vast majority of submission finishes. That's not in dispute.
- But limiting yourself to one tool when five are available is a mistake — "no builder would perform complex carpentry with one tool, so no good strangle expert would go in with one tool when he has five available."
- Once you understand the category A / category B split (front–rear–opposite / side–reverse) and use the triangles as a team rather than relying on the best-known one, whenever one triangle fails you can move to another within the same category **without releasing your position** — you don't need to reset.

## Why this matters practically

- A large part of the course is spent on the interface between the five triangles — how they relate to each other and how a broader mental image of "what counts as a triangle" increases overall performance with the position.
- This principle is presented as a mindset shift as much as a technical one: train yourself to see all five as available options from a given position, not just the front triangle.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Front triangle is statistically dominant and will remain your primary tool — this principle isn't an argument against that.
- The other four triangles aren't backups to abandon the system for — they're teammates within the same category that let you continue an attack when one specific triangle stalls out.
- Category A (front/rear/opposite) and Category B (side/reverse) triangles link to each other; losing one triangle inside a category doesn't mean losing the position.
