# Rear Triangle From Back 2

## Overview

A second family of entries from turtle position — this time routing through a **back crucifix**. The key idea: threatening an armlock forces the opponent to bridge, and that bridge is what delivers them into the rear triangle entry.

## Sequence 1 — Sweep Into A Back Crucifix

1. From turtle, tight waist, secure the opponent's hand, post, bring your knee around the corner.
2. Sweep into the opponent's leg.
3. Sit down into a **back crucifix** — knee comes in, classic crucifix position.
4. Put pressure on the opponent's trapped arm. If they don't react, the arm gets hurt — so they're forced to bridge to protect it.
5. When they bridge: sweep your knee around behind them and tilt them down to the mat.
6. Move back and away from the opponent (standard rear-triangle spacing).
7. Foot goes inside the opponent's armpit — sets up an easy transition.
8. Legs pointing the same direction (unified) → trap triangle → hand-assist → figure-four → finish.

Net effect: you were working from **back → back crucifix → rear triangle**.

## Sequence 2 — Armlock-Or-Strangle Dilemma Off The Bridge

1. Secure a hand, lock it, go around the corner into the back crucifix.
2. Whether you're actually threatening a strangle or an armlock here, the opponent bridges to avoid the armlock pain.
3. Bring your knee around the corner — you land in a strong attacking position.
4. Common opponent reaction: they slide their body down yours to make strangling with the arms harder. This is actually the ideal moment — come up to an elbow, shift away, come to a seated position.
5. Foot inside the armpit, wrist-to-wrist, unified legs → trap triangle → figure-four → strangle.

## Key Takeaways

- The back crucifix is a **bridge position**, not a dead end — an opponent bridging to protect their arm from the crucifix walks themselves directly into the rear triangle entry.
- You don't need to commit to "strangle or armlock" up front from the crucifix — either threat produces the same bridge reaction, which produces the same triangle entry.
- If the opponent slides down to make the strangle harder to reach, that's your cue to come up to an elbow and reset to a seated entry rather than fighting for it flat.
