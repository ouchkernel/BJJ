# The Clamp

## Overview

The single most commonly used method of entering triangles from bottom position — described as more effective than any other supine entry, and something you must master to reliably fight into triangles from your back against resistance. The clamp is essentially an **upper-body version of Ashi Garami**: the same physical principle, applied to the shoulder girdle instead of the hips.

---

## What is the clamp?

For any guard where you're on your back and your opponent is in close — closed, open, doesn't matter which — get onto your side and bring one knee in front of one of your opponent's shoulders and the other knee behind the opposite shoulder.

- The **far shoulder** is blocked in front by one knee.
- The **near shoulder** is blocked behind by the other knee.
- The opponent's head and one arm end up trapped between your knees.

**Why it controls distance:** if the opponent pushes into you, the front knee stops them. If they pull away, the back knee stops them. Either way, the distance between your head and theirs is now governed entirely by you.

## Getting in

- The entry only requires the same shrimping motion used in a basic elbow escape — except instead of elbow-escaping inside the opponent's hips, you're escaping inside their **shoulders**, which is easier since shoulders aren't as strong as hips.
- Once in the clamp, a simple movement of your foot across your opponent's body creates a perpendicular angle (you're now looking directly into their ear) — this is what creates the room to extract your bottom leg and go over the top into a trap triangle.

## How it maps to Ashi Garami

In a basic Ashi Garami: a knee in front of the hip stops the opponent coming forward, a knee behind the knee stops them going back — distance between your chest and their knee is governed by your legs. The clamp is the exact same idea, moved to the shoulder girdle: knee in front, knee behind, one inside, one outside, distance governed by knee position, angle governed by turning across.

## Mechanics of the knee action

It's not primarily a squeeze — squeezing isn't the strongest motion available. It's a **scissoring** action: one knee pulls back toward your chest while the other pushes forward. If the opponent tries to pull their trapped arm out, this scissoring makes it very difficult, and it becomes easy to find the angle needed to extract your bottom leg and go into the triangle.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The clamp = upper-body Ashi Garami. Same principle (knee in front, knee behind, both sides blocked), applied to the shoulder girdle instead of the hips.
- Entry uses the same shrimp/elbow-escape motion you already know — just aimed at the shoulders instead of the hips (and easier, since shoulders are weaker).
- Distance is entirely governed by your knee position once the clamp is set — push-in and pull-away are both blocked.
- The knee action is a scissor (one back, one forward), not a squeeze.
- Considered the single most important supine triangle entry to master in this whole system.
