# Fake Hip Sweep To Front Triangle

## Overview

Closes the volume with another kuzushi (off-balancing) based entry from closed guard — this time using a sweep threat (rather than a real sweep) to force the opponent's hand to the mat as a base, which then becomes the triangle opening.

## Sequence

1. From closed guard with the opponent's hands on your biceps, bring both hands to the inside.
2. First layer of kuzushi: pull the opponent's hands down to the mat.
3. Second layer: turn your hips out to the side, coming up from flat on your back to an elbow, then from an elbow to a hand.
4. Note on strength: this isn't a strong position by raw arm strength — the opponent is bending you toward the mat. The actual strength comes from **hip position**, not the arm: use your leg to bring your hips higher than the opponent's hips, so your chest nearly faces down to the mat. This makes it much harder for them to push into you.
5. Plant your foot, bring your body up, and threaten the opponent's balance enough that they're forced to **base out with a hand** to avoid falling — essentially a fake sweep. With hands wrapped around your waist and you driving forward, they have no choice but to post a hand; they cannot avoid it.
6. The moment the hand posts, take your feet off the mat and balance on the opponent's seat — nearly all your weight transfers onto their head, letting you move your head from close to far and pull them directly into the triangle.
7. From here, expect resistance in the form of stacking or posturing — maintain the quality of your position (knee and shin close together as you turn the corner) so that pulling away becomes very difficult.
8. Lock the figure-four for the strangle.

## Key Takeaways

- This is a variant of the kuzushi principle already covered — instead of pushing a knee to off-balance, the mechanism here is a controlled forward drive that forces a defensive hand-post, functioning like a fake sweep.
- The real source of pressure/control throughout is **hip elevation via the leg**, not arm strength — get your hips above theirs.
- Once the opponent posts a hand to survive the "sweep," nearly all your weight shifts onto their head, which is what makes pulling them directly into the triangle so easy.
