# Fake Hip Sweep To Front Triangle

## Overview

A kuzushi (off-balancing) entry from closed guard that uses the threat of a sweep to strip the opponent's base, making your own legs momentarily weightless and easy to throw into the triangle.

---

## Sequence

1. From closed guard, bring your hands to your opponent's biceps on the inside.
2. Pull their hands down to the mat.
3. Shift your hips out to the side — this lets you come up first to an elbow, then to a hand, foot planted on the mat.
4. Force the opponent's hand out to the side, giving yourself space for the coming triangle. Do this by making a good "seat" — driving forward so your opponent is forced to base out with a hand for their own support, the way they would if they were actually about to be swept. They have no choice: without basing out, they'd fall over.
5. As soon as they base out, take your feet off the mat and balance on your opponent's set/base — now essentially 100% of your weight sits on their head. This is what makes your legs weightless and easy to move.
6. From here, move your head from close to far and pull your opponent into the triangle.
7. This is where the opponent's resistance shows up — they'll try to stack you, posture up, or otherwise defend. Keep the quality of your position: scoop your hand inside their leg.
8. As they try to stand or move, focus on bringing your knee around the corner so your knee and shin stay close together — this makes it very difficult for them to pull away.
9. Lock the figure-four and finish the strangle.

## Alternate detail

In some reps, the weight transfer is emphasized by literally throwing your legs across the opponent so your weight lands fully on them — this is what makes the legs weightless enough to swing freely over the shoulders as the opponent postures up or tries to move; a quick turn brings everything around the corner into the lock.

---

## Key Takeaways

- This is a kuzushi entry, not a grip-based one — the mechanism is forcing a base-out reaction (like a real sweep threat) to strip the opponent's structure.
- The "fake" part: you're not actually completing a sweep — you're using the threat of one to force the opponent into a compromised, weight-transferred position that opens the triangle.
- Once your weight is fully on the opponent's head, your legs become weightless and easy to swing over.
- Finish detail: keep knee and shin close together as the opponent tries to move — this denies them the space to pull free.
