# Front Triangle, Opposite Triangle & Elbow Position — Quick Reference

## When to switch to Hantai Sankaku

- Opponent's elbow blocks at your hip, bringing their shoulder into a front triangle → switch.
- Your hips already ended up on the "wrong" side for a conventional front triangle → don't reposition, just spin into Hantai Sankaku directly.

## Reactive sequence (off elbow defense)

1. Control posture.
2. Gain angle — **opposite** direction from a normal front-triangle turn.
3. Line up north-south (check: foot touches mat in that direction).
4. Foot/shin up to their rear deltoid — removes the shoulder.
5. Knee under the armpit.
6. Figure-four → tight strangle.

## Proactive rule of thumb

Front triangle: opponent's left arm trapped → your hips go right. Hips end up wrong side? → just go straight to Hantai Sankaku, don't fix the angle.

## Flexibility check

- [ ] Can you bring your knees to your chest?
- [ ] Can you roll up onto your shoulders/toward the floor?

That's it — no full splits required. Stomach size matters more than leg length here (and you can control that).

## Why bother with both

Front triangle's weaknesses (elbow position, bad hip angle) = opposite triangle's strengths, and vice versa. One-two combination.
