# Triangles Out Of Scrambles — Quick Reference

## Definition

Scramble = neither athlete has a controlling grip advantage over the other's movement.

## Classic entry scenario

Both on knees, facing off. One has a tight waist grip, other has an overhook/whizzer.

## Check before you attack

- [ ] Opponent hasn't locked a body lock on you (can crunch you backward to your back)
- [ ] Opponent hasn't secured a front headlock
- [ ] Your head isn't dropping too far forward

Manage head position actively while hunting the scramble-triangle opportunity — don't let it be an afterthought.

## Do this, in order

1. Hand on the mat, drive **forward** — never straight across into his body.
2. That forces the opponent to post his own hand down (or get driven down/blinded).
3. Walk him down onto his hip, piling on pressure until the hand is pinned to the floor.
4. Rotate your hips toward your own down-hand — still never push across his body.
5. Throw your far leg across his body, hand into his armpit, at the same time.
6. Hang your weight off him so your other leg goes weightless.
7. That leg goes straight into the triangle.
8. Don't let go early — releasing too soon lets him yank the wrist free and kill the entry.
9. Stay locked, scoop inside his legs as he postures up — good tight lock.

## Key concept

**Directionality of force.** Drive forward through the hips, not across the body — that's what forces the hand down and opens the entry.

## Going to your back mid-entry?

- Matters if you care about **top position** (wrestling-style rules).
- Doesn't matter in standard jiu-jitsu with **no penalty for your back touching** — finish the triangle regardless.
