# Attacking Turtle With Side Triangle 3

## Overview

Shows the link between the two entry styles already covered — the **seated** Yoko Sankaku (from front headlock) and the **supine** Yoko Sankaku (the original standing/kneeling entry) — as a combination attack: if the seated strangle isn't finishing, transition into a pin, then reapply the triangle from there.

## Sequence

1. From a front headlock, knee in, stuff the opponent's head, come up vertical and hook into their leg.
2. Tight waist, hand to the floor, sit down with control into the seated Yoko Sankaku, extend the leg, foot above the knee, lock on.
3. If the strangle isn't landing (opponent pulling their elbow back, resisting successfully) — don't force it. Instead: tight waist grip, grab the opponent's ankle.
4. Pull your head around the corner as if looking around their hamstring, adjusting your body position to pull them **on top of you**, sending them across your body.
5. Result: the opponent is now in a **pin position** on the floor underneath you.
6. From the pin, lock a kimura on the near arm as usual, then make the standard enhancement adjustment — turn belly down, knee in — and relock the triangle. Now it's "ungodly tight" on the neck.
7. As the opponent tries to turn up or move out, squeeze with the legs for a tight finish.

## Key Takeaways

- This is a **combination**, not a separate technique: seated Yoko Sankaku that isn't finishing converts into a pin (via the ankle-grab/hamstring-reach detail), and the pin resets into a fresh, tighter triangle attempt.
- Having both the seated and supine versions in your toolkit means you can cycle an opponent between pin and strangle repeatedly, rather than being stuck once one version stalls.
- The belly-down-shift enhancement still applies on the relock — it's what makes the second attempt "ungodly tight" compared to the first.
