# Partner Pummeling Drills

## Overview

First drill that brings in a partner, and the first one that trains what the instructor calls the single most important thing when entering a triangle: getting the knees up to the chest with the body hollowed out, so a real one-in-one-out position can be built against resistance. Also called the "no-hands drill."

---

## The no-hands drill

1. Start in closed guard, training partner's hands on your biceps (not down by your hips — that ruins the drill), shoulders up, arms only slightly bent.
2. Flare your elbows out to create space for your knees to move inside.
3. Open your guard and turn your body out to one side. You can assist by placing a foot on the floor or on your partner's hip.
4. Pommel one foot inside and over the partner's shoulder (without hands) and begin locking up a triangle.
5. Return to the start; repeat side to side, threading the foot through and around each time, always locking figure-four at the end. The partner's hands never leave your biceps throughout.
6. Volume: 20–50 repetitions per side to build the pattern.

## Key mechanical detail

- Your hips need to go offline while your knee points **down at the mat**, not up at the ceiling — that's what lets your knee thread through and lock the figure-four. A knee pointed at the ceiling blocks the thread even if your hips are correctly offset.
- This is the same pattern you'll later use to bring your foot through, around, and into a locked figure-four from live positions.

---

## Key Takeaways

- This drill trains getting knees to chest + a hollowed body against resistance — described as the single most important entry skill.
- Partner's hands stay on your biceps the whole drill; you're training your ability to work around a resisting grip, not against no resistance at all.
- Mechanical cue to internalize: knee points to the mat, not the ceiling, or the foot won't thread through.
