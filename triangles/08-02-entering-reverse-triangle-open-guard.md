# Entering Reverse Triangle (From Open Guard)

## Overview

The mechanics of the reverse triangle are impressive, but no one just lets you get into position on a resisting opponent — you need a reliable entry. This lesson covers entering from an open guard scenario.

---

## Starting position

- In open guard, one foot placed across the training partner's stomach (fairly standard open guard setup).
- Both hands gripping the opponent's triceps, one foot in inside position.
- Not a difficult position to get into — this is meant to be a common, accessible starting point.

## Entry sequence

1. Create space by extending your outside leg (e.g. left leg).
2. Pommel that foot around the training partner so it ends up on the other side of their head.
3. This fits your leg into a good attacking position.
4. Extend the other leg (e.g. right) to get underneath the training partner.
5. Pass a hand underneath the opponent's ribcage and begin turning around the corner to face them.
6. Checkpoint: confirm your knee is underneath their ear (the single most important prerequisite established in the mechanics lesson).
7. Bring everything down to the shoulder joint and lock the figure-four.
8. Apply a **reverse tight waist grip** with your free hand — this prevents the opponent from bench-pressing or pulling away as you finish.

Once locked, the strangle is described as "ungodly tight" — a reverse triangle applied well reportedly feels like being beheaded; people who've only felt a poorly-applied version often dismiss the move, but done correctly it's devastating.

## Variation shown from a different starting detail

Same entry, slightly different setup emphasis:
1. Start in inside position with hand control on the training partner.
2. Place a foot on the opponent's hip.
3. Extend hips out, pommel the foot around the corner and kick over the opponent's neck.
4. Turn through and lock.
5. Bring your quadriceps all the way down to the opponent's tricep to fully eliminate their shoulder.
6. Hug underneath, lock the figure-four.
7. When the opponent pulls out and away, it's very difficult for them to escape — the stranglehold becomes immensely tight.

A third pass through the same open-guard entry (shown from a different camera angle) reinforces the same checkpoints: extend to create space, pommel the leg around and over the neck, place the knee just below the ear, turn underneath the opponent (legs pointing the same direction as the opponent to prevent them yanking free), lock the figure-four, apply the reverse tight waist, then finish.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Entry starts from a common, non-exotic open guard position — foot on stomach, tricep grips.
- Core mechanical sequence: extend leg → pommel foot around opponent's head → extend other leg underneath → turn around the corner → confirm knee below the ear → lock figure-four → reverse tight waist grip to prevent escape.
- Bringing your quadriceps/knee all the way down to the opponent's tricep is what fully eliminates their shoulder from the lock.
- The reverse tight waist grip is what shuts down the opponent's ability to bench-press or yank away once you're locked in.
- This is a highly effective, repeatable way to enter the reverse triangle from open guard — the instructor demonstrates it three times from different angles to drill the same checkpoints.
