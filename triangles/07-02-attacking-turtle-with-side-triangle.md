# Attacking Turtle With Side Triangle

## Overview

First live application of Yoko Sankaku: attacking an opponent in turtle position. The entry routes through a kimura threat — when the opponent defends the kimura by basing out wide, that basing motion is exactly what opens the side triangle.

## Sequence

1. From behind a turtled opponent, secure their hand, drawing it in with a good grip.
2. Second hand to the floor, knees off the mat, move around the opponent with a strong body.
3. Get a two-on-one grip on the hand itself.
4. Move your knee over the opponent's head, bring it in and down — this levers their arm out and away from their body.
5. With the arm levered out, thread your hand through and begin setting a **kimura** grip.
6. Attempt to lever the opponent over with the kimura. If they simply fall over, go straight into the kimura finishing system.
7. If instead the opponent bases their hand out wide to stop the kimura roll — that's the cue to switch to Yoko Senkaku.
8. Step your foot right over the opponent's armpit, come up into a low squat, then fall **straight back** (not to the side) over their head — this takes them over into side-triangle range.
9. You now have the one-in-one-out position. Turn belly down (as they reach to defend the kimura by locking their hands together, they hand you the entry).
10. One leg straightens, the other retracts (standard side-triangle finishing mechanic), lock a kimura on the near arm with the hand on their stomach, or continue to the strangle.

## The Underlying Dilemma

This is explicitly framed as a **dilemma attack**: the opponent has to choose between letting the kimura roll them over, or basing out to stop it — and basing out is exactly what delivers the side triangle. Whichever they pick, you have an answer.

## Key Takeaways

- Turtle attacks route through a kimura threat first — the opponent's own defense against being rolled (basing wide) is the trigger for the side-triangle entry, not a separate technique to hunt for.
- Fall **straight back** over the opponent's head when taking them down, not to the side — that's what lands you correctly for the triangle.
- Once in the one-in-one-out position, the belly-down shift and the standard "one leg extends, one retracts" finishing mechanic apply exactly as covered in the mechanics chapter.
