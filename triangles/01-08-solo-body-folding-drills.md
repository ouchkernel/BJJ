# Solo Body Folding Drills

## Overview

Companion set to the pummeling drills — where pommeling builds foot dexterity, these drills build the "folding" ability: bringing the knees up toward the armpits while hollowing the body forward. Almost every triangle involves this hollowed, folded shape; a triangle is essentially never applied with an arched body.

---

## The drills

1. **Toe touches over the shoulder, walking side to side** — come up and touch your toes over one shoulder, then walk across to the other side while looking in the direction you're moving. Keeps knees close to your own shoulders as your toes skim the mat. Recover to a lateral position between reps.
2. **Straight-leg fold and touch** — start on the floor, fold the body into a hollow, bring the (straightened) legs over to touch the floor, then come up and lock a triangle shape on both sides in turn.
3. **Crucifix-arm direction switch** — arms out in a crucifix position, bring the legs up and touch, then slide across the mat and switch direction, looking to the opposite side each time. Trains folding the body over in either direction, gets you comfortable with weight supported on your two shoulders, and builds the core strength needed to bring knees to chest.

## Why this matters

- These are recommended as a pre-class warm-up, same as the pommeling drills.
- They build the inverted, shoulder-supported, hollowed body shape that almost every bottom-position triangle depends on.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Folding the body forward (hollow shape) is the default triangle body position — arched posture essentially never applies here.
- Three drills, increasing demand: simple toe-touch walk → straight-leg fold with a triangle lock on each side → crucifix direction-switch drill.
- Use these as a warm-up, same as the pummeling drills from the previous chapter.
