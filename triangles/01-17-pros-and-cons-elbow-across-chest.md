# Pros And Cons Elbow Across Chest

## Overview

A detail deliberately left uncoached for most of the course: bringing the opponent's trapped elbow across your chest once the triangle starts locking up. Unlike the head-pull method, this one gets a genuine pros-and-cons treatment — it isn't simply recommended.

---

## The technique

- From a front triangle, as you begin locking up, take the opponent's elbow (the one trapped by your legs) and move it across your own chest, then perform the strangulation from that position.

## The case for it (pros)

- Makes your body structurally strong in the position.
- Does a good job of bringing your head and the opponent's elbow closer together, which is generally a good thing for triangle mechanics.

## The case against it (cons)

- Some of the most effective triangle **counters** happen specifically because the elbow was brought across the chest — moving the arm there can hand the opponent the exact opening they need to escape.
- As a general rule, most of the time there are actually advantages to **not** moving the arm across the chest.
- The right call depends on circumstance: appropriate in certain situations, the wrong move in others. There's no universal answer.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Bringing the elbow across the chest is a real, sometimes-useful detail — not a mandatory step, and not a mistake either. It's situational.
- Pros: stronger structure, head and elbow drawn closer together.
- Cons: it's also a common source of the counters that beat triangles — meaning the default lean, most of the time, is to leave the arm where it is rather than bring it across.
