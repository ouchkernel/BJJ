# Introduction To The 5 Triangles

## Overview

A guided walkthrough of all five triangle variations, giving each its Japanese name and basic identifying feature, before the course spends the bulk of its time on the front triangle specifically. The goal here is simply to make sure you know what's being referred to throughout the rest of the series.

---

## The five triangles

1. **Front triangle — Amote Senkaku.** "Senkaku" = triangle/three sides; "Amote" = from the front. The one most people already know: leg goes across, locks a figure-four, strangle applied. Statistically the most successful triangle in modern competition; the instructor's student Gordon Ryan has had notable competition success with variations of it. Usually worked from bottom position, but also from various top positions.
2. **Rear triangle — Ushiro Senkaku.** Used once you've gotten around behind an opponent — very often a fast, effective switch when strangling with the arms alone is proving difficult.
3. **Opposite-side triangle — Hantai Senkaku.** Locking the triangle in what looks like the "wrong" or unconventional way. An excellent supplement to front-triangle attacks that have been shut down by the opponent's posture.
4. **Side triangle — Yoko Senkaku.** Common in judo, rarer in jiu-jitsu — described as an underused addition worth developing.
5. **Reverse triangle — Gyaku Senkaku.** Statistically the least productive of the five in terms of finishes, but still an important, complete part of the system.

## How they group

- Front, rear, and opposite-side form **category A** — you can rotate between them around the opponent's head and shoulder without releasing your legs.
- Side and reverse form **category B** — same interchangeability rule applies within that pair.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Five triangles, five Japanese names: Amote (front), Ushiro (rear), Hantai (opposite-side), Yoko (side), Gyaku (reverse).
- Front triangle remains the workhorse and gets the majority of course time, but knowing the other four by name and basic identifying feature is a prerequisite for the rest of the series.
- Category grouping (A: front/rear/opposite, B: side/reverse) determines which triangles you can move between without resetting your position.
