# Lower Back Off Ground, Onto Points Of Shoulders — Quick Reference

## The problem: lower back flat on the mat

- Stacking becomes trivial — opponent's head driving forward collapses your triangle immediately.
- You can't form an angle — too much drag from back-on-floor contact.
- You can't track posturing — your body doesn't follow when they posture up.

## The fix

Lift the lower back off the mat. Weight goes onto the **points of your shoulders.**

## Payoff

| Opponent tries | Result with shoulders-up |
|---|---|
| Stack you | Drives them into the strangle instead — bad for them |
| Posture / stand up | You stay hooked in, lock holds behind the head |
| — | Low friction lets you turn freely underneath them |

## Connects to

The solo body-folding / shoulder-weight drills from earlier in the course — this is where that training actually gets used.
