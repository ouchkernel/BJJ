# Rear Triangle To Opposite Triangle

## Overview

Follows directly from the front-triangle conversion — this time shows how a stalled Ushiro Sankaku links to the third Category A triangle: the **opposite triangle (Hantai Sankaku)**.

## Sequence 1 — Opposite-Direction Conversion

1. Start in a stalled Ushiro Sankaku (struggling to finish, at risk of the opponent stripping your foot).
2. Turn out to the side, move in on the opponent.
3. This time, go the **opposite direction** from the front-triangle conversion covered previously.
4. Get a hold of the opponent's head, scoop inside their leg, turn the corner, come through with both feet pointing forward.
5. Result: the same one-in-one-out position (one arm locked inside your triangle, one arm locked outside), shoulder eliminated via good foot position.
6. Descend, wedge around the opponent's head and shoulders — this makes posturing up very difficult.
7. Lock the figure-four, strangle for the finish.

## Sequence 2 — Off A Foot-Grab Defense

1. Opponent secures your foot, preventing the triangle from locking.
2. Bring your foot over the top, push and clear the legs. If they stay where they are, there's arm lock danger — so they're forced to get up.
3. This time, instead of going to the front triangle, go the **opposite direction**: catch the hand, scoop in.
4. Form an initial 90° angle — but the real angulation comes from the **pendulum action of your leg**, swinging your body around the corner.
5. Lock everything into place, form tight wedges, hand-assist so the lock becomes a full figure-four.
6. When the opponent tries to pull away, it doesn't work — secure a quick, powerful finish.

## Key Takeaways

- Category A triangles genuinely back each other up: "as one fails, you just switch right to another." The rear triangle can convert to *either* the front triangle or the opposite triangle depending on which way the opponent's escape attempt takes them.
- The choice of which direction to go (front triangle vs. opposite triangle) is dictated by which way the opponent turns or moves — you're reading their reaction, not picking in advance.
- In the opposite-triangle conversion specifically, the true angle comes from a **pendulum swing of your leg**, not just an initial 90° turn.
