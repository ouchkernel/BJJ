# Upper Body To Lower Body

## Overview

Closing chapter of Volume 1. Reframes the triangle as more than a submission attempt in isolation — it's an upper-body threat generator that predictably opens up the opponent's legs, and this chapter covers converting a stalling triangle into a leg attack rather than abandoning the position.

---

## The core idea

- A triangle attempt is "an outstanding opportunity to create strong upper body threats that create predictable defensive reactions, which make his lower body vulnerable."
- Build the mental habit: every time you feel a triangle attempt failing, go directly into the opponent's lower body instead of resetting or giving up the position entirely.
- The goal is to make this transition as automatic as possible — a failing triangle isn't a dead end, it's a trigger for the next attack.

## Key Takeaways

- Treat the triangle as a two-purpose weapon: a strangle attempt now, and an upper-body pressure generator that opens leg-attack opportunities if the strangle doesn't land.
- Build the reflex: triangle attempt failing → go to the lower body immediately, don't just reset.
- This chapter is the bridge from Volume 1's strangle-focused material into the leg-entry and X-guard-adjacent material referenced going into later volumes.
