# Putting It All Together — Front Triangle

## Overview

Synthesis lesson: combines every mechanical detail from the preceding front-triangle lessons (shoulder-line rule, eliminate-the-shoulder rule, knee-below-ear, inner-thigh-to-tricep, posture control, scoop arm) into one continuous movement, then walks through how that combined position automatically shuts down the opponent's standard defenses.

---

## Recap: short legs are not the limiter

- Leg length helps but isn't the deciding factor — good body positioning (perpendicular angle, strangle leg above the shoulder line, effective scoop arm) compensates for short legs.
- Ironically, short-legged athletes who get the position right often end up with a *tighter* triangle than long-legged athletes, since a tighter lock is inherent to a smaller frame executed correctly.

## The combined sequence

1. Start from the trap triangle.
2. Punch through and get a hold of the opponent's head (posture control).
3. Scoop a hold of the opponent's leg; bring your lower back off the ground, both feet pointing forward.
4. Place a wedge behind the opponent's head (still open — feet not yet crossed) — hold the head here to prevent yanking out.
5. Drive forward and up, turning your shoulders in, making the shoulder disappear.
6. From here, lock the figure-four — wedges close around the head and one arm, shoulder excluded.

## Why this shuts down standard defenses

Once the combined position is achieved:
- **Stacking** — opponent driving forward into you just drives them further into the strangle; it doesn't break the lock.
- **Posturing / standing up** — you stay hooked into the leg; even if they get all the way to their feet, the lock (positioned directly behind the head) doesn't come undone.
- **Turning to angle** — if you already have the scoop arm inside their leg, their attempt to turn to an escape angle becomes almost impossible.
- **Elbow-to-the-floor / splitting the triangle** — having the opponent's arm caught underneath your arm (rather than centered) prevents this; contrary to popular teaching, you do NOT need to pull the elbow fully across the body to finish. What's essential is closing the triangle around the smallest possible volume (head + one arm) and excluding the shoulder — elbow position itself is optional.

---

## Key Takeaways

- This lesson is a synthesis, not new content — it chains together shoulder-line, shoulder-elimination, knee-below-ear, inner-thigh-to-tricep, posture control, and scoop-arm placement into one fluid motion.
- Correct combined positioning defeats the four standard triangle defenses (stack, posture/stand, turn-to-angle, elbow-to-floor) essentially automatically — you're not fighting each defense individually, you're occupying a position where none of them work.
- Common misconception debunked: pulling the elbow fully across the body is *not* required to finish. What matters is minimal trapped volume (head + one arm) with the shoulder excluded — elbow position is optional, not essential.
