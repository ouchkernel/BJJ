# Your Body And The Triangle Strangle

## Overview

Transitions from theory into the physical prerequisites for triangle work. Most people are far more coordinated with their upper body (hands) than their lower body (feet/legs) — everyday life trains fine motor skill into the hands, not the feet. Triangle training deliberately closes that gap. The good news: the actual minimum physical requirements are very low.

---

## Why lower-body coordination lags upper-body coordination

- Humans use hands for high-coordination tasks constantly (dialing a phone, playing an instrument) and legs mostly for walking/running — low-coordination tasks.
- Because triangles are fundamentally a stranglehold performed with the legs, this asymmetry is the main reason triangles feel harder to learn than arm-based strangles like the rear naked choke or guillotine, even for otherwise coordinated athletes.

## The four minimum physical requirements

1. **Foot dexterity** — feet must be able to move to a precise spot on the opponent's upper body, with the same kind of coordination most people already have in their hands.
2. **Knee flare** — sitting with one foot in front of the other, bring both knees down toward the floor without using your hands, while also bringing your elbows to the mat. This is the baseline flexibility check — most viewers can already do it without a warm-up.
3. **Knees to chest** — the ability to bring your knees up toward your own armpits. Needed for almost all successful triangles from bottom position.
4. **Shoulder-stand fold** — the ability to come up onto the points of your shoulders (lower back off the ground, body inverted) and move from side to side. Required for most triangles worked from bottom position.

## Waist size vs. leg length

- The most common belief is that leg length is the limiting factor for triangles. This course disputes that: **waist size is the bigger inhibitor**.
- You can't change your leg length, but you can change your waist size. If your own stomach blocks you from bringing your elbows to the floor during the knee-flare check, that's the thing to address (training/diet), not your legs.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The physical bar to start triangle training is low — most people already clear all four minimum requirements without a warm-up.
- Foot dexterity, knee flare (elbows-to-mat, no hands), knees-to-chest, and the shoulder-stand fold are the four things to check and drill.
- Waist size, not leg length, is described as the more common physical inhibitor for triangle success.
