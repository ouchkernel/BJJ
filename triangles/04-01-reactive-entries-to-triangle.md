# Reactive Entries To Triangle

## Overview

Up to this point in the course, triangle entries were attacker-initiated — you generated grips and kuzushi and fought your way in. This chapter covers the opposite situation: the opponent is actively passing your guard, and you react to the grips and pressure he gives you along the way. Three reactive entries are shown, all against common guard-passing attempts.

---

## Entry 1 — Opponent has double scoop grips, hands locked

Opponent controls both hips with locked hands (a common, effective pass). You're one pommel away from a one-in-one-out situation.

1. Bring hips up, point knees straight up — body forms a straight line. This makes it harder for the opponent to block wrist access.
2. Access both of the opponent's wrists, thumb-post inside them.
3. Shimmy your legs out and straight ("like taking off a tight pair of pants").
4. Get one knee inside the opponent's wrist, point the knee outward — this starts a pommeling action that draws the opponent in.
5. Climb to the armpit, cut across the back of the opponent's neck, lock a trap triangle.
6. As the opponent pulls away: pull in with the knees, feed the arm across, get head access, turn the corner, lock the figure-four directly over the opponent's head.

## Entry 2 — Opponent controls both hips, hands unlocked

Similar starting problem, different grip.

1. Bring body up to deny the opponent the ability to throw your legs side to side; access wrists via double thumb-post.
2. Shimmy hips, bring one knee inside the opponent's elbow.
3. Hips climb, throw the leg over the top, anchor the leg to the opponent's back, lock feet behind him.
4. As he pulls away, knees pull in — the harder he pulls, the easier it becomes to swim around the corner and control the head.
5. Turn everything in, knee points into the crotch, strong finishing position.

## Entry 3 — Opponent underneath both legs, hands unlocked

1. Form an angle on the opponent — don't stay lined up directly in front of him (that's when he can stack you).
2. Take hips off the mat, pommel a leg through and around, secure the opponent's arm — classic one-in-one-out.
3. As he pulls away, it's the same trap-triangle sequence: pull in, posture battle, turn the corner, tight figure-four.

## Entry 4 — Over-under guard pass

Opponent controls both hips from an over-under pass, comes up off the floor and puts weight on you.

1. Hold the opponent's far wrist, use a tilting action to tip the opponent down to the mat.
2. Use your knee as a lever, bring the second leg down, control the opponent's elbow — the position tilts over and a leg is freed.
3. As he pulls away, pull in with the knees, go over the shoulder, control the head, make the same turn into the triangle.

A second variation of the same entry: use a tilting motion to directly extract the foot, then cross ankles into the trap triangle before making the same turn to the figure-four.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Reactive entries follow the same skeleton every time: pommel a leg in → one-in-one-out → trap triangle → as the opponent posts/pulls away, pull in with the knees and turn the corner → figure-four.
- Against locked-hand double scoops and unlocked variants, the fix is the same: get the hips off the mat, access the wrists, and pommel a knee in to start the draw-in.
- Forming an angle (not staying square to the opponent) is what prevents getting stacked when he's underneath both your legs.
- Over-under passes are countered with a tilting/off-balancing action using the knee as a lever, which directly frees a leg into the triangle.
