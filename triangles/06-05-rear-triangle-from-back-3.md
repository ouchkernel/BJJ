# Rear Triangle From Back 3

## Overview

A third entry family from the back — this time from an already-established back control (body triangle, hooks in, etc.) rather than from turtle position. Central technical detail: **head alignment** directly over the opponent's head.

## Sequence 1 — From Established Back Control

1. From any standard back control (body triangle, feet crossed, etc.), lift one of the opponent's hands and push the other down — this locks up their primary defensive hand and their secondary defensive hand separately.
2. Push the down hand further, foot goes over the top.
3. Take your strangle hand to the **wrong side** of the opponent's head (crossing over) and go wrist-to-wrist.
4. Use your knee to push the opponent down toward the mat.
5. Bring your head over their head — **directly above**, in a straight line. If your head sits too far to the side, hitting the triangle becomes very difficult.
6. This head alignment automatically puts your legs in position — an initial trap triangle locks up easily.
7. Hand-assist brings everything up into a figure-four, and you're set to strangle.

## Sequence 2 — From Rear Mount, Falling To The Side

1. From behind the opponent (rear mount situation), fall over to the side.
2. Lift and bring your foot over the opponent's forearm.
3. Use your knees to move the opponent's head out of the way so you can get your forearm in behind them (their head being close in can block your hand — knees clear the way).
4. Hand goes in, lift and lock, connect their knuckles to your chest.
5. Turn and bring your head **directly over their head** again — the same alignment principle as Sequence 1.
6. Lower legs lined up (unified) → trap triangle → adjust to figure-four → strangle.

## Key Takeaways

- The defining technical detail of this entry family is **head alignment**: your head stacked directly over the opponent's head automatically lines up your legs for the trap triangle. Head too far to the side = hard to hit the triangle at all.
- Isolate one arm high (primary defensive hand) and one low (secondary/pushed down) before committing to the wrist-to-wrist grip.
- Works from multiple established back-control variations (body triangle, hooks, rear mount) — the alignment principle is what matters, not the specific back-control grip you started with.
