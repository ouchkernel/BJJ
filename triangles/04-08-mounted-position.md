# Front Triangle Mounted Position

## Overview

Triangle attacks from full mount. As with the side-position entries, the prerequisite is getting one of the opponent's elbows above his shoulder line — something he won't give up voluntarily.

---

## The ratchet-walk method

1. Get underneath the opponent's head and arm, base with your own arms.
2. Place your forearm/wrist underneath the opponent's elbow.
3. Start walking the elbow outward.
4. Whenever you hit strong resistance (he's pulling the elbow down sharply as you lift), **straighten your arm**, then resume walking.
5. This is a ratchet action: every inch gained is kept — you never go backward. Repeat straighten-and-walk until the elbow reaches up toward the crown of the head.
6. Lock in with both arms around the head and shoulder.

## Entry into one-in-one-out

1. With the head/arm controlled, draw the opponent's arm across the centerline.
2. Take your chin around the **outside** of the opponent's arm.
3. Bring your knee up to your elbow, sit out to the side, hug the head inward.
4. As the opponent's arms come in defensively, take your hand and push his wrist down.
5. Step right over into a good attacking position — the legs curl underneath, achieving one-in-one-out.
6. Forehead to the mat, lock the figure-four, strangle.

## Variant entry — high leg penetration

Same ratchet-walk arm control, but instead of the hand-over-hand step-over method above, use the same "head over own knee" mechanic taught for side position: catch the opponent's wrist, push it under his own neck, head over your own knee, high-leg action puts the foot over the opponent's shoulder. One-in-one-out achieved the same way. Catch, head to the floor, lock the figure-four. Works whether you stay mounted or slide through to bottom position once the triangle is locked — the effect is the same either way.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Elbow control from mount uses a "ratchet" walking action: walk forward, straighten the arm on resistance, never lose ground, repeat until the elbow reaches the crown of the head.
- Two different finishing routes from the same arm control: a hand-over-hand step-over, or the high-leg/head-over-knee mechanic reused from side position.
- Once the triangle is locked from mount, it doesn't matter whether you stay mounted or slide through to bottom — the strangle works the same either way.
- The universal triangle progression restated here: working grip → one-in-one-out → eliminate the shoulder → trap triangle → figure-four.
