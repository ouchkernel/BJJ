# Rear Triangle From Referees Position

## Overview

A specialized application that hinges on correctly reading a specific defensive posture: the **referee's position**, distinct from turtle. This position shows up when attacking armlocks from turtle and the opponent shifts their arm defense — that shift is the cue to switch to Ushiro Sankaku.

## Turtle vs. Referee's Position

- **Turtle**: knees and elbows on the mat — everything (arms, neck) hidden and protected from submissions.
- **Referee's position**: elbows **off** the mat, body locked out, arms exposed. Common across wrestling styles. It's a defensive bottom position typically aimed at a standing escape, not at hiding limbs.
- The intents are opposite: turtle protects the limbs; referee's position raises the head and chest to facilitate standing back up.
- When attacking armlocks from turtle, opponents will often shift into extended arms with elbows off the floor (i.e., into referee's position) as part of their defense — that shift is exactly the trigger for switching to the rear triangle.

## Sequence 1 — Off A Blocked Armlock Roll

1. Attacking a jujigatame roll-over on a turtled opponent; their far arm comes up and blocks the roll.
2. Insert your foot to create the **one-in-one-out** position.
3. Use your thigh to bring their head down and turn them over.
4. Quickly lock the figure-four, strangle.

## Sequence 2 — Off An Extended, Standing-Threat Arm

1. Attacking armlocks from turtle; opponent extends their arm and threatens to stand up.
2. Hook your foot through and around to establish one-in-one-out.
3. Knee pushes into their belly button, turns them over.
4. Switch legs, trap triangle → figure-four → strangle.

## Sequence 3 — Mirror Angle

1. Same mechanics from the opposite facing direction: foot goes across and hooks, roll the opponent through.
2. Switch legs, trap triangle → hand-assist → finish.

## Key Takeaways

- The tell for this whole entry family is the opponent's **elbow position** — elbows off the mat while defending an armlock (referee's position) is fundamentally different from turtle, and it's specifically what opens the door to Ushiro Sankaku.
- The mechanism is consistent across all three sequences: one-in-one-out via a hooked foot, then a turn using the thigh/knee, into trap triangle → figure-four.
- This is a defense-reading skill as much as a technique — recognizing the referee's-position shift while you're mid-armlock-attack is what triggers the switch.
