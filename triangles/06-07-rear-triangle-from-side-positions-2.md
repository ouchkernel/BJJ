# Rear Triangle From Side Positions 2

## Overview

A more sophisticated side-position entry for opponents whose defensive arms are locked in too resolutely for the basic "dig underneath the elbow" method (covered in the previous chapter) to work. Also introduces the system-wide rule that defines every triangle.

## The Wrist-First Trick

Instead of starting your penetration at the elbow, start at the **wrist**:

1. From a side pin, use an underhook / supinated grip to dig in underneath the opponent's wrist.
2. Punch your hand in and secure the wrist immediately, right from the start.
3. Once secured, when the opponent yanks their arm away, it's already stuck — there's nothing to escape.
4. From there, go over the top and lock the back of your hand over their forearm — you've now secured the arm via the wrist instead of fighting for the elbow first.

## Adding Pressure (Sprawl Action)

1. Take your shoulder to the opponent's opposite hip — an action similar to a sprawl.
2. Knees come off the mat, legs separate, shoulder turns down. This makes it very difficult for the opponent to pull their arm free.
3. Hand on the floor, foot-to-foot movement, turn the corner, drive toward the far shoulder — this puts strong pressure on the opponent underneath you.
4. Reach for the opponent's wrist, grab your own wrist, sit down into the lock.
5. Pass your foot all the way through, second leg comes out — two unweighted legs.
6. Foot into the armpit, trap triangle → hand-assist → foot behind the lat muscle → straight finish.

## Alternate Version — Live-Training Detail

- Dig your hand through and lock while staying chest-close to the opponent (in live rolling you can't just reach up from a distance — proximity matters).
- Use the lift of one hand to feed the other hand in.
- Shoulder over the opponent's elbow, sprawl action, drive toward the shoulder to free the arm.
- Clear the legs, one foot ends up outside the opponent's arm and one inside — the **"one in one out"** position.

## The Universal Triangle Rule

Every triangle, without exception, comes down to one simple formula: **one of the opponent's arms trapped outside your legs, and one trapped inside.** If both arms end up on the same side, there is no triangle — you must actively create the "one in, one out" split. Once you have it (foot in the armpit = confirmation), the rest follows the standard pattern: lock a trap triangle, hand-adjust everything up, lock the figure-four, strangle.

## Key Takeaways

- Against tightly-locked defensive arms, penetrate at the **wrist first**, not the elbow — it's harder for the opponent to strip once secured.
- The sprawl-style shoulder pressure (shoulder to opposite hip, knees off mat, legs apart) is what actually pins the arm in place long enough to finish the entry.
- **"One in, one out" is the universal definition of a triangle** — this rule is stated explicitly here and applies to every variation in the system, not just the rear triangle.
