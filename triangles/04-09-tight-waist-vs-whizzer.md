# Triangle From Tight Waist Vs. Whizzer

## Overview

Applies the lateral-hip-movement principle (the core mechanism behind every triangle entry shown so far: collar ties from closed guard, overhooks, tight-waist-vs-whizzer situations) to underhook-based entries from bottom half guard. Specifically targets defeating the whizzer (crossface-style over-the-top pressure) by choosing an underhook target the opponent's whizzer can't collapse.

---

## The core mechanism restated

If your hips are weightless (hanging off the opponent rather than bearing load), lateral hip movement becomes easy — and easy lateral hip movement is what lets you throw your legs into a triangle. This has been the same underlying mechanism in every entry so far; this chapter applies it via underhooks.

## Starting position

Bottom position, hand framed across the opponent as a base, knee shield digging toward an underhook.

## Grip target: far trapezius, not the usual targets

- Normal underhook targets: the opponent's **far hip** or **near shoulder**.
- Problem: going to either of those targets exposes you to the full effect of the opponent's whizzer (over-hook/crossface-style pressure), which tends to collapse you back down to your shoulder.
- Fix: bring the underhook hand up and across to the **far trapezius** instead. This strongly negates the whizzer's effect — it's much harder for the opponent to flatten you from there.

## Entry sequence

1. From the underhook (far trapezius target), come up to an elbow underneath the opponent.
2. Place your feet at the opponent's knee.
3. Come up to a hand next to the opponent.
4. Throw a foot over the opponent's lower back — creates the same weightless-hip effect used everywhere else in the system.
5. Lock your feet (just locking feet is enough initially — the underhook arm will be slightly awkward at first).
6. Lift the underhooked arm free and go back over it, back to controlling the opponent's head.
7. As he pulls away: hook inside his legs, knee as a wedge behind his head, keep ankle and knee close together.
8. The harder he pulls away, the easier the figure-four lock and the strangle become.

## Half guard variant

1. Start in bottom half guard, framing.
2. Come up into the attacking position, hand across.
3. Come off the floor, step over the opponent's far hip — this is what lets your body hang off him and lock a trap triangle.
4. Bring your arm through, punch it over the top, hold his head.
5. Turn the corner, strong strangulation.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Every triangle entry runs on the same fuel: weightless hips → easy lateral movement → legs thrown into the triangle.
- Against a whizzer, avoid the standard underhook targets (far hip, near shoulder) — go to the **far trapezius** instead to neutralize the whizzer's flattening effect.
- Works from both a knee-shield-into-underhook setup and directly from bottom half guard.
- Once locked, the finishing pattern is the same as always: knee wedge behind the head, keep ankle/knee tight, the opponent pulling away tightens the strangle.
