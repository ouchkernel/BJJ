# Relationship Between Front Triangle And Other Submissions

## Overview

Introduces a new category of triangle entry: threatening a *different* submission first, so the opponent's own defensive reaction hands you the triangle. This chapter covers the classic and most common example — threatening an armlock (Juji Gatame / Ude Gatame) from closed guard and converting the defense into a triangle — starting with the basic version, then a more advanced two-stage setup.

---

## Basic ("Jiu-Jitsu 101") method

1. From closed guard, control an armlock: wrist, elbow, foot on the hip, come up to the side, turn — standard Juji Gatame entry.
2. If the opponent yanks the arm free: let the arm go, bring your leg around the other side, turn across in the opposite direction, into the triangle.

This works, and has for years — but is well known enough that most training partners will already recognize and defend it.

## Advanced method: two-stage arm attack

At beginner levels, simply tracking an arm and turning to an angle is usually enough for an armlock, because opponents don't yet recognize the danger. At higher levels, opponents see the grip and the foot-on-hip cue coming and pull out early — killing both the armlock and any natural transition to a triangle.

**The two-stage fix:**
1. Get postural control first.
2. Draw the elbow across the centerline, throw the legs up into a **top lock** — feet locking on top of the opponent's shoulders (rather than the traditional closed-guard lock around the lower back).
3. Because the lock is higher, it's much harder for the opponent to posture away — this forces him into a defensive arm configuration.
4. From there, go into the armlock a second time.

## The triangle payoff

- The top lock creates an excellent early switch opportunity into the triangle.
- Opponent sees the armlock danger and pulls the elbow out to defend → this **gives you the trap triangle on the opposite side** almost for free.
- Lock the figure-four from there for the finish.

---

## Key Takeaways

- A new entry category: threaten a different submission, let the opponent's defense create the triangle opportunity.
- Basic armlock-to-triangle (letting the arm go, turning across) works but is well known and easy for experienced partners to anticipate.
- The **top lock** (feet locked over the shoulders rather than the lower back) is the key mechanical upgrade: it denies posture, forcing the two-stage sequence to actually threaten the arm a second time.
- When the opponent defends the armlock by pulling the elbow free, that pull directly creates a trap triangle on the opposite side.
