# Entries To Front Triangle - Seated Position

## Overview

Transitions from finishing mechanics into entries. Argues for starting triangle entries from a **seated** bottom position rather than the traditional supine (flat-on-back) closed guard position most people are taught first, and explains why.

---

## Why seated, not supine

- ~90% of people learn their first triangle from supine closed guard — flat on the back. The instructor deliberately doesn't teach it that way.
- Seated position offers much greater mobility: the ability to turn, move, get the lower back off the ground, and spin on the shoulders — all things already established as central to good triangle mechanics.
- It's also much easier to move both yourself *and* the opponent from a seated position in ways that set up the "one-in-one-out" scenario the whole triangle system depends on.
- Conclusion: all front triangle training in this course begins from bottom position, **seated**, not supine.

## What's needed: an effective working grip

Nothing happens without first securing a grip. Two-on-one gripping is introduced here as the primary tool — and is described as also being the single grip family most responsible for actual triangle finishes in competition.

### Basic seated entry sequence (with opponent on two knees)
1. Left hand pushes opponent's right hand to the centerline.
2. Right hand secures a pronated grip on the wrist.
3. Slide up the forearm to an elbow grip: thumb closes over the humerus, four fingers control right at the cusp of the elbow.
4. Take the opponent's wrist across your body to your own hip, head forward, elbows tucked to your body — this hides the one weak point of the grip (opponent pulling their thumb back toward themselves).

### From there, entering the triangle
1. Place a foot on the opponent's hip on the same side as the trapped arm.
2. This creates an **unweighted leg** — since your weight is on your buttocks in the seated position, this leg becomes highly mobile and hard for the opponent to catch.
3. As the opponent tries to catch your foot, circle around their attempt and place the foot on their bicep instead — pulling them into the beginning of the trap triangle.
4. Throw the legs into the trap triangle, both feet pointing forward (unified).
5. As the opponent brings their knees in to try to posture, use the knee-pull to bring their head to you.
6. Get the scoop grip, turn the corner, place the wedges, lock the figure-four.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Start all front triangle entries from seated, not supine** — seated offers superior mobility for both you and manipulating the opponent, which is exactly what triangle entries require.
- Two-on-one gripping is the foundational entry tool — described as the single most decisive grip family for actual triangle finishes in competition, not just a good teaching tool.
- Core entry motif: create an **unweighted leg** off the seated position, use it to circle around the opponent's attempts to catch it, and land it on the bicep to begin the trap triangle.
- This entire sequence is just the "one-in-one-out → trap triangle → knee pull → figure-four" story from the previous lesson, applied concretely.
