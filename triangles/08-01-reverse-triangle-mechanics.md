# Reverse Triangle — Gyaku Sankaku

## Overview

The reverse triangle (*gyaku sankaku*) is the lowest-scoring of the five triangles in international competition — but not one to ignore. Done well, it may be the **tightest** strangle of all five triangles; done poorly, it's the **weakest and least effective**. It demands more precision than the others.

---

## What the move is

- Start with the training partner in a supine position. Can be applied from top or bottom.
- Step over into a **one-in-one-out** situation: one of the opponent's arms ends up outside your legs, one ends up inside. (This one-in-one-out setup is universal across all five triangles, regardless of type.)
- Bring your leg across the opponent's shoulder line — this is the "strangle leg." You need to eliminate the opponent's shoulder from the equation: everything comes up above the shoulder line with the strangle leg.
- From this position, any back-heel action creates a very strong strangulation.

## The critical mechanical detail: knee below the ear

Same governing principle as the front triangle: the proper placement for strangulation is **your knee below the opponent's ear**, directly on the carotid artery, with your shin across (above) the line of the opponent's shoulders, perpendicular to their spine.

- If your knee ends up **above** the opponent's ear, the strangle is close to worthless — the opponent can simply push you off like a bench press, no matter how hard you squeeze.
- When you lock the figure-four with the knee correctly placed below the ear, the opponent's shoulder is eliminated from the triangle entirely — nothing left between your legs but neck and arm — and the strangle comes on immediately.
- This holds true whether applied from top or bottom position (mirrored mechanics).

## Assessment

- Statistically the lowest-scoring of the five triangles in competition, but still capable of remarkable victories and worth adding to your arsenal.
- The gap between "done well" and "done poorly" is unusually large for this triangle — most of the variance comes down to your precision on the knee/ear/carotid placement, not the opponent's resistance.

---

## Key Takeaways

- One-in-one-out setup (universal to all triangles) + strangle leg above the opponent's shoulder line = the base position.
- **Knee below the ear, on the carotid artery** is the single detail that determines whether this triangle is devastating or worthless.
- Shin sits across/above the line of the shoulders, perpendicular to the spine, to fully eliminate the opponent's shoulder from the lock.
- Works identically from top or bottom position — mechanics simply mirror.
- Reputation: least-scoring in competition, but arguably the tightest strangle of the five when executed with correct knee placement.
