# Intro To Rear Triangle — Ushiro Sankaku

## Overview

Opens the rear triangle (Ushiro Sankaku) section of the system. Central claim: of all five triangles, this is the easiest one for a short-legged person to lock on a big, broad-shouldered opponent — and the reason comes down to a mechanical accident of the position itself, not extra skill.

## Why Short Legs Struggle With Broad Shoulders (And Why This Triangle Is Different)

- Locking a triangle on a big person with short legs comes down to two things: **alignment** with the opponent, and **eliminating the shoulder** from the triangle.
- In a front triangle, keeping the opponent's shoulder *out* of your lock takes active work — forming a good angle, getting your inner thigh to brush their tricep. Skip that work and the shoulder gets caught inside the triangle, weakening the strangle.
- In the rear triangle, the position itself almost guarantees shoulder elimination. Moving around the training partner and locking in naturally forces their shoulder out of the triangle — it's hard to get this wrong.
- Visual confirmation: once locked, you can see the opponent's rear deltoid clearly outside the triangle — only the head and arm are caught inside.

## The Hip-Height Detail

- If your hips sit **below** the opponent's shoulder line, you'll struggle to lock a full triangle at all — demonstrated live, even against a training partner who isn't especially broad-shouldered.
- Bring your hips **above** the shoulder line, and locking becomes easy — only the head and arm end up trapped, producing a strong strangle.
- This is the single adjustment to check first if the lock feels loose.

## Recommendation

If you've historically struggled with short legs versus broad-shouldered opponents on other triangles, start building confidence with Ushiro Sankaku specifically — it's the most forgiving of the five. Success here tends to transfer: confidence gained from finishing people with the rear triangle carries over and makes the other triangles easier too.

## Key Takeaways

- Rear triangle is the most forgiving of the 5 triangles for short-legged people against broad-shouldered opponents — shoulder elimination happens almost automatically from the position itself.
- Front triangle requires active work (angle + inner thigh to tricep) to eliminate the shoulder; rear triangle does it for you.
- Check hip height first: hips above the opponent's shoulder line = easy lock with only head+arm trapped. Hips below = you'll fight the lock.
- If broad-shouldered opponents give you trouble elsewhere, build your triangle confidence here first and let it transfer.
