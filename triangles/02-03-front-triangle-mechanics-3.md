# Front Triangle Mechanics 3 — Knee Position, Inner Thigh, and Foot Direction

## Overview

Third mechanics lesson in the front triangle series — the positioning of the strangle leg gets the same precision most people already give their arm when doing a rear naked strangle. Covers where the knee folds relative to the ear, where the inner thigh contacts the opponent's arm, and which direction the lower legs should point.

---

## Knee below the ear, not above it

- Common mistake: placing the crook of the knee **on top of the opponent's ear**. This drops strangle effectiveness considerably and makes it easy for the opponent to posture and defend the carotid artery.
- Correct target: the crook of the knee directly on the opponent's **carotid artery**, i.e. **below** the ear.
- Getting this right means the far knee becomes a perfect wedge behind the opponent's head, and the eventual lock of the triangle sits directly **behind the head** rather than behind the shoulder.
- A lock behind the head survives posturing and even standing up — demonstrated live, the strangle stays on and tightens as the opponent stands.
- A lock behind the shoulder (the common error) leaves too much mass inside the triangle, lets the opponent posture easily, and the opponent's elbow can jam the squeeze so no real contraction is possible.
- **Watchword: knee of the strangle leg below the ear, lock behind the head and neck — not behind the shoulder.**

## Inner thigh: tricep, not lat muscle

- When turning into a triangle, the inner part of your thigh should press against the opponent's **tricep**, not their **lat muscle**.
- If the inner thigh ends up on the lat muscle, this gives the opponent an opening to posture up, and the triangle's integrity is compromised from the start.
- Correct version: inner thigh brushes along the tricep as you turn the corner — when the opponent tries to pull the arm out, everything stays locked in place, and the shoulder ends up outside the lock (good).

## Foot direction: both feet point forward

- Simple rule: **both lower legs should point in the same forward direction**, not down toward the opponent's hips.
- If the lower legs point down toward the hips, the inner thigh can't stay pinned on the tricep properly.
- Throwing the legs so both feet point forward ensures the inner thigh sits against the tricep, making it very difficult for the opponent to yank the arm free, and producing an unusually tight strangle immediately once the figure-four locks.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Knee position:** below the ear, not above it — this is one of the single biggest correctable errors in triangle finishing.
- **Inner thigh contact point:** tricep = good (locks the arm, shoulder stays excluded). Lat muscle = bad (gives the opponent a posturing opportunity).
- **Foot direction:** both feet point forward, not down toward the hips — this is what keeps the inner thigh pinned to the tricep.
- These are small positional adjustments, but they produce large, concrete differences in finishing percentage — whether the figure-four is "unusually tight" from the moment it closes, or loose and easy to escape.
