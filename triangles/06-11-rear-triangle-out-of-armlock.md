# Rear Triangle Out Of Armlock

## Overview

Covers the relationship between the cross armlock (jujigatame) and the rear triangle — specifically how a compromised armlock, where the opponent defends by attacking your cross-face leg, converts directly into Ushiro Sankaku.

## The Escape Being Countered

- In any jujigatame, you have a **cross-face leg** (controls the head) and a **torso leg** (controls the torso).
- A common (not the most technical, but real and effective) opponent escape: push your cross-face leg away and over their head, then re-lock their hands or turn out.
- If they succeed in removing the cross-face leg and you still try to finish the armlock, their head comes over your leg and the armlock is gone.

## Sequence 1 — Countering The Cross-Face Leg Escape

1. The moment you feel the opponent attacking and removing your cross-face leg over the top, curl that foot around their head so your **knee sits at the crown of their head**.
2. It's now in the opponent's own interest to keep pushing over your leg (to escape) — let them.
3. As they do, take your other foot just over their shoulder and rotate your body around, as if looking over their head.
4. This opens the door to bring your legs in and lock a **trap triangle**.
5. Catch their wrist as usual, hand-assist everything north, lock up Ushiro Sankaku.
6. Result: their escape attempt (which killed your armlock) becomes the setup for a successful strangle. Twisting or moving out from here is very difficult.

## Sequence 2 — Staying Crowded Over The Head

1. Same starting problem — armlock integrity compromised at the cross-face leg.
2. Key detail: keep everything **crowded over the opponent's head**. The further out you are, the easier it is for them to escape.
3. As they push their head over the top (their own escape motion), that's exactly the cue you want.
4. Take your foot out, get a hold of it, turn an angle, and walk up.
5. Collect the opponent's knuckles to your chest, apply pressure — the strangle is complete.

## Key Takeaways

- A jujigatame compromised at the cross-face leg is not a lost position — the opponent's own escape movement (pushing your leg over their head) is the raw material for a direct entry into Ushiro Sankaku.
- Curl your foot so the knee sits at the crown of their head, then let their escape motion carry your body around into the triangle.
- Staying crowded over the opponent's head throughout is what keeps the conversion available — distance is what lets them actually escape clean.
