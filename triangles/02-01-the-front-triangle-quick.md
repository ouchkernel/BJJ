# The Front Triangle — Quick Reference

## The myth to kill

"My legs are too short" — mostly not true. Good mechanics beats leg length.

## Two rules, memorize both

1. **Strangle leg stays above the opponent's shoulder line.** Drop below it before locking figure-four = you'll only ever get half a triangle.
2. **Eliminate the shoulder.** Knee goes directly in front of the opponent's deltoid so their shoulder visually disappears from your triangle. If you can still see the shoulder — reset, you don't have it yet.

## Two legs, two jobs

- **Strangle leg**: crosses the neck perpendicular to the spine (not aligned with it). Does the choking.
- **Support leg**: locks the strangle leg in place.

## Do this

1. Get the strangle leg perpendicular across the neck, not lined up with the spine.
2. Turn your knee in front of their deltoid — shoulder disappears from the lock.
3. Check: can you (or a camera) see the shoulder? If yes, redo the angle.
4. Lock figure-four only once shoulder is excluded and leg is above the shoulder line.

## Why it matters

A real triangle lock is a narrow space — barely wider than a forearm. Trap the shoulder inside and you need way more leg length than you have. Exclude the shoulder and even short legs close it easily.
