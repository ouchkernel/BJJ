# Triangle Vs Single Leg

## Overview

Opens a run of five chapters on using the front triangle (and related triangles) as a counter to the single leg takedown — described as arguably the most common takedown and reversal in the sport. This chapter covers the base scenario and the first method: the step-over.

## The scenario

- Opponent shoots in and secures a single leg with the head on the inside, both knees down, looking to put you on your back. This can arise from a standing shot, a bottom-position escape (opponent gets an underhook and comes up into a single leg), or a half guard top scramble.
- However it happens, the moment the opponent is in on a single leg down on the mat is a prime opportunity to attack with a triangle rather than just defend the takedown.

## Method: step-over

1. Put in a tight waist on the opponent (don't leave both your legs on the mat — that lets them switch to a double leg and finish the takedown).
2. Take the free leg off the mat.
3. Stuff the opponent's head down.
4. Use your knee as a blocker next to the opponent's elbow; post your other hand on the floor (never fall to an elbow or shoulder — you need a height advantage: your head and hips must end up higher than theirs).
5. Pull the opponent's trapped elbow up to your hip.
6. In one motion, step your body across, catch the opponent's head with your hand, switch that hand to the floor, and bring your knee around the corner.
7. Head goes to the floor, lock the figure-four, and finish with a strangle (or arm lock).

## Key detail

Basing on your **hand**, not an elbow or shoulder, is what preserves the height advantage needed to get your head down and into position for the triangle. Falling to an elbow collapses the height difference and ruins the entry.

## Key Takeaways

- Single leg takedowns/reversals are one of the best opportunities in the sport to attack with a triangle, not just defend.
- Core sequence: tight waist + free leg off the mat → stuff the head → knee blocks the elbow, hand bases on the floor → pull the elbow to your hip and step over → head to the floor → figure-four.
- Always base on a hand, never an elbow or shoulder — losing height kills the entry before it starts.
