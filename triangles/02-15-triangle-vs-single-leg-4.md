# Triangle Vs Single Leg 4 — Side Triangle (Yoko Sankaku) Counter

## Overview

Switches from front-triangle-family counters to the **yoko sankaku** (side triangle) as another way to deal with a strong single-leg wrestler down on the mat.

## Entry

1. Control the opponent's head, take your leg off the mat.
2. Come up with your body to create space between you.
3. Hook your foot into the opponent's armpit.
4. Return to a tight waist, post your hand on the floor.
5. Fall — **to the rear corner, not straight to the side** (falling across the opponent's body is a worse outcome; sitting back and posting the hand is the correct fall direction).
6. This turns the opponent over.

## Control and finish

1. Switch off to a **Kimura lock** on the near-side arm for control/security.
2. Put your thigh underneath the opponent's head, come up, go belly-down, and extend your leg as far as it will go toward the far arm.
3. Return to the security of the Kimura grip as your default anchor.
4. From here you have a strangle hold available, plus — as with any sankaku position — arm lock and wrist lock finishes on both of the opponent's arms.

## Key detail: heel deep in the armpit

The whole entry is built around getting the leg in **as deep as possible** into the opponent's armpit before locking — popping up first so that when you return to the strangle, the effect is fast and tight.

## Key Takeaways

- Side triangle (yoko sankaku) is another legitimate answer to a strong single-leg wrestler, alongside the front-triangle-family counters covered earlier.
- Fall to the **rear corner**, not straight across the opponent's body — this determines whether the entry works cleanly.
- The Kimura lock is the security blanket throughout this sequence — return to it whenever the position gets uncertain.
- Once locked, sankaku positions offer strangle, arm lock, and wrist lock finishes on both arms, not just the strangle.
