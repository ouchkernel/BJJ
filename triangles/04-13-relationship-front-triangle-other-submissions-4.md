# Relationship Between Front Triangle And Other Submissions 4 (Omoplata)

## Overview

Fourth and final submission-to-triangle relationship: the **omoplata**. Omoplata is easy to enter but notoriously hard to actually finish, especially without a gi — its most common defensive response (a forward roll to relieve pressure) is exactly what sets up the triangle conversion covered here.

---

## Why omoplata sets up triangles well

- Easy to enter, hard to finish (especially no-gi).
- Common defense: opponent forward-rolls to relieve the shoulder pressure.
- That forward roll is the trigger for this conversion.

## Entry to omoplata

1. From a standard guard situation, apply **the clamp**: knee in front of the opponent's shoulder, second knee behind the opposite shoulder — he can't easily yank out.
2. Feed your foot through and across in front of his face/head.
3. Go around the corner into the omoplata.

## The head-position race

- Opponent wants his head to rise (the higher it gets, the less vulnerable he is).
- Your job: keep the head down. Use your straightened leg to push and hold the head down long enough to threaten.
- If he's naive and stays, the omoplata finishes there.

## Converting the forward roll into a triangle

1. When the opponent capitulates into a forward roll: extract your arm, bring your head well forward.
2. Your leg comes around behind you as he rolls up.
3. As he comes up out of the roll: drive across him, hug the tricep.
4. Post your hand on the floor, back-step across his body — locks the one-in-one-out.
5. Right foot out, left foot around the corner, catch him — you're now in **Mote Sankaku** (front triangle).
6. Head down brings the feet light — quickly lock the figure-four for a strong finish, from either top or bottom.

## Detail pass (pendulum mechanics)

- Clamp → pass foot through/around, turn out to the side.
- Use the outside leg like a pendulum: weight goes on the inside leg, pendulum with the outside leg to rise and attack.
- Keep everything out and away to the side — if the opponent's head rises, he can put you on your back. This is what forces him into the forward roll.
- When the roll happens: hands down, catch him, back-step around the corner, lock up, rise, foot comes out, capture the head, switch into the front triangle, rise-lock-strangle.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Omoplata's value here is the same pattern as Ude Gatame: it's a real threat that reliably provokes a specific defensive reaction (the forward roll) that hands you a triangle.
- Keeping the opponent's head down (via a straightened leg) is what forces the forward-roll decision in the first place — if his head rises, the position reverses on you.
- The pendulum action (weight on the inside leg, swing with the outside leg) is the mechanical tool that lets you track and follow the opponent's forward roll into position.
- Once the roll happens: extract, drive across, back-step, and you land directly in Mote Sankaku (front triangle) — a fast, low-risk conversion.
