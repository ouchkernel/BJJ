# Linking Side Triangle And Arm Bar (Juji Gatame)

## Overview

Two ways the side triangle links directly with armbar (juji gatame) attempts: converting a poorly-positioned armbar attempt into a side triangle, and deliberately using a "beginner's mistake" in a spinning armbar entry to set up the strangle instead.

---

## From a near-side-foot armbar attempt

1. Starting point: attempting an armbar with your foot on the near side (a beginner-style entry, closer to a lockdown-type armbar descent).
2. Take that near foot and place it through the opponent's armpit — same one-in-one-out prerequisite as always.
3. Tilt to the side and extend your bottom leg through, straightening your legs.
4. **Same lesson as always applies here**: attempting to lock the triangle directly from this position requires very long legs and usually fails for anyone else. The fix is the same belly-down shift — foot to the mat, come up, shift so your leg kicks all the way through.
5. Coming back from the shift puts you in a vastly superior position: the opponent's shoulder is eliminated from the triangle, and the strangulation pressure is immense.

## From a spinning armbar entry

The classic spinning juji gatame: underhook, post the far hand, spin around the corner as the opponent locks their hands defensively.

### The common beginner error
Placing your knee **inside** the opponent's armpit as you spin into the armbar. This traps your foot in a bad position when you go to finish, and — critically — you end up with **no cross-face leg**, which every real juji gatame needs. Without it, the opponent can easily subvert the armbar and escape even if you bring both feet across.

### Turning the mistake into a weapon
Once you understand why that knee placement fails the armbar, you can use it **deliberately** — not to attempt the armbar at all, but specifically to transition into yoko senkaku:

1. Take the underhook as normal.
2. **Deliberately** place your knee inside the opponent's armpit (this is now a setup, not a mistake).
3. Sit through into the position.
4. Come up and lock a kimura.
5. Tilt toward the hips, kick your bottom leg through.
6. Belly-down shift — punch your leg as far through as it will go.
7. Come back into an extremely tight stranglehold as the opponent tries and fails to pull away.

This works as a genuine ruse: to the opponent, it looks like a normal (if slightly off) spinning armbar attempt. In reality you never intended to finish the armbar at all.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Any armbar attempt that leaves your leg badly positioned for a triangle can be salvaged with the same belly-down shift used throughout this system.
- The classic spinning-armbar beginner error (knee inside the armpit, killing the cross-face leg) is a real technical mistake **for the armbar** — but a deliberate, effective setup **for the side triangle**.
- Same finishing mechanics apply once you're in position: kimura grip, tilt to hips, kick the leg through, belly-down shift, lock.
