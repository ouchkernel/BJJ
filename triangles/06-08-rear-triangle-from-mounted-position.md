# Rear Triangle From Mounted Position

## Overview

Addresses an apparent contradiction: mount is a face-to-face position, so how do you get to a *rear* triangle from there? Answer — the entry rotates the opponent's arm and your own body position around until the same rear-triangle mechanics apply.

## Setup

Mounted on the opponent, who is in a fairly standard defensive posture underneath you.

## Sequence

1. Start with **inside position** on the opponent's hands — always begin this entry from inside position.
2. Bring one hand through and start walking it out (don't worry about where their other hand goes).
3. This gives you access to secure the opponent at the head — hand goes underneath, grip the lat muscle on the opposite side. Take your other hand underneath the opponent's arm, working your elbow up toward their elbow.
4. **Critical detail: keep your own elbow off the mat.** If your elbow touches down and the opponent clamps over your arm, they can bridge and roll you. This is the central battle of the whole entry.
5. Walk your arm out and away, in the direction your fingers are pointing — avoid walking straight over the opponent's head.
6. Cycle **"walk and straighten, walk and straighten"** repeatedly until the opponent's elbow rises above their own **shoulder line** (the straight line through both of their shoulders). Your goal: get their elbow above that line. Their goal: keep it below.
7. Once the elbow is above the shoulder line: bring your pelvis/hips forward, hug the opponent, take your head **outside** their elbow, connect your chin to your bicep, and begin trapping their arm across the body.
8. Bring your arms through — hug over the head, take one elbow out, bring your knee up to touch your own elbow — this lets you go through and capture the opponent's arm at the wrist.
9. Extract your foot — you now have two unweighted feet. Separate them and put one foot into the armpit — this is the "one in, one out" position, the ultimate goal of every triangle entry.
10. Connect their arm/elbow tight to your chest, hands go wrist-to-wrist and overhead. Unified legs (pointing the same direction) → lock a trap triangle → hand-assist, knees come up north → lock the full figure-four → strangle.

## Key Takeaways

- The entire entry is a battle over one detail: getting the opponent's elbow **above their own shoulder line** while keeping your own elbow off the mat.
- "Walk and straighten" is the repeating micro-cycle that wins that battle — you rarely get the elbow above the line in one motion.
- Once elbow position is won, the rest of the sequence (hug, trap the arm, capture the wrist, one-in-one-out, trap triangle → figure-four) follows the same universal pattern as every other rear-triangle entry.
