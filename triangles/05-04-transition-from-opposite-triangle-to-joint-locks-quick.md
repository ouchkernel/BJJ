# Opposite Triangle → Joint Locks — Quick Reference

## The one diagnostic that matters

- [ ] Knee **under** the armpit → strangle works, squeeze and finish.
- [ ] Knee **above/outside** the armpit → strangle is DEAD. Zero chance, no matter how hard you squeeze. Stop trying, switch immediately.

## When knee is high: go to kimura

1. Push away so your knees clear your own chest.
2. Catch the opponent's wrist.
3. Bring their elbow up to shoulder line (tuck your own elbow to your armpit to force this).
4. Fist just inside your knee.
5. Finish the kimura.

## Full sequence recap

1. Working grip (e.g. elbow 2-on-1).
2. Foot penetrates over opponent's leg → trap triangle (one arm in, one out).
3. Turn same direction opponent is facing (driven by outside leg).
4. Knee gets under armpit → figure-four, strangle.
5. Knee ends up on top of shoulder instead → don't force it, go straight to kimura.

## 4-step system for hantai sankaku

1. Grip.
2. Trap triangle.
3. Manage transition to figure-four — win here if knee position is good.
4. Knee position bad / strangle not working → **kimura** (best joint-lock option for this position).
