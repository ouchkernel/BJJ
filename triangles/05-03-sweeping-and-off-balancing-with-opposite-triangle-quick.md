# Sweeping & Off-Balancing With Opposite Triangle — Quick Reference

## The big idea

Front triangle + opposite triangle + rear triangle (category A) = one system. Get one, you can usually get the other two by changing angle. Play all three, opponent can't keep up.

## Combo 1: sweep forward → front triangle

1. Lock hantai sankaku figure-four (head pinched).
2. Drive your body **forward** (not sideways).
3. Opponent bracing backward can't stop the knockover.
4. Opponent recovers up → usually lands right back in your original front triangle.
5. Finish: slide inside leg, turn corner, eliminate shoulder, strangle.

## Combo 2: sweep to the side → rear triangle

1. Sweep opponent onto their back from hantai sankaku.
2. Opponent refuses to turn back in (smart — avoids giving you front triangle)?
3. Base hand on floor.
4. Other hand under opponent's trapped arm (kimura optional here, not required).
5. Plant foot, shift hips, rotate around opponent's body.
6. Gather opponent's fist to your chest.
7. → Ushiro sankaku (rear triangle). Close knees over each other. Strangle.

## Either way, you win

- Opponent turns in → front triangle.
- Opponent stays down → rear triangle.

## The skill to actually train

Moving your legs freely around the opponent's head/shoulders so you can switch between all three triangle types on demand.

## The 4-step triangle system (recap)

1. Working grip.
2. Trap triangle — one arm in, one arm out, head caught in the middle.
3. Manage transition: trap triangle → full figure-four. (Most finishes happen here.)
4. Strangle not working? → go to a joint lock.
