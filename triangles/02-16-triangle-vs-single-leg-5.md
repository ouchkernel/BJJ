# Triangle Vs Single Leg 5 — The Slouch Entry To Side Triangle

## Overview

A second, favorite method for entering the side triangle (yoko sankaku) against a single leg — using a slouch rather than a fall to the rear corner. Explicitly flagged as carrying some risk, since you're trading top position for bottom, so precision matters.

## Entry: slouch instead of fall

1. Opponent is deep on the single leg, head on the inside, as before.
2. Hook in on the opponent and start **slouching** down rather than falling to a side/rear corner.
3. From the slouch, nothing stops you from grabbing your own foot and pulling it high up into the opponent's armpit.
4. Lock the figure-four, making whatever adjustments are needed to get everything satisfactory.
5. Return to the waist grip and apply the strangle.

## Key detail: heel high in the armpit, not low on the tricep

- The further your heel travels down toward the tricep instead of staying high in the armpit, the weaker the strangle becomes.
- Actively hike the foot high before locking the figure-four.

## Managing the risk

- You're seated on your buttocks during this entry — a real risk since you started on top and are trading it for bottom position, so this has to be executed correctly.
- Sequence to do it safely: stuff the head, hand posts on the floor, pull back with one leg while slouching with the other, grab your foot and lock the figure-four high in the armpit (hand contact preferred, elbow contact at minimum).
- Don't let your foot dangle loose while adjusting — if the opponent's arm comes free in that gap, they can complete a double leg and finish the takedown.
- If the strangle alone doesn't finish, keep the tight waist, grab the opponent's foot, and turn them over onto their back — you end up in the same yoko sankaku position and can finish from top instead.

## Key Takeaways

- The slouch entry is a favorite alternative to falling to the rear corner for getting into the side triangle off a single leg.
- Heel must stay **high in the armpit** — low on the tricep weakens the strangle significantly.
- This entry briefly cedes top position, so precision matters: don't let the foot dangle mid-adjustment, and know the fallback (turn the opponent over) if the direct strangle doesn't finish.
