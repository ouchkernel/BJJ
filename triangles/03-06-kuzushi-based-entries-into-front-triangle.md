# Kuzushi Based Entries Into Front Triangle

## Overview

A different category of entry from the grip-based methods covered so far: instead of relying on a specific hand grip, you off-balance (kuzushi) your opponent so they're forced to post a hand on the mat — and once a hand touches the mat, the triangle is available almost regardless of what upper-body grip you happen to have.

---

## The method: push on the knee

1. Place a foot on your training partner's knee, **toes pointing outward** — never toes pointing upward, which slips off. Curve your foot around the knee to form an effective hook.
2. Combine a slight pull (on whatever grip you have) with a push through that foot.
3. This off-balances your opponent enough that they have to put a hand down on the mat, or face-plant. All humans are hardwired to recover their balance — this reflex is universal and can't be trained away.
4. It doesn't matter what grip you're using when this happens — bicep ties, an inside bicep/tricep tie, whatever. The moment a hand touches the mat, the triangle is right there.

### From a hand-down position
5. Come up to the head and pull the opponent forward.
6. Cover the arm, cover the hip, turn the corner, knee to the crotch — the same finishing pattern as every other entry in this course.
7. Lock the figure-four for the strangle.

### Drilling note
This can be drilled repeatedly and spontaneously in a hand-fighting exchange — every time the opponent's hand touches the mat (voluntarily or from the push), go straight into head control and the same corner turn.

## Push, don't kick

A critical detail: you always **push** the opponent's leg, never kick it. A kick only makes your training partner angry and doesn't create the same off-balancing effect. A push destabilizes them; a kick doesn't.

## When this applies

General rule of thumb for lower-body vs. upper-body attacks: when an opponent stands over you, their legs are extended and vulnerable, so you attack the legs (Ashi Garami). When an opponent is on two knees, their legs are harder to attack, so upper-body attacks — including this kuzushi entry — make more sense.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Off-balancing (kuzushi) is a grip-independent way into the triangle — it works with whatever grip you already have.
- Push the opponent's knee, toes out, curved hook, combined with a pull — this forces a hand to the mat via a universal human balance reflex.
- **Push, never kick** the leg — kicking has less mechanical effect and just provokes your partner.
- General rule: opponent standing over you → lower-body attacks. Opponent on two knees → upper-body attacks (like this entry).
- Finish pattern is unchanged: cover arm, cover hip, turn corner, knee to crotch, figure-four.
