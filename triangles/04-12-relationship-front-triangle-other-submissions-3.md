# Relationship Between Front Triangle And Other Submissions 3 (Ude Gatame)

## Overview

Third submission-to-triangle relationship: **Ude Gatame** (straight armlock), attacked from top position with an underhook/clamp setup. Ude Gatame is described as not the tightest or highest-percentage submission on its own — but it reliably scares opponents into overreacting, which opens up the triangle.

---

## Why Ude Gatame works as a setup tool

- It's a serious threat — if the opponent doesn't react, the arm breaks.
- It's not the tightest hold in the sport and is comparatively easy to escape directly — but that's not the point. It "scares the crap out of people," causing defensive overreactions that open other submissions, especially the triangle.

## Setup

1. Opponent working in your guard, you secure any form of underhook (e.g. via a collar tie pulled through).
2. Set the underhook up with a variation of **the clamp**: knee in front of the shoulder, knee behind, extended arm with the underhook.
3. Go around and apply Ude Gatame pressure: hand on your own elbow, hips away from him, head toward him.
4. If he doesn't react, he's finished right there.

## The common escape (and how it opens the triangle)

- Standard defensive reaction: bring the head up, rotate the elbow in.
- This is a **battle for head position** — the closer his head gets to you, the easier it is for him to rotate the elbow to safety.
- Counter: if you feel you're losing the Ude Gatame, extend your leg across his shoulders, foot inside the far shoulder.
- As his head comes in toward you (his own escape motion), use **foot penetration** — foot comes back, and as the Ude Gatame is lost and he turns in, catch right over the top directly into the trap triangle.
- Standard finish: cover the head, scoop the legs, feet pointing forward, knee wedge behind his head, figure-four.

## Alternate entry (under-the-arm clamp variant)

1. Secure an initial underhook, pull the opponent down into a clamp-like position — but **under** the arm this time (not over, as in the standard clamp).
2. Attack Ude Gatame. When you feel it fail, touch knee and ankle together on the far shoulder.
3. Extract and penetrate with the foot over the opponent's neck — lock the trap triangle.
4. Control posture (he'll try to sit up), scoop inside his leg, lock figure-four, strangle.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Ude Gatame's real value here isn't its own finishing rate — it's how reliably it triggers an overreaction (head-up, elbow-rotate escape) that hands you a triangle.
- The escape battle is fundamentally about **head position** — the opponent bringing his head toward you to rotate free is exactly what sets up your foot penetration into the trap triangle.
- Two setups shown: over-the-arm clamp and under-the-arm clamp — both funnel a failed Ude Gatame into the same trap-triangle-to-figure-four finish.
